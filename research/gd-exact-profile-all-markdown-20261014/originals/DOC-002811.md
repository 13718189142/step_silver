## 1. Exact target and coefficient convention

Fix a finite nonnegative word h=(h_0,...,h_(n-1)), S=sum h_i, and M=1+2S.
The trailing parameter is d=1. The ambient Euclidean dimension is independent
of this scalar d and is unrestricted. For a nonempty compact convex body D
containing zero, P denotes its Euclidean projection and

    U_t(X)=max_(g in D) [2<X,g>-t||g||^2], t>0.

The physical trajectory is x_(i+1)=x_i-h_i g_i, g_i=P(x_i). The terminal
g_n=P(x_n) is included although it supplies no update. The sole auxiliary
sample is p=P(x_0/M). Its input is x_0/M; p supplies no physical step.
Normalize ||x_0||^2=1 and maximize

    V=U_1(x_n)-U_M(x_0)
     =2<x_n,g_n>-||g_n||^2-2<x_0,p>+M||p||^2.       (1)

Use k=n+3 formal vectors in the order (x_0,g_0,...,g_n,p). Let e_x,e_i,e_p
be their coefficient columns. These bookkeeping columns are not physical
vectors. Define labels L=(0,...,n,p,star), also of size k, and columns

    a_i=e_x-sum_(j<i) h_j e_j, b_i=e_i, 0<=i<=n;
    a_p=e_x/M, b_p=e_p; a_star=b_star=0.

Thus X_i is represented by a_i and G_i by b_i. For columns u,v put
sym(u,v)=(uv^T+vu^T)/2. The full Gram Z has size k by k. The original row
for EVERY ordered pair i!=j in L is

    Q_(i,j)=<X_j-G_j,G_i-G_j>
           =<A_(i,j),Z> <=0,
    A_(i,j)=sym(a_j-b_j,b_i-b_j),                  (2)

where <A,Z>=sum_(r,s) A_rs Z_rs. Both orientations are retained. Rows with
i=star test the zero generator. Rows with j=star are identically zero and
are retained, with their original matrices and returned solver multipliers.
Self rows are identically zero; the model includes precisely all distinct
ordered pairs. The normalization matrix is E=e_x e_x^T. The objective is

    C=2sym(a_n,b_n)-b_n b_n^T-2sym(e_x,e_p)+M e_p e_p^T.

The exact primal is

    maximize <C,Z> subject to Z>=0, <E,Z>=1,
                              <A_(i,j),Z><=0 (i!=j).    (3)

Every displayed expression is linear in Z apart from the PSD cone.

## 2. Necessity and sufficiency, including degeneracy

If G_j=P_D(X_j), projection optimality gives (2) for every generator
G_i in D, including zero. To see necessity without invoking interpolation,
differentiate ||X_j-(G_j+t(G_i-G_j))||^2 at t=0+ along the segment in D.
All actual vectors therefore give a feasible Gram of (3), with objective
(1). Repeated projections, repeated inputs and lower dimensional bodies
cause no exception.

Conversely take any EXACT feasible Z of (3). The real symmetric spectral
factorization Z=B^T B exists because Z is PSD. Retain its positive
eigenspaces, or use k coordinates and zero rows for zero eigenvalues.
Normalization makes rank at least one. Let the columns of B realize
x_0,g_0,...,g_n,p and form the SINGLE fixed body

    D_Z=conv{0,g_0,...,g_n,p}.

It is nonempty, compact and convex. It may have redundant or coincident
generators and need not have interior in the chosen ambient space.
For each label j and any z=sum_i theta_i G_i in D_Z, with nonnegative
theta_i summing to one, (2) gives

    <X_j-G_j,z-G_j>=sum_i theta_i Q_(i,j)<=0.

Consequently

    ||X_j-z||^2
      =||X_j-G_j||^2+||z-G_j||^2-2<X_j-G_j,z-G_j>
      >=||X_j-G_j||^2+||z-G_j||^2.

This proves G_j is the unique projection of X_j on D_Z, including in every
degenerate case. For star it also follows from 0 in D_Z. The coefficient
identities a_(i+1)=a_i-h_i b_i give the exact GD updates. Induction hence
identifies the physical labels with the actual trajectory on this one
body. The auxiliary projection is exactly at x_0/M. Completing the square
in 2<X,g>-t||g||^2 shows its maximizer is P_D(X/t), so the objective equals
(1), without a second optimization or extra physical step.

Thus the feasible objective sets coincide between (3) and all normalized
finite dimensional projection-body histories. In the necessity direction,
the original body can also be replaced by this finite hull, preserving
every listed projection and both endpoint maxima. Rank reduction is
allowed analytically; no assertion of full dimensionality is needed.
For a nonzero unnormalized start, scaling x and D by 1/||x_0|| scales all
projections accordingly and scales V by 1/||x_0||^2. This explains the
normalization for the universal class, while keeping one body throughout
each history.

The exact feasible set is bounded: the zero-generator normal implies
||g_i||<=||x_i|| and ||p||<=1/M, while
||x_(i+1)||<=(1+h_i)||x_i||. All Gram entries are then bounded by
Cauchy-Schwarz. It is closed and nonempty (all gradients zero), hence
compact. These observations need no conic strong duality. None turns a
floating output with defects into an exact feasible Gram.

## 3. Mandatory zero calibration: h=(1)

Write y=x-g, q=P(y), p=P(x/3), b=(g+2q)/3. Here M=3.
The accepted leaf identity Part B (3.1), or its direct expansion, is

    ||x||^2-||y+q||^2=-2Q_(0,1)-4Q_(1,0)+||g-q||^2.

The convex mixture b belongs to D. Completion of squares and comparison
with the actual maximizer p give the exact identity

    U_3(x)-U_1(y)
     =[-2Q_(0,1)-4Q_(1,0)+||g-q||^2]/3
       +3||p-b||^2-2Q_(0,p)-4Q_(1,p) >=0.          (4)

The last two rows are the actual p-input rows: for example
Q_(0,p)=<x/3-p,g-p>. Formula (4) retains all needed factors. The body
D={0}, with ||x||=1, has g=q=p=0 and attains V=0. Thus the exact universal
optimum of (3) for h=(1) is zero. The timed child will build this witness
using the same original coefficient/matrix builder and check all ordered
rows and normalization exactly with Fraction arithmetic before its one solve.

## 4. Mandatory positive calibration: h=(3/2,9/4,3/2)

On D=[-1,1] and x_0=1 the exact physical input/gradient pairs coincide:

    x_0=g_0=1,
    x_1=1-(3/2)*1=-1/2=g_1,
    x_2=-1/2-(9/4)*(-1/2)=5/8=g_2,
    x_3=5/8-(3/2)*(5/8)=-5/16=g_3.

All four inputs are in D; even the boundary input x_0=1 has X_0-G_0=0.
S=21/4, M=23/2 and p=P(2/23)=2/23. Star also has X_star-G_star=0.
Therefore EVERY original ordered normal for this example is exactly zero.
Normalization is one. The endpoint values are

    U_1(x_3)=25/256,
    U_(23/2)(1)=2/23,
    V=25/256-2/23=(575-512)/5888=63/5888>0.       (5)

The finite-hull replacement is [-1/2,1] and also realizes these samples;
the named witness itself remains D=[-1,1]. The child will reproduce all
