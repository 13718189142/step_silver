## 2. A finite dual interpolant with an explicit compact domain

For i=0,...,n put

    f_i=f(x_i), y_i=x_i-g_i, delta_i=f_i-||g_i||^2/2,
    ell_i(g)=<y_i,g>-delta_i,
    p(g)=max(0,ell_0(g),...,ell_n(g)),
    K=conv{0,g_0,...,g_n}.

We use only the necessity of the original ordered smooth-convex
interpolation inequalities, accepted fact1252b8823bb5a290:

    Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2<=0,

including the optimum sample (0,0,0). In particular delta_i>=0, p(0)=0
and p(g)>=0 everywhere. At each g_i,

    ell_j(g_i)-ell_i(g_i)=Q_ij<=0,
    ell_i(g_i)=<x_i,g_i>-f_i-||g_i||^2/2>=0.

Thus p(g_i)=ell_i(g_i). Define the globally finite function

    phi(x)=max_(g in K){<x,g>-||g||^2/2-p(g)}.

The maximizer is unique, because the objective is1-strongly concave on
the compact convex set K. The usual two optimality inequalities give
firm nonexpansiveness of its maximizing gradient, so phi is differentiable
and1-smooth. More explicitly, if g,g' maximize at x,x', convexity of
p+indicator_K gives <x-x',g-g'> >= ||g-g'||^2. Uniqueness and compactness
also give differentiability by the elementary maximum-envelope argument.

For every g in K, the i-th affine anchor gives

    <x_i,g>-||g||^2/2-p(g)
       <= f_i-||g-g_i||^2/2.

Equality holds at g=g_i. Therefore phi(x_i)=f_i and grad phi(x_i)=g_i
at EVERY original query and the actual terminal. In particular its GD run
from x_0 using h is exactly the given finite run. Also phi>=0 and phi(0)=0.

Set

    M=max_(0<=i<=n)||g_i||,
    Z=max(0,p(g_0),...,p(g_n)).

These are finite constants of the given finite run. Convexity of p and
the finite vertex description of K imply0<=p(g)<=Z on K. Every g in K
has norm at most M, so phi is globally M-Lipschitz. No unbounded dual
domain or uncomputed supremum is used to choose Z.

## 3. The body, complete minimum set, and exact extra-coordinate dynamics

For R>0 define

    D_R={(g,u): g in K, p(g)/R<=u<=Z/R}.

This is nonempty, compact and convex, and contains(0,0). It is a finite
polytope: K has a finite polytope description, and the two vertical bounds
are a finite list of linear inequalities because p is a finite affine
maximum. Degenerate K and Z=0 are included; full dimension is not needed.

Completing the square proves

    F_R(z)=||z||^2/2-dist(z,D_R)^2/2,
    grad F_R(z)=P_(D_R)(z).

The projection is unique and firmly nonexpansive by its two normal
inequalities. These facts also prove directly that F_R is convex,
differentiable and1-smooth. Its definition as a maximum of affine functions
proves convexity. Since0 belongs to D_R, its minimum value is0 and

    argmin F_R=D_R^polar
      ={z:<z,v><=0 for EVERY v in D_R}.

For completeness, if <z,v>>0 for some v in D_R, then tv belongs to D_R
for0<=t<=1 and t<z,v>-t^2||v||^2/2>0 for sufficiently small positive t.
This proves the reverse implication in the minimum-set formula. The
polar is a closed convex cone, not generally a singleton.

The chosen z_star=(0,-R) lies in this COMPLETE minimum set since every
u>=p(g)/R>=0. Its distance from z_0=(x_0,-R) is exactly||x_0||.

Write an actual lifted iterate as z_i=(X_i,-R t_i), with t_0=1.
For any t>=1 and fixed g in K, the objective as a function of u is

    <X,g>-R t u-||g||^2/2-u^2/2,

which is strictly decreasing for u>=0. Thus the optimizing coordinate
is exactly u=p(g)/R. If g_R(X,t) is its horizontal maximizer, then

    F_R(X,-Rt)=max_(g in K){<X,g>-||g||^2/2-p(g)
                             -(t-1)p(g)-p(g)^2/(2R^2)},
    grad F_R(X,-Rt)=(g_R(X,t),p(g_R(X,t))/R).

The ordinary unmodified GD updates are therefore

    X_(i+1)=X_i-h_i*g_R(X_i,t_i),
    t_(i+1)=t_i+h_i*p(g_R(X_i,t_i))/R^2.

For S=sum_(i<n)h_i, nonnegativity of all h_i and0<=p<=Z yield

    1<=t_i<=1+SZ/R^2, for EVERY i=0,...,n.

No coordinate is held fixed, no step is rescaled, and the same static
body D_R is used throughout the entire original chronological run.

## 4. Explicit whole-run and terminal error

Define

    Delta_R=(S+1/2)Z^2/R^2,
    A=Z*sqrt(2S+1),
    Pi_i=product_(0<=j<i)(1+h_j), Pi_0=1.

For every visited t_i and every g in K, the extra objective penalty

    d_R(g)=(t_i-1)p(g)+p(g)^2/(2R^2)

lies in[0,Delta_R]. Let g_K(X)=grad phi(X). The1-strong concavity of
the unperturbed maximization, together with optimality of g_R, gives

    (1/2)||g_R-g_K||^2
       <= [<X,g_K>-||g_K||^2/2-p(g_K)]
           -[<X,g_R>-||g_R||^2/2-p(g_R)]
       <= d_R(g_K)-d_R(g_R)<=Delta_R.

Hence ||g_R(X_i,t_i)-grad phi(X_i)||<=A/R. Because grad phi is
1-Lipschitz and grad phi(x_i)=g_i, the horizontal error E_i=||X_i-x_i||
obeys

    E_0=0,
    E_(i+1)<=(1+h_i)E_i+h_i*A/R,
    E_i<=(A/R)(Pi_i-1).

The last estimate follows by induction from Pi_(i+1)=(1+h_i)Pi_i;
zero steps cause no exception. No claim of a word-uniform bound on Pi_i
is made. All step values are finite for each given finite word.

The value penalty is uniformly bounded as well:

    phi(X_i)-Delta_R<=F_R(z_i)<=phi(X_i).

Since phi is M-Lipschitz and phi(x_i)=f_i,

    |F_R(z_i)-f_i|<=M*(A/R)(Pi_i-1)+Delta_R.

In particular, with the EXPLICIT terminal error

    e_R=M Z sqrt(2S+1)(Pi_n-1)/R+(S+1/2)Z^2/R^2,

we have |F_R(z_n)-f_n|<=e_R and e_R->0 as R->infinity. If Z=0, every
penalty and error vanishes exactly; D_R=K x {0}. All these formulas also
cover degenerate K and M=0, although a positive terminal value forces
M>0.

For a fully explicit sufficient choice to make e_R<=varepsilon>0, put

    C=M Z sqrt(2S+1)(Pi_n-1), E=(S+1/2)Z^2,
    R>=max(1,2C/varepsilon,sqrt(2E/varepsilon)).

This is an analytic parameter choice, not a computational replay.
