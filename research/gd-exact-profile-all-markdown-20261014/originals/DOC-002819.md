## 1. Target, result, and scope

The original target is the uniform all-word lower bound

\[
\mathcal W_n(h)\ge(1-o(1))/B_n,
\qquad B_n=2S_\bullet(n)+1,
\]

with the original all-split Zhang--Jiang profile, finite-dimensional convex 1-smooth functions, an attained minimum, the original selected minimizer and radius, and the actual final iterate. Here \(\mathcal W=2\mathcal R\).

This attempt does **not** obtain that lower bound, or even its required disk-witness viability test on the complete inherited-parameter family \(H_m\). It obtains an explicit quantitative obstruction to the proposed measure-selection shortcut. For every enclosing \(m\ge1536\), there is an annular sector of actual initial points with all of the following properties:

1. Its invariant area is bounded below by one positive constant independent of \(m\).
2. Its original radius \(|v_0|\) is bounded above and below by positive absolute constants.
3. Every trajectory is noncollapsed and undergoes genuine reflection at every bridge of level at least 1.
4. Every point in the sector has actual original-radius terminal payoff at most
   \[
   4^{-2^m}.
   \]

In particular, the whole sector has payoff \(o(1/D_m)\), where \(D_m=1+2\sum H_m\). The supplied accepted comparison \(B_{2^m-1}/D_m<47/50\) also makes its payoff \(o(1/B_{2^m-1})\). The proof is uniform over the whole inherited-parameter family; it is not a finite-horizon example.

This is an obstruction to drawing a terminal lower bound from preserved positive area and the occurrence of reflections. It does not upper-bound the whole disk family, the remaining reflecting region, or the unrestricted function class. Another initial point may still have the required payoff.

## 2. One static admissible function and the original radius

Let \(K=B(e_1,1)\subset\mathbb R^2\) and define the single global function

\[
F_K(x)=\max_{g\in K}\{\langle x,g\rangle-\tfrac12\|g\|^2\}.
\]

It is convex as a supremum of affine functions. Completing the square gives the unique maximizer \(P_Kx\). Projection is 1-Lipschitz: the two projection variational inequalities imply

\[
\|P_Kx-P_Ky\|^2\le\langle P_Kx-P_Ky,x-y\rangle.
\]

The upper and lower envelope inequalities, together with continuity of projection, give differentiability and \(\nabla F_K=P_K\). Thus \(F_K\) is globally convex and 1-smooth.

Since \(0\in K\), \(F_K\ge0\). Its minimum set is precisely

\[
M=\{(\xi,0):\xi\le0\}.
\]

Indeed, for such an \(x\), every \(g\in K\) has \(g_1\ge0\), so the defining maximum is zero at \(g=0\). If \(x_1>0\), a sufficiently small positive horizontal \(g\) gives a positive value. If \(x_2\ne0\), choose a sufficiently small boundary point of \(K\) whose second coordinate has the sign of \(x_2\); its first coordinate is quadratic in its second coordinate, and the defining value is positive. This also covers \(x_1\le0,x_2\ne0\).

Write

\[
x=(1-u,v),\qquad u\ge1,\qquad r=\sqrt{u^2+v^2}.
\]

Then \(r\ge1\), and projection onto the disk gives

\[
\nabla F_K(x)=(1-u/r,v/r),\qquad F_K(x)=r-u.
\]

For every \(h\ge0\), the actual GD map is therefore

\[
u'=u+h(1-u/r),\qquad v'=(1-h/r)v,
\tag{2.1}
\]

or, with \(p=(u,v)\),

\[
T_h(p)=h e_1+(1-h/r)p.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Since \(u'\ge u\), the region \(u\ge1\) is invariant for every nonnegative word. On its axis \(v=0\), the point is a minimizer and is fixed by every step. The closest original minimizer to \(x_0=(1-u_0,v_0)\) is exactly \((1-u_0,0)\), so the original squared radius is \(v_0^2\), not \(u_0^2+v_0^2\). Consequently the actual normalized payoff is

\[
\Pi_h(u_0,v_0)
=\frac{2(r_n-u_n)}{v_0^2}
=\frac{2}{r_n+u_n}\frac{v_n^2}{v_0^2}
=\frac{2}{r_n+u_n}\prod_{t=0}^{n-1}(1-h_t/r_t)^2.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Every state in this expression belongs to the one actual trajectory. No intermediate radius is independently selected.

## 3. Jacobian, inverse, sign charts, and actual image domain

For \(r>0\), differentiation of (2.2) gives

\[
DT_h=(1-h/r)I+(h/r^3)pp^{\mathsf T}.
\]

Its radial eigenvalue is 1 and its tangential eigenvalue is \(1-h/r\). Hence

\[
\det DT_h=1-h/r=v'/v\quad(v\ne0).
\tag{3.1}
\]

On any chart \(v\ne0,v'\ne0\), put \(q=\log|v|\). The determinant in \((u,q)\) coordinates is

\[
\frac{v}{v'}\det DT_h=1.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus the positive measure \(du\,d\log|v|=du\,dv/|v|\) is preserved, with the input and output sign charts retained. Reflection changes the sign chart; it creates no extra multiplicity.

For completeness, suppose \(p'\) has a nonaxis preimage with \(u>0\), and set \(R_b=\|p'-h e_1\|\). The identity

\[
u'-h=(1-h/r)u
\]

determines the branch:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
u'>h:&r=h+R_b,\quad p=(1+h/R_b)(p'-h e_1),\\
u'<h:&r=h-R_b>0,\quad p=(1-h/R_b)(p'-h e_1).
\end{array}
\tag{3.3}
\]

The second branch requires \(R_b<h\). In either branch the additional condition \(u\ge1\) must still hold. A nonaxis point with \(u'=h\) has no preimage with \(u>0\). The circle \(r=h\) collapses to \((h,0)\). In the actual invariant patch it is nonempty only for \(h\ge1\). Once collapsed, the trajectory remains at this minimizer.

An explicit image description records the domain restriction. Let \(U\ge1\), use the strict nonaxis domain \(u>U\), and assume \(h>0\). For terminal \(u'\ge U+h\), every \(|v'|>0\) has a preimage in this domain. For

\[
U<u'<U+h,\qquad u'\ne h,
\]

the image consists exactly of

\[
0<|v'|<
\frac{|u'-h|\sqrt{h^2-(U+h-u')^2}}{U+h-u'}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

There is no image with \(u'\le U\), nor a nonaxis image with \(u'=h\). Formula (3.4) follows by substituting (3.3) into \(u>U\); in the reflected branch the same inequality also enforces \(R_b<h\). For \(h=0\) the map is the identity. The boundary \(u=U\) is obtained by replacing the appropriate strict inequality with equality.

For a finite word, exclude points whose trajectory first reaches one of its collapsed circles. Each first-hit set is a smooth circle arc pulled back through finitely many local diffeomorphisms, and has zero invariant area on compact subsets away from \(v=0\). There are finitely many such sets. Off their union, the composed map is injective and preserves invariant area. The sector constructed below avoids every collapsed circle, so no exceptional-set removal is needed there.

## 4. Why the immediate area comparison has no finite target volume

Take an initial rectangle \(1<u_0<U\), \(A<q_0<B\), of finite positive invariant area. Let \(S=\sum h_t\). Equation (2.1) gives \(1<u_n<U+S\). If every original-radius payoff in this rectangle were below \(\kappa\), then its image would lie inside

\[
1<u<U+S,\qquad
q<\frac12\log[d_\kappa(2u+d_\kappa)],
\quad d_\kappa=\frac{\kappa e^{2B}}2.
\tag{4.1}
\]

This follows from \(r-u<d_\kappa\) and \(v^2=(r-u)(2u+r-u)\). The set in (4.1) has **infinite** invariant area, because it extends to \(q=-\infty\) at every allowed \(u\). Preserving a finite positive initial area therefore gives no contradiction for any \(\kappa>0\).

A finite-volume argument must instead control the actual image near \(q=-\infty\), including caustic distortion and the original value of \(q_0\). Removing the collapsed curves themselves only removes null sets and does not provide this estimate. The next section exhibits the failure quantitatively in the precise inherited family requested in the task, with a bounded original radius and no exact collapse.

## 5. A positive-area reflecting sector with negligible payoff on every inherited H_m

Fix an enclosing \(m\ge1536\), and keep

\[
\varepsilon=\frac1{4m},\quad\beta=1-\varepsilon,
\quad c=(1+\sqrt2)(1+\varepsilon),\quad a=\beta/c
\]

fixed throughout all children. Let \(H_0\) be empty and

\[
H_{j+1}=(H_j,\ell_j,H_j),\qquad \ell_j=1+a c^j.
\]

Here \(\ell_0\) is the leaf step; it is not a time index. Elementary rational bounds give

\[
12/5<c<3,\qquad 1/3<a<1/2,\qquad 4/3<\ell_0<2.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For example, \(\varepsilon<1/16\), \(\sqrt2<3/2\), and \(\sqrt2>7/5\) give \(c<85/32\), \(a>6/17>1/3\), and the stated bounds.

Set the absolute constants

\[
\zeta=1/64,\qquad \eta_0=\zeta^2/8.
\]

Define the initial annular sector

\[
E_m=\left\{
\begin{aligned}
u_0&=(\ell_0+\delta)\cos\theta,\\
v_0&=(\ell_0+\delta)\sin\theta,\\
\eta_0\ell_0/2&\le\delta\le\eta_0\ell_0,\\
3/4&\le\cos\theta\le4/5,\quad 0<\theta<\pi/2
\end{aligned}
\right\}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

All its points have \(u_0>1\), so the global function and radius calculation in Section 2 apply. Moreover

\[
4/5<|v_0|<3.
\tag{5.3}
\]

In polar coordinates \(du\,dv/v=dr\,d\theta/\sin\theta\). Therefore

\[
\mu(E_m)=\frac{\eta_0\ell_0}{2}
\int_{\arccos(4/5)}^{\arccos(3/4)}\frac{d\theta}{\sin\theta}
>
\frac{2\eta_0}{3}
\int_{\arccos(4/5)}^{\arccos(3/4)}\frac{d\theta}{\sin\theta}>0.
\tag{5.4}
\]

The final lower bound is independent of \(m\).

### 5.1 Whole-word product and weighted-prefix bounds

Assign the formal multiplier bound \(b_0=\zeta\) to each leaf and \(b_j=c^j\) to a level-\(j\) bridge for \(j\ge1\). These are analytic bounding quantities; no array or mathematical program was generated.

For a complete \(H_j\), let \(Q_j\) be the product of these bounds. For \(j\ge1\), direct counting gives

\[
Q_j=\zeta^{2^{j-1}}c^{2^j-j-1}
=c^{-j-1}d^{2^{j-1}},\qquad d=\zeta c^2<1/4.
\tag{5.5}
\]

The identity uses \(2^{j-k-1}\) occurrences of level \(k\). In particular, \(Q_1=\zeta\), and \(Q_{j+1}=Q_j^2c^j\).

Every formal prefix product in every \(H_j\) is at most 1. This follows by induction: the first child has this property; the product through its following bridge is

\[
Q_j c^j=c^{-1}d^{2^{j-1}}<1;
\]

and every prefix in the right child is this product times a child prefix.

Let \(P_t\) denote the formal product before step \(t\), and define the weighted prefix sum for the complete \(H_j\) by

\[
J_j=\sum_{t\in H_j}h_t P_t^2.
\]

The full physical prefix is used in this definition. With \(A_j=c^{2j}Q_j^2\), concatenation gives

\[
J_1=\ell_0<2,\qquad
J_{j+1}=(1+A_j)J_j+\ell_jQ_j^2.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Since \(\ell_j\le2c^j\), we have \(\ell_jQ_j^2\le2A_j\), and

\[
A_j=c^{-2}d^{2^j}<16^{-2^{j-1}},\qquad
\sum_{j\ge1}A_j<1/15.
\]

Thus \(J_{j+1}+2\le(1+A_j)(J_j+2)\). For nonnegative \(A_j\) with sum below 1, the finite product \(\prod(1+A_j)\) is at most \(1/(1-\sum A_j)\); this follows from \(1+A_j\le(1-A_j)^{-1}\) and \(\prod(1-A_j)\ge1-\sum A_j\). Consequently

\[
J_j+2<4\cdot\frac{15}{14}=\frac{30}{7},
\qquad J_j<\frac{16}{7}<3
\quad\text{for every }j\ge1.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Both (5.5) and (5.7) use the same enclosing \(m\) parameters in all children.

### 5.2 Bootstrap for the actual disk trajectory

The first leaf step is exact:

\[
p_1=(\ell_0+\delta\cos\theta,\delta\sin\theta).
\tag{5.8}
\]

In particular \(u_t\ge u_1>\ell_0\) for all \(t\ge1\). For any nonleaf level \(j\ge1\), regardless of reflection,

\[
|1-\ell_j/r_t|
\le\max\{1,\ell_j/\ell_0-1\}
\le c^j,
\tag{5.9}
\]

because \(r_t\ge u_t\ge\ell_0\), and
\(\ell_j/\ell_0-1=a(c^j-1)/\ell_0<c^j\).

We now close the leaf bound \(|1-\ell_0/r_t|\le\zeta\). Assume it has held at all previous leaf occurrences. The actual transverse coordinates then obey

\[
|v_t|\le\frac{|v_1|}{\zeta}P_t\le\frac\delta\zeta
\quad(t\ge1),
\]

and (5.7) bounds the entire accumulated drift by

\[
\begin{aligned}
u_t-u_1
&=\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}
\frac{h_s v_s^2}{r_s(r_s+u_s)}\\
&\le\frac{3\delta^2}{2\ell_0^2\zeta^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.10}
\]

The terms are actual earlier iterates; the bound by the complete positive sum \(J_m\) is valid for every prefix. Also

\[
r_t-u_t\le\frac{v_t^2}{2\ell_0}
\le\frac{\delta^2}{2\ell_0\zeta^2}.
\]

Using \(\ell_0\ge1\) and \(\eta=\delta/\ell_0\le\eta_0\), we get

\[
\frac{r_t}{\ell_0}
\le1+\eta+\frac{2\eta^2}{\zeta^2}
\le1+\frac{5\zeta^2}{32}
<1+\frac\zeta2.
\tag{5.11}
\]

Since \(r_t\ge\ell_0\), (5.11) implies

\[
0\le1-\ell_0/r_t<\zeta.
\]

This establishes the next leaf bound and closes the bootstrap for every step of the entire \(H_m\), with constants independent of \(m\).

Every nonleaf bridge actually reflects. Indeed,

\[
\frac{\ell_1}{\ell_0}-1
=\frac{a(c-1)}{\ell_0}>1/6>\zeta/2,
\]

so (5.11) gives \(r_t<\ell_1\le\ell_j\) at every level \(j\ge1\). The first leaf has \(r_0>\ell_0\), and all later leaves have \(r_t\ge u_t>\ell_0\). Thus no step collapses a point in \(E_m\). The composed map is injective and preserves \(\mu(E_m)\) exactly. This pays all sign and caustic boundaries for this particular sector.

### 5.3 Original-radius terminal payoff

At the end of the complete word, the actual transverse coordinate satisfies

\[
|v_n|\le\frac{|v_1|}{\zeta}Q_m,
\qquad
\frac{|v_1|}{|v_0|}=\frac\delta{\ell_0+\delta}.
\]

Since \(r_n+u_n\ge2\ell_0\), equation (2.3) yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pi_{H_m}(u_0,v_0)
&\le\frac1{\ell_0}
\frac{\delta^2}{(\ell_0+\delta)^2}
\frac{Q_m^2}{\zeta^2}\\
&\le\frac{\zeta^2}{64\ell_0}
c^{-2m-2}d^{2^m}
<4^{-2^m}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.12}
\]

This is an upper bound for **every actual original start in the positive-area sector**, including its original selected minimizer; there is no terminal renormalization.

Finally,

\[
S_m=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}2^{m-j-1}\ell_j
<\frac{2c^m}{c-2}<5c^m,
\quad D_m<11c^m<11\cdot3^m.
\]

Therefore

\[
D_m\sup_{E_m}\Pi_{H_m}
<11\cdot3^m\,4^{-2^m}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{5.13}
\]

The accepted comparison supplied in the task would also give
\(B_{2^m-1}\sup_{E_m}\Pi_{H_m}\to0\). The proof of (5.12)--(5.13) itself does not depend on that accepted comparison or on the R6 quadratic draft.

## 6. What is and is not closed

The parent Jacobian and inverse formulas are correct on their stated noncollapsed charts, once the actual \(u\ge1\) image restrictions are retained. The invariant measure is real, but the most immediate bad-payoff region has infinite invariant area. More decisively, the exact inherited \(H_m\) maps a uniformly positive-area, finite-original-radius, genuinely reflecting sector to payoffs smaller than the target scale by the explicit factor in (5.13). No point in this sector supplies the missing gain.

An area argument would need an additional estimate selecting a different initial subset and controlling its actual image relative to the threshold

\[
\frac{2(r_n-u_n)}{v_0^2}\ge\frac{1-o(1)}{B_{2^m-1}}.
\]

Such an estimate must keep the dependence on the original \(v_0\), prevent the caustic-induced transverse contraction quantified above, and retain constants through every inherited level. Neither area preservation, injectivity, nullity of the collapsed circles, bounded horizontal drift, nor the occurrence of reflections supplies it. This attempt has no such selection estimate. It therefore does not pass the lower-bound viability test on \(H_m\), and it makes no all-word exact-profile claim.

The result should not be recorded as minimax progress or as a disproof of the disk family's possible usefulness. It is a quantitative reason not to renew the invariant-measure argument without a new terminal selection mechanism.
