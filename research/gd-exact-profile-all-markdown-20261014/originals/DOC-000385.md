# Uniform scalar-quadratic bound on the actual inherited-parameter word

Main-agent manual derivation, 2026-10-09 local time. **UNVERIFIED DRAFT**. No mathematical program, numerical array, solver, additional model or formal submission. This is an auxiliary baseline bound, not an upper bound for the full smooth-convex class and not a resolution of the original minimax problem.

## Statement

Fix an integer m>=4, and keep its parameters fixed throughout the word:

\[
 \varepsilon=\frac1{4m},\quad \beta=1-\varepsilon,
 \quad c=(1+\sqrt2)(1+\varepsilon),\quad a=\beta/c.
\]

Let H_0 be empty and H_{j+1}=(H_j,1+a c^j,H_j) for 0<=j<m. Write S_j=sum H_j and D_m=1+2S_m. For the scalar quadratic f(x)=lambda x^2/2, 0<=lambda<=1, define its actual GD polynomial

\[
 p_j(\lambda)=\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}
       [1-\lambda(1+a c^i)]^{2^{j-i-1}}.
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{D_m\sup_{0\le\lambda\le1}\lambda p_m(\lambda)^2
       \le\frac9{16}.}                                      \tag{Q}
\]

The supremum is precisely the worst normalized objective over the scalar quadratic pool, with W=2R. It is also the supremum over all finite-dimensional convex quadratic functions with Hessian spectrum in [0,1], by spectral decomposition and the original minimizer-radius normalization. This last sentence does not extend (Q) to nonquadratic functions.

## Elementary parameter and mass bounds

For m>=4,

\[
 \frac{15}{16}\le\beta<1,\qquad
 \frac{12}{5}<c<\frac{21}{8},\qquad
 \frac5{14}<a<\frac12.
\]

Indeed c<=17(1+sqrt2)/16<21/8, c>=1+sqrt2>12/5, and a=beta/c. The endpoint rational comparisons follow by squaring positive quantities. Since c>=1+sqrt2 and beta<=1,

\[
 2+a\le c.
\]

The actual masses satisfy S_0=0 and S_{j+1}=2S_j+1+a c^j. Induction gives

\[
 S_j\le c^j-1,\qquad D_m<2c^m.                         \tag{1}
\]

Another induction, for j>=1, gives

\[
 S_j\ge\frac{a c^{j+1}}2.                              \tag{2}
\]

For j=1, a c^2/2=beta c/2<21/16<19/14<1+a=S_1. If (2) holds at j, then

\[
 2S_j+1+a c^j-\frac{a c^{j+2}}2
 \ge1+a c^j(1+c-c^2/2)>0,
\]

because 1+c-c^2/2>0 for c<=21/8. Thus (2) holds at j+1. Also S_m>=1.

## All frequencies, with no sampling

The bridge sizes b_i=1+a c^i strictly increase. Frequencies at a polynomial root give zero and require no special estimate. For every other lambda in (0,1], there are three cases.

### All factors positive

If lambda b_{m-1}<1, then 1-u<=exp(-u) yields

\[
 0<p_m(\lambda)\le e^{-\lambda S_m}.
\]

Therefore

\[
 D_m\lambda p_m(\lambda)^2
 \le\frac{2S_m+1}{2eS_m}
 \le\frac3{2e}<\frac9{16}.                            \tag{3}
\]

Here max_{t>=0} t exp(-2S_m t)=1/(2eS_m). The elementary bound e>8/3 follows from its positive power series (the sum through degree three is exactly 8/3).

### All factors negative

If lambda b_0>1, then for every i,

\[
 |1-\lambda b_i|=\lambda b_i-1\le b_i-1=a c^i.
\]

The finite geometric-sum identity gives

\[
 |p_m(\lambda)|
 \le a^{2^m-1}c^{2^m-m-1}
 =\beta^{2^m-1}c^{-m}\le c^{-m}.
\]

By (1),

\[
 D_m\lambda p_m(\lambda)^2<2c^{-m}
 \le2(5/12)^4<\frac9{16}.                            \tag{4}
\]

### The first negative level is k with 1<=k<m

Then lambda b_i<1 for i<k and lambda b_i>1 for i>=k. Put

\[
 r=m-k\ge1,\qquad z=a c^k\lambda,\qquad
 T=c z p_k(\lambda).
\]

All factors defining p_k are positive, so (2) gives

\[
 T=a c^{k+1}\lambda p_k(\lambda)
 \le a c^{k+1}\lambda e^{-S_k\lambda}
 \le \frac{a c^{k+1}}{eS_k}\le\frac2e<\frac34.       \tag{5}
\]

Moreover lambda<1/b_0=1/(1+a), while lambda b_k>1. Consequently

\[
 z>1-\lambda>\frac{a}{1+a}.                           \tag{6}
\]

The higher-level factors obey |1-lambda b_i|<=lambda a c^i. Keeping their exact multiplicities,

\[
 |p_m(\lambda)|
 \le p_k(\lambda)^{2^r}
       z^{2^r-1}c^{2^r-r-1}
 =\frac{T^{2^r}}{z c^{r+1}}.                          \tag{7}
\]

Combining (1), (6), (7), and a c=beta,

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 D_m\lambda p_m(\lambda)^2
 &<\frac{2T^{2^{r+1}}}{a z c^{r+2}}\\
 &<\frac{2(1+a)}{\beta^2}\,
          c^{-r}T^{2^{r+1}}\\
 &<\frac{3}{(15/16)^2(12/5)}\,(3/4)^4
 =\frac9{20}<\frac9{16}.                              \tag{8}
 \end{aligned}
\]

The last step uses r>=1, c>1 and 0<=T<3/4. This covers every remaining lambda. At lambda=0 the normalized objective is zero. Equations (3)--(8) prove (Q) within this draft.

## What this buys, conditionally on the accepted benchmark comparison

For exactly this same enclosing-m word, accepted fact 931b9653ce0f7201 gives B_{2^m-1}/D_m<47/50 for m>=1536. If the present draft is correct, then

\[
 B_{2^m-1}\sup_{\lambda\in[0,1]}\lambda p_m(\lambda)^2
 <\frac{423}{800},
\]

and the standard noncrossing Huber witness on the same word gives B_{2^m-1}/D_m<47/50. Thus this actual asymptotic word fails to reach the exact target through the portfolio consisting of that standard Huber witness and all convex quadratics. This is not an optimization over every Huber scale, start or crossing itinerary. It fills a previously missing scope check when testing this specific baseline portfolio.

It does not imply W(H_m)<=9/(16D_m), or W(H_m)<=1/D_m, or a negative answer to the original problem. A nonlinear adversary may have a much larger last-iterate objective. No formal review has been submitted.
