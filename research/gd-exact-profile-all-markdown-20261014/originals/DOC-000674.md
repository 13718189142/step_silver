## An exact necessary first-query capacity

Use the full weighted-prefix formulation with first physical step h0=1+a, 0<a<1, more than one query, q>0, nonnegative original multipliers, and exact value flow. Let mu_i=lambda_(i,star), nu_i=lambda_(star,i); let out_i,in_i count only other physical query labels. With N_ii=mu_i+out_i, N_ij=-lambda_ij and B=sym(NL), the first diagonal is

    B_00=mu_0+(1-h0)out_0=mu_0-a out_0.

The full Schur condition therefore forces

    0<=mu_0<=4q,
    out_0<=[mu_0-mu_0^2/(4q)]/a<=q/a.

Value flow at the nonterminal label0 gives in_0=mu_0+out_0-nu_0. Maximizing the right side over0<=mu_0<=4q gives the additional bound

    in_0<=q(1+a)^2/a.

No contact assumption, nu=0 premise, sparsity, or diagonal-residual restriction was used. These are necessary first-query bounds, not sufficient certificates or a whole-source exclusion. All notation and algebra should be independently checked before use.

## Why a different landing measure is genuinely different

The current native source-profile report says its oriented cycle law sends the root's return traffic into each left query in proportion to h_i/H. The root requires traffic of order D while this rule sends a fraction of order1/D to query0. That creates order-one outgoing traffic at a query whose capacity is order1/D. Changing only cycle intensity cannot fix that mismatch. This paragraph is a scope summary of the native report, not a new general theorem.

For a left block of nonnegative step masses k_i and total H, define its local clock t_i=1+sum_(j<i)k_j. A different explicit landing distribution is proportional to k_i t_i. Its normalization is exactly

    Z1=sum k_i t_i=H+(H^2-sum k_i^2)/2.

If max k_i<=theta H with theta<1, then Z1>=H+(1-theta)H^2/2. Thus its first weight can be of order1/H^2 instead of1/H. Traffic of order H then produces order1/H at that boundary, which removes the preceding order mismatch. All constants and traffic bounds still need proof on the actual same-m hierarchy.

A further danger is accumulation over many ancestors. In a hypothetical law with node traffic of order H_v^2/D, a linear-clock first weight of order H_v^-2 contributes order1/D from each ancestor; this alone does not give a uniform first-query bound. A quadratic-clock distribution proportional to k_i t_i^2 could change that order, but no corresponding normalization bound, complete coefficients, global flow or PSD proof is asserted here. This is a conditional scaling diagnostic, not an exclusion of every linear-clock law or proof of the quadratic rule.
