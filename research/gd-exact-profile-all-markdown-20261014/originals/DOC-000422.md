## 5. A finite last-crossing absorption lemma

This is the comparison used in this round. It treats the true quadratic
caps, the moving interval and all large steps exactly. It is NOT a
continuous-time limit or an approximation by the total word mass.

Consider(4.4) on a finite recursive word H_L with positive step values
b_0,...,b_(L-1), for fixed sigma>0, cap_ratio>0, beta>0 and A_2. Suppose
its incoming state has w_in<A_2 and positive width g_in=A_2-beta*v_in.
Let

    P_j=sum_{h in H_j, h>=2} h,
    z_in=beta*v_in-w_in,
    kappa_tail=1+beta^2.                            (5.1)

Empty sums are0. Suppose the following FINITE inequalities hold:

    max(z_in,0) <= kappa_tail*sigma*P_L/2;          (5.2)
    sigma*(b_j-cap_ratio*P_j)/2 <= g_in,
                                     0<=j<L;       (5.3)
    sigma*(cap_ratio*b_j-kappa_tail*P_j)/2 <= g_in,
                                     0<=j<L.       (5.4)

Then the actual pair has entered its flat interval by the end of H_L.

Proof. If the incoming point is in the interval, absorption already holds.
Otherwise w_in<A_2 forces it to be on the left, with z_in>0. Assume for
contradiction that the terminal pair is active. Since a point in the closed
flat interval is absorbing, EVERY sample, including every post-step sample,
has then been strictly on the left or strictly on the right. There are
finitely many samples, so if a side change occurs it has a last occurrence.

While consecutive samples stay on the left, z=beta*v-w>0 evolves by

    z^+=z-h*kappa_tail*min(sigma,z/kappa_tail)/2.    (5.5)

If h>=2 and z<kappa_tail*sigma, the right side is(1-h/2)z<=0,
contradicting staying on that side. Consequently every such high step
has q=sigma and decreases z by h*kappa_tail*sigma/2. This also covers
the cap boundary; if it would land at0 that would be absorption or a
side change, which is excluded on a constant-side segment. Low steps
h<2 still cause nonnegative decrease. Likewise, on a constant right-side
segment, t=w-A_2>0 evolves by

    t^+=t-h*min(cap_ratio*sigma,t)/2,               (5.6)

so every h>=2 must have r=cap_ratio*sigma and decreases t by precisely
h*cap_ratio*sigma/2. This proves both necessary high-step mass losses
without neglecting any low step or near-boundary query.

If there is no side change, the whole H_L stays left, and(5.5) gives
z_in>kappa_tail*sigma*P_L/2, contrary to(5.2).

Otherwise let the last side change occur at a step of level j. Every
occurrence of b_j in H_L is immediately followed by a full H_j. This
follows by induction from H_(j+1)=(H_j,b_j,H_j): a node's right subtree
is read immediately after the node, before any ancestor or later subtree.
That complete following H_j lies in the original remaining tail.

For a left-to-right change, at the preceding sample write z=beta*v-w>0
and g=A_2-beta*v>=g_in. The new right margin satisfies exactly

    t^+=h*q/2-z-g < b_j*sigma/2-g_in.              (5.7)

By lastness of the crossing the following H_j remains strictly right.
Equation(5.6) therefore requires t^+>cap_ratio*sigma*P_j/2.
Combined with(5.7) this contradicts(5.3).

For a right-to-left change, at the preceding sample put t=w-A_2>0.
The left coordinate v is unchanged on this step, and the new left margin
satisfies

    z^+=h*r/2-t-g < b_j*cap_ratio*sigma/2-g_in.     (5.8)

The following H_j remains strictly left, so(5.5) requires
z^+>kappa_tail*sigma*P_j/2, contradicting(5.4).
All cases contradict terminal activity. The flat interval is reached,
which proves the finite lemma.

This proof is valid even if the gap increases substantially and if many
earlier crossings occurred. It does not compare two different words of
equal mass. The exact following-subtree property is indispensable.

After dividing by p(q_(i-1))+q_(i-1), the integrand is a weighted mean

    1<=y<=Y_beta<=beta*exp(2/beta).                 (6.4)

    K_bound(beta)=6 beta^(-1) exp(2/beta).          (6.6)

Combining M=u+y/beta with(6.4)--(6.5) proves

    |M-C_beta*S/2|<=K_bound(beta),
    |u-C_beta*S/2|<=K_bound(beta),

These are estimates for arbitrary ordered finite ordinary blocks. They
have been derived jointly, rather than borrowed from a source exponent

    K_bound(beta)<=(3m/4)exp(m/4)<=m(4/3)^m.        (6.8)

The last estimate follows from exp(1/4)<sum_(r>=0)(1/4)^r=4/3.
