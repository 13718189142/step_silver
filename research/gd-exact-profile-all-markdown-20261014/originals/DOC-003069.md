## 1. Parameters and body

Fix one integer m>=1536 throughout. Define

  epsilon=1/(4m), beta=1-epsilon, lambda=1+sqrt(2),
  c=lambda(1+epsilon), a=beta/c,
  H_0=(), H_(j+1)=(H_j,1+a*c^j,H_j), 0<=j<m,
  n=2^m-1, S=sum H_m, D_m=1+2S.

All children use these SAME parameters. The word has exactly n physical
steps, numbered0,...,n-1. Put h_i=(H_m)_i. Its last step is1+a.
Let

  v_1=(1,0), v_2=(-1/2,sqrt(3)/2), v_3=(-1/2,-sqrt(3)/2),
  D=conv{v_1,v_2,v_3},
  F(x)=max_(g in D) [x dot g-||g||^2/2],
  x_(i+1)=x_i-h_i grad F(x_i),
  E_m(x_0)=2F(x_n)/||x_0||^2, x_0!=0,
  V_triangle(m)=sup_(x_0!=0) E_m(x_0).

All norms and inner products are the usual real Euclidean ones. The three
vertices have norm1, sum0, and pairwise inner product-1/2. Consequently

  D={x: v_i dot x>=-1/2 for i=1,2,3},
  {x: ||x||<=1/2} subset D subset {x: ||x||<=1},
  -D subset 2D.

For each edge opposite v_i put n_i=-v_i, t_i=J n_i, where
J(u_1,u_2)=(-u_2,u_1), and b=sqrt(3)/2. Its endpoints are the two
points (1/2)n_i +/- b t_i.

The dihedral group generated by permutations of the vertices preserves D,

an arbitrary initial point by a symmetry axis.

## 2. Legality, full minimum, metric, and all original rows

Completing the square shows that the maximizer in F is the unique Euclidean
projection g=P_D(x). The defining projection inequality is

  (x-g) dot (z-g)<=0 for every z in D.                 (2.1)

It follows either by differentiating the squared distance along the segment
from g to z, or conversely by expanding ||x-z||^2-||x-g||^2. Thus (2.1)
is necessary and sufficient and supplies a direct check of every guard below.

For two inputs x,y and projections g,p, add (2.1) with z=p and z=g to get

  ||g-p||^2<=(g-p) dot (x-y)<=||g-p|| ||x-y||.

Hence projection is1-Lipschitz. The maximizing inequalities give, for an
increment u,

  g dot u <= F(x+u)-F(x) <= P_D(x+u) dot u.

Their difference is at most||u||^2, proving differentiability with gradient
g and smoothness1. Convexity follows from F being a maximum of affine
functions of x. The function is finite everywhere because D is compact.

Since0 belongs to D, F>=0 and F(0)=0. For x!=0 choose
0<t<min{1,1/(2||x||)}. Then tx belongs to D and
F(x)>=(t-t^2/2)||x||^2>0. Thus the complete minimum set is exactly{0}.
The true initial distance is ||x_0||; there is no unused carrier or selectable
closer minimum.

For all original samples i=0,...,n let g_i=P_D(x_i), f_i=F(x_i), and add
the optimum sample x_*=g_*=0,f_*=0. Set z_j=x_j-g_j. Since
f_i=x_i dot g_i-||g_i||^2/2, direct expansion gives, for EVERY ordered pair,

  Q_ij := f_i-f_j+g_i dot(x_j-x_i)+||g_i-g_j||^2/2
        = (g_i-g_j) dot z_j <=0.                     (2.2)

The last inequality is (2.1) for g_j and z=g_i. This includes every pair
with the terminal n and every pair involving *. No reduced row family or
terminal replacement is being used.

For any spatial scale s>0,
F_(sD)(x)=s^2 F_D(x/s). Thus the run from s x_0 is the scale-s version
of this run, and its normalized payoff is identical. Multiplication of F
by any L>0, with steps h_i/L, also preserves the points and normalized
payoff. The report uses the original W=2R normalization throughout.

## 3. Seven closed projection regions, with every guard

There are seven face labels: C for the whole triangle, E_i for an edge,
and V_i for a vertex. Associate to each a symmetric orthogonal projection
matrix P_f, vector b_f orthogonal to its range, and closed region R_f:

  C:   P_C=I, b_C=0,
       R_C={x: -v_i dot x<=1/2 for all i};

  E_i: P_E=t_i t_i^T, b_E=n_i/2,
       R_E={x: -n_i dot x<=-1/2,
                  t_i dot x<=b, -t_i dot x<=b};

  V_i: P_V=0, b_V=v_i,
       R_V={x: (v_j-v_i) dot x<=-3/2 for both j!=i}.

On R_f the actual projection is

  P_D(x)=P_f x+b_f,                                 (3.1)
  2F(x)=x^T P_f x+2b_f dot x-||b_f||^2.            (3.2)

For C this is x itself. For E_i write x=z n_i+u t_i: the guards are
z>=1/2, |u|<=b, and (3.1) is n_i/2+u t_i. Its residual is
(z-1/2)n_i and (2.1) follows from n_i dot g<=1/2 on D. For V_i,
(2.1) for the other two vertices is exactly the two displayed guards;
linearity gives it for their entire convex hull. Conversely each projection
lies in the interior of D, in an edge, or at a vertex, giving one of these
seven regions by (2.1). Shared boundaries are permitted: the formulas agree
there because the projection is unique. These facts prove both coverage
and the absence of a spurious branch.

Formula (3.2) follows by inserting (3.1) into x dot g-||g||^2/2 and using
P_f^2=P_f=P_f^T and P_f b_f=0. It remains valid at every shared boundary.

For orientation only, an alternative full two-coordinate description uses
q_i=(2/3)x dot v_i, with sum q_i=0 and
||x||^2=(3/2)sum q_i^2. If A=max q_i and B=-min q_i, then
A/2<=B<=2A and the ordered triple is(A,B-A,-B). The core guard is
B<=1/3; the vertex guard is2A-B>=1; otherwise the two upper coordinates
form the active edge. The next ordered triple must actually be sorted
again after an overrelaxed step. Nothing in this report presumes that its
old order persists or that equal coordinates can split.

## 4. Every physical prefix and the complete initial core

For a positive integer q let nu_2(q) be the largest integer r>=0 for which
2^r divides q. For an integer ell>=0 let sbit(ell) be the number of ones
in its binary expansion. The original ruler word satisfies

  h_(q-1)-1=a c^(nu_2(q))=beta c^(nu_2(q)-1),
                      1<=q<=n.                    (4.1)

Indeed the bridge of H_(j+1) is at q=2^j, and for0<r<2^j the right
child has nu_2(2^j+r)=nu_2(r). This proves (4.1) by induction on the
literal concatenation.

For every physical prefix length1<=ell<=n,

  sum_(q=1)^ell nu_2(q)=sum_(r>=1) floor(ell/2^r)
                     =ell-sbit(ell),
  p_ell:=product_(i=0)^(ell-1)(h_i-1)
         =beta^ell c^(-sbit(ell))<=beta/c=a<1/2.      (4.2)

To prove the second counting identity write ell as sum e_j2^j. Each bit
contributes sum_(r=1)^j 2^(j-r)=2^j-1 to the floor sum. All sums are finite.
The bound uses beta<1, sbit(ell)>=1, and c>1+sqrt(2)>2.

Set p_0=1 and p=p_n=beta^n c^(-m). If x_0 belongs to D, then

  x_ell=(-1)^ell p_ell x_0, 0<=ell<=n.               (4.3)

This is a genuine induction on the physical updates: for every nonempty
prefix a positive scale <=a maps D into D, while a negative scale of
magnitude <=a maps D into2aD subset D. Thus each point predicted by (4.3)
is in D, its actual projection is itself, and the next update gives (4.3)
again. This checks every intervening prefix, including those containing
large bridges. It is not an inference from the final product alone.

Consequently every nonzero x_0 in D has

  E_m(x_0)=p^2=beta^(2n)c^(-2m).                     (4.4)

For later comparisons derive the mass formula within this joint draft.
Put S_j=sum H_j, A_j=1+S_j, and v_mass=a/(c-2). The recursion gives
A_(j+1)=2A_j+a c^j and hence

  A_j=(1-v_mass)2^j+v_mass c^j.                     (4.5)

Substitution checks the initial value and every step. Since
lambda(lambda-2)=1, c>lambda and beta<1 imply0<v_mass<1. For j>=1,
A_j<c^j. Therefore D_m=2A_m-1<2c^m<c^(2m), and

  p^2<1/D_m.                                      (4.6)

The middle strict inequality uses c^m>2.

## 5. Full fixed-face classes: exact terminal scores and own radii

A fixed-face class here requires the projection to belong to the designated
closed face at every original query AND at the actual terminal. Shared
boundary labels cause no difficulty.

### 5.1 A fixed vertex

Fix a vertex v. Let its normal cone be
N_D(v)={w: w dot(g-v)<=0 for every g in D}.
The full fixed-vertex class is exactly

  x_0=(S+1)v+w, w in N_D(v).                       (5.1)

Necessity follows by summing the constant gradient v and using the terminal
vertex guard. Sufficiency holds because every prefix has
x_i=(1+S-s_i)v+w, where s_i=sum_(t<i)h_t, and v belongs to N_D(v).
Thus the residual w+(S-s_i)v stays in that cone. All the guards hold.

Write t=v dot w>=0 and w=t v+w_perp, with w_perp perpendicular to v.
The nonnegative sign follows by testing g=0 in the normal inequality.
The actual terminal is v+w and

  E_m(x_0)=(1+2t)/[(S+1+t)^2+||w_perp||^2]
          <=1/D_m.                                (5.2)

Indeed (S+1+t)^2-D_m(1+2t)=(t-S)^2. Equality is attained at
w=S v, which is legal, giving x_0=D_m v. No transverse coordinate was
dropped from the denominator in (5.2).

### 5.2 A fixed edge

Fix its orthonormal n,t as in Section1 and write x_0=z_0 n+u_0 t.
The full fixed-edge class is exactly

  z_0>=(S+1)/2, |u_0|<=b.                          (5.3)

Necessity follows from the initial and terminal edge guards and the normal
update. For sufficiency each physical prefix is

  z_i=z_0-s_i/2>=1/2,
  u_i=(-1)^i p_i u_0,

whose tangential guards follow from p_0=1 and (4.2). Thus these are the
actual updates and the terminal remains on the same edge region. By (3.2),

  E_m(x_0)=[z_0-D_m/4+p^2 u_0^2]/[z_0^2+u_0^2]
          <=1/D_m.                                (5.4)

The normal inequality is z_0^2-D_m(z_0-D_m/4)=(z_0-D_m/2)^2>=0;
the tangential inequality is (4.6). Equality holds at u_0=0,z_0=D_m/2,
which satisfies (5.3). Therefore

  V_triangle(m)>=1/D_m.                            (5.5)

These exact fixed-face optima do not bound histories that change faces.
In particular (5.5), combined with accepted931's B_n/D_m<47/50, is NOT
an upper bound on B_n V_triangle(m).

## 6. A legal-prefix obstruction to the radial envelope shortcut

A tempting one-step ingredient for propagating the inscribed-ball radius
would be

  ||x-h P_D(x)||<=max{h-1, ||x||-h/2}, h>=1.        (FALSE)

It is false for every h>2, including an actual bridge of the fixed word.
Use one edge frame n,t and choose its endpoint v=n/2+b t. Put

  x=2h n+b t, r=||x||=sqrt(4h^2+3/4).

The edge/vertex boundary guard is exact, so P_D(x)=v. Its next point is
(2h-h/2)n+(1-h)b t. Direct symbolic expansion gives

  ||x-hv||^2-(r-h/2)^2
     =h(r-2h)+(3/4)h(h-2)>0.                       (6.1)

Also r-h/2>=3h/2>h-1, so the right side of the proposed envelope is
r-h/2. This proves the failure, with an explicit transverse component.

To realize this at an actual original query, use the level2 bridge
h=1+a c^2=1+beta c. Here beta>3/4 and c>12/5, so h>2. This is physical
query3, preceded by H_2. Initialize at

  x_0=x+S_2 v.

The residual x-v=(2h-1/2)n is in N_D(v), and adding any nonnegative
multiple of v preserves that cone. Thus every preceding gradient is v,
and summing those actual steps lands at x at query3. The same static F
then defines every remaining original point and row by Section2; no
claim about its later payoff is made in this counterexample.

This refutes the radial-envelope proof ingredient, not V_triangle=1/D_m
and not a full-function upper bound. The second method below retains the
actual coordinates and does not use this failed envelope.

## 7. Compactness and an explicit finite guard list for every history

First reduce the unrestricted initial radius without losing an optimizer.
Since ||P_D(x)||<=1, every actual run obeys

  ||x_n||<=||x_0||+S, F(x_n)<=||x_n||,
  E_m(x_0)<=2(r+S)/r^2, r=||x_0||.                 (7.1)

For r>=R:=4D_m, the last quantity is strictly less than1/D_m. One way
to check this is that r^2-2D_m r-2D_m S is increasing for r>=4D_m,
and at4D_m it equals7D_m^2+D_m>0. Meanwhile the punctured ball of
radius1/2 is covered by (4.4) and has the strictly smaller value (4.6).
A point attaining1/D_m exists by (5.2). The unrestricted maximum is
therefore attained in the compact annulus1/2<=r<=R.

It is harmless to use the containing square

  K_R={x: -R<=x_1<=R, -R<=x_2<=R}

for the finite formula: it contains a global optimizer and every extra
point is still a legal original initial point.

Let alpha=(sigma_0,...,sigma_n) range over all7^(n+1) strings of the seven face
labels. There is a label at the terminal n. Define literal affine products
from the original h, with empty products equal to I:

  U_i=I-h_i P_(sigma_i),
  M_0=I, e_0=0,
  M_i=U_(i-1) ... U_0,
  e_i=-sum_(s=0)^(i-1) h_s [U_(i-1) ... U_(s+1)] b_(sigma_s).
                                                        (7.2)

These are finite written products and sums, not executed recurrences.
Every U_i is invertible: its eigenvalues are1 and/or1-h_i, with h_i>1.
In particular each M_i is invertible.

Write the individual guards of Section3 as r_guard dot y<=t_guard. Pull every such
guard at time i back to the same original input x:

  (M_i^T r_guard) dot x <= t_guard-r_guard dot e_i. (7.3)

Adjoin the four square guards. This gives a fully specified finite list
(c_l,d_l), l=1,...,L_alpha, with c_l in R^2, d_l real. Each c_l is
nonzero, since the original normals are nonzero and M_i is invertible.
Let

  C_alpha={x: c_l dot x<=d_l for every l}.          (7.4)

This notation describes the proof, not an unevaluated feasibility oracle:
Sections8--9 give explicit points and literal sign tests, so no search for
an x satisfying (7.4) is left in the final expression.

If x satisfies (7.3), the actual history equals M_i x+e_i by induction:
the selected projection is the actual one, and its update is
U_i y-h_i b_(sigma_i).
Conversely every actual history has such a label at every time and hence
belongs to some C_alpha. Shared boundary histories may have several
labels, but the actual points and objective agree.

If sigma_0=C, then x belongs to D, all actual points are in D by Section4,
and the payoff is p^2. All these strings may be skipped. If sigma_0!=C,
the initial edge or vertex guard gives ||x||>=1/2; thus C_alpha stays
away from0, even when it is lower dimensional. Every C_alpha is compact
because the square guards are included.

## 8. Explicit vertex and edge candidates; no continuous maximization

For one string with sigma_0!=C abbreviate
M=M_n,d=e_n,P=P_(sigma_n),b_0=b_(sigma_n).
By (3.2) the actual numerator on C_alpha is the quadratic

  q_alpha(x)=x^T A x+2ell dot x+k,
  A=M^T P M,
  ell=M^T(Pd+b_0),
  k=d^T P d+2b_0 dot d-||b_0||^2.                 (8.1)

The exact payoff is q_alpha(x)/||x||^2. Define a sign filter on any
explicit point z: retain it precisely when c_l dot z<=d_l for every
l and ||z||^2>0. These are finitely many specified real-number comparisons.

Define Root2(A_0,B_0,C_0) as follows. If A_0!=0 and
Delta=B_0^2-4A_0C_0>=0, list(-B_0+sqrt(Delta))/(2A_0) and
(-B_0-sqrt(Delta))/(2A_0). If A_0=0,B_0!=0, list -C_0/B_0.
Otherwise list nothing. Repeated roots may be kept once. The identically
zero polynomial is handled in the proofs below and contributes no roots.

### 8.1 Vertices

For every pair l<r whose row normals are linearly independent, solve the
two linear equations by the displayed inverse

  z= [c_l^T; c_r^T]^(-1) [d_l; d_r].               (8.2)

Retain it if it passes the sign filter. These candidates include every
vertex of a nonempty compact polygon C_alpha, including a singleton.
Matrix inversion in (8.2) means the explicit two-by-two adjugate divided
by its nonzero determinant; it is not a solver invocation.

### 8.2 Stationary points on each guard line

For each guard c dot x=d_0 put

  C_0=||c||^2>0, u=d_0^2/C_0,
  z_0=(d_0/C_0)c, v=Jc.

The line is x=z_0+t v, z_0 dot v=0, ||v||^2=C_0. Put

  a_e=v^T A v,
  b_e=v dot(Az_0+ell),
  c_e=z_0^T A z_0+2ell dot z_0+k.

Its ratio is

  (a_e t^2+2b_e t+c_e)/(u+C_0 t^2).

For every root returned by

  Root2(-b_e C_0, a_e u-c_e C_0, b_e u),            (8.3)

form z=z_0+t v and apply the sign filter. This list includes every
nonconstant stationary point in the relative interior of a legal edge.
Indeed differentiating the ratio gives twice the polynomial

  -b_e C_0 t^2+(a_e u-c_e C_0)t+b_e u

divided by the positive squared denominator. If the guard line passes
through0, a generated point at0 is discarded; C_alpha itself cannot
contain0. If this polynomial vanishes identically, the ratio is constant
on the connected feasible segment, and its endpoints have the same value
and are already covered by (8.2). Compactness supplies those endpoints.
This also handles a one-dimensional C_alpha.

## 9. Interior candidates and the singular case

At an interior stationary point x!=0 let z=q_alpha(x)/||x||^2 be its
value. Differentiating in both coordinates gives

  (A-zI)x=-ell,
  ell dot x+k=0.                                  (9.1)

If det(A-zI)!=0, these equations give the explicit candidate

  x=-(A-zI)^(-1)ell                               (9.2)

and the polynomial equation

  Pi(z):=k det(A-zI)-ell^T adj(A-zI)ell=0.         (9.3)

This has degree at most two. Its coefficients can be written without a
formal determinant symbol. If
A=[A_11 A_12; A_12 A_22] and ell=(ell_1,ell_2), then

  Pi(z)= k z^2
       +[-k(A_11+A_22)+ell_1^2+ell_2^2] z
       +k(A_11 A_22-A_12^2)
        -A_22 ell_1^2+2A_12 ell_1ell_2-A_11 ell_2^2.  (9.4)

Use Root2 on these three coefficients. For each listed real z with
nonzero det(A-zI), form (9.2) and retain it under the sign filter. Equations
(9.1) then hold, and the ratio at this point is exactly z.

If Pi is identically zero, its quadratic coefficient gives k=0 and its
linear coefficient then gives ||ell||^2=0. Thus (9.2) would only give0,
which is excluded. No nonsingular interior stationary candidate is missed
by letting Root2 return no roots in this case.

It remains to justify discarding singular interior stationary values.
Suppose x is interior, (9.1) holds, and A-zI is singular. Pick a nonzero
w in its kernel. Symmetry and (A-zI)x=-ell imply ell dot w=0. For every
real t, a direct expansion gives

  q_alpha(x+t w)-z||x+t w||^2=0.                  (9.5)

For example the coefficient of t^2 vanishes by the kernel condition,
the linear coefficient vanishes by (9.1), and the constant term is zero
by the definition of z. Intersect the line x+R w with the compact polygon.
It contains a nontrivial segment about x and has boundary endpoints.
The polygon is separated from0, so the ratios there are defined and are
also z by (9.5). Those endpoints are covered by the boundary reasoning
in Section8. If A=zI and ell=k=0, the ratio is constant everywhere,
and any polygon vertex suffices. This proves that no singular stationary
family requires an additional feasibility problem or free parameter.

## 10. The exact finite full-profile expression

Let Z_alpha be the finite list of all points retained from (8.2), (8.3),
and (9.2). It is generated solely by the literal products (7.2),
nonzero determinant divisions, Root2, and the explicit sign filter.
Empty lists contribute no value. Define the finite set of real numbers

  L_m={p^2,1/D_m}
      union over all alpha with sigma_0!=C
             {q_alpha(z)/||z||^2: z in Z_alpha}.     (10.1)

Then the exact whole-initial-state identity is

  V_triangle(m)=max L_m.                            (10.2)

Proof of the upper direction: a global maximum exists in the square by
Section7. If its initial label is C, its value is p^2. Otherwise it is a
maximum of the continuous ratio over some C_alpha. If that polygon is a
point or a line, Section8 supplies a candidate of the same value. In two
dimensions, a boundary maximum is a vertex, a nonconstant edge stationary
point, or on a constant edge, all covered by Section8. An interior maximum
satisfies (9.1); either (9.2) supplies it or (9.5) moves the same value to
the boundary. Thus its value occurs in L_m.

Proof of the lower direction: every sign-filtered candidate belongs to a
legal full original history by Section7 and is evaluated with its own
actual Euclidean radius and the actual terminal polynomial. The extra
values p^2 and1/D_m are attained by Sections4--5. Thus no value in L_m
exceeds V_triangle(m). This proves (10.2).

No convex hull of one nonlinear image is propagated through another map;
no incompatible history is selected for a child. Every guard for the
entire string is imposed on the SAME explicit point z. In particular,
real support reentries and coordinate-order reversals are included.

For fixed m all entries before Root2 belong to
K=Q(sqrt(2),sqrt(3)), a field of degree4 over Q: m and epsilon are rational,
all h_i,D_m are in Q(sqrt(2)), and the body coordinates and guard normals
are in K. Vertices are in K^2. An edge candidate is in K(sqrt(Delta))^2
for one Delta in K. An interior candidate is in K(z)^2 with z satisfying
one quadratic over K. Its payoff belongs to that same field. The maximum
of a finite set is one of its members. Therefore

  [Q(V_triangle(m)):Q]<=8.                          (10.3)

This degree statement concerns the value selected by the exact finite
list; it does not claim that the exponentially many sign tests have been
simplified, or that the maximum is known to be a particular listed value.
