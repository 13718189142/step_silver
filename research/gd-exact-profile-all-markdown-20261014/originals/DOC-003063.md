## 1. Selected concrete family and result

All norms and inner products are Euclidean. Let A be a symmetric positive
definite matrix on a finite-dimensional real space. Write

    d_min = smallest eigenvalue of A,
    d_max = largest eigenvalue of A,  kappa = d_max/d_min,
    E_A = {g : <g,A g> <= 1},
    F_A(x) = max_(g in E_A) {<x,g> - ||g||^2/2}.

I denotes the identity matrix and ||u||_A^2=<u,A u>. The d_l and x_l in
principal-coordinate formulas denote the eigenvalues of A and the
corresponding coordinates of x. Along a trajectory write g_i=g(x_i),
t_i=t(x_i), v_i=v(x_i), and f_i=F_A(x_i).

The selected family has 1 <= kappa <= 2. Thus the squared ratio of its
longest to shortest semiaxis is at most 2. Dimension, orientation, scale,
individual eigenvalues, and initial point may all depend on the ENTIRE
original word. At least two distinct active eigenvalues are permitted;
no common scalar quadratic orbit is assumed. This family is centered.
It does not include arbitrary translated or more eccentric ellipsoids.

    sum_l d_l x_l^2/(1+t(x)d_l)^2 = 1,                     (1)

## 2. One legal function, its actual query, and the true minimum

E_A is nonempty, compact and convex. Completing the square in its defining
maximum shows that its unique maximizing g is the Euclidean projection
of x onto E_A. Existence follows from compactness; uniqueness follows from
strict convexity of ||x-g||^2 on a convex set.

For x in E_A this gives g=x. For x outside, the left side of (1) is
continuous, strictly decreasing on [0,infinity), starts above 1, and
tends to zero. Thus its unique root t is positive, even if some x_l
vanish or some eigenvalues coincide. For g=(I+tA)^(-1)x one has
<g,A g>=1 and, for every z in E_A,

    <x-g,z-g> = t(<g,A z>-1) <= 0,

by Cauchy--Schwarz in the A inner product. Expanding squared distances
then shows ||x-z||^2>=||x-g||^2. This proves the query formula without a
black-box Lagrange multiplier assertion. Write x=(I+tA)g throughout,
including t=0 in the core.

For two projection pairs (x,g),(y,p), their two normal inequalities add
to ||g-p||^2<=<g-p,x-y>. In particular ||g-p||<=||x-y||. Comparing the two
defining maxima for F_A gives

    <g,z> <= F_A(x+z)-F_A(x) <= <g(x+z),z>.

The difference between the left expression and the middle expression is
between zero and ||z||^2. Thus F_A is differentiable, its gradient is g,
and that gradient is 1-Lipschitz. F_A is convex as a maximum of affine
functions of x and is finite by compactness.

Since 0 is feasible, F_A>=0, and F_A(0)=0. If x!=0, choose epsilon>0
small enough that epsilon*x belongs to E_A and epsilon<2. Its test value
epsilon(1-epsilon/2)||x||^2 is positive. Hence argmin F_A={0}, proving the
true minimum and the paid initial radius exactly. Multiplication of F_A
by any L>0 and use of steps h_i/L preserves every point and P_A. This is
the project's W=2R normalization.

The actual value is

    F_A(x)=<x,g>-||g||^2/2
          =||g||^2/2+t<g,A g>
          =||g||^2/2+t.                                 (10)

The last equality holds also in the core because t=0. In particular
F_A(x)=||x||^2/2 there. Actual GD gives the first formula of (2), and the
next t is evaluated at that actual x_plus by (1). No state is reset.

For any two actual samples, including the optimum sample with x=g=t=0,
the complete smooth-convex residual in the convention of accepted

    Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2
        =<x_j-g_j,g_i-g_j>
        =t_j(<g_j,A g_i>-<g_j,A g_j>) <= 0.              (11)

If t_j=0 the last expression is zero. If t_j>0, its final inner product
is at most 1 and <g_j,A g_j>=1. This proves every ordered remote,
adjacent, and optimum inequality at once. In particular Q_(i,*)=0 and

    Q_ij=-(t_j/2)||g_i-g_j||_A^2.                        (12)

Thus for t_i,t_j>0 the ratio Q_ij/t_j equals Q_ji/t_i. A one-way zero

restrictions of this centered ellipsoid, not claims about general

## 3. Genuine coupling and a fully exterior nonlinear example

In principal coordinates put

    D_t=sum_l d_l^2 x_l^2/(1+t d_l)^3 > 0

at an exterior point. Implicit differentiation of (1) gives

    partial t/partial x_l = [d_l x_l/(1+t d_l)^2]/D_t.

For distinct k,l,

    partial g_k/partial x_l
      =-[d_k x_k/(1+t d_k)^2]
        [d_l x_l/(1+t d_l)^2]/D_t.                      (13)

This is nonzero when both coordinates are nonzero. In these coordinates
the query is not a direct sum of independent scalar quadratic queries.
Each denominator shares the changing t. No reduction of the entire
family to a fixed quadratic or one common polynomial orbit is made.
Indeed the Hessian is I in the interior core, whereas differentiating
<g(x),A g(x)>=1 at an exterior point gives the nonzero left null vector
(A g(x))^T for the gradient Jacobian. That exterior Jacobian exists by
(1) and is singular. Thus this function cannot be a fixed quadratic in
any orthonormal coordinate system.

The exterior regime is nonempty and supports changed gradient directions
at every physical query. For ANY nonnegative word of sum S, choose

    A=diag(1,2),  g_0=(1/sqrt(2),1/2),

## 4. One-step multiplier drainage, including zero and huge steps

    1+t d-h <= 1+u d.

    |1+t d-h| <= 1+u d.
