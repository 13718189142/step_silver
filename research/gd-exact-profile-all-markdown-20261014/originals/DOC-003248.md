## 1. Family, definitions, and resolved scope

All vector spaces are finite-dimensional real Euclidean spaces. Matrices
are symmetric unless otherwise specified, and matrix inequalities refer
to positive semidefinite order. The identity matrix is denoted I.

For positive radii r_minus,r_plus, define the scalar asymmetric Huber
function phi by phi(0)=0 and

    phi'(s)=max(-r_minus,min(s,r_plus)).                 (1)

Thus phi(s)=s^2/2 inside [-r_minus,r_plus],
phi(s)=r_plus*s-r_plus^2/2 for s>=r_plus, and
phi(s)=-r_minus*s-r_minus^2/2 for s<=-r_minus.
Both joins include their boundaries, where the values and derivatives agree.

Consider ONE STATIC function

    F(x)=x^T A0 x/2 + sum_(j=1)^r alpha_j phi_j(u_j^T x), (2)

where r is any nonnegative integer, A0>=0, every u_j is a unit vector,
alpha_j>0, and every phi_j has its own positive radii. Assume

    H0=A0+sum_j alpha_j u_j u_j^T <= I.                 (3)

The number of ridges, dimension, matrices, directions, and both radii may
depend arbitrarily on the horizon. The ridges need not be orthogonal.
The quadratic part need not commute with any ridge matrix.

For a fixed finite nonnegative word h=(h_0,...,h_(n-1)), set

    S=sum_(i<n)h_i, K=1/(1+2S),
    P_h(t)=product_(i<n)(1-h_i*t),
    x_(i+1)=x_i-h_i grad F(x_i), 0<=i<n.                (4)

The normalized payoff is

    V=2F(x_n)/dist(x_0,argmin F)^2,                     (5)

and initial points in the minimizer set are omitted.

A **fixed-region run** means that, for EACH ridge separately, all samples
x_0,...,x_n, INCLUDING the actual unqueried terminal sample, belong to one
of that ridge's three closed intervals: its core, its positive outer ray,
or its negative outer ray. Different ridges may choose different regions.
At a shared boundary either compatible region may be chosen. No condition
is imposed on signs or correlations of the full gradients.

The main quantitative conclusion of this joint draft is:

* If P_h(t)^2 <= 1/(1+2S*t) for every t in [0,1], every fixed-region run
  of (2) has V<=K. This includes all true normalization costs.
* On the ORIGINAL diagnostic H_m defined in Section 5, for every m>=128,
  that polynomial inequality holds, and the supremum of V over the entire
  fixed-region class is EXACTLY K_n(H_m)=1/(1+2 sum H_m).
* More broadly, if only the QUERIED samples x_0,...,x_(n-1) share a fixed
  region and the actual terminal sample is unrestricted, then
  K <= sup V <= 1/[1+2S-2(1+a)] on that same H_m. This larger class is
  asymptotically Huber-tight as well; a terminal region change alone
  cannot produce a fixed positive relative improvement.
* Both classes' exact original-benchmark ratios tend to Z_1/(2e)<4/5. Here
  Z_1=lim_(m->infinity)(1+sqrt(2))^(-m) B_(2^m-1), as in accepted
  `04219554e41d9750`, and e is the exponential of 1.

This closes the fixed-queried-region subclass with arbitrary quadratic
coupling and any number of subunit-curvature ridges, allowing any actual
terminal region. It does NOT close trajectories that change a ridge
region within the queried prefix. Section 9 gives a genuinely coupled static example
with two physical sign changes and actual noncommuting region visits.
It refutes dropping the fixed-region premise even on a word satisfying
the same polynomial inequality. Its payoff is explicitly compared with
the stronger already accepted scalar construction and is NOT presented
as new lower-bound strength.

## 2. Global legality, minimizers, and every original pair

Each phi_j is differentiable, convex, nonnegative, and zero only at zero.
For any x,z, monotonicity and the Lipschitz bound in (1) provide numbers
d_j in [0,alpha_j] such that

    grad F(x)-grad F(z)
      =[A0+sum_j d_j u_j u_j^T](x-z).

If u_j^T(x-z)=0 its contribution is zero and d_j may be taken as zero.
The displayed matrix lies between zero and H0, and hence between zero
and I. Its operator norm is at most 1. This proves global 1-smoothness;
convexity also follows directly from the sum in (2).

Conversely, all radii are positive, so a ball about zero lies in every
core. In that ball F(x)=x^T H0 x/2. Thus global 1-smoothness forces (3).
Condition (3) is exact for this centered model, not merely a sufficient
trace bound.

The minimizer set is precisely the subspace

    N=ker(A0) intersect intersection_j u_j^perp.        (6)

Indeed every summand of (2) is nonnegative, and their simultaneous
vanishing is exactly (6). Let z_0 be the orthogonal projection of x_0
onto N and replace every x_i by x_i-z_0. The objective, gradient, ridge
coordinates, GD recursion, and fixed-region condition are unchanged,
because all gradients lie in N^perp. The initial squared norm after
this replacement equals the TRUE squared distance in (5). We therefore
work below with x_0 in N^perp and denominator ||x_0||^2.

The pair convention is the original one:

    Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2,
    f_i=F(x_i), g_i=grad F(x_i), x_star=g_star=f_star=0.

Every Q_ij is nonpositive, including both terminal directions and both
optimum directions. To spell out the accepted necessity argument from
`1252b8823bb5a290`, fix i and define
J(z)=F(z)-<g_i,z>. Convexity and grad J(x_i)=0 make x_i a global
minimizer of J. Smoothness gives
J(x_j-grad J(x_j))<=J(x_j)-||grad J(x_j)||^2/2.
The minimum J(x_i) is at most this value. Rearranging gives Q_ij<=0.
This one argument checks every ordered pair of the actual trajectory;
no subset of remote, zero-step, optimum, or terminal rows is discarded.

## 3. A sharp curvature price, including shifted ridge cores

This section treats a broader geometry than (2): allow an additional
linear term and shifts in every ridge argument, and let each scalar
convex ridge have a nonempty open interval where its second derivative
is the constant alpha_j>0. Assume the whole sum is globally 1-smooth
and its quadratic Hessian A0 is positive semidefinite.

At a point in one ridge core, second differences in an arbitrary
direction z, using nonnegativity of the second differences of all
other convex terms, give

    A0+alpha_j u_j u_j^T <= I.                         (7)

For two NONPARALLEL directions u,v, their shifted open core slabs
intersect. In fact the map x -> (u^T x,v^T x) is surjective onto R^2:
on span(u,v) its matrix has determinant 1-<u,v>^2>0. Thus any desired
pair of interior core arguments can be achieved simultaneously.
At such a point the same second-difference argument gives

    A0+alpha u u^T+beta v v^T <= I.                    (8)

These arguments require no differentiability of other summands at that
point. For sufficiently small real t the two selected core terms have
second differences exactly t^2 alpha<u,z>^2 and t^2 beta<v,z>^2,
while 1-smoothness bounds the total symmetric second difference by
t^2||z||^2.

Writing rho=<u,v>, the largest eigenvalue of
alpha uu^T+beta vv^T on their span is

    [alpha+beta+sqrt((alpha-beta)^2+4alpha beta rho^2)]/2.

Consequently (8) implies alpha,beta<=1 and the sharp necessary bound

    alpha beta rho^2 <= (1-alpha)(1-beta).             (9)

To obtain (9), square the nonnegative inequality
sqrt((alpha-beta)^2+4alpha beta rho^2)<=2-alpha-beta
and cancel. With A0=0 it is also sufficient for this pair's matrix to
be bounded by I, so this numerical curvature price is sharp.

There is a sharp bound on coupling to A0 as well. For one ridge let
s=1-alpha and write A0 relative to u and u^perp as

    A0 = [ a  b^T ; b  C ].

If b is nonzero, put v=b/||b|| and c=v^T C v. Positivity of A0 and
of I-A0-alpha uu^T, restricted to span(u,v), gives

    ||b||^2<=a c,  ||b||^2<=(s-a)(1-c),
    0<=a<=s, 0<=c<=1.

If a<=s(1-c), the first bound is at most s c(1-c). If
a>=s(1-c), the second bound is at most that same quantity. Hence

    ||(I-uu^T)A0 u|| <= sqrt(1-alpha)/2.              (10)

The case b=0 already satisfies this. Equality is attained in dimension
two by u=e_1 and

    A_s=(1/2)[s,sqrt(s);sqrt(s),1], alpha=1-s,
    0<s<1.                                          (11)

Both A_s and I-A_s-alpha e_1e_1^T are rank-one positive semidefinite
matrices. This is an ACTUAL available coupling, not a forbidden one.

In particular a ridge of full individual curvature alpha=1 satisfies
A0 u=0: (7) gives u^T A0 u=0, and positive semidefiniteness implies
A0 u=0. Any two nonparallel full-curvature ridge directions are
orthogonal by (9). Parallel shifted ridge terms must be grouped into
one scalar function; they need not have overlapping cores.

It follows that a sum consisting entirely of full-curvature convex
ridges plus a PSD quadratic, with any shifts and any linear term,
separates orthogonally into scalar ridge groups and an independent
quadratic on their orthogonal complement. If its minimum is attained,
its minimizer set is the Cartesian product of the scalar minimizer
sets and the quadratic minimizer affine subspace. Squared distance to
that product is the sum of squared component distances. GD separates
in the same coordinates. For every h the true-distance payoff is
therefore a weighted average of the actual scalar-group payoffs and
quadratic eigenmode payoffs

    t * product_(i<n)(1-h_i*t)^2, 0<=t<=1.             (12)

A component starting in its minimizer set stays there and contributes
zero to both numerator and denominator. This proves that rotating or
adding such full-curvature ridges cannot yield a genuinely coupled
multidimensional improvement over the best component on the SAME word.
It does not bound arbitrary scalar multi-return profiles by K_n(h).

## 4. Exact terminal algebra and the fixed-region bound

Return to the centered model (2). For a fixed-region run absorb all
always-core ridges into the quadratic matrix and write

    A=A0+sum_(j in Core) alpha_j u_j u_j^T,
    b=sum_(j in Outer) epsilon_j q_j u_j,
    D0=sum_(j in Outer) q_j^2/alpha_j,                 (13)

where epsilon_j is +1 or -1 for its chosen outer ray, and
q_j=alpha_j times its radius on that ray. Every actual sample satisfies

    f_i=x_i^T A x_i/2+b^T x_i-D0/2,  g_i=A x_i+b.    (14)

Condition (3) becomes

    A+sum_(j in Outer) alpha_j u_j u_j^T <= I.         (15)

Let L=I-A. For every vector z, scalar Cauchy--Schwarz and (15) give

    (b^T z)^2 <= D0 * z^T L z.                       (16)

Consequently b is orthogonal to ker L. Define L^dagger by reciprocating
its positive eigenvalues and assigning zero to its zero eigenvalues.
Putting z=L^dagger b in (16), or treating b=0 separately, proves

    D0 >= b^T L^dagger b.                            (17)

Thus the intercept cost required by global smoothness is retained even
when many nonorthogonal ridge forces add in the same direction.

Define polynomials (with their continuous values at t=0)

    R_h(t)=(1-P_h(t))/t, R_h(0)=S,
    T_h(t)=(1-P_h(t)^2)/t, T_h(0)=2S.

Unrolling g_i=A x_i+b yields

    x_n=P_h(A)x_0-R_h(A)b.                           (18)

This identity is also proved inductively using
R_(prefix+h)(t)=(1-h*t)R_prefix(t)+h. Substituting (18) into (14),
and using I-A R_h(A)=P_h(A), gives the EXACT identity

    2f_n=x_0^T A P_h(A)^2 x_0
           +2b^T P_h(A)^2 x_0-D0-b^T T_h(A)b.        (19)

No radius, value, forcing term, or terminal quantity has been removed.

Assume now the polynomial condition

    P_h(t)^2 <= 1/(1+2S*t), 0<=t<=1.                 (20)

Diagonalize A orthogonally. For one eigenvalue t in (0,1), abbreviate
p=P_h(t)^2 and

    d=1/(1-t)+(1-p)/t.

By (17), its contribution to the right side of (19) is bounded by

    t p X^2+2p X B-d B^2,                            (21)

where X,B are the coordinates of x_0,b on this eigenvector. We claim
(21)<=K X^2. First d>0 by (20), and
t p<=t/(1+2S*t)<=K. The determinant needed to complete the square is

    (K-t p)d-p^2
       =[K-p(K+t(1-K))]/[t(1-t)] >=0,               (22)

because K/[K+t(1-K)]=1/(1+2S*t). Thus the two-variable quadratic
K X^2-(21) is positive semidefinite.

At t=0, p=1, and its contribution is at most
2XB-(1+2S)B^2<=K X^2 by completing a square. At t=1, (16) gives
B=0, and the contribution is P_h(1)^2 X^2<=K X^2 by (20).
Summing all eigenvector inequalities proves 2f_n<=K||x_0||^2.
Section 2 identified that norm with the true closest-minimizer distance.
This proves the first quantitative claim in Section 1, including zero
eigenvalues, unit eigenvalues, empty outer sets, and zero physical steps.

Equality is attained by the actual scalar global Huber witness for ANY
nonnegative word: take A0=0, one unit ridge of curvature 1, both radii
q=1/(1+2S), and initial point x_0=1. All iterates are
x_i=1-q sum_(t<i)h_t >=(1+S)/(1+2S)>=q. The fixed positive outer
region condition includes the terminal point. Direct substitution gives
2F(x_n)=1/(1+2S), the initial true distance is 1, and the minimizer is
unique. Hence whenever (20) holds the restricted supremum is exactly K.

## 5. The resolvent inequality on the ORIGINAL H_m, with all curvatures

Fix an integer m>=128, and keep the following enclosing-horizon
parameters fixed in EVERY child word:

    sigma=1+sqrt(2), c=sigma(1+1/m), a=(1-1/m)/c,
    d_j=1+a*c^j,
    H_0=(), H_(j+1)=(H_j,d_j,H_j), 0<=j<m.

The actual physical word is h=H_m, of length n=2^m-1. Write
S_j=sum H_j, M_j=1+S_j, r_c=c-2, and A_mass=a/r_c. Counting the
occurrences of each bridge gives

    M_j=A_mass*c^j+(1-A_mass)*2^j,
    P_j(t)=product_(l=0)^(j-1)(1-d_l*t)^(2^(j-l-1)).   (23)

Here P_j is the polynomial of the complete physical word H_j; P_0=1.

The following bounds follow from squaring the rational comparisons
7/5<sqrt(2)<10/7 and using m>=128:

    12/5<c<49/20, 3/8<a<sigma-2<c-2<9/20,
    127/128<=ac<1, 0<A_mass<1, c*r_c<5/4.            (24)

For the upper c bound, c<(17/7)(129/128)=2193/896<49/20.
For the lower a bound, a>(127/128)/(49/20)>3/8. The upper bound on
a follows from a=(m-1)/[(m+1)sigma]<1/sigma=sigma-2.
In particular (23) gives M_j<=c^j and

    t*S_j >= a*c^j*t/r_c-t.                          (25)

We prove (20) for this exact h. At t=0 it is equality. Fix t in (0,1].
If every d_l*t<1 for 0<=l<m, all factors are nonnegative and
1-z<=exp(-z) gives

    P_m(t)^2 <= exp(-2S_m*t) <= 1/(1+2S_m*t).        (26)

If a factor is zero the required conclusion is immediate. Otherwise
let j be the first level with d_j*t>=1. Set

    v_l=a*c^l*t, Y_l=c*v_l*|P_l(t)|.

If j=0 then Y_0=ac*t<=1. If j>=1, all factors of P_j are on their
nonnegative side, and t<1/d_0<8/11. From (25),

    Y_j<=c*v_j*exp(-v_j/r_c+t)
         <=c*r_c*exp(t-1)<1.                         (27)

The middle bound maximizes v exp(-v/r_c) over v>=0, with maximum
r_c/e. The last inequality uses c*r_c<5/4 and
exp(3/11)>1+3/11=14/11>5/4.

For every l>=j, the exact physical recursion in (23) gives

    Y_(l+1)=[1-(1-t)/v_l]*Y_l^2 <=Y_l^2<=1.          (28)

The bracket is between zero and one, since d_l*t>=1 and t<=1.
Consequently, with z=a*c^m*t,

    P_m(t)^2 <= 1/(c^2*z^2).                         (29)

Activation implies t>=1/d_(m-1). Since m>=3, (24) implies

    z>=c*[a*c^(m-1)]/[1+a*c^(m-1)]
       >(12/5)*(54/25)/(1+54/25)=648/395>3/2.        (30)

Also S_m<c^m by (23). Therefore

    (1+2S_m*t)P_m(t)^2
       <=[1+(16/3)z]/[(144/25)z^2]
       <=25/36<1.                                  (31)

For the final inequality the function z^(-2)+(16/3)z^(-1) decreases
for z>0, and its value at z=3/2 gives 25/36 after multiplication by
25/144. Equations (26) and (31) cover EVERY t in [0,1]. In particular
there was no curvature grid, schedule replay, or fixed-curvature premise.

## 6. Same-word scalar comparison and the ORIGINAL benchmark

We also rederive that the best accepted Huber/single-cut value on this
word is the global Huber value, so the comparison is on the SAME word.
For a cut at level j its squared numerator is (a*c^j)^2. Holding the
total S_m and bridge d_j fixed, the denominator is

    (1+P)^2[1+2(S_m-d_j-P)],

where P is the actual preceding mass. Its logarithm has strictly
negative second derivative in the interior of the allowed mass range.
Thus its minimum over the interval between the first and last occurrence
is at an endpoint. The palindrome H_m makes the following mass at its
first occurrence at least S_(m-1), and its preceding mass is S_j. At
the last occurrence the preceding mass is at least S_(m-1), and the
following mass is S_j. Put M=M_(m-1).

From (23), a*c^j<=r_c*M_j, M_j<=M, and M_m<=c*M. The first-occurrence
cut is at most r_c^2/(2M-1). The last-occurrence cut is at most

    r_c^2*M_j^2/[M^2(2M_j-1)] <= r_c^2/(2M-1),

because x^2/(2x-1) is nondecreasing for x>=1. Finally c<5/2,
r_c<1/2, and M>=1 give

    r_c^2(2M_m-1) <=(5M-1)/4 <=2M-1.

Every cut in accepted `b98bb807d514e80d` is therefore no larger than
1/(2M_m-1). Its global Huber term attains that value. Hence

    sup_fixed-region V = K_n(H_m)=1/(2S_m+1).         (32)

For the limit, divide (23), at j=m, by sigma^m. Since a/(c-2)->1,
(1+1/m)^m->e, and (2/sigma)^m->0, one gets

    M_m/sigma^m -> e.

The accepted ray theorem `04219554e41d9750` supplies
B_(2^m-1)/sigma^m -> Z_1. Thus the exact restricted ratio has limit

    B_(2^m-1)*sup_fixed-region V -> Z_1/(2e).         (33)

For an explicit strict gap, its accepted upper enclosure at b=1,j=0
gives Z_1<=(sqrt(B_1)+3/100)^2. The accepted original recurrence gives
B_1=4. Therefore Z_1<=41209/10000. The positive exponential series
gives e>1+1+1/2+1/6=8/3, whence

    Z_1/(2e)<123627/160000<4/5.                      (34)

No fit, substituted recurrence, internal capacity, or scalar radius
of a higher-dimensional witness is used in this comparison.

## 7. Release the terminal region, retaining the original last step

Continue with exactly the full-horizon parameters of Section 5, and put

    ell=d_0=1+a, S_pre=S_m-ell,
    h_pre=H_m with its last physical entry ell deleted.

This is a deletion in the SAME word with the SAME m,a,c. It is not a
smaller ruler with recomputed parameters. Its polynomial is

    P_pre(t)=P_m(t)/(1-ell*t),                        (35)

where the right side is interpreted as polynomial division. We prove

    P_pre(t)^2 <= 1/(1+2S_pre*t), 0<=t<=1.            (36)

At t=0 this is equality. If any factor is zero, P_pre is zero too:
the factor 1-ell*t has multiplicity 2^(m-1)-1>=1 after deletion,
and all other level factors retain their positive multiplicities.
If there is no activated level d_j*t>=1, the exponential proof (26)
applies directly to the truncated word with its exact sum S_pre.

We may therefore assume positive t, no zero factors, and an activated
level. Define q=|1-ell*t|>0 and z=a*c^m*t. Section 5 supplied

    (1+2S_m*t)/(c^2*z^2)<=25/36,
    Y_(l+1)<=Y_l^2 after the first activated level.    (37)

If t<=1/(10ell), then q>=9/10 and (29),(37) give

    (1+2S_pre*t)P_pre(t)^2
       <=(25/36)/(9/10)^2=625/729<1.                (38)

For the remaining t>1/(10ell), the first activated level j is at most
5. Indeed ell<3/2, hence t>1/15; also
c^4>(12/5)^4>25 and c>2, so d_5>1+(3/8)*50=79/4>15.

First suppose 1<=j<=5. The proof of (27) has the uniform strengthening

    Y_j < (441/400)*(11/14)=4851/5600<7/8.           (39)

All factors in P_j are nonnegative and at most one, and one is q, so
|P_j|<=q. First activation also gives
v_j=c*a*c^(j-1)*t<c, since d_(j-1)*t<1. Thus Y_j<=c^2*q.
For positive M,C,q and integer k>=1, the elementary inequality

    min(M,Cq)^k/q <= C*M^(k-1)                      (40)

follows by treating Cq<=M and Cq>=M separately. Applying it with

    Y_m/q <= c^2*(7/8)^(2^(m-j)-1).

Here k>=32, since m>=128 and j<=5. Squaring, using (37), and using

    (1+2S_pre*t)P_pre(t)^2
       <=(25/36)c^4*(7/8)^(2(k-1))
       <(25/36)(625/16)/64=15625/36864<1.            (41)

For completeness, (7/8)^8<1/2 follows, for example, from

six such eighth-power bounds suffice in (41).

It remains to treat first activation j=0. Put delta=ac=1-1/m.

M=delta^2,C=5/2,k=2^(m-1). It gives

    Y_m/q <=(5/2)delta^(2^m-2).

As 2^(m+1)-4>=4m for m>=4, delta<=exp(-1/m) and e>2 imply

    (1+2S_pre*t)P_pre(t)^2
       <=(25/36)(25/4)delta^(2^(m+1)-4)
       <=(625/144)exp(-4)<625/2304<1.                (42)

All cases in (36) are now proved, including curvatures arbitrarily
close to the removed factor's root. No division by a vanishing factor

## 9. A static multiple-sign escape, and its honest payoff comparison

The next example attacks the fragile extension that the polynomial
condition alone might bound ALL trajectories of (2) by the global Huber

It also shows that the curvature rigidity at alpha=1 is not valid for
arbitrarily small positive slack. It is a scope example, not a new
competitive lower bound on H_m or on arbitrary words.

Take the fixed physical word

    h=(1,4,1,4,1), S=11, K=1/23.                    (46)

Its polynomial is P_h(t)=(1-t)^3(1-4t)^2. For 0<=t<=1/4, all factors
are nonnegative, so P_h(t)^2<=exp(-22t)<=1/(1+22t). For t>=1/4 put

function of z is z^2(3-4z)(9-20z), and the maximum is

    (9/20)^3*(6/5)^2=6561/50000<1/7.

It follows that (1+22t)P_h(t)^2<=23/49<1 on this second interval.
Thus (20) holds on ALL of [0,1] for the same word (46).

Let phi have radii r_minus=3/4 and r_plus=1/2. Fix ANY
