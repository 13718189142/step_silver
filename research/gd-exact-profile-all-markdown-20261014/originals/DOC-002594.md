## 4. Mixed leaves have a concrete, nonzero price

Every legal balanced depth-J tree, J>=5, in THIS same three-vector family
must therefore contain a leaf with rho<=19/20 inside each of its disjoint
depth5 subtrees. There are2^(J-5) such subtrees. Hence at least one leaf
in32 must incur the uniform core deficit(14). This is a necessary density
condition, not a claim that paying it is sufficient for interpolation.

The normalization cost is exact. For a legal node, its true initial
distance is R_L by the retained positive-cone identity, its endpoint norm
squared is r^2 N_L N_R/R_R^2, and A_parent*r=A_L A_R. Consequently

 kappa_parent=kappa_L*kappa_R,
 kappa_root=product_(all leaves)kappa_leaf.             (18)

No lost leaf factor may be omitted. If M_plus denotes the supremum of
kappa over this legal atomic family, the density condition gives

 kappa_root <= M_plus^(2^J-2^(J-5))
                  *(9983/10000)^(2^(J-5)).             (19)

The explicit actual seed in the full joint manuscript has kappa>1, so
M_plus>1; replacing any further deficit factors by M_plus preserves(19).
A zero endpoint makes kappa_root=0 and satisfies(19) directly; the axis
arguments only concern nonzero endpoint nodes.
For an elementary finite bound, R^2>m^2>11, A<6 and
N<=eta+e<1+z<3 imply M_plus<10. The sharper value is left open; the
coarse bound does not claim decay of(19). In particular this result does
NOT exclude all mixed-leaf policies. It identifies the exact normalization
cost that any such horizon-dependent escape must pay. The original
objective-profile target is still unresolved, including the conversion
