## 1. One fixed finite body with variable lower walls

On a finite space with positive weights p_i, let E be the Euclidean space of mean-zero real variables, with inner product E[XY]. Fix a nonnegative variable a with mean a_bar>0 and define

    K_a={g in E: g_i>=-a_i for every i},
    F_a(X)=max_(g in K_a) E[Xg]-E[g^2]/2.

K_a is nonempty, compact and convex; it contains0. F_a is convex1-smooth, has gradient P_(K_a), and attains its minimum0 at the original selected point0. Positive walls give a unique minimum; uniqueness is not needed below. The initial squared radius relative to this selected minimum is E[X0^2], with no replacement by a later radius. A fixed weighted Euclidean isometry identifies this with an ordinary finite-dimensional instance.

The physical projection for a mean-zero X is

    g_i=max(X_i-t,-a_i),   E g=0,

where t>=0. Equivalently E(X-t+a)_+=a_bar. The actual update remains X'=X-hg. The wall labels a, all weights and the final body are fixed before the forward trajectory. A backward atom split duplicates the entire already-built future record, including its current wall label, before an explicitly justified modification.

## 2. Exact inverse formulas, including the wall cross moment

Take h=1+d>1, a mean-zero next law Y, and a real t satisfying

    Z=t+d*a-Y>=0.

Choose a right-branch probability omega_i in[0,1] with E[omega Z]=d*a_bar. The two inverse values are

    X_L=Y-h*a=t-a-Z,
    X_R=t-a+Z/d.

Their projection gradients are -a and -a+Z/d, respectively. Their weighted mean is0, as is their gradient mean, and both physical h updates give the same Y. Thus these are actual inverse points of one projection body. The mean-zero condition also forces t>=0.

Put J=E[omega Z^2], J_a=E[omega a Z], and M_a=E[a^2]. Direct expansion gives

    Var X=Var Y-2h E[aY]+h^2 M_a+2h*t*a_bar
                         -(2h/d)J_a+(d^-2-1)J,
    F_a(X)=t*a_bar+(d+1/2)M_a-E[aY]-(h/d)J_a+J/(2d^2),
    ||P_(K_a)X||^2=M_a-(2/d)J_a+J/d^2.

For the value formula, write X-g=t on the upper branch and X-g=t-Z on the lower branch. Since E g=0, F_a(X)=E[g^2]/2+E[(1-omega)aZ]. This proves the displayed formula without a missing normal contribution.

For nonnegative LOCAL cost coefficients alpha,beta (not the benchmark sequences), define

    C_(alpha,beta)(X)=Var X+2alpha F_a(X)+beta||P_(K_a)X||^2,
    T=h+alpha, C0=1+alpha+beta,
    K0=h^2+alpha(2d+1)+beta.

Then the actual full cost is

    C_(alpha,beta)(X)
     =Var Y-2T E[aY]+K0 M_a+2T*t*a_bar
       -[2(h(1+alpha)+beta)/d]J_a+(C0/d^2-1)J.       (1)

The wall cross moment is real data, not a free scalar reserve.

## 3. The same primitive inverse-cost identity survives

Assume C0>d^2. Define

    w=C0/d^2-1>0, gamma=d+2T/w,
    R=Y+gamma*a.

Choose the unique z with E(R-z)_+=gamma*a_bar. Since E R=gamma*a_bar, one has z>=0. Select all atoms with R>z, optionally a fraction of boundary atoms with R=z, and none with R<z. Let I denote this possibly split selected set, p=P(I)>0, a_I=E[a*1_I]. Set

    t=z+(d+gamma)(a_bar-a_I)/p.                    (2)

This t is nonnegative and gives E[1_I Z]=d*a_bar. It is an admissible inverse threshold if and only if the actual support guard

    t>=max_i(Y_i-d*a_i)                            (3)

holds. Unselected atoms already satisfy (3), since R_i<=z and a_i>=0. Selected atoms still need it. No physical child is rescaled in this construction.

Write U=(R-z)_+. The auxiliary SAME-body projection is

    q=P_(K_a)(Y/gamma)=U/gamma-a.

From w( gamma-d )=2T and direct substitution,

    2(h(1+alpha)+beta)/d=w(d+gamma),
    K0=w*gamma*d.

Therefore the last two terms of (1) combine as w E[1_I Z(t-R)]. Using E U=gamma*a_bar gives

    C_(alpha,beta)(X)
     =Var Y-2T E[aY]+K0 M_a+2T*z*a_bar
                        -w(d+gamma)E[aU]+w E[U^2].             (4)

Also, by expanding the actual q,

    U_gamma(Y):=2gamma F_a(Y/gamma)
       =2z*a_bar+E[U^2]/gamma-2E[aY]-gamma*M_a,
    ||q||^2=E[U^2]/gamma^2-2E[aU]/gamma+M_a.

Consequently (4) is exactly

    C_(alpha,beta)(X)=Var Y+T U_gamma(Y)+B_gamma||q||^2,
    B_gamma=w gamma^2-T gamma=gamma(wd+T)>0.         (5)

Thus the guarded identity from R9 is not limited to equal lower walls. In particular alpha=C_L-1, beta=C_L(C_L-1) and d(C_L+C_R+d)=C_L*C_R yield gamma=C_R and B_gamma=(C_L+d)(C_L+C_R+d), exactly as in the R9 parent note. This remains a conditional attained identity, not a closed composition theorem.

## 4. A genuine future-preserving repair of the support guard

Suppose an entire finite future physical trajectory has already been built with this body, starting at Y and including every future terminal evaluation. Suppose EACH selected atom i in I is unsaturated at every such query: its recorded gradient satisfies

    g_i^(k)=X_i^(k)-t_k

for every future query k. Equality at the clipping boundary is allowed; a strictly clipped lower branch is not.

Increase a_i, only on selected atoms, to any larger finite values. Each old unsaturated projection formula remains unchanged at every future query: max(X_i^(k)-t_k,-a_i) still equals the recorded g_i^(k). Other atoms and all means remain unchanged. Therefore every recorded t_k still solves the projection equation, all future actual gradients/iterates and objective values stay EXACTLY the same. The terminal objective is preserved, and0 remains a legal selected minimizer.

The auxiliary projection q in Section3 is also unchanged. On selected atoms U_i increases by gamma times the wall increase; both E U and gamma*a_bar increase equally. Thus z, selected massp and the set I can be kept fixed (a selected boundary atom may become strictly selected). Because a_bar-a_I is unchanged, (2) keeps the SAME t. On each increased atom Y_i-d*a_i decreases, while unselected atoms already satisfy (3). Choose

    new a_i>=max(old a_i,(Y_i-t)/d)

on the selected atoms. This makes the support guard hold everywhere. Formula (5) then constructs an actual predecessor, at the SAME cost on its right-hand side: VarY, q and U_gamma(Y) did not change.

All choices can be made backwards before the actual forward run. Atom duplication preserves its whole recorded future, and the change above is justified by every recorded query, not only its endpoint. The terminal function is one fixed K_a after all changes; there is no function change during execution.

This is a zero-additional-cost guard repair under an explicit persistent-unsaturation condition. It is NOT permission to increase the wall of an atom that was strictly saturated at any already-built future query. At such a query the old gradient generally changes. Nor does it prove that the selected upper-tail atoms at the next inverse step enjoy the condition.

The hypothesis can be weakened to just the selected atoms that actually require an increase: those with Y_i-d*a_i>t. All other selected walls can be left fixed. Such a violating atom necessarily has Y_i>0, since t>=0 and a_i>=0. This observation narrows the required persistent-unsaturation property; it does not prove it after a reflected inverse branch.

If an already constructed proof also relies on other auxiliary evaluations, their projection records must be included among the samples preserved by this operation, or their changed contributions must be paid separately. Preserving the physical future alone preserves its objective and chronology, but does not automatically preserve a previous auxiliary-potential bookkeeping argument.

## 5. The actual remaining composition requirement

An all-word or full-H_m construction must preserve enough persistent-unsaturated atoms to perform Section4 at every needed inverse join, while controlling the entire prefix cost and terminal scale. The current note does not prove that invariant, nor give an original variance bound at the benchmark scale. It does identify an actual operation that repairs the previous real guard while preserving all built future data; it is stronger than renaming that guard.

Any attempt using this operation must show, for every selected atom and every later physical query, why no strict lower saturation occurred. A high terminal value alone does not imply that history property. If the needed selected atom has already saturated, the free repair is unavailable and the cost of any alternative must be paid explicitly.
