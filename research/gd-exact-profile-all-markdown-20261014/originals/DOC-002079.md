All eligibility endpoints are included. There is no constraint linking
parameters in neighboring stages, and no ordering between kappa_i and
epsilon_i is assumed. In particular, the kappa_i>=1 branch is retained.

The hierarchy for the comparison is fixed by ONE enclosing pair
integer m>=1536 and real 0<tau<=1/4:

    lambda=1+sqrt(2), beta=1-tau/m,
    c=lambda(1+tau/m), a=beta/c,
    H_0=(), H_(j+1)=(H_j,1+a c^j,H_j), 0<=j<m,
    H_(m,tau)=H_m, S_j=sum H_j, n_m=2^m-1,

Every child uses exactly this enclosing (m,tau). Parameters are allowed

## 2. One local curve: exact ordered backward states

This section is a local lemma with kappa,epsilon denoting ONE stage's
parameters. Its proof is repeated for every i with its own pair; no
cross-stage identification is made. Suppress the subscript i in (1).
For 0<=theta<=theta_sp set

    R(theta)=C exp(-kappa theta),
    e(theta)=(cos theta,-sin theta),
    t(theta)=(-sin theta,-cos theta),
    v(theta)=R(theta)e(theta)=(p(theta),-q(theta)),
    n(theta)=e(theta)+kappa t(theta),
    K=conv({0} union {v(theta):0<=theta<=theta_sp}).      (4)

The endpoint slopes are (C,0) and (epsilon,-1). With Delta=phi-theta,

    <n(theta),v(phi)>/R(theta)
        =exp(-kappa Delta)(cos Delta+kappa sin Delta).

The derivative in Delta is
-(1+kappa^2)exp(-kappa Delta)sin Delta. Since |Delta|<pi/2, the maximum
is one at Delta=0. The origin has smaller support value. Thus n(theta)
supports the full K at v(theta), and (0,1) also supports K at (C,0).
Differentiating the coordinates gives

    p'=-R(kappa cos theta+sin theta)<0,
    q'=R(cos theta-kappa sin theta),
    p>=epsilon, q>=0, p>=epsilon q.                    (5)

The sign of q' gives exactly qmax in (2M), including both branches.

For a chronological local list alpha_1,...,alpha_r>=0, initialize
r_r=v_r=(epsilon,-1). Suppose r_j=(u_j,-y_j), with current projected
arc point v_j=v(theta_j). For alpha=alpha_j, use the following backward
definition. If

    y_j<=kappa[u_j+(alpha-1)C],                          (6)

set v_(j-1)=(C,0), r_(j-1)=r_j+alpha(C,0). Otherwise let theta be the
unique root in (0,theta_j] of

    <r_j,e(theta)-kappa^(-1)t(theta)>/R(theta)=1-alpha, (7)

and set v_(j-1)=v(theta), r_(j-1)=r_j+alpha v(theta).
These are exact definitions; no root finder or recurrence is executed.

Here is a proof that the definition exists and that v_j=Proj_K(r_j).
Inductively u_j>=epsilon, y_j>=1, and y_j/u_j<=1/epsilon. Before any cap,

    r_j=R(theta_j)[(1+nu_j)e(theta_j)+kappa nu_j t(theta_j)],
    nu_j>=0.                                           (8)

It holds at j=r with nu_r=0. Under (6), let
mu=u_j+(alpha-1)C>=y_j/kappa>0. Then

    r_(j-1)-v(0)=mu n(0)+(0,kappa mu-y_j)

is a nonnegative combination of supporting normals, proving the
projection. It also gives y_(j-1)<=kappa(u_(j-1)-C). Consequently every
earlier backward step remains capped, including zero steps.

If (6) fails the current state cannot already be capped. By (8), the
left side of (7) is one at theta_j; at zero it is smaller than 1-alpha.
Its derivative is

    (kappa+kappa^(-1))(u_j cos theta+y_j sin theta)/R(theta)>0.

There is therefore exactly one root, with theta=theta_j when alpha=0.
The polar angle of r_j is at least theta_j by (8), so <r_j,t(theta)>
is nonnegative at the root. Equation (7) then states that
r_j+(alpha-1)v(theta) is a nonnegative multiple of n(theta), proving
the preceding projection and (8). Adding alpha(p,-q) and using (5)
preserves u_j>=epsilon, y_j>=1, and y_j/u_j<=1/epsilon.

Define the ACTUAL ordered outputs

    P=sum_(j=1)^r alpha_j p(theta_(j-1)),
    Q=sum_(j=1)^r alpha_j q(theta_(j-1)),
    s_loc=sum_(j=1)^r alpha_j.

Then r_0=(epsilon+P,-1-Q), P>=epsilon Q, and 0<=P<=Cs_loc.
For consecutive uncapped angles theta_old<=theta_new, put
Delta=theta_new-theta_old. Taking the tangent component of the exact
backward update gives

    nu_old=B_D+A_D nu_new,
    A_D=exp(-kappa Delta)(cos Delta+sin Delta/kappa),
    B_D=exp(-kappa Delta)sin Delta/kappa.                (9)

Both coefficients are nonnegative. Write d_sp=1-kappa^2. Direct
differentiation gives
A_D'-(d_sp/kappa)A_D=-(1+kappa^(-2))exp(-kappa Delta)sin Delta<=0.
If kappa<1, A_D<=exp(d_sp Delta/kappa), B_D<=Delta/kappa, and
1+d_sp B_D<=exp(d_sp Delta/kappa). Therefore

    1+d_sp nu_old<=exp(d_sp Delta/kappa)(1+d_sp nu_new).

Multiplication along the decreasing angles proves nu_j<=T. If
kappa>=1, A_D<=1 and B_D<=Delta/kappa give nu_j<=T by summing angle
differences. Hence every uncapped y_j is at most C[1+(1+kappa)T]. A cap
leaves y_j unchanged, proving

    0<=Q<=H_sp, P>=epsilon Q, P<=Cs_loc.               (10)

Every actual arc slope also satisfies

    p+q>=1,
    P+Q>=s_loc,
    (epsilon+P)^2+(1+Q)^2>=(1+epsilon+s_loc)^2/2.       (11)

Indeed R>=sqrt(1+epsilon^2)>=1 and cos theta+sin theta>=1. The last
line is (x+y)^2<=2(x^2+y^2), with x=epsilon+P and y=1+Q. These are
inequalities for the actual ordered outputs, not definitions by mass.

Two further estimates are needed for main-family gluing:

    y_j>=q(theta_j), u_j-p(theta_j)>=-N.               (12)

Before a cap, (8) gives y_j-q_j=R nu_j(sin theta_j+kappa cos theta_j)
>=0; after a cap q_j=0. A nonnegative horizontal component of the
normal in (8) gives u_j>=p_j. Otherwise theta_j>arctan(1/kappa),
so p_j<=kappa qmax, while u_j>=epsilon. At a cap u_j>=C. This proves
both claims with the exact N in (2M).

## 3. Local envelopes, exact zero wedges, and sample values

Choose a local checkpoint b_loc>=1+epsilon^(-2) and a positive sigma
such that

    sigma(P+epsilon b_loc)<=1,
    sigma[P+epsilon+(1+Q)/epsilon]<=1.                 (13)

Set

    z_circ=(1-sigma(epsilon+P),sigma(1+Q))=(c_1,c_2),
    U=1-sigma(P+epsilon b_loc), Y=sigma Q,
    V={0,v(0),v_0,...,v_r},
    phi(z)=sigma max_(v in V)<v,z-z_circ>,
    f_loc(x)=min_z[phi(z)+||x-z||^2/2].                (14)

All actual planes and both endpoints remain present. By (5), each
(p,-q)=[(p-epsilon q)/C](C,0)+q(epsilon,-1), with nonnegative
coefficients. Thus the COMPLETE zero set of phi is

    Z_phi={z:z_1<=c_1,
             epsilon(z_1-c_1)-(z_2-c_2)<=0}.           (15)

The endpoint planes prove necessity; the nonnegative representation
proves sufficiency. Conditions (13) imply c_1>=c_2/epsilon>=0, so phi
vanishes on z_1<=0,z_2>=0. Also U>=0, and for any slope in V,

    <v,(U,Y)-z_circ>=sigma[(1-b_loc)epsilon p+q]<=0.

Consequently phi(U,y)=0 whenever y>=Y. These two zero regions use only
THIS stage's epsilon and do not require the next stage's epsilon.

For completeness, if J is a nonnegative finite maximum of affine
functions with a zero, its envelope env_1 J has a unique proximal
minimizer z at every x. This follows from coercivity and strict
convexity of J(z)+||x-z||^2/2. The vector g=x-z belongs to partial J(z).
For a second proximal pair z',g', subgradient monotonicity gives

    ||g-g'||^2<=<g-g',x-x'>.

The trial point z yields
env_1 J(x+w)<=env_1 J(x)+<g,w>+||w||^2/2. The subgradient inequality
at z, applied to the proximal point z' for x+w, yields
env_1 J(x+w)>=env_1 J(x)+<g,w>+||w-(z'-z)||^2/2. These inequalities
prove differentiability with gradient g and its 1-Lipschitz property.
Convexity follows by taking convex combinations of trial pairs in the
jointly convex defining objective. Nonnegativity and attainment give
min env_1 J=0 and argmin env_1 J={J=0}.

At each x_j^loc=z_circ+sigma r_j, projection of r_j onto K shows that
z_j^loc=x_j^loc-sigma v_j is proximal: for every w in V,
<w-v_j,r_j-v_j><=0, so sigma v_j is an active subgradient of phi there.
Thus the exact local gradient and value are

    grad f_loc(x_j^loc)=sigma v_j,
    f_loc(x_j^loc)=sigma^2[<v_j,r_j>-||v_j||^2/2].      (16)

The local chronological path starts at (1,0), follows ALL alpha_j,
reaches (1-sigma P,sigma Q), and its checkpoint b_loc sends it to
(U,Y+sigma b_loc). During the ordinary block the first coordinate is
at least U and the second belongs to [0,Y]. These claims follow by
subtracting successive alpha_j sigma v_(j-1) and noting p,q>=0.

## 4. Source assembly with independent stage parameters

For each i<k apply Sections 2--3 with its own (kappa_i,epsilon_i),
the local list block_i/2, and b_loc=b_i/2. In THIS source assembly,
P'_i,Q'_i and r'_(i,j),v'_(i,j) always denote these HALF-step outputs
and states. Use exactly

    sigma'_i=1/[C_i s_i/2+epsilon_i b_i/2+A_i].          (17)

By (10) and (1), both conditions (13) hold. Define the resulting local
objects phi'_i,f'_i,c'_(i,1),c'_(i,2),U'_i,Y'_i by (14). Let

    G'_i=sigma'_i b_i/2=b_i/(C_i s_i+2A_i+epsilon_i b_i),
    D'_0=1, ell'_0=0,
    D'_(i+1)=D'_i G'_i,
    ell'_(i+1)=D'_i sigma'_i Q'_i,
    delta'=D'_k/(1+s_k).                               (18)

For d>0 define Hub_d(z)=0 for z<=0, z^2/2 for 0<=z<=d, and
dz-d^2/2 for z>=d. Its derivative is respectively 0,z,d, so it is
convex and 1-smooth. In R^(k+1), with x_0=e_0, define the SINGLE
STATIC function

    F(x)=(1/2)sum_(i<k)(D'_i)^2
          f'_i((x_i-ell'_i)/D'_i,x_(i+1)/D'_i)
                 +(1/2)Hub_(delta')(x_k-ell'_k).        (19)

All scales are positive and finite and are fixed before the run.
Every term is nonnegative, and zero is a common minimizer by (15).
Each scaled local term has its 1-smooth upper quadratic bound on the
two coordinates before the factor 1/2. Every coordinate is counted at
most twice, including the tail. Hence

    F(x+w)<=F(x)+<grad F(x),w>+||w||^2/2.

This proves the advertised global smoothness. More explicitly, for a
convex differentiable function satisfying this upper bound, apply it
to V(z)=F(z)-<grad F(y),z> at x with w=-grad V(x). Since y minimizes
V, this gives

    F(x)-F(y)-<grad F(y),x-y>
                      >=||grad F(x)-grad F(y)||^2/2.  (20)

Adding the exchanged inequality and applying Cauchy--Schwarz proves
that grad F is 1-Lipschitz on the full space.

At the start of stage i, coordinates j<i are frozen at
ell'_j+D'_j U'_j, the current coordinate is ell'_i+D'_i, and later
coordinates are zero. During this stage, x_i>=ell'_i and
0<=x_(i+1)<=ell'_(i+1). Every preceding component is at its OWN
post-checkpoint zero region: its first local coordinate is its U'_j,
and its second is at least Y'_j. Every future component is at its OWN
pre-activation zero region, with first local coordinate at most zero
and second zero. For j=i+1 the first assertion uses
x_(i+1)<=ell'_(i+1); for j>i+1 it uses ell'_j>=0. These separate tests
retain epsilon_j in each wedge and never transfer an epsilon value
from a neighbor. The tail is also inactive until its stage.

Thus the current summand is the only one with nonzero gradient. At
every ordinary query and at the query for the checkpoint, its exact
point is the embedded local x_j^loc, and its gradient and value are

    grad F(x)=D'_i sigma'_i v'_(i,j)/2 on coordinates (i,i+1),
    F(x)=(D'_i sigma'_i)^2
                [<v'_(i,j),r'_(i,j)>-||v'_(i,j)||^2/2]/2. (21)

All other gradient coordinates vanish. A physical step h_t therefore
implements the local step h_t/2 exactly. At checkpoint b_i the next
coordinate becomes ell'_(i+1)+D'_(i+1), and coordinate i freezes at
ell'_i+D'_i U'_i. This proves the induction through every physical
query, including zero steps and all eligibility equalities.

In the tail, if u is the already executed physical tail mass, then
x_k-ell'_k=D'_k-delta' u/2>=delta'. Therefore

    grad F(x)=delta' e_k/2,
    F(x)=[delta'(D'_k-delta' u/2)-(delta')^2/2]/2,
    2F(x_n)=(D'_k)^2/[2(1+s_k)].                      (22)

All earlier summands remain zero because the tail coordinate is at
least ell'_k. This includes the unqueried terminal sample.

The COMPLETE minimum set of (19) is the polyhedron

    C_source={x:x_i<=zeta'_i,
      epsilon_i x_i-x_(i+1)<=epsilon_i zeta'_i-D'_i c'_(i,2), i<k;
      x_k<=ell'_k},
    zeta'_i=ell'_i+D'_i c'_(i,1).                       (23)

Necessity and sufficiency follow from nonnegativity of all summands
and the exact local wedges (15). It contains zero. When k>=1, e_0
violates its first horizontal constraint, so
0<rho_S^2:=dist(e_0,C_source)^2<=1.

Here is an exact finite, non-executed formula for the TRUE distance,
retaining all later rows. Let A_rad have rows e_i^T and
(epsilon_i e_i-e_(i+1))^T for i<k, followed by e_k^T. Let t_rad be
their right sides from (23). For each linearly independent row subset I,
let A_I be the submatrix, r_I=A_I e_0-(t_rad)_I,
K_I=A_I A_I^T, and lambda_I=K_I^(-1)r_I. Include the empty subset
with value zero. Then

    rho_S^2=max_(I independent,lambda_I>=0) r_I^T K_I^(-1)r_I. (24)

For any feasible x and any nonnegative row weights lambda,
||e_0-x||^2>=2lambda^T r_I-||A_I^T lambda||^2 by completing a square.
This proves each listed value is a lower bound. At the actual
projection x_*, e_0-x_* is in the cone of active row normals. To
justify that assertion, the finitely generated cone is closed: remove
linear dependences from any nonnegative representation until an
independent subset remains, giving a finite union of closed injective
images of orthants. If the normal were outside this cone, its residual
from projection onto the cone would have nonpositive inner product
with each active row and positive inner product with e_0-x_*. A small
move in that residual direction would preserve the active inequalities
and, by their strict slacks, every inactive inequality, contradicting
projection optimality. Reduce the normal's nonnegative representation
to an independent active subset. Activity then gives r_I=K_I lambda_I
and equality in (24). This is an identity, not a projection computation.

A quantitative bound uses just the two FIRST rows, while keeping the
entire polyhedron in the feasible set. Put
z_bar=(c'_(0,1),c'_(0,2),0,...,0). Then

    e_0-z_bar=sigma'_0(P'_0-epsilon_0 Q'_0)e_0
       +sigma'_0(1+Q'_0)(epsilon_0 e_0-e_1).           (25)

Both coefficients are nonnegative by (10), and the first two rows
are equalities at z_bar. Thus <e_0-z_bar,x-z_bar><=0 for every x in
the FULL C_source, whether or not z_bar is feasible for later rows.
Expanding squared distance and then using (11) with s_loc=s_0/2 gives

    rho_S^2>=(sigma'_0)^2[(epsilon_0+P'_0)^2+(1+Q'_0)^2]
            >=(sigma'_0)^2(s_0+2)^2/8.                (26)

Combining (18), (22) and (26), with D'_1=sigma'_0 b_0/2, proves

    E(F,h)<= b_0^2/[(s_0+2)^2(1+s_k)]
            *product_(i=1)^(k-1)
               [b_i/(C_i s_i+2A_i+epsilon_i b_i)]^2.  (27)

This is an UPPER comparison of the actual payoff, with the actual
half-step outputs used to prove its radius bound. It does not identify
the true distance with a one-stage terminal projection. For k=0,
the source family is F=Hub_(1/(1+s_0))/2 from 1. Its closest radius
is exactly 1 and its payoff is 1/[2(1+s_0)].

For any two actual samples a,b, including every minimizer and the
terminal sample, (20) implies the complete directed residual
Q_ab=F(x_a)-F(x_b)+<grad F(x_a),x_b-x_a>
+||grad F(x_a)-grad F(x_b)||^2/2 is nonpositive. All physical gradients
belong to R^(k+1), so their full Gram matrix is positive semidefinite.
No interpolation row is removed.

## 5. Main assembly with independent stage parameters

For each i<k use its own parameters in Sections 2--3, now with the
FULL physical ordinary list block_i and b_loc=b_i. Write P_i,Q_i and
r_(i,j),v_(i,j) for these full-step outputs and states. Define exactly

    M_i^tr=P_i+max(epsilon_i b_i,epsilon_i+(1+Q_i)/epsilon_i),

Conditions (13) hold by definition. Let phi_i,c_(i,1),c_(i,2),U_i,Y_i
be the corresponding (14). Define one STATIC finite maximum and its

    Phi_i(z)=D_i^2 phi_i((z_i-ell_i)/D_i,z_(i+1)/D_i), i<k,

sigma_i(1+Q_i-y_j+q_j), which belongs to [0,sigma_i(1+Q_i)]. Its

using the full eligibility (2M) and (12). Embed this local proximal

first local input is its own U_(i-1), and its second input is at least
ell_i/D_(i-1)=sigma_(i-1)(1+Q_(i-1))>=Y_(i-1). Earlier terms have

Every further term has first input -ell_j/D_j<=0 and second zero.

J. The vector x-z=D_i sigma_i v_(i,j) is its subgradient, proving that

    grad f(x)=D_i sigma_i v_(i,j) on coordinates (i,i+1),

                   [<v_(i,j),r_(i,j)>-||v_(i,j)||^2/2]. (31)

earlier coordinates are frozen at ell_j+D_j U_j, the current coordinate

    D_i sigma_i(Q_i+b_i)=ell_(i+1)+D_(i+1).

This proves the state induction through every physical entry. Zero
steps retain the identical gradient and value by the local definition;
the proof places no upper bound on an ordinary step.

In the tail, if u<=s_k is its already executed mass, the actual last
coordinate is ell_k+D_k-delta u. Its proposed proximal coordinate

The preceding term is in its post-checkpoint zero region; every earlier

    grad f(x)=delta e_k,
    f(x)=delta(D_k-delta u)-delta^2/2,
    2f(x_n)=D_k^2/(1+2s_k).                            (32)

This includes s_k=0, with the endpoint proximal coordinate equal to
ell_k. It proves the advertised physical terminal value, without an
extra hidden step.

The COMPLETE minimum set is

    C_main={x:x_i<=zeta_i,
      epsilon_i x_i-x_(i+1)<=epsilon_i zeta_i-D_i c_(i,2), i<k;
      x_k<=ell_k},
    zeta_i=ell_i+D_i c_(i,1).                          (33)

The point z_bar=(c_(0,1),c_(0,2),0,...,0) is feasible for (33).
For the first stage it is its center. For the second stage its first

inputs are nonpositive. For stages i>=1, each second local input is zero. When k=1,

The first-stage normal identity (25), now unprimed and using full-step
outputs, proves <e_0-z_bar,x-z_bar><=0 for every feasible x. Therefore

    rho_M^2=dist(e_0,C_main)^2
           =sigma_0^2[(epsilon_0+P_0)^2+(1+Q_0)^2]>0.  (34)

There is no epsilon_1 in this normal calculation or feasibility test.
Combining (28), (32), and (34) yields the EXACT actual payoff

    E(f,h)=(b_0-1)^2/
       {[(epsilon_0+P_0)^2+(1+Q_0)^2](1+2s_k)}
        *product_(i=1)^(k-1)[(b_i-1)/M_i^tr]^2.        (35)

For k=0 use f=Hub_(1/(1+2s_0)) from 1. Its closest radius is 1 and

    Q_ab=J(z_a)-J(z_b)+<grad f(x_a),z_b-z_a><=0

by the subgradient inequality. This includes all physical samples,

## 6. The unchanged hierarchy and a local parameter dichotomy

Let

    r_h=c(c-2), v_h=a/(c-2)=beta/r_h,
    M_j=1+S_j, d_j=a c^j, u_j=d_j/M_j.                 (36)

Here u_j is hierarchy notation, distinct from the local horizontal
coordinate in Section 2. Writing x=tau/m gives

    r_h=1+(4+2sqrt(2))x+lambda^2 x^2>1>beta,
    M_j=v_h c^j+(1-v_h)2^j,
    0<v_h<1, 1<=M_j<=c^j, a<=u_j<=c-2.              (37)

The formula solves M_(j+1)=2M_j+d_j with M_0=1, by direct
substitution. Its inequalities follow from 2<c and 0<v_h<1. Throughout
the stated domain,

    2<c<121/50, 1/4<a<=u_j<21/50<1/2,
    0<r_h-1<2/m, r_h<2.                              (38)

For example, sqrt(2)<283/200 implies lambda<483/200, and
c<=lambda(1+1/6144)<484/200. Also beta>3/4, c<3 give a>1/4.
The expansion in (37) and 4+2sqrt(2)<7, lambda^2<6 give
r_h-1<7/(4m)+3/(8m^2)<=31/(16m)<2/m.

For EACH local pair with C_i<r_h, and only for that pair, (1) gives

    epsilon_i^2<C_i^2-1<4/m+4/m^2<=6/m<=1/256,
    epsilon_i<1/16, theta_sp,i>23/16,
