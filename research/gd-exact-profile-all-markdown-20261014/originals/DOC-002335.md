## Independent attack: primitive envelope

First lambda^3=7+5sqrt(2)>14 because (7/5)^2<2.
Thus lambda^15>14^5=537824>524288=2^19, and rho>19/15>1.

For t>0 set E(t)=(1+t)^rho-1>0 and Q(z)=log E(exp z).
Writing t=exp z gives
Q'(z)=rho*t*(1+t)^(rho-1)/E(t).
Differentiating once more and collecting the numerator gives
Q''(z)=rho*t*(1+t)^(rho-2)
 *[(1+rho*t)E(t)-rho*t*(1+t)^rho]/E(t)^2
=rho*t*(1+t)^(rho-2)
 *[(1+t)^rho-1-rho*t]/E(t)^2.
The bracket has value zero at t=0 and strictly positive derivative for t>0,
so Q is convex. Its midpoint inequality at z and -z yields
E(t)E(1/t)>=(2^rho-1)^2=2.

For positive x,y and T=(x^(1/rho)+y^(1/rho))^rho, multiplication gives
(T-x)(T-y)>=2xy. Equivalently T^2-(x+y)T-xy>=0.
Its negative root is strictly negative and T>0, so T>=Omega(x,y).
If x=0 or y=0, the asserted inequality is equality directly; at x=y=0 both
sides vanish. No use of log 0 or division by 0 is needed.
Omega is coordinatewise nondecreasing on the closed nonnegative quadrant
because both its linear part and the polynomial under its square root are.
Induction uses k+1 and n-k, both positive for every allowed integer split,
whose sum is n+1. The n=0 case is equality.

## Independent attack: sharper scalar inequality and global maximum

It suffices to use p=19/30 in (5/6)x^(2p)+(1-x)^p<=1 because the bases are
in [0,1] and p>0. Endpoint powers are well-defined including base zero.
Let Psi(x)=(5/6)x^(19/15)+(1-x)^(19/30).
Its derivative on (0,1) is
(19/18)x^(4/15)-(19/30)(1-x)^(-11/30).
A critical point therefore satisfies
x^(4/15)(1-x)^(11/30)=3/5,
equivalently x^8(1-x)^11=(3/5)^30.

The logarithmic derivative of x^8(1-x)^11 is
(8-19x)/(x(1-x)); it is negative on (8/19,1).
For x>=2/3 the left side is at most 2^8/3^19. The strict comparison to the
critical value follows from the exact integer identities

can be checked by successive integer multiplication, for example from

Thus every critical point lies strictly below 2/3.

Substituting the derivative relation gives the exact critical value

## Independent attack: dominant induction

For x,y>=0,
sqrt(G(x,y))=(sqrt(y)+sqrt(y+8x))/2.
Squaring proves this identity. The expression is the larger nonnegative root
of z^2-sqrt(y)z-2x=0, and is positive unless both x and y vanish.
For a positive trial z, nonnegativity of this polynomial puts z above that
root; the other root is nonpositive. G is coordinatewise nondecreasing.

Set kappa=12/5. At every split, A=k+1>=1, F=n-k>=1, N=A+F=n+1.
The trial z=sqrt(kappa)N^(rho/2) has nonnegative root polynomial exactly when
(2/kappa)(A/N)^rho+(F/N)^(rho/2)<=1.

using the already established C bound, controls each split and hence its
full maximum. B_0=1<=12/5 is included.
