# A quantitative terminal gain from any negative primitive-energy witness

Status: self-contained UNVERIFIED candidate. No new fact_submit is allocated.
The only accepted predecessor used is1252b8823bb5a290. This directly constructs
one legal function resisting ALL appended final steps; it does not assume the
unverified terminal SDP or primitive-equivalence candidates.

## 1. Input and output

Fix a nonnegative prefix h_pre of length k and sum S. Write

    t=(-1+sqrt(8S+9))/4,  b_0=1+t,
    r=1/(2t+1),  q=r^2,  c=1/(S+1).

Thus t=r(S+1), c t=r, and q=1/[1+2(S+b_0)]. Define on a smoothness-one
prefix trajectory

    V_prim=2sum_(i<k)h_i(f_i-f_k)
       +sum_(i<k)h_i(1-h_i)||g_i||^2-S(S+1)||g_k||^2.   (1)

Suppose a legal finite-dimensional prefix witness has V_prim<0. The following
finite construction gives a normalized legal prefix and one fixed convex
1-smooth function F whose trajectory under h_pre satisfies, for EVERY b>=0,

    2F(x_k-b g_k) >= q+epsilon Delta/2 > q.             (2)

All of epsilon and Delta are explicit in the supplied witness and finite
Gram/jet operations below. Therefore

    inf_(b>=0) W_(k+1)(h_pre,b) >= q+epsilon Delta/2.    (3)

The bound retains every old anchor. Its constants depend on the actual
negative-energy witness, its normalization, and the prefix geometry; no
horizon-uniform comparison with the exact Zhang--Jiang profile is claimed.

First compress zero steps. Energy(1) is unchanged, since a zero step's
coefficient vanishes and the final query can be retained as representative.
The all-zero case has V_prim=0, so a negative witness leaves a nonempty
positive prefix. Work with that prefix until restoring duplicates at the end.

## 2. Saturating every outgoing optimum constraint

We may first arrange that the k+1 prefix gradients are linearly independent,
while retaining a negative energy. If they are not, take a direct sum with a
small positively weighted actual quadratic prefix whose gradients are independent.
Such an auxiliary prefix is obtained from k+1 distinct eigenvalues in(0,1):
the gradients evaluate z P_i(z), where P_i(z)=product_(u<i)(1-h_u z), and
the P_i have distinct degrees0,...,k with nonzero leading coefficients.
After division of each row by its nonzero eigenvalue this is an invertible
polynomial evaluation matrix. Choose all initial coordinates nonzero.

Energy(1) is linear in the Gram/value data of a direct sum. If the original
energy is -D_0<0 and the auxiliary energy is E_aux, a weight
0<zeta<=D_0/[2(D_0+|E_aux|)] keeps the combined energy at most -D_0/2.
Its gradient Gram matrix is positive definite. This direct sum is an actual
smooth convex function and supplies every finite-prefix Q inequality.

For this independent-gradient prefix choose

    f_*<min_(0<=i<=k)(f_i-||g_i||^2/2).

Solve the finite linear system for z_*:

    <g_i,z_*>=f_*-f_i+<g_i,x_i>-||g_i||^2/2,
                         0<=i<=k.                    (4)

Independence guarantees a solution, for example in the span of the gradients
using their positive definite Gram matrix. Append the jet(z_*,0,f_*).
Then EVERY outgoing optimum residual Q_(i,*) is zero, while EVERY incoming
one is strictly negative:

    Q_(*,i)=f_*-f_i+||g_i||^2/2<0.

All finite-prefix inequalities are unchanged. Translate z_* to zero and
subtract f_* from all values. The interpolation construction in the accepted
fact gives a globally convex 1-smooth function with exactly those jets and
minimum zero. Its initial distance R=||x_0-z_*|| is positive; if it were zero,
the nonzero independent g_0 would be the gradient at a minimizer.
Scale vectors by1/R and values by1/R^2. Denote this normalized prefix by B.
It now satisfies

    ||x_0^B||=1,  Q_(i,star)^B=0 for every i,
    E_B:=V_prim(B)<0.                                 (5)

The chosen minimizer is allowed to differ from the original witness's
minimizer. Every interpolation direction has been checked before making
that change; no function value or normalization is independently reset.

## 3. One explicit two-dimensional reference prefix

Put omega=1/(1+q t). Let A be the even Huber function with threshold q,
and let C have derivative min(c,x) and value C(0)=0. Use the fixed product

    F_A(z_1,z_2)=omega A(z_1/sqrt(omega))
                 +(1-omega)C(z_2/sqrt(1-omega)),
    x_0^A=(sqrt(omega),sqrt(1-omega)).

Both coordinate functions are convex differentiable and 1-smooth; hence
so is F_A, with minimum zero. For P_i=sum_(u<i)h_u its full prefix is

    x_i^A=x_0^A-P_i g_A,
    g_A=(sqrt(omega)q,sqrt(1-omega)c).

The first scalar coordinate stays above q because q(S+1)<1; the second
stays at least c because c(S+1)=1. Thus these gradients and positions are
the actual full trajectory for the prescribed order of h_pre.
Define its prefix proximal data y_i^A=x_i^A-g_A and
delta_i^A=F_A(x_i^A)-||g_A||^2/2. At its terminal point for b_0, put

    w_A=(sqrt(omega)q,-sqrt(1-omega)c t).

Direct scalar evaluation gives the following identities:

    <g_A,w_A>=0,
    2<x_k^A,w_A>-||w_A||^2=q,
    delta_k^A=<y_k^A,w_A>,
    x_k^A-w_A=b_0 g_A+(1-r)y_k^A.                     (6)

For the orthogonality, use q=c^2t^2 and 1-omega=omega q t.
For the last identity, the second coordinate is immediate and the first
reduces to r[1-q(S+1)]=(1+t)q. The remaining identities follow by evaluating
the two scalar values, each of which has normalized terminal error q and
zero outgoing interpolation residual to the optimum.

The two vectors g_A and y_k^A are independent: y_k^A has positive first
coordinate and zero second coordinate, while g_A has positive second
coordinate. For each earlier i<k the exact inactive-plane margin is

    margin_i:=delta_i^A-<y_i^A,w_A>
          =(1-omega)b_0 c^2(S-P_i)>0.                 (7)

This follows by evaluating the capped-coordinate terminal-to-i residual;
the Huber coordinate's corresponding residual is zero. Positive compressed
steps guarantee S-P_i>0.

## 4. The negative energy provides a positive perturbation slope

For prefix B set

    y_i^B=x_i^B-g_i^B,  delta_i^B=f_i^B-||g_i^B||^2/2,
    V_B=x_k^B-b_0 g_k^B-(1-r)y_k^B,
    Phi_B=2(1-r)delta_k^B+||V_B||^2,
    Delta=-q E_B>0.

The following identity is obtained by expanding V_B and telescoping
||x_0^B||^2-||x_k^B||^2 along GD:

    q||x_0^B||^2-Phi_B
      +2q sum_(i<k)h_i Q_(i,star)^B
      +2q(S+2)Q_(k,star)^B=q E_B.

Indeed V_B=r[x_k^B-(S+2)g_k^B], because t+r=r(S+2). Expansion cancels
all x_i--g_i pairings against the star terms and leaves precisely(1).
Using the normalization and saturation in(5), it follows that

    Phi_B=q+Delta.                                    (8)

Let u_1 be the unique vector in span(g_A,y_k^A) solving

    <g_A,u_1>=-<g_k^B,V_B>,
    <y_k^A,u_1>=delta_k^B-<y_k^B,V_B>.                 (9)

Its existence follows from the positive definite two-vector Gram matrix
identified after(6). Put D=||u_1||^2>=0 and for i<k put

    remote_i=delta_i^B-<y_i^B,V_B>-<y_i^A,u_1>.

Choose explicitly

    epsilon=min{1/2, Delta/[2(D+Delta)],
                    min_(i<k) margin_i/[2(margin_i+|remote_i|)]}. (10)

Every entry is positive, so 0<epsilon<=1/2. Computing u_1 requires only
a two-by-two linear system; no optimization or numerical sweep is involved.

## 5. Retaining all prefix constraints and all terminal anchors

Take the actual direct-sum prefix C with weights1-epsilon and epsilon:

    x_i^C=(sqrt(1-epsilon)x_i^A,sqrt(epsilon)x_i^B),
    g_i^C=(sqrt(1-epsilon)g_A,sqrt(epsilon)g_i^B),
    f_i^C=(1-epsilon)f_i^A+epsilon f_i^B.

It is realized by the corresponding scaled product of the two legal
functions. Its initial squared norm is one, its minimum value is zero,
and every ordered prefix Q is the same convex combination of the two
valid Q values. Thus it retains the complete prefix, not just selected edges.
Define one FIXED terminal test vector

    w_C=(sqrt(1-epsilon)[w_A+epsilon u_1/(1-epsilon)],
                                          sqrt(epsilon)V_B).

Equation(9) gives <g_k^C,w_C>=0. For the last prefix plane its other
equation gives delta_k^C-<y_k^C,w_C>=0. For EVERY earlier plane,

    delta_i^C-<y_i^C,w_C>
        =(1-epsilon)margin_i+epsilon remote_i
                                      >=margin_i/2>0, (11)

where the last inequality follows from(10). The optimum plane has value zero.
Consequently the maximum of the prefix conjugate affine planes at w_C is
EXACTLY zero. No earlier plane can defeat the displayed terminal lower bound.

## 6. An exact gain expression for one globally legal function

By accepted fact1252b8823bb5a290 the maximal prefix interpolant

    F=[v -> ||v||^2/2+max_j(<y_j^C,v>-delta_j^C)]^*

is a globally convex differentiable 1-smooth function with minimum zero,
interpolating EVERY prefix jet C. Its trajectory under h_pre is therefore
exactly C. The test-vector orthogonality and(11) imply, for EVERY b>=0,

    2F(x_k^C-b g_k^C)
       >=2<x_k^C,w_C>-||w_C||^2=Gamma_epsilon.

Expanding this final expression, then using(6) and(9), gives

    Gamma_epsilon
       =(1-epsilon)q+epsilon Phi_B
                             -epsilon^2 D/(1-epsilon)
       =q+epsilon Delta-epsilon^2 D/(1-epsilon).        (12)

For clarity, the cross term from the A block is
2epsilon<b_0 g_A+(1-r)y_k^A,u_1>. Combined with the B block this equals

    epsilon[2<x_k^B,V_B>-||V_B||^2
       -2b_0<g_k^B,V_B>
       +2(1-r)(delta_k^B-<y_k^B,V_B>)]=epsilon Phi_B,

by the definition of V_B. This checks every term in(12).

The second bound in(10) gives

    epsilon D/(1-epsilon)<=Delta D/(2D+Delta)<=Delta/2.

Substituting in(12) proves(2)-(3). F is fixed before the final step is chosen;
the same single test vector proves its bound on the entire nonnegative ray.

Restore zero steps by repeating their actual query jets. The duplicate
conjugate planes add nothing to the finite maximum, and every trajectory
identity is unchanged. The actual normalized initial distance remains one.
For any prescribed L>0, replace F with L F and divide its objective by L.

The construction turns ANY concrete failure of intrinsic primitivity into
an explicit strictly stronger final-stage lower bound. Its gain can be small
because the saturation radius, independent-gradient perturbation, and remote
anchor margins can deteriorate. Controlling those constants along a whole
schedule remains an additional quantitative problem.
