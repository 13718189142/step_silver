# Positive-height signed interval simplexes: construction and a scoped loss

Status: **complete joint draft, not verifier accepted**. No optimization,
installation, or fact submission is used. Accepted facts
`1252b8823bb5a290` and `b98bb807d514e80d` supply, respectively, the
smooth-convex interpolation convention and the exact benchmark recursions.
Every other assertion used below is proved in this document. The sharper
benchmark comparison was recalled from shared finding `70e9da73936cb7b3`
as a lead; its complete elementary proof is included here and that finding
is not a correctness premise.

This is a new positive-height construction, rather than an extension of
the previous zero-height bound. It gives one explicit finite-dimensional
projection function, one value array, the actual initial distance, all
original interpolation pairs, and a two-increment Gram decomposition.
All endpoint heights after the first can be strictly positive.

The quantitative conclusion is a failure of a precisely specified rule:
after a coordinate vanishes from the gradient, its proximal coordinate is
set to zero. Uniformly over every dyadic cutoff and every legal amplitude
choice in this rule, on the actual H_m with m>=128,

    V_can < (275/648)/M_m < 1/(2M_m) < 1/(2M_m-1).

Here V_can is the normalized last objective. For the exact benchmark,

    limsup_(m->infinity) B_(2^m-1) sup V_can
        <= 275/[162 exp(1)] < 1.

This does not bound the unrestricted W_n(H_m), all positive-height
reflection/extinction histories, or the full two-increment cone.
Section 8 identifies the missing signed residual and an exact necessary
propagation condition. Section 9 refutes a forward-only repair on the
actual H_m with an explicit positive residual in an original pair.

## 1. The physical schedule and the generating rule

Fix m>=128. Throughout this whole horizon, put

    sigma=1+sqrt(2), c_rul=sigma(1+1/m),
    a_rul=(1-1/m)/c_rul,
    H_0=(), H_(j+1)=(H_j,1+a_rul c_rul^j,H_j),
    M_j=1+sum H_j, n=2^m-1.

The parameters are not recomputed inside the recursion. Choose an integer
0<=ell<m, and put q=m-ell, k=2^q-1, C=M_ell. Then the exact word is

    H_m=(H_ell,1+r_0,H_ell,...,1+r_(k-1),H_ell),
    r_j=a_rul c_rul^(ell+nu_2(j+1)), 0<=j<k.             (1)

The integer nu_2(v) is the largest d for which 2^d divides v. Each of
the k+1 ordinary cells has mass C-1. Its ending query is the checkpoint
query, except in the last cell, where it is the original terminal sample.
In particular the physical final step is never changed.

In R^k with its orthonormal coordinate vectors e_0,...,e_(k-1), choose
positive amplitudes Aamp_j,Bamp_j for 0<=j<k, and define

    p_0=sum_(l=0)^(k-1) Aamp_l e_l,
    p_j=-Bamp_(j-1)e_(j-1)+sum_(l=j)^(k-1)Aamp_l e_l,
                                                    1<=j<=k.       (2)

Thus coordinate l is initially Aamp_l, becomes -Bamp_l in cell l+1,
and then vanishes. There is no persistent extra coordinate in this rule.
Set

    P_can=conv{p_0,...,p_k},
    F_can(x)=max_(v in P_can)(<x,v>-||v||^2/2),
    X_0=C p_0.                                                     (3)

The family being screened consists of actual trajectories of this ONE
function starting from this X_0 and having the gradients in (2) on their
whole respective cells. The initial endpoint has proximal point zero.

Let Z_j be the endpoint of cell j and Y_j=Z_j-p_j. Actual GD with these
cell gradients requires

    Y_0=0,  Y_(j+1)=Y_j-r_j p_j-Cp_(j+1).                         (4)

A query inside cell j, with remaining ordinary mass u in [0,C-1], has

    X_(j,u)=Y_j+(1+u)p_j.                                         (5)

Introduce one freely chosen parameter r_k>=0. It is a terminal shape
parameter, NOT an additional physical step. Define

    wpre_0=r_0,  wpre_j=C+r_j (1<=j<=k),
    Rpre_j=sum_(v=0)^(j-1)wpre_v (1<=j<=k+1),
    Lpre_j=Rpre_j+C.

The extra hypothesis of the cancellation rule is

    Bamp_(j-1)=Rpre_j Aamp_(j-1)/(C+r_j), 1<=j<=k.                 (6)

For j<k this says exactly that coordinate j-1 of the proximal point
becomes zero after that coordinate leaves the gradient. For j=k,
(6) just parameterizes the terminal amplitude with nonnegative terminal
height. Indeed that height is
Rpre_k Aamp_(k-1)Bamp_(k-1)-C Bamp_(k-1)^2.
Every positive terminal amplitude with nonnegative height has a unique
r_k>=0 in (6).

Directly expanding (4) coordinate by coordinate gives

    Y_j=-r_j Bamp_(j-1)e_(j-1)
               -Lpre_j sum_(l=j)^(k-1)Aamp_l e_l, 1<=j<=k.        (7)

For example, an older coordinate l<j-1 has coefficient
-Rpre_(l+1)Aamp_l+(C+r_(l+1))Bamp_l=0 by (6). The current coordinate
is C Bamp_(j-1)-Rpre_j Aamp_(j-1)=-r_j Bamp_(j-1).
An untouched coordinate l>=j accumulates the coefficient -Lpre_j.

Put

    Ecan_j=sum_(l=j)^(k-1)Aamp_l^2,  Ecan_k=0,
    Ncan_0=Ecan_0, Ncan_j=Ecan_j+Bamp_(j-1)^2 (j>=1),
    Kcan_j=Ecan_j-Aamp_(j-1)Bamp_(j-1) (j>=1).

Coordinate multiplication gives

    ||p_j||^2=Ncan_j,
    <p_i,p_j>=Kcan_j whenever i<j.                                (8)

These are correlated gradients, generally with negative cross products.
Their full initial norm is C^2 Ecan_0. Also, independently of r_k,

    M_m=Rpre_k+2C.                                                (9)

Both sides equal (k+1)C+sum_(j<k)r_j by counting the physical cells.

## 2. One explicit amplitude law and one smooth convex function

Choose any r_k>0, for example r_k=C. All Rpre_j,Lpre_j are now explicit
finite real numbers. Set Aamp_0=1. Use (6) to determine Bamp_0. For
j=1,...,k-1 recursively set

    Aamp_j^2 = r_j Bamp_(j-1)^2 /
                 [Lpre_j(1+Rpre_(j+1)/(C+r_(j+1)))],
    Aamp_j = positive square root of the preceding expression,
    Bamp_j = Rpre_(j+1) Aamp_j/(C+r_(j+1)).                       (10)

Every amplitude is positive. These are finite formulas; no feasibility
problem or optimization is hidden in the definition. Their key identity is

    Lpre_j Aamp_j(Aamp_j+Bamp_j)=r_j Bamp_(j-1)^2,
                                                       1<=j<k.  (11)

We first justify the function in (3). Projection onto the compact convex
set P_can has a unique minimizer of squared distance. Completing the
square in (3) makes this projection its unique maximizing v. A vector p
in P_can is the projection of x precisely when

    <x-p,v-p><=0 for every v in P_can.                            (12)

Necessity follows by considering the segment from p to v. Sufficiency
follows by expanding ||x-v||^2. Applying (12) at x and x' and adding gives
||p-p'||^2<=<p-p',x-x'>, hence the projection is 1-Lipschitz. Comparing
the two maxima in (3) gives

    0<=F_can(x')-F_can(x)-<p,x'-x>
        <=<p'-p,x'-x><=||x'-x||^2.

Thus F_can is everywhere finite, convex, differentiable, and 1-smooth,
with gradient the projection onto P_can.

The origin is in the interior of P_can. To exhibit its barycentric
coefficients, choose alpha_0>0 and recursively set

    alpha_(l+1)=(Aamp_l/Bamp_l)sum_(i=0)^l alpha_i,

then choose alpha_0 so the total sum is 1. Every coefficient is positive,
and the l-th coordinate of sum alpha_i p_i is zero. The vectors
p_1,...,p_k are linearly independent: their coordinate matrix is triangular
with nonzero diagonal -Bamp_0,...,-Bamp_(k-1). The same coordinate
recursion makes the space of linear relations among all k+1 vertices
one-dimensional. Its positive generating relation has nonzero sum, so
there is no affine dependence. Consequently P_can is a full-dimensional
simplex and the displayed positive convex combination is interior.

Since 0 is an interior point, the only vector x satisfying
<x,v><=0 for all v in P_can is 0. By (12) this is precisely the set
of x with projection zero. Therefore F_can has UNIQUE minimizer 0,
F_can(0)=0, and its true initial squared distance is C^2 Ecan_0.

## 3. All original interpolation pairs, with shared positive heights

Define delta_can_j=<Y_j,p_j>. For j>=1, (7) gives

    delta_can_j=r_j Bamp_(j-1)^2-Lpre_j Ecan_j.                  (13)

For every s<j, coordinate multiplication gives

    <Y_j,p_s>=-r_j Aamp_(j-1)Bamp_(j-1)-Lpre_j Ecan_j
                 <=delta_can_j.                               (14)

For every s>j the corresponding value is

    <Y_j,p_s>=-Lpre_j Kcan_s.                                   (15)

To control ALL future vertices at once, use (6) and (11) to compute

    Kcan_(j+1)-Kcan_j
       =Aamp_(j-1)Bamp_(j-1)-Aamp_j(Aamp_j+Bamp_j)
       =C Bamp_(j-1)(Aamp_(j-1)+Bamp_(j-1))/Lpre_j >0.           (16)

Moreover Kcan_k=-Aamp_(k-1)Bamp_(k-1)<0, so every Kcan_j is negative.
Equation (11) also gives, for 1<=j<k,

    delta_can_j=-Lpre_j Kcan_(j+1)>0.                           (17)

Thus (15) is at most delta_can_j for every s>j, by (16).
The terminal height is delta_can_k=r_k Bamp_(k-1)^2>0. At j=0,
Y_0=0 satisfies every normal comparison with equality. Equations
(14)--(17) establish (12) at every endpoint, including against vertex 0,
which belongs to the convex hull. They retain both orientations and every
older and later vertex.

The same argument covers all original samples inside each ordinary cell.
For any i,j, <p_j,p_i-p_j><=0. For i<j, this follows from
Kcan_j-Ncan_j=-Bamp_(j-1)(Aamp_(j-1)+Bamp_(j-1))<0.
For i>j, Kcan_i<=Ecan_i<=Ecan_j<=Ncan_j. Add u times this inequality
to the endpoint normal, with u>=0. Thus (12) holds at every position (5),
and its actual gradient is p_j. Equations (4)--(5) now verify every
physical GD step on the original word.

There is ONE value array, namely

    f_(j,u)=delta_can_j+(u+1/2)Ncan_j.                          (18)

This is its actual value under F_can. For an explicit all-pair check,
the residual convention in accepted fact 1252b8823bb5a290 is

    Q_ab=f_a-f_b+<g_a,X_b-X_a>+||g_a-g_b||^2/2.

Substituting (5),(18) gives exactly

    Q_((i,u),(j,v))=<Y_j+v p_j,p_i-p_j><=0.                   (19)

The inequalities against the optimum jet (0,0,0) are

    Q_((j,u),star)=0,
    Q_(star,(j,u))=-delta_can_j-u Ncan_j<=0.                   (20)

No independent selection of values for different pairs has occurred.
The normalized terminal objective is exactly

    V_can=(1+2r_k)Bamp_(k-1)^2/(C^2 Ecan_0).                 (21)

Finally, this legal trajectory belongs to the requested increment cone
by an explicit decomposition. In coordinate l<k-1 the only gradient
changes are -(Aamp_l+Bamp_l) at the transition from cell l to l+1,
and Bamp_l at the transition from cell l+1 to l+2. Coordinate k-1
has only its first change before the terminal sample. The Gram of all
gradient increments is the sum, over l, of the outer products of these
two-supported lists. Each is a real PSD block. For the full Gram retain
also the initial-vector and initial-gradient entries C Aamp_l and Aamp_l
in the same scalar coordinate lists. No original initial row or gradient
has been removed; every weight in this finite decomposition is 1.

## 4. Necessary product loss for EVERY legal cancellation-rule member

The amplitudes need not follow (10) in this section. Suppose any positive
amplitudes satisfying (6) give an actual trajectory of (3). The endpoint
normal comparing current j to future vertex j+1 is necessary. Equations
(13),(15) show that it is exactly

    Lpre_j Aamp_j(Aamp_j+Bamp_j)<=r_j Bamp_(j-1)^2,
                                                   1<=j<k.      (22)

Define

    lambda_can_j=r_j Rpre_j/[(C+r_j)(C+Rpre_j)] in (0,1).

Substituting (6) into (22) gives

    Aamp_j^2/Aamp_(j-1)^2
       <= [wpre_(j+1)/wpre_j] lambda_can_j
                                  [Rpre_j/Rpre_(j+2)].          (23)

Indeed Rpre_(j+2)=Rpre_(j+1)+wpre_(j+1), and the right side
expands to r_j Rpre_j^2 wpre_(j+1) divided by
wpre_j^2 Lpre_j(Rpre_(j+1)+wpre_(j+1)). This is precisely the
coefficient obtained from (22).

The products in (23) telescope, giving

    Bamp_(k-1)^2/Aamp_0^2
      <= Rpre_k r_0 Rpre_2 /
             [wpre_k wpre_1(Rpre_k+wpre_k)]
                     * product_(j=1)^(k-1)lambda_can_j.         (24)

For k=1 the empty-product identity still holds, with
Rpre_2=Rpre_1+wpre_1. For k>=2, the quantities r_0,Rpre_2,wpre_1
are physical prefix data, independent of the terminal shape parameter.

Let d_can=2C-1>=1. Combining Ecan_0>=Aamp_0^2 with (21),(24) yields

    V_can <= Rpre_k r_0 Rpre_2/(C^2 wpre_1)
          * (2wpre_k-d_can)/[wpre_k(Rpre_k+wpre_k)]
          * product_(j=1)^(k-1)lambda_can_j
      < 2r_0 Rpre_2/(C^2 wpre_1)
          * product_(j=1)^(k-1)lambda_can_j,  k>=2.              (25)

The last strict inequality follows from d_can>0 and Rpre_k>0.
It is uniform in every legal terminal amplitude and in all amplitudes
before it. Positive endpoint heights have not been set to zero.

## 5. Uniform loss on the actual ruler, over every cutoff

The physical mass recursion is M_(j+1)=2M_j+a_rul c_rul^j. Hence

    A_mass=a_rul/(c_rul-2),
    M_j=A_mass c_rul^j+(1-A_mass)2^j.                           (26)

For m>=128,

    sigma<c_rul<5/2,  3/8<a_rul<sigma-2<c_rul-2<1/2,
    3/4<A_mass<1,
    (3/4)c_rul^j<M_j<=c_rul^j.                                (27)

For explicit rational checks, sigma<17/7 implies
c_rul<2193/896<5/2; a_rul>(127/128)/(5/2)=127/320>3/8;
and a_rul<1/sigma=sigma-2. These prove every inequality used in (27).
Set t_can=a_rul c_rul^ell/C. Then

    0<t_can<c_rul-2<1/2,  M_m/C<(4/3)c_rul^q.                 (28)

For q>=2, the first three checkpoint excesses are
r_0=t_can C, r_1=c_rul t_can C, r_2=t_can C. Therefore

    2r_0 Rpre_2/(C^2 wpre_1)
       =(2t_can/C)[1+t_can/(1+c_rul t_can)]<3/(2C),
    lambda_can_1
       =[c_rul t_can/(1+c_rul t_can)]
           [t_can/(1+t_can)]<5/27.                            (29)

There are exactly 2^(q-1)-1 lowest-level checkpoints among indices
1,...,k-1. Each has lambda_can_j<1/3, because r_j=t_can C.
Index 1 is the first high checkpoint and is not among them. All other
factors are less than 1. Equations (25),(28),(29) give, for q>=3,

    M_m V_can
      < (10/27)(5/2)^q 3^(-(2^(q-1)-1))
      <=625/2916.                                             (30)

At q=3 the expression is 625/2916. Its ratio at q+1 to its value at q
is (5/2)3^(-2^(q-1))<1, proving the last inequality for every q>=3.

When q=2, k=3 and

    M_m/C=4+(2+c_rul)t_can<25/4,
    Rpre_2/C=1+(1+c_rul)t_can<11/4,
    lambda_can_2< (1/3)(11/15)=11/45.

The same product bound now gives the sharper estimate

    M_m V_can < (25/4)(3/2)(5/27)(11/45)=275/648.             (31)

When q=1, there is just one checkpoint. Writing A=Aamp_0,B=Bamp_0,
the terminal numerator before normalization is

    2r_0 AB-(2C-1)B^2 <= r_0^2 A^2/(2C-1).

Since M_m=(2+t_can)C and C=M_(m-1)>=2, this gives

    M_m V_can <=(2+t_can)t_can^2 C/(2C-1)<5/12.              (32)

As 5/12<275/648 and 625/2916<275/648, (30)--(32) prove

    sup_(all cutoffs and legal cancellation amplitudes)
          M_m V_can < gamma_can:=275/648<1/2.                (33)

The no-checkpoint, constant-gradient extension with terminal proximal
point zero has payoff 1/M_m^2 and also obeys this bound for m>=128.
It can be realized by the projection function for the segment [0,p],
starting at M_m p; zero is a closest minimizer. This degenerate extension
is not needed for the simplex construction in Sections 1--3.

In particular (33) is below the legal Huber witness 1/(2M_m-1), whose
existence is part of accepted fact b98bb807d514e80d. The new construction
therefore supplies no gain even over that baseline.

## 6. Comparison with the actual exact benchmark

For completeness, the required sharper scalar bound follows directly
from the EXACT recursions in accepted fact b98bb807d514e80d. Let
rho=log_2(sigma)>1. Their primitive shifted mass C_n=S_circle(n)+1
has join

    Omega(A,B)=[A+B+sqrt((A+B)^2+4AB)]/2,

and their dominant denominator B_n has join

    G(A,D)=2A+D/2+sqrt(D^2+8AD)/2.

We prove

    C_n<=(n+1)^rho, B_n<=1+4n^rho.                            (34)

For t>0 set J(t)=(1+t)^rho-1. Differentiating with t=exp(z) gives

    d^2/dz^2 log J(exp(z))
      =rho t(1+t)^(rho-2)
            [(1+t)^rho-1-rho t]/J(t)^2 >0.

The bracket is positive because it vanishes at 0 and its derivative
rho[(1+t)^(rho-1)-1] is positive. Convexity at z and -z implies
J(t)J(1/t)>=J(1)^2=(sigma-1)^2=2. For A=t^rho B and
D=(1+t)^rho B, this says (D-A)(D-B)>=2AB, so
D^2-(A+B)D-AB>=0. The positive root of that polynomial is Omega(A,B).
Thus

    Omega(A,B)<=(A^(1/rho)+B^(1/rho))^rho,

including zero arguments by continuity. Induction in the exact primitive
recursion proves the first bound in (34). Also G(A,D)<=4A+D, since
sqrt(D^2+8AD)<=D+4A. Induct in the dominant recursion and use
(k+1)^rho+(n-k-1)^rho<=n^rho to prove its second bound. The last
power inequality follows by integrating the increasing derivative of
x^rho; it includes a zero summand.

Equation (26) gives, with e=exp(1),

    M_m/sigma^m
      =(1+1/m)^m[A_mass+(1-A_mass)(2/c_rul)^m] -> e.           (35)

Indeed a_rul tends to 1/sigma=sigma-2, so A_mass tends to 1,
and 2/c_rul is bounded above by 2/sigma<1. Consequently (33)--(35)
prove the finite and asymptotic exact-profile bounds

    B_n V_can < gamma_can [1+4(2^m-1)^rho]/M_m,
    limsup_(m->infinity) B_(2^m-1) sup V_can
       <=4 gamma_can/e=275/(162e)<1.                          (36)

The supremum includes all legal amplitudes and all cutoffs. If q=m-ell
tends to infinity, the right side of (30) tends to zero, so the exact-B
ratio tends to zero as well. No fitted dyadic constant or assumed
asymptotic phase for B_n was used.

## 7. What has, and has not, been excluded

The constructor (10) proves nonemptiness and simultaneous attainability
of every original pair, including optimum and remote pairs. The bound
(33) applies more broadly to every legal projection trajectory satisfying
the stated cancellation rule, even when (22) is strict. It does not assume
an acute cone: in the explicit constructor every distinct-gradient cross
inner product is strictly negative by (16).

The assumptions of the obstruction include (2), the fixed initial point
X_0=Cp_0 (equivalently Y_0=0), the one projection function (3), and the
extinguished-coordinate cancellation in (6). Positive endpoint heights
alone do not evade it. General joint value arrays, different initial
proximal points, a persistent final coordinate, different two-change
histories, and nonzero extinct-coordinate residuals are outside its scope.
No unrestricted upper certificate and no common-array A2 separator is
claimed.

## 8. Exact residual equations for a changed rule

Drop cancellation but retain (2)--(5), positive amplitudes, and the same
projection function. For 1<=j<=k define its effective endpoint parameter

    tau_eff_j=Rpre_j Aamp_(j-1)/Bamp_(j-1)-C.                 (37)

Here Rpre_j uses ONLY physical r_0,...,r_(j-1). If the data are legal,
the optimum normal requires tau_eff_j>=0; it is strictly positive for
j<k, since Ecan_j>0. The frozen proximal coordinate l<k-1 is

    z_ext_l=(r_(l+1)-tau_eff_(l+1))Bamp_l.                    (38)

The exact endpoint coordinates are now z_ext_l for l<j-1,
-tau_eff_j Bamp_(j-1) at l=j-1, and -Lpre_j Aamp_l for l>=j.
Its height is

    delta_j=tau_eff_j Bamp_(j-1)^2-Lpre_j Ecan_j.

For future vertices s>j the normal inequalities are exactly

    Lpre_j(Ecan_j-Kcan_s)<=tau_eff_j Bamp_(j-1)^2.            (39)

For every old vertex 0<=s<j they are exactly

    -1_(s>=1) Bamp_(s-1)z_ext_(s-1)
      +sum_(l=s)^(j-2)Aamp_l z_ext_l
      <=tau_eff_j Bamp_(j-1)(Aamp_(j-1)+Bamp_(j-1)).         (40)

These formulas follow by multiplying the displayed endpoint coordinates
by p_s-p_j. An empty sum is zero. They retain all old residuals, including
their signs. Cancellation is exactly z_ext_l=0.

In particular the next-future inequality and the next-old inequality
give a necessary propagation condition. For 1<=s<k, they read

    Lpre_s Aamp_s(Aamp_s+Bamp_s)
                <=tau_eff_s Bamp_(s-1)^2,
    (tau_eff_s-r_s)Bamp_(s-1)^2
                <=tau_eff_(s+1)Bamp_s(Aamp_s+Bamp_s).

Substitute Bamp_s/Aamp_s=Rpre_(s+1)/(C+tau_eff_(s+1)) and
Rpre_(s+1)=Lpre_s+r_s. If tau_eff_s>r_s, cancel the positive
Bamp_(s-1)^2 and rearrange; if tau_eff_s<=r_s, use tau_eff_(s+1)>=0.
The resulting necessary condition in both cases is

    tau_eff_(s+1)
       >= C Lpre_s(tau_eff_s-r_s)_+
                       /[r_s(Lpre_s+tau_eff_s)].               (41)

Thus a negative extinct residual can increase the future budget in (39),
but the same residual imposes a positive cost in the next old comparison.
Equation (41) is only necessary; it does not replace the remote sums in
(40) or establish a competitive construction.

## 9. An actual-ruler countercheck against forward-only repair

For every actual H_m above with q>=2, the following exact relaxed data
pass ALL future endpoint normal comparisons and ALL optimum comparisons,
with strictly positive later endpoint heights, yet fail an old pair.
They are a counterexample to sufficiency of that one-sided test, NOT a
legal lower-bound witness.

Use the same physical Rpre_j,Lpre_j, and choose

    tau_eff_1=2r_1, tau_eff_2=r_2/100,
    tau_eff_j=r_j for 3<=j<k, and tau_eff_k=C.                 (42)

For k=3 the last assignment is just tau_eff_3=C. Set Aamp_0=1,
Bamp_0=Rpre_1/(C+tau_eff_1), and, for 1<=j<k, set

    Aamp_j^2=tau_eff_j Bamp_(j-1)^2 /
                [Lpre_j(1+Rpre_(j+1)/(C+tau_eff_(j+1)))],
    Bamp_j=Rpre_(j+1)Aamp_j/(C+tau_eff_(j+1)).                 (43)

All amplitudes are positive. The same algebra as in (16), now using
(37),(43), shows that Kcan_(j+1)-Kcan_j is strictly positive and
delta_j=-Lpre_j Kcan_(j+1)>0 for j<k. Since Kcan_k<0 and
delta_k=tau_eff_k Bamp_(k-1)^2>0, every future normal in (39) and
every optimum comparison holds. Adding ordinary remaining mass preserves
these inequalities because <p_j,p_s-p_j><=0, as proved in Section 3.

But the endpoint-2 normal against vertex 1, equivalently the original
residual Q_((1,0),(2,0)), equals

    (tau_eff_1-r_1)Bamp_0^2
       -tau_eff_2 Bamp_1(Aamp_1+Bamp_1)
      =r_1 Bamp_0^2
         [1-2tau_eff_2 Rpre_2/((C+tau_eff_2)Lpre_1)].           (44)

Here r_0=t_can C, r_1=c_rul t_can C, r_2=t_can C. Thus

    Rpre_2/Lpre_1=1+c_rul t_can/(1+t_can)<11/6,
