# All relative phases of two scalar secant bands

Status: complete candidate proof, NOT verifier-accepted. No verifier request
is allocated by the current TASK or master guidance. Every new estimate used
in this document is proved here. Earlier C18/C19 findings are motivation,
not premises. The only accepted fact cited is b98bb807d514e80d for the exact
benchmark definitions. No external theorem or numerical search is used.

## 1. Statement

Set c_sc=13/5 and a_sc=c_sc^(-17). Define

    Hsc_0=(), Hsc_(j+1)=(Hsc_j,bsc_j,Hsc_j),
    bsc_j=a_sc c_sc^j, Nsc_j=2^j-1,
    ssc_j=sum Hsc_j=a_sc(c_sc^j-2^j)/(c_sc-2).

Let f be a fixed differentiable convex function on the real line with
1-Lipschitz derivative g, f(0)=0=min f. On the two rays choose arbitrary
constants M_slow,M_fast with 0<M_slow<=M_fast<=1. Either physical ray can
be called slow. Suppose each nonzero queried point on ray sigma satisfies

    M_sigma/c_sc <= |g(x_t)|/|x_t| <= M_sigma.             (1)

The constants, derivative, and initial point can depend on the horizon m.
No relation between the two scales is imposed. The derivative may be
nonperiodic and asymmetric, with any number of knots or repeated visits.
Only the finitely many queried secants are constrained by (1).

Put K_sc=192*27^4 and C_sc=200000*c_sc^3*K_sc^2/a_sc. Then GD under the
complete word Hsc_m, starting from any x_0!=0, satisfies

    2 f(x_(Nsc_m))/x_0^2 <= C_sc c_sc^(-m).              (2)

In particular the supremum over this entire class, divided by the exact
U_(Nsc_m)^ZJ, tends to zero. Each ray's secants must fit in its own band of
width factor13/5, but the two bands can have arbitrary relative scale and
phase. This closes the phase restriction in C19. It is still a restricted
scalar class, not all scalar functions and not unrestricted W.

Within the proof abbreviate c_sc,a_sc,bsc_j,ssc_j by c,a,b_j,S_j. These
are local aliases, unrelated to the Huber slopes c,a in the project glossary.
All matrix products below use maximum and multiplication, not addition.

## 2. Exact two-state upper envelope

For ray sigma put

    d_sigma(j)=(1-b_j M_sigma/c)_+,
    u_sigma(j)=(b_j M_sigma-1)_+,
    B_j=[[d_slow(j),u_fast(j)],[u_slow(j),d_fast(j)]].

Rows denote the output ray, columns the input ray. A scalar step with
queried secant lambda on ray sigma has multiplier1-b_j lambda. Its
same-ray magnitude is at most d_sigma(j), and its opposite-ray magnitude
at most u_sigma(j). A zero coefficient excludes that nonzero transition.

Define (A odot B)_(i,j)=max_k A_(i,k)B_(k,j), and let E_0 be the identity
matrix. The exact recurrence for the relaxed sign-path envelope is

    E_(j+1)=E_j odot B_j odot E_j.                       (3)

Induction by the two intermediate signs in the word decomposition shows
that E_j(output,input) bounds every actual radius ratio. This proves an
upper bound even though independently maximizing its entries need not
produce one legal function. No such realization is asserted.

Let I be the first integer with b_I M_fast>1. Write

    t=b_I M_fast, s=b_I M_slow.

Then I>=18, 1<t<=c, and 0<s<=t. Every checkpoint before I has upper
secant product at most1, so E_I is diagonal. Its entries are

    p_s=p(s), p_f=p(t),
    p(z)=product_(h in Hsc_I)(1-h z/(c b_I)).             (4)

All these factors are positive. With eta=13/8, the sum formula gives
S_I/b_I>=eta: (2/c)^3<1/2 and (2/c)^2<4/5 imply (2/c)^17<1/40.
Consequently

    p(t)/p(s) <= exp[-5(t-s)/8].                         (5)

This follows termwise from log(1-u)-log(1-v)<=-(u-v) for 0<=v<=u<1.
We shall also use, for 0<z<=c,

    p(z) <= exp[-5z/8-z^2/70],  p(z)>1/27.             (6)

For the first inequality, log(1-v)<=-v-v^2/2 and the level counts give

    sum_(h in Hsc_I) h^2/b_I^2
       =[1-(2/c^2)^I]/(c^2-2).

Since 2/c^2<1/3 and I>=18, the coefficient of z^2 in the logarithm is
larger than (26/27)/(2c^2(c^2-2))>1/70. For the lower bound, every
v=h z/(c b_I) is at most1/c, and their sum is at most5/3. Integration
gives log(1-v)>=-v/(1-1/c), so log p(z)>=-65/24>-3. The elementary
factorial-series bound e<3 gives p(z)>1/27.

## 3. A common-band product estimate proved from scratch

For M in (0,1] set

    q_sec(z)=max(1-z/c,z-1),
    P_sec(m,M)=product_(j=0)^(m-1)
                     q_sec(a M c^j)^(2^(m-j-1)).

The empty product is1. We prove

    M P_sec(m,M)^2 <=(60000/a)c^(-m).                    (7)

Let k be the largest integer with y=a M c^k<=13/9. Then k>=17 and
5/9<y<=13/9. The first branch of q_sec holds through k; its second
branch holds afterwards. For m>=k+1, log(1-v)<=-v on levels below k
gives

    log P_sec(m,M)/2^(m-k-1)
      <=-5y/4+log(1-y/c)
          +sum_(ell=1)^(m-k-1)2^(-ell)log(c^ell y-1).    (8)

For m=k+1 this is at most-25/36. Otherwise keep ell=1 exactly, extend
the positive terms ell>=2 to infinity, and replace their logarithms by
ell log c+log y. The result is

    E(y)=-5y/4+(1/2)log[c y(c-y)^2(cy-1)].               (9)

On the indicated interval E''(y)<-1/2. Indeed its three negative terms
are -1/(c-y)^2, -1/(2y^2), and -c^2/(2(cy-1)^2); the last two alone
have total magnitude greater than2/9+c^2/18>1/2. At y=4/5,
E'=5/216 and c y(c-y)^2(cy-1)=113724/15625<(27/10)^2. Since
e>163/60, E(4/5)<log(162/163)<=-1/163. The tangent parabola therefore
gives E(y)<=-1/163+25/46656<-1/200. Thus

    P_sec(m,M)<=exp[-2^(m-k-1)/200]  for m>=k+1.         (10)

For m<=k every factor is at most1 and M<=(13/(9a))c^(-m). For
m=k+J, J>=1, equation(10) bounds M P_sec^2 by
(13/(9a))c^(-m)c^J exp(-2^J/200). As c<4 and z^2 exp(-z)<=1 for
z>=0, this is at most (520000/(9a))c^(-m), proving(7).

## 4. Separated activation: s<=1/c

This case is included to keep the statement independent of unverified C19.
At level I write

    A=p_s^2(1-s/c), D=p_s p_f(t-1), F=p_f^2(1-t/c).

Then E_(I+1)=[[A,D],[0,F]]. At I+1 a fast input must cross and a slow
input cannot cross. Hence

    E_(I+2)=[[A^2(1-s), A max(D(1-s),F(ct-1))],[0,0]].

Its right/left nonzero-entry ratio is at most max(R_1,R_2), where

    R_1=(t-1)/(1-s/c) *exp[-5(t-s)/8],
    R_2=(1-t/c)(ct-1)/[(1-s/c)(1-s)] *exp[-5(t-s)/4].

Both increase with s. R_1 increases with t up to c; R_2 decreases with
t from1, since its logarithmic derivative is
-1/(c-t)+c/(ct-1)-5/4, is decreasing, and equals-1/4 at t=1.
Thus R_1<=(169/90)exp(-18/13)<1 and
R_2<=(169/90)exp(-10/13)<1. The latter inequalities follow respectively
from exp(18/13)>31/13>169/90 and
exp(10/13)>349/169>169/90.

If E_j=[[A_j,R A_j],[0,0]] with R<=1, recurrence(3) preserves R and

    A_(j+1)=A_j^2 max(d_slow(j),R u_slow(j))
           <=A_j^2 q_sec(b_j M_slow).

Its initial A_(I+2) is P_sec(I+2,M_slow). Every later nonzero endpoint
is therefore on the slow ray, with radius at most P_sec(m,M_slow)|x_0|.
Equation(7) supplies the required bound in this case.

## 5. Overlapping activation: one off-diagonal entry controls all levels

Now assume 1/c<s<=t<=c. At level I use all four entries

    E_(I+1)=[[A,D],[C,F]],
    A=p_s^2(1-s/c), D=p_s p_f(t-1),
    C=p_s p_f(s-1)_+, F=p_f^2(1-t/c).

At I+1 put d=(1-s)_+, u_s=cs-1>0, u_f=ct-1>0. Thus
B_(I+1)=[[d,u_f],[u_s,0]]. The two relations

    C d=0,  C u_f<=D u_s                              (11)

hold: for s<=1 the first entry C vanishes, while for s>1 the second
inequality reduces to (t-1)/(s-1)>=(ct-1)/(cs-1).

Set L=max(A d,D u_s). Direct multiplication in(3) gives new entries

    A_0=A L,                F_0=F D u_s,
    D_0=max(A D d,D^2 u_s,A F u_f),
    C_0=max(A F u_s,C^2 u_f),
    E_(I+2)=[[A_0,D_0],[C_0,F_0]].                     (12)

These entries satisfy, without any division by a potentially zero entry,

    A_0 F_0<=C_0 D_0,  C_0 u_f<=D_0 u_s.             (13)

For the first inequality use D_0>=D L and C_0>=A F u_s. For the
second, the two possibilities in C_0 are bounded by D_0 u_s/u_f:
D_0>=A F u_f treats the first, and D_0>=D^2 u_s together with(11)
treats the second.

For every j>=I+2 both rays must cross at checkpoint j, even at the
smallest permitted secants. Write u_s(j)=b_j M_slow-1 and
u_f(j)=b_j M_fast-1. Their ratio u_f(j)/u_s(j) decreases with j.
Suppose E_j=[[A_j,D_j],[C_j,F_j]] satisfies

    A_j F_j<=C_j D_j, C_j u_f(j)<=D_j u_s(j), D_j>0.

Multiplication by the pure crossing B_j yields exactly

    D_(j+1)=D_j^2 u_s(j),
    A_(j+1)=A_j D_j u_s(j),
    F_(j+1)=F_j D_j u_s(j),
    C_(j+1)=max(A_j F_j u_s(j),C_j^2 u_f(j)).           (14)

The first product inequality persists because the term A_j F_j u_s(j)
occurs in C_(j+1). The ratios A_j/D_j and F_j/D_j are constant, while
C_(j+1)/D_(j+1)<=C_j/D_j. Together with decreasing u_f/u_s, this proves
the second product inequality at the next level. Equation(13) starts
the induction. The full two-state envelope is therefore controlled by
the scalar recurrence for D_j in(14).

For clarity its starting value in(12) is the maximum of three terms:

    T_1=p_s^2 p_f^2 (t-1)^2(cs-1),
    T_2=p_s^3 p_f (1-s/c)(t-1)(1-s)_+,
    T_3=p_s^2 p_f^2 (1-s/c)(1-t/c)(ct-1).              (15)

The positivity D_0>0 and a uniform ratio bound follow as follows. Every
entry in(12) is less than16. If t<=2, T_3>=(1/27^4)(72/845)>
1/(12*27^4). If t>=2 and s<=3/4, T_2>=(1/27^4)(37/208) exceeds that
same bound. If t>=2 and s>=3/4, T_1>=(1/27^4)(19/20) does so. Thus

    max(A_0,D_0,C_0,F_0)/D_0 <= K_sc=192*27^4.         (16)

By(14) this ratio bound holds at every later level.

## 6. The off-diagonal recurrence decays uniformly

We prove, writing J=m-I-2>=0,

    D_m <= exp[-2^J/400].                             (17)

Here D_(I+2)=D_0 in the notation of(12). To avoid ambiguity, recurrence(14)
propagates each of the three starting terms in(15) independently; their
maximum is the propagated D_0 because positive powers commute with max.

The future factors are c^ell s-1 with ell=2,3,..., and are all greater
than1. For any starting term T, their complete infinite logarithmic
budget, divided by 2^J, is bounded above by

    log T + log(c^2)+(1/2)log[s(c^2 s-1)].              (18)

To derive(18), index future factors by k=1,2,..., so they equal
c^(k+1)s-1. Keep the first correction log(1-1/(c^2 s)) exactly with
weight1/2 and drop the later negative corrections. The sums of 2^(-k)
and k2^(-k) are1 and2, giving(18). Extending a finite tail is valid
because all added original logarithms are positive.

### Case 1/c<s<=1

In this range p_s contains exactly the low factors of P_sec(I+2,M_slow),
whose base value is

    p_s^4 (1-s/c)^2 q_sec(cs).

From(5),

    p_f(t-1)/[p_s(1-s/c)] <= c/e<1.                    (19)

Indeed the logarithmic expression increases with s and t on s<=1,
t<=c and equals c/e at s=1,t=c. Thus T_1 and T_2 are at most this
base value. At all future levels q_sec(c^ell s)=c^ell s-1. The index k
in(10) is either I or I+1, so their propagated values are at most
exp[-2^J/400].

For T_3 use p_s^2 p_f^2<=exp[-5(s+t)/4]. For fixed s the logarithm
of exp(-5t/4)(1-t/c)(ct-1) decreases for t>=1. Replacing t by1 in
(18) therefore gives the upper bound

    F_low(s)=-5(s+1)/4+log[(c-s)(c-1)^2]
                         +(1/2)log[s(c^2 s-1)].        (20)

On [1/c,1], F_low''<-1: the last two negative curvature terms alone
have magnitudes greater than1/2+1/2. At s=2/3,

    F_low'(2/3)=-407/7627,
    (c-s)^2(c-1)^4 s(c^2s-1)
       <(163/60)^4(13/12)<exp(49/12).

The last upper comparison uses e>163/60 and exp(1/12)>13/12. Thus
F_low(2/3)<-1/24. Since (407/7627)^2/2<1/600, its tangent parabola
proves F_low(s)<-1/25 throughout the interval. This is stronger than
the exponent required by(17).

### Case 1<=s<=t<=c

T_2=0. First, the function p(t)^2(1-t/c)(ct-1) is decreasing for
t>=1. The derivative of 2log p is at most-5/4, and the remaining
logarithmic derivative is -1/(c-t)+c/(ct-1). Therefore

    T_3<=p_s^4(1-s/c)^2(cs-1)
         <=P_sec(I+2,M_slow).

The index k in(10) is I-1 or I, so the same propagation proves(17)
for this term.

For T_1 use the quadratic improvement in(6). Its bound in(18) is

    F_high(s,t)=-5(s+t)/4-(s^2+t^2)/35+2log(t-1)
                  +log(cs-1)+log(c^2)
                  +(1/2)log[s(c^2s-1)].               (21)

The part depending on t has second derivative at most-25/32. Its
derivative at t=c is-26/175. Completing the tangent parabola gives

    F_high(s,t)<=F_high(s,c)+1/60.                     (22)

The function s->F_high(s,c) has second derivative less than-2/5.
For example the four positive magnitudes have sum bounded below by

    2/35+c^2/(c^2-1)^2+1/(2c^2)
                              +c^4/(2(c^3-1)^2)>2/5.

At s=7/4 its derivative is -213097/10765020, with magnitude less
than1/40. The argument of its combined logarithm is

    (c-1)^2 c^2 (cs-1)*sqrt[s(c^2s-1)] <268<exp(28/5).

The first inequality follows on squaring rational quantities. The
second follows from
(163/60)^5[1+3/5+(3/5)^2/2+(3/5)^3/6]>268.
Its tangent-parabola correction is less than1/1000. Hence(22) is
strictly less than

    28/5-87/16-3929/14000+1/60+1/1000
       =-211/2100<-1/10.

This proves(17) for T_1 and completes all cases. Every displayed
rational comparison is a finite arithmetic inequality; none asserts
a numerical scan or an optimized trajectory.

## 7. Radius, complete objective area, and early horizons

In the overlapping case equations(16)--(17) give

    |x_(Nsc_m)|<=K_sc exp[-2^(m-I-2)/400]|x_0|,
                                                    m>=I+2. (23)

First suppose the bands hold everywhere on their rays. Then integration
from the minimizer gives f(x)<=M_sigma x^2/2. In(23) use M_sigma<=M_fast
and M_fast=t/(a c^I)<=c/(a c^I). With J=m-I-2, the normalized error is
at most

    (c^3 K_sc^2/a)c^(-m)c^J exp(-2^J/200)
       <=(40000 c^3 K_sc^2/a)c^(-m).                   (24)

For m<=I the signs cannot change, all envelope factors are at most1,
and M_fast<=(c/a)c^(-m). For m=I+1 every entry of E_(I+1) is at most
c-1, giving the bound c^2(c-1)^2 c^(-m)/a. These are smaller than(24).
The separated case was bounded in section4 by(7), including its own
two early ranges by the same estimates. Thus the global-band assertion
holds at every horizon.

For the stated queried-only hypotheses take m>=1. The final step of
Hsc_m is a<1/2. Smoothness and g(0)=0 ensure it preserves any nonzero
ray. Write x_pre for the last query and x_end for the terminal point.
If a query or iterate already has zero gradient, the remaining objective
is zero. Otherwise monotonicity and(1) give, on its ray sigma,

    |g(x_end)|<=|g(x_pre)|<=M_sigma |x_pre|
                    <=M_sigma |x_end|/(1-a).

The entire area between0 and x_end obeys

    f(x_end)=integral_0^|x_end| |g(sigma r)| dr
                <=|x_end| |g(x_end)|.

Thus the preceding endpoint estimate with M_sigma x_end^2 is enlarged
by at most2/(1-a)<4. Formula(2), with the chosen C_sc, follows. This
retains all function-value area, even if the derivative is arbitrary
between or outside the queries. At m=0, 1-smoothness directly gives
2f(x_0)<=x_0^2, also smaller than(2).

## 8. Exact benchmark comparison and scope

The accepted fact b98bb807d514e80d defines the exact benchmark. Write
C_n=S_circle(n)+1 and B_n=2S_bullet(n)+1. Its shifted recursions are

    C_0=B_0=1,
    C_n=max_(0<=j<n) Omega(C_j,C_(n-j-1)),
    B_n=max_(0<=j<n) Gamma(C_j,B_(n-j-1)),
    Omega(A,B)=(A+B+sqrt((A+B)^2+4AB))/2,
    Gamma(A,B)=2A+B/2+sqrt(B^2+8AB)/2.

Here is an elementary stronger growth estimate, proved rather than
imported from an unverified finding. Homogeneity and the defining
quadratic of Omega show that
Omega(A,B)<=(A^(3/4)+B^(3/4))^(4/3) is equivalent to
t^(4/3)+(1-t)^(4/3)+[t(1-t)]^(4/3)<=1 on[0,1]. Put u=t^(1/3),
v=(1-t)^(1/3). The difference from1 equals

    u^3 v^3[1/(1+u+u^2)+1/(1+v+v^2)-uv]>=0,

because each reciprocal is at least1/3 and uv<=2^(-2/3)<2/3.
Induction gives C_n<=(n+1)^(4/3). Also Gamma(A,B)<=B+4A by squaring
the square-root comparison, and induction using
u^(4/3)+v^(4/3)<=(u+v)^(4/3) gives B_n<=1+4n^(4/3).

Consequently(2), divided by U_(Nsc_m)^ZJ=1/B_(Nsc_m), is at most

    C_sc c_sc^(-m)[1+4(2^m-1)^(4/3)] ->0,

since c_sc^3=2197/125>16. All constants are uniform over the function,
the two secant scales, their relative phase, and horizon-dependent knots.

The newly closed gap is arbitrary relative phase of two bounded secant
bands. The proof makes no claim when a single ray's queried secants
span more than13/5. The independent factor-four relaxation already
has divergent illegal trajectories; dropping (1) requires new fixed-profile
information, not merely rerunning the matrix recursion. No fact graph
node has been created by this document.
