## 1. Original orientation, objective, and formal coordinates

Let n>=0, h=(h_0,...,h_(n-1)) be a finite nonnegative word, s=sum h_i,
C=1+s, and I={0,...,n}. Keep every original gradient coordinate, including
the terminal coordinate and every coordinate at a zero step. Fixed
physical vectors x_i,g_i in a finite-dimensional Euclidean space obey

    x_(i+1)=x_i-h_i g_i.

For sampled values f_i put

    Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2,
    V(f)=2sum_(i<n)h_i(f_i-f_n)
          +sum_(i<n)h_i(1-h_i)||g_i||^2-sC||g_n||^2.       (1)

Accepted1252b8823bb5a290 proves Q_ij<=0 for every ordered pair of actual
smooth convex samples, and constructs a single interpolant from these
inequalities. Assume initially that all these finite inequalities hold.
An optimum sample will be retained explicitly when it is part of the
fixed geometry; Section3 also handles the case where its location is free.

For the original nonnegative multipliers Lambda_ij, put Lambda_ii=0 and

    L=diag(Lambda e)-Lambda,
    omega=(-2h_0,...,-2h_(n-1),2s)^T,
    L^T e=omega.                                         (2)

Here e is the (n+1)-entry all-ones column. Thus omega_i is outgoing minus
incoming flow. The TERMINAL is the source, of mass2s, and index i<n is
a sink of mass2h_i. Reversing these arrows gives the wrong value problem.

With T_ii=1, T_it=h_t for t<i, and T_it=0 for t>i, let

    D=diag(h_i(2-h_i) for i<n, -s(s+2)),
    M=D+(LT+T^T L^T)/2.

Accepted04219554e41d9750 gives, on every physical realization,

    V(f)+sum_(i!=j)Lambda_ij Q_ij=<M,Gram(g_0,...,g_n)>.   (3)

The full certificate requires M PSD on ALL of R^(n+1). Nothing below
deletes a coordinate, restricts Lambda support in that feasibility
question, or identifies formal gradients at zero steps. Sparse flows
constructed below solve an auxiliary transport minimum; they are NOT
claimed to have PSD matrices.

## 2. An exact finite price, with elementary proof and attained flow

Fix legal base values f_i and put w_ij=-Q_ij>=0 for distinct i,j. Regard
w_ij as the cost of the directed arc i->j. Let d_i be the least cost of
a path from n to i, with d_n=0. The complete finite graph makes every
distance finite. A minimizing path exists: a repeated-vertex segment
has nonnegative cost and can be deleted, so it is enough to consider
finitely many simple paths.

For replacement values f_i+z_i, the complete constraints are exactly

    z_i-z_j<=w_ij.                                      (4)

Summing along an n->i path gives z_n-z_i<=its cost. Therefore

    z_i-z_n>=-d_i,
    V(f+z)>=V(f)-Pflow,
    Pflow=2sum_(i<n)h_i d_i.                             (5)

All h_i are nonnegative, including possibly zero. The simultaneous
choice z_i=-d_i attains every lower bound in(5): the distance inequality
d_j<=d_i+w_ij is precisely its feasibility in(4). Thus this is an
attained minimum of the value problem, not only a lower bound from a
dual program.

The same number is the exact minimum slack cost of the ORIGINAL flow:

    min_{Lambda>=0, L^T e=omega} sum Lambda_ij w_ij
       =Pflow.                                         (6)

To prove the lower bound, multiply w_ij>=d_j-d_i by Lambda_ij and sum.
The result is

    sum Lambda_ij w_ij >= -sum_i omega_i d_i
                         =2sum_(i<n)h_i d_i.

For attainment, choose a shortest n->i path for every i<n with h_i>0
and send mass2h_i along that path. Sum these path flows. Intermediate
vertices have equal incoming and outgoing mass; the endpoints give
exactly(2). Each path contributes2h_i d_i. Zero-weight sinks need no
path, but their vertices and all their available arcs remain present.

Combining(3) with(6) gives a necessary condition for any full certificate:

    V(f)>=Pflow.                                        (7)

The selected optimal values give the same condition as V(f-d)>=0. Indeed
their new slacks are w'_ij=w_ij+d_i-d_j, and every prescribed-flow array
satisfies

    sum Lambda_ij w'_ij=sum Lambda_ij w_ij-Pflow.

The Gram matrix and the formal certificate matrix do not change with
the values. The full PSD restriction can impose more than the minimum
over flows in(6); it has not been dropped from the original problem.

### Zero costs, coincident gradients, and fixed optimum constraints

No strict positivity of costs is needed. A zero-cost directed cycle
has every arc of cost zero, and(4) then forces equal shifts around that
cycle. Distances and the path-flow construction remain valid without
choosing a unique shortest path.

If two base gradients coincide, Q_ij+Q_ji=0, so legality forces BOTH
residuals to vanish. Hence replacement shifts at these two samples are
equal, even when their positions differ. If two base positions coincide,
the sum of the two residuals is ||g_i-g_j||^2; legality first forces
coincident gradients and then coincident values. These are properties of
actual jets, not extra formal equations in the PSD certificate.

One may also include a fixed optimum jet x_star=g_star=f_star=0 as an
extra graph vertex. Let d_i^* be distances in this larger graph. Then
z_i=d_star^*-d_i^* is feasible, has z_star=0, and minimizes the same
value objective subject to ALL finite and optimum pairs. Its improvement
is2sum h_i d_i^*. In general d_i^*<=d_i, so this price need not equal
the original finite-flow price. They WILL agree in every concrete family
below. The distinction is important: optimum edges are not original
primitive multipliers and cannot silently be used as such.

## 3. The optimized finite values give one static function and real minima

This section also explains why(5), without a fixed optimum vertex, is
not merely a formal jet result. For any legal replacement values f'_i
on the finite samples, set

    y_i=x_i-g_i, beta_i=||g_i||^2/2-f'_i,
    H(v)=||v||^2/2+max_(i in I){<y_i,v>+beta_i},
    F(x)=sup_v{<x,v>-H(v)}.                              (8)

This is the construction in accepted1252b8823bb5a290, written out here
to identify its minimizers. H is finite, continuous, 1-strongly convex,
and has quadratic growth. The supremum has one maximizer v(x). Strong
subgradient inequalities give

    <x-x',v(x)-v(x')>>=||v(x)-v(x')||^2,

and the maximizing inequalities give

    0<=F(x')-F(x)-<v(x),x'-x>
      <=<v(x')-v(x),x'-x><=||x'-x||^2.

Thus F is everywhere finite, convex, differentiable and1-smooth, with
gradient v(x). The all-pair inequalities say that at v=g_i the i-th
quadratic in H is active and has gradient x_i. Consequently v(x_i)=g_i
and F(x_i)=f'_i. This is ONE fixed F used for all steps.

Let beta_max=max_i beta_i, and A={i:beta_i=beta_max}. The exact minimizer
set of F is

    argmin F=conv{y_i:i in A},   min F=-beta_max.          (9)

Indeed gradient F(x)=0 is equivalent to x being a subgradient of H at0.
The subgradients there are exactly the convex hull of the active affine
slopes y_i. To check the reverse inclusion without an unproved formula,
if x is outside that convex hull, a separating vector u has
<x,u> greater than every <y_i,u> for i in A. For sufficiently small
positive t, inactive affine pieces remain inactive at tu, and
H(tu)-H(0)=t max_(i in A)<y_i,u>+t^2||u||^2/2,
contradicting the subgradient inequality for x. The forward inclusion
follows by convexly combining the active supporting inequalities.

In particular a real minimizer always exists, even if none was fixed in
the graph. The true initial distance is exactly

    dist(x_0,conv{y_i:i in A}).                           (10)

No arbitrary radius or changed function is inserted into the GD run.
The specific families below either preserve the optimum0 or identify
the closest minimizer explicitly.

## 4. Entire unit-curvature repaired-cut family: price exactly zero

This is the main complete redundancy result. It works for any n>=1,
any nonnegative word, and any cut j with0<=j<n and h_j>1. Put

    P_j=sum_(t<j)h_t, T_j=sum_(t>j)h_t,
    eta=(h_j-1)/(1+T_j)>0,
    p_0=1, p_i=product_(t<i)(1-h_t) for0<=i<=j.

Choose an amplitude epsilon>=0. Define two scalar coordinate arrays:

    xB_i=1+sum_(t=i)^(j-1)h_t, gB_i=1        (i<=j),
    xA_i=gA_i=p_i                            (i<=j),

    R_k=sum_(t=k)^(n-1)h_t                    (k>j),
    xB_k=-eta*(1+R_k), gB_k=-eta              (k>j),
    xA_k=p_j*xB_k, gA_k=-eta*p_j              (k>j).

Empty sums have value zero. At the cut,
xB_(j+1)=1-h_j=-eta*(1+T_j), and the same equality multiplied by p_j
holds for coordinate A. Before the cut the A coordinate follows unit
quadratic GD. After it both coordinates have constant gradients. Hence
the vectors

    X_i=(xB_i,sqrt(epsilon)*xA_i),
    G_i=(gB_i,sqrt(epsilon)*gA_i),
    F_i=<X_i,G_i>-||G_i||^2/2                            (11)
