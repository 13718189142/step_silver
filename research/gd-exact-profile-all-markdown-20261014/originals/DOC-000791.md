# Robust nonstationary positive-value relay with exponential growth
Status: complete explicit construction, UNVERIFIED; no new verifier allocation.
This extends the periodic signed-history example by allowing independently varying modulation at every stage and a strictly growing gradient scale. All proof ingredients are included here.

## 1. Schedule class and value
Fix
a=9/10, T=23/10, kappa=1/10, chi=6/5, q_g=901/900.
Choose any m>=1 and ANY numbers v_i in[1,chi],0<=i<=m, with v_0=1.
Define alpha_i=q_g^i v_i and
b_i=T-1+(T/a)alpha_(i+1)/alpha_i,0<=i<m.
The supplied schedule is h=(1,b_0,...,1,b_(m-1)), of length2m.
Every b_i belongs to[3,5]; these are variable checkpoints, not a constant schedule.

Then one explicitly defined nonseparable convex1-smooth function in R^(m+1) gives
W_(2m)(h)>=(85/121) q_g^(2m) v_m^2
          >=(85/121)(901/900)^(2m).                     (1)
Its exact closest-minimizer normalization is used. Meanwhile the entire quadratic-class objective is at most
max{1/(2 e m),(4/5)^(2m)} ->0.                           (2)
Thus the nonlinear error grows exponentially on a robust nonstationary class with freely chosen v_i.

Equivalently, this is an explicit condition on a supplied checkpoint schedule: the multiplicative partial products
v_i=product_(j=0)^(i-1) [(9/(23 q_g))(b_j-13/10)]
must all lie in[1,6/5]. The empty product v_0=1 is fixed. This condition is sufficient, not asserted for arbitrary schedules.

## 2. One global function and exact residual identity
Let zeta_0=e_0 and
zeta_(i+1)=-a zeta_i+sqrt(1-a^2)e_(i+1).
Their Gram matrix is real PSD with
<zeta_i,zeta_j>=(-a)^|i-j|.
Set omega_i=alpha_i zeta_i and
J(z)=max{0,max_i(<omega_i,z>-kappa alpha_i^2)}, f=env_1 J.
This is one finite nonnegative convex max-affine function, so its envelope is convex, differentiable and1-smooth, with minimum0. The direct proximal optimality condition proves that if plane i is active at x-omega_i then
grad f(x)=omega_i, f(x)=<omega_i,x>-(1/2+kappa)alpha_i^2.
Subgradient monotonicity gives the1-Lipschitz gradient estimate, ensuring full global legality, not only local interpolation.

Start at X_0=T alpha_0 zeta_0=T zeta_0, and set the intended block starts
X_(i+1)=X_i-(1+b_i)omega_i.
Let Z_i=X_i-T omega_i. Define
B_i=(T/a)alpha_(i+1), D_i=B_i(1-a^2).
Then
Z_0=0, Z_(i+1)=Z_i-B_i(zeta_i+a zeta_(i+1)).
Consequently
<Z_i,zeta_j>=0 for j>=i,
<Z_i,zeta_j>=-S_(j,i) for j<i,
S_(j,i)=sum_(l=j)^(i-1)D_l(-a)^(l-j).                   (3)
This follows by taking inner products of the update: zeta_l+a zeta_(l+1) is orthogonal to every later zeta_j, and its earlier inner products are(1-a^2)(-a)^(l-j).

At either real query x=X_i-u omega_i,u=0,1, the proposed proximal point is Z_i+d omega_i with d=T-u-1. The terminal query has d=T-1. Put delta=T-2=3/10, so all these d are>=delta.
The current plane has value(d-kappa)alpha_i^2>=alpha_i^2/5>0.
For an old index j its deficit relative to the current plane is
d alpha_i(alpha_i-C_ij alpha_j)
 +alpha_j S_(j,i)-kappa alpha_i^2+kappa alpha_j^2,        (4)
where C_ij=(-a)^(i-j). Future indices have the same expression with S=0.

## 3. Uniform estimates for the signed history
Put theta=a q_g=901/1000 and K=(T/a)(1-a^2)=437/900.
For L=i-j>0,
S_(j,i)/q_g^j=K q_g sum_(r=0)^(L-1)(-theta)^r v_(j+r+1).
Define the negative constant
S_floor=K q_g(1-theta chi)/(1-theta^2).
Using v in[1,chi] on positive and negative terms gives:
if L is even,
S_(j,i)/q_g^j >= S_floor(1-theta^L);                    (5)
if L is odd,
S_(j,i)/q_g^j >= S_floor
 +K q_g theta^(L-1)(theta chi-theta^2)/(1-theta^2)
 >=S_floor.                                           (6)
Thus old residuals may be positive; only their exact quantitative cost is bounded.

The following rational bounds will suffice:
theta^2<13/16, theta^2>4/5,
theta chi-1<13/160,
K q_g<39/80,
|S_floor|<169/800.                                     (7)
For the last inequality divide the product of the preceding bounds by
1-theta^2>3/16:
(39/80)(13/160)/(3/16)=169/800.
All numbers here are exact finite rational inequalities.

Every coefficient of d in(4), including future comparisons, is positive.
For even separation in a past comparison,
C_ij alpha_j/alpha_i=(a/q_g)^L v_j/v_i<=a^2 chi<1.
For a future even separation the ratio is
theta^L v_j/v_i<=theta^2 chi<39/40<1.
Odd correlations are negative. Therefore checking d=delta suffices.

Future planes pass for arbitrary amplitude ratios. At even separation their deficit at d=delta is
(delta-kappa)alpha_i^2+kappa alpha_j^2-delta C_ij alpha_i alpha_j.
This quadratic form is positive definite because
delta^2 C_ij^2<=delta^2 a^4=59049/1000000
 <4kappa(delta-kappa)=80000/1000000.
At odd separation every term has a nonnegative sign.

For past comparisons normalize(4) by q_g^(2j). Set
z=q_g^L v_i>=v_i>=1, y=v_j in[1,chi].
If L is odd, (6) and the positive cross term give the lower bound
(delta-kappa)z^2+kappa y^2+S_floor y
 >=3/10-(169/800)(6/5)=93/2000>0.                       (8)

If L is even, the part
(delta-kappa)z^2-delta a^L z y
increases with z>=1, since its derivative is at least
2/5-(3/10)a^2 chi>0.
Replace z by v_i; then replace a^L by the larger theta^L to lower the expression further. Using(5), the result is
F(v_i,y,w)=(delta-kappa)v_i^2+kappa y^2
 -delta w v_i y+S_floor(1-w)y,
where w=theta^L lies in[0,theta^2].
This is affine in w, so only endpoints need checking.
At w=0, the estimate in(8) applies.
At w=theta^2, F increases with v_i on[1,chi]:
2(delta-kappa)v_i-delta theta^2 y
 >=2/5-(3/10)(13/16)(6/5)>0.
It decreases with y on that interval, since
2kappa y-delta theta^2 v_i+S_floor(1-theta^2)
 <6/25-(3/10)(4/5)=0.
Its minimum is therefore at v_i=1,y=chi. By(7),
F(1,chi,theta^2)
>43/125-(3/10)(13/16)(6/5)-(39/80)(13/160)(6/5)
=127/32000>0.                                          (9)
This proves every old-plane comparison at every lag, including those with S<0.

The zero, current, past and future checks establish all prescribed gradients. In particular at each checkpoint
J(X_i-2omega_i)=alpha_i^2/5>0,
so the new mechanism carries a positive proximal value rather than resetting to the minimum.

## 4. Exact minimizer distance and objective
The minimizer set equals
P_min={z:<omega_i,z><=kappa alpha_i^2 for all i}.
Because alpha_0=1 and alpha_i>=1, y_proj=kappa zeta_0 belongs to P_min: odd correlations are negative; even ones are at most1. Constraint0 is active and
X_0-y_proj=(T-kappa)omega_0.
This is a nonnegative active-normal certificate, so y_proj is the exact closest minimizer. The squared initial distance is
(T-kappa)^2=(11/5)^2=121/25.
The exact terminal value is
2f(X_m)=2(T-kappa-1/2)alpha_m^2=(17/5)alpha_m^2.
Dividing gives(1).

The gradients omega_0,...,omega_m are independent with adjacent nonzero correlations, and their squared norms alpha_i^2 vary. Independence allows each zero/plane hinge to be isolated; its envelope Hessian is the rank-one projector onto omega_i. Adjacent projectors do not commute, proving nonseparability in every fixed orthonormal basis.

## 5. Actual negative history and the quadratic comparison
This family strictly escapes the old sign restriction. Choose any eligible consecutive modulation pattern
v_j=chi,v_(j+1)=1,v_(j+2)=chi.
Then
S_(j,j+2)=K q_g^(j+1)(1-theta chi)<0.
Nevertheless(8)--(9) certify the full old plane. The construction therefore does not merely hide a nonpositive-residual assumption behind new notation.

The checkpoints obey
13/10+(23/9)q_g(5/6)<=b_i<=13/10+(23/9)q_g(6/5),
an interval contained in[3,5]. For the quadratic multiplier
(1-lambda)(1-b_i lambda),
on0<=lambda<=1/5 it is nonnegative and<=exp(-lambda); on1/5<=lambda<=1 its absolute value is<=4/5 by the endpoints and its single quadratic vertex. Thus spectral diagonalization gives(2) for every finite-dimensional convex quadratic.

This is a rigorous candidate lower bound for the displayed nonstationary class, not the exact target theorem. The bounded-modulation/partial-product condition is explicitly retained; no claim says arbitrary schedules satisfy it or can be partitioned into such blocks without losing the target constant.
