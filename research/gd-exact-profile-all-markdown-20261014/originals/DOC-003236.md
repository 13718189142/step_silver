## 2. Fixed real slopes, function, and complete minimum set

N=3r+q. Partition the original N coordinates into three groups G_1,G_2,G_3

    E_N={x in R^N: sum_l x_l=0}.

    s_l=sigma(e_l-u_N), 1<=l<=N,
    D=conv{s_l:1<=l<=N},
    F(x)=max_(g in D) [<x,g>-||g||^2/2], x in E_N.           (3)

Their exact Gram matrix is

    <s_l,s_k>=sigma^2(1_(l=k)-1/N).                          (4)

The function does not change during the run. The maximization in (3) is
over the entire convex hull, not merely over the original vertices. The
original affine dictionary is max_l <s_l,x>, with all its offsets zero;
(3) is the fixed quadratic smoothing used throughout this report.

Existence of a maximizing g follows from compactness of D; strict concavity
of the quadratic gives uniqueness. Completing the square identifies g as
the Euclidean projection of x onto D. Its exact condition is

    <x-g,v-g><=0 for every v in D.                            (5)

<x-x',g-g'> >= ||g-g'||^2. Hence ||g-g'||<=||x-x'||. Evaluating (3) at
each other's maximizers gives a nonnegative first-order remainder bounded
above by <x'-x,g'-g>, hence by ||x'-x||^2. Therefore F is differentiable,
convex, finite everywhere, and grad F=g is 1-Lipschitz. Convexity also
follows directly by taking the supremum of the affine functions in (3).

with every w_l> -sigma/N belongs to D because w/sigma+u_N has positive
entries summing to one. Thus for any x!=0, a sufficiently small t in (0,1)

    F(x)>=(t-t^2/2)||x||^2>0.
