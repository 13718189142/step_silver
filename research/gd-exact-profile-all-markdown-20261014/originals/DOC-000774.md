# Exact optimality screen for a three-copy, two-bridge primitive join

Status: complete unverified construction-class obstruction, not a statement about unrestricted minimax optimality. No fact submission under the current allocation.

A concrete candidate was
c=2/3, d=(5sqrt(7)-11)/6, M=3+c+d=(11+5sqrt(7))/6,
J(a)=[a,1+cA,a,1+dA,a], A=1+sum a.
It has cdM=1 and M>F(1,1+sqrt(2)), where F is the exact shifted primitive join. Thus it passes the full-curvature product balance and suggests a larger primitive capacity than two ordinary binary joins. It is nevertheless invalid. The general argument below screens all positive c,d in this motif and proves that none can exceed the existing binary factor when the seed is quadratic-balanced.

Definitions. At L=1 write x_(i+1)=x_i-h_i g_i, g_i=grad f(x_i), f_i=f(x_i). For a nonnegative schedule h with sum s=R-1 define its saturated intrinsic primitive energy
V_h=2 sum_(i<n) h_i(f_i-f_n)+sum_(i<n)h_i(1-h_i)||g_i||^2-sR||g_n||^2.
The primitive property means V_h>=0 on every smooth convex trajectory. The following necessary tests are explicit legal one-dimensional functions, so no conjectural composition theorem is used.

1. An exact cut constraint for every primitive schedule.
Choose any step b>=1, let P be the sum of preceding steps, and T the sum of following steps. Set a_minus=(b-1)/(T+1). Use f'(x)=clip(x,-a_minus,1), f(0)=0, and x_0=P+1. Its prefix stays on the positive affine ray and arrives at1. The b-step arrives at -(b-1). All tail iterates stay on the negative affine ray, and the final point is -a_minus. This follows from nonnegative partial sums and (T+1)a_minus=b-1. The degenerate b=1 case is harmless.
At each sample, Q_(i,star)=f_i-g_i x_i+g_i^2/2=0, since the function is quadratic in its core and affine with the matching offset on each tail. A direct expansion using the GD recursion yields
V_h=x_0^2-x_n^2-2s f_n-sR g_n^2+2 sum_i h_i Q_(i,star).
Thus this trajectory gives V_h=(P+1)^2-R^2 a_minus^2. If h is primitive it follows that
(b-1)R <= (P+1)(T+1).                                      (CUT)
This is a necessary primitive-energy inequality, not merely the objective single-cut lower bound.

2. Full-curvature condition.
For f(x)=x^2/2 and x_0=1, all Q_(i,star)=0, and V_h=1-R^2 product_i(1-h_i)^2. Hence a primitive schedule must obey
R |product_i(1-h_i)| <=1.                                  (QUAD)

3. The three-copy motif cannot exceed the ordinary two-join factor.
Let a be any nonnegative block with shifted sum A>0 and |product_i(1-a_i)|=1/A. This includes each standard quadratic-balanced basic primitive block. Form J(a) with c,d>0 and put M=3+c+d, so its shifted sum is R=MA.
The two bridge instances of (CUT) give
Mc<=2+d, Md<=2+c.
Condition (QUAD) gives Mcd<=1, because the endpoint quadratic multiplier has magnitude cd/A.
Writing t=c+d=M-3 and u=(M-1)/(M+1), the cut conditions are exactly c,d<=u. If M<=4 there is already no gain over F(1,1+sqrt(2))>4. Suppose M>4. Both c,d lie in [t-u,u]. At a fixed sum t, the product c(t-c) is concave, so
cd >= u(t-u)=(M-1)(M^2-3M-2)/(M+1)^2.
Combining this with Mcd<=1 gives
M^4-4M^3-1<=0.
This polynomial is strictly increasing for M>3, and has one root M_*>4. Put lambda=1+sqrt(2) and C=F(1,lambda). Its defining equation C^2-(1+lambda)C-lambda=0 gives lambda=C(C-1)/(C+1). Substitution into lambda^2-2lambda-1=0 gives C^4-4C^3-1=0. Therefore M_*=F(1,lambda), and necessarily
M<=F(1,1+sqrt(2)).
The existing two-step binary parenthesizations of three copies attain this scalar factor. This argument rules out a larger primitive factor for the displayed three-copy motif, regardless of a proposed richer certificate.

4. Exact hostile instance for the displayed new constants.
Take a=[sqrt(2)], A=lambda, c=2/3 and d=(5sqrt(7)-11)/6. One has 0<d<2/3. Use f'(x)=clip(x,-1/4,1), f(0)=0, and x_0=A. The first sqrt(2)-step reaches1; the first bridge reaches -cA. Every remaining step stays on the negative affine ray because their total is T=(2+d)A-1 and
-cA+T/4 <= -1/4 iff d<=2/3.
The endpoint is x_5=-cA+[(2+d)A-1]/4. On the negative affine ray, the primitive terminal expression x_5^2+2s f_5+sR g_5^2 equals (x_5-s/4)^2. Since s=MA-1,
x_5-s/4=-A[c+(1+c)/4]=-(13/12)A.
Thus V_J=A^2-(13A/12)^2=-(25/144)A^2<0. This is a direct failure of the proposed primitive certificate in dimension one. It does not rely on reversing schedules or on a general H-duality assertion.
