## 6. Exact scalar extremum of the hereditary relaxation

has a recursive maximizing split at one bridge index. The following
argument proves the statement without importing an unverified premise.

t_i>=c_*=sqrt(2)-1>0. Global equality gives

Fix a maximizer. Write F_I=log(1+sum_I h_i)+sum_I log t_i, and set
F_empty=0. Call an interval tight when F_I=0. Two tight intervals
cannot overlap without containment. Indeed, if their left-only, common,
right-only pieces have masses a>0,b>0,c>0, then

 F_I+F_J-F_(I union J)-F_(I intersection J)
  =log[(1+a+b)(1+b+c)/((1+b)(1+a+b+c))]>0,

because the numerator minus denominator in the ratio is ac>0. Tightness
of I,J and nonnegativity of the other two F values contradict this.
Nor can two nonempty tight intervals be adjacent: their union is an
interval, and
F_I+F_J-F_(I union J)=log[(1+a)(1+c)/(1+a+c)]>0.

The inclusion-maximal proper tight intervals are therefore disjoint,
and every two are separated by at least one uncovered index. At least
one index is uncovered: a single proper interval cannot cover the whole
word, and multiple intervals covering it would include an adjacent pair.

There cannot be two uncovered indices u,v. Perturb t_u to t_u+epsilon
and t_v to t_v-epsilon, with a sufficiently small nonzero epsilon of
sign such that epsilon(t_v-t_u)<=0. Positivity is preserved. The total

alone until this expression returns to1. Such an increase exists because
the expression is continuous, strictly increasing in t_u and unbounded.
All proper interval expressions are nondecreasing under this increase,
so none becomes invalid. The resulting feasible word has strictly larger
capacity, a contradiction.

There is exactly one uncovered index j. All indices to its left form
one proper tight interval, if any; otherwise two maximal intervals on
