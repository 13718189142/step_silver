# Discrete Hardy tools for the chronological-kernel interface

Read-only primary-source search by main, 2026-09-27. This note is contextual
strategy, not an accepted Danus fact, a matched theorem, or a proof.

1. Laurent Miclo, *An example of application of discrete Hardy's inequalities*,
[author PDF](https://perso.math.univ-toulouse.fr/miclo/files/2012/04/Hardy.pdf).
Proposition1 on PDF page3 concerns positive sequence weights mu,nu and the
best squared-norm constant A for weighted prefix sums. Its tail-prefix
quantity B satisfies B<=A<=4B. The paper applies such estimates to spectral
gaps of birth-and-death processes. These are quantitatively explicit but
factor-loss bounds. They are not sharp constant-one domination for our
time-inhomogeneous kernel. A claimed application must exhibit the actual
weights, boundary conditions and transformation, preserving terminal clock,
constant column and all signed remainder terms. The presence of a birth
representation alone is not enough. Total positivity likewise does not
remove the loss or prove covariance domination.

2. Ali Barki, *Sharp Weighted Discrete p-Hardy Inequality and Stability*,
[arXiv2501.00299v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00299v1).
Section2 treats two positive measures. Proposition2.1 bounds the best Hardy
constant between B and p^p/(p-1)^(p-1) times B. Proposition2.3 separately treats
the zero-sum sequence class; Remark2.2 explains why this distinction matters.
Section3's Theorem3.3 builds an optimal graph Hardy weight from a positive
superharmonic function, assuming a connected graph, finitely supported
nonnegative potential, properness and bounded neighbor oscillation.
This is a possible source of an exact supersolution method, not a theorem
already matched to our finite, horizon-dependent, signed birth-coordinate
matrix. Any use needs an explicit finite specialization and boundary proof;
neither graph positivity nor bounded oscillation is automatic here.
