## 1. Fixed target and actual baseline witnesses

Set the smoothness constant to 1. For a prescribed finite nonnegative physical word $h=(h_0,\ldots,h_{n-1})$, let $S=\sum h_t$ and

\[
 W_n(h)=\sup\frac{2(f(x_n)-f_\star)}{\|x_0-x_\star\|^2},
 \qquad x_{t+1}=x_t-h_t\nabla f(x_t).
\]

The supremum is over all finite dimensions, convex differentiable functions with 1-Lipschitz gradient and an attained minimum, and original initial/minimizer pairs. Thus $W=2R$. The exact benchmark is $U_n^{\rm ZJ}=1/B_n$, where $C_0=B_0=1$ and

\[
 C_n=\max_{k+l=n-1}\Omega(C_k,C_l),\qquad
 B_n=\max_{k+l=n-1}G(C_k,B_l),
\]
\[
 \Omega(x,y)=\frac{x+y+\sqrt{x^2+6xy+y^2}}2,
 \qquad G(x,y)=2x+\frac y2+\frac{\sqrt{y^2+8xy}}2.
\]

Every integer split is included. The missing affirmative statement is

\[
 \forall\epsilon>0\ \exists N_\epsilon\ \forall n\ge N_\epsilon\
 \forall h\in[0,\infty)^n:\quad W_n(h)\ge(1-\epsilon)/B_n,
 \tag{1}
\]

with $N_\epsilon$ independent of $h$. No such threshold is produced here.

The standard noncrossing Huber witness and the full scalar-quadratic pool give

\[
 W_n(h)\ge\frac1{2S+1},\qquad
 W_n(h)\ge\lambda\prod_{t=0}^{n-1}(1-\lambda h_t)^2
 \quad(0\le\lambda\le1).
 \tag{2}
\]

For the first, take derivative $\operatorname{clip}(x,-1,1)$, minimum 0, and start $2S+1$; all iterates stay on the positive affine ray, giving normalized value $1/(2S+1)$. For the second, take $f(x)=\lambda x^2/2$, start 1 and minimizer 0. These are lower witnesses, not an upper certificate for $W$. Throughout, “Huber baseline” means exactly this standard noncrossing witness; it does not mean every possible Huber scale, starting point or crossing itinerary.

## 2. Line 1: the actual R5 simplex and a quantitative failure of tight-guard selection

### 2.1 Full construction and projection audit

Partition the complete physical word into $M\ge2$ phases. For $i<M$, phase $i$ contains an ordinary block of mass $C_i-1\ge0$, followed by a checkpoint $1+d_i$, with $d_i>0$. The last block has mass $C_M-1\ge0$. Then

\[
 S=\sum_{i=1}^M C_i+\sum_{i=1}^{M-1}d_i-1.
\]

Choose $A_i>0$ with

\[
 p_i:=A_i^{-1},\quad \sum_i p_i=1,\quad
 P_i=\sum_{j=i}^M A_jC_j,\quad A_i d_i\ge P_{i+1}\quad(i<M).
 \tag{3}
\]

Let $u=(\sqrt{p_i})_i$, $V=u^\perp$, and

\[
 w_i=\sqrt{A_i}e_i-u,\qquad K=\operatorname{conv}\{w_i\}_{i=1}^M.
\]

The inner products are $\langle w_i,w_j\rangle=-1$ for $i\ne j$ and $\|w_i\|^2=A_i-1$. Also $\sum p_iw_i=0$, and the vertices span $V$. Thus 0 is in the relative interior of this simplex. Use the single static function on $V$

\[
 F_K(x)=\max_{g\in K}\{\langle x,g\rangle-\tfrac12\|g\|^2\}.
 \tag{4}
\]

Completing the square gives the unique maximizing gradient $P_Kx$; projection is nonexpansive, so $F_K$ is convex and 1-smooth. Since $K$ contains a relative neighborhood of 0, every nonzero $x\in V$ has positive value in (4) by testing a sufficiently small positive multiple of $x$. Consequently its unique minimizer in $V$ is 0. At a proposed query $x$, a proposed vertex $w_i$ is its gradient exactly when

\[
 \langle x-w_i,w_j-w_i\rangle\le0\quad\hbox{for every }j.
 \tag{5}
\]

Put

\[
 \bar P=\sum_i p_iP_i,\qquad
 X_0=\sum_i\sqrt{p_i}P_i e_i-\bar P u.
\]

Its original squared radius is

\[
 R^2=\sum_i p_iP_i^2-\bar P^2
     =\sum_{i<j}p_ip_j(P_i-P_j)^2>0.
 \tag{6}
\]

The last inequality follows because every $C_i>0$, so the $P_i$ strictly decrease. Prescribe gradient $w_i$ in phase $i$. If $q_j$ is the physical step mass already spent with gradient $w_j$, and $s=\sum q_j$, then exact GD gives

\[
 \langle X,w_j\rangle=P_j-\bar P+s-A_jq_j.
\]

Writing $z_j=P_j-A_jq_j$, the row (5) for $j\ne i$ is $z_j-z_i+A_i\le0$; the self-row is identically zero. Throughout an ordinary block, including the query at its checkpoint, $q_i\le C_i-1$, so $z_i-A_i\ge P_{i+1}\ge0$. Future phases have $z_j=P_j\le P_{i+1}$. Every earlier phase has permanently

\[
 z_j=P_j-A_j(C_j+d_j)=P_{j+1}-A_jd_j\le0.
\]

In the last phase the terminal inequality is $z_M-A_M\ge0$. These statements check all old, future and adjacent rows, including the terminal query. The row against 0 follows from its positive convex representation. No step is deleted, and zero steps inside blocks cause no change to the argument.

The exact terminal numerator and resulting witness are

\[
 N:=2F_K(X_n)=2S+1+A_M-2\bar P,
 \qquad E_{\rm cen}=N/R^2.
 \tag{7}
\]

### 2.2 What the negative centering cancels, and what tight guards lose

Define the nonnegative actual guard slacks

\[
 \delta_i=d_i-p_iP_{i+1}\quad(i<M).
\]

Interchanging finite sums gives

\[
 \bar P=\sum_i C_i+\sum_{i<M}p_iP_{i+1}
       =S+1-\sum_{i<M}\delta_i,
\]
\[
 N=2\sum_{i<M}\delta_i+(A_M-1).
 \tag{8}
\]

Also $P_i-P_j=\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}C_k/p_k$. Hence the variance (6), for fixed $p$, cancels the terminal block mass $C_M$ entirely. This is a genuine cancellation, but $C_M$ still affects feasibility and (8); it cannot be discarded from selection.

If every guard is tight, then $\bar P=S+1$. The terminal supports are $-1$ on every earlier vertex and $A_M-1$ on the last vertex. Since the vertices span $V$, these are exactly the supports of $w_M$, so

\[
 X_n=w_M\qquad\hbox{when all guards are tight}.
 \tag{9}
\]

This restriction has an explicit unbounded quantitative cost already on arbitrary two-block physical words. For $M=2$, let $r=p_1/p_2>0$ and $T=2C_2-1\ge1$. Equation (3) becomes $r\le d_1/C_2$; (6)--(7) become

\[
 R^2=C_1^2/r,\qquad N=2d_1-Tr,
 \qquad E_{\rm cen}(r)=\frac{2d_1r-Tr^2}{C_1^2}.
 \tag{10}
\]

The unconstrained maximizer $r=d_1/T$ is feasible because $T\ge C_2$. Therefore

\[
 \sup E_{\rm cen}=\frac{d_1^2}{C_1^2(2C_2-1)}.
 \tag{11}
\]

The tight choice $r=d_1/C_2$ instead gives

\[
 E_{\rm tight}=\frac{d_1^2}{C_1^2C_2^2},\qquad
 \frac{E_{\rm tight}}{\sup E_{\rm cen}}
       =\frac{2C_2-1}{C_2^2}\longrightarrow0
       \quad(C_2\longrightarrow\infty).
 \tag{12}
\]

These are full physical words: either ordinary block may have any finite length and any nonnegative step sizes with the displayed mass. This rules out treating tight backward selection as a lossless quantitative theorem. It does not rule out the complete centered-simplex family, and it does not say that these particular words defeat either baseline in (2).

I did not obtain a selection of nonzero slacks and a partition for arbitrary words that compares (7) with $1/B_n$. The exact recurrence roots do not follow merely from (8). No unknown child-attainability condition or all-word inequality is assumed here.

### 3.1 Static geometry and parameter selection for any fixed partition

Use the same $M\ge2$ phases. Let

\[
 Q_i=C_i+d_i\quad(2\le i<M),\qquad Q_M=L\ge C_M.
\]

Here $L$ is a dimensionless construction parameter; the smoothness constant remains 1. Choose $a_1>0$. In $\mathbb R^{M-1}$, use

\[
 w_1=a_1e_1,\quad
 w_i=-b_i e_{i-1}+a_i e_i\ (2\le i<M),\quad
 w_M=-b_Me_{M-1}.
 \tag{13}
\]

Require positive coefficients and

\[
 b_2Q_2=d_1a_1,\qquad
 b_{i+1}Q_{i+1}=Q_i a_i\quad(2\le i<M),
 \tag{14}
\]
\[
 C_i a_i(a_i+b_{i+1})\le b_i^2d_i\quad(2\le i<M).
 \tag{15}
\]

For $M=2$, (14) reads $b_2L=d_1a_1$, and (15) is empty. For larger $M$, an explicit positive solution is

\[
 b_2=d_1a_1/Q_2,\qquad
 a_i=b_i\sqrt{\frac{d_iQ_{i+1}}{C_i(Q_i+Q_{i+1})}},\qquad
 b_{i+1}=Q_i a_i/Q_{i+1}.
 \tag{16}
\]

These choices make (15) equalities. The direction scale $a_1$ cancels from normalized performance.

The positive coefficients proportional to $(d_1,Q_2,\ldots,Q_M)$ annihilate $\sum\theta_iw_i$, by (14). The vectors span $\mathbb R^{M-1}$, because the first $M-1$ vectors form a triangular full-rank system. Their only linear dependence therefore has positive coefficients. They are affinely independent and 0 is an interior point of their simplex $K=\operatorname{conv}\{w_i\}$. Use exactly (4). Its unique minimizer is 0, and its gradient is $P_Kx$, as proved above.

### 3.2 Every physical query and every cross-history row

Start at

\[
 X_0=C_1a_1e_1,\qquad \|X_0\|^2=C_1^2a_1^2.
 \tag{17}
\]

During the first ordinary block, at spent mass $t\in[0,C_1-1]$, the point is $(C_1-t)a_1e_1$. Its residual against $w_1$ is a nonnegative multiple of $e_1$, with nonnegative own support, nonpositive support on $w_2$, and zero support on every other vertex. Thus all rows (5) hold. The checkpoint lands at $-d_1a_1e_1=-b_2Q_2e_1$.

Inductively, phase $i$, $2\le i<M$, starts at $-b_iQ_ie_{i-1}$. At ordinary spent mass $t\in[0,C_i-1]$, its point and own residual support are

\[
 X=-b_i(Q_i-t)e_{i-1}-t a_i e_i,
\]
\[
 H_i(t):=\langle X-w_i,w_i\rangle
   =b_i^2(Q_i-t-1)-(t+1)a_i^2.
\]

The residual support on the next vertex is $(t+1)a_i b_{i+1}$. Condition (15), together with $t+1\le C_i$, gives

\[
 H_i(t)\ge(t+1)a_i b_{i+1}\ge0.
 \tag{18}
\]

The residual support on the immediately preceding vertex is

\[
 -a_{i-1}b_i(Q_i-t-1)\le0,
\]

because $Q_i-t-1\ge d_i>0$. The residual support on every nonneighboring vertex is 0. Subtracting the nonnegative own support $H_i(t)$ checks (5) against every old and future vertex, and against 0. At the checkpoint query the same inequalities apply. The total mass $Q_i=C_i+d_i$ of this phase cancels its old coordinate exactly, leaving

\[
 -Q_i a_i e_i=-b_{i+1}Q_{i+1}e_i.
\]

This completes the induction without an unexamined old coordinate.

In the last phase, $X=-b_M(L-t)e_{M-1}$, $0\le t\le C_M-1$. Its residual against $w_M$ is a nonpositive multiple of $e_{M-1}$. Its own support is $b_M^2(L-t-1)\ge0$, its previous-vertex support is nonpositive, and every other support is 0. Thus all final rows also hold. All physical steps, including zero ordinary steps and arbitrarily large finite checkpoints, are preserved in this one static function.

### 3.4 A genuine all-depth test on the inherited-parameter hierarchy

Fix an enclosing integer $m\ge1536$, and keep its parameters fixed at every recursive depth:

Let $H_0$ be empty and $H_{j+1}=(H_j,1+a c^j,H_j)$. Write

 V_j=1+\sum H_j,\quad D_m=2V_m-1,\quad
 \eta=c-2,\quad \alpha=\frac a{c-2}.
\]

\[
 V_j=\alpha c^j+(1-\alpha)2^j.
 \tag{20}

\[
 \frac{12}{5}<c<\frac{49}{20},\quad

 \tag{21}

For the upper bound on $c$, use $1+\sqrt2<29/12$ and $\varepsilon\le1/6144$. For the lower bound on $\alpha=\beta/[c(c-2)]$, use $\beta>999/1000$ and $c(c-2)<441/400$; this ratio exceeds $9/10$. Its upper bound follows from $c(c-2)>1$ and $\beta<1$.

Select any nonempty subsequence of checkpoints on the rightmost recursive root-to-leaf path. Denote their levels by

\[
 m>r_1>r_2>\cdots>r_k\ge0,

The checkpoint at level $r_i$ is $1+d_i$, $d_i=a c^{r_i}$. The exact physical phase masses, including every skipped checkpoint and every skipped subtree, are

 C_i=V_{r_{i-1}}-V_{r_i}-d_i\quad(1\le i\le k),

 \tag{22}

\[

 \frac{d_i}{C_i}\le\eta c^{-(g_i-1)}.

 C_i\ge(\alpha c^{g_i}-1-\eta)V_{r_i}
       \ge c^{g_i-1}V_{r_i}.

The last inequality follows from
$c^{g_i-1}(\alpha c-1)> (12/5)(29/25)>29/20>1+\eta$.

 Q_i=C_i+d_i\ge V_{r_i},\qquad
 Q_{i+1}=V_{r_i}-V_{r_{i+1}}<V_{r_i}.

\[
 \frac{Q_{i+1}}{Q_i+Q_{i+1}}<\frac12.
 \tag{24}
