## 2. The real centered simplex and its static smooth objective

Let N>=3 be an integer, let E_N={x in R^N:sum_j x_j=0} with the
inherited Euclidean metric, let u=(1/N,...,1/N), and choose sigma>0.
The intrinsic real dimension is N-1. Define every slope and every offset by

    v_j=sigma(e_j-u), 1<=j<=N,       offset_j=0,
    D=conv{v_1,...,v_N},
    J(y)=max_j <v_j,y>,
    F(x)=max_(g in D)(<x,g>-||g||^2/2), x in E_N.                (5)

There is no additional coordinate, positive carrier, variable metric, or
rescaling of an iterate. The Gram of the REAL slopes is

    <v_i,v_j>=sigma^2(1_(i=j)-1/N).                            (6)

For a literal R^(N-1) realization, an orthonormal basis of E_N is

    b_k=(e_1+...+e_k-k e_(k+1))/sqrt(k(k+1)), 1<=k<N.

The matrix U with these columns satisfies U^T U=I and U U^T=I-11^T/N.
The exact isometry X -> U X turns the slopes into sigma U^T e_j,
and leaves the scale sigma and all inner products unchanged. No Walsh
representation is needed or invoked.

The maximizer g=P_D(x) is unique by strict convexity of squared distance.
It is characterized by

    g in D, <x-g,v_j-g><=0 for ALL j.                           (7)

Adding the corresponding inequalities at x and x' gives
<x-x',g-g'>>=||g-g'||^2, hence P_D is 1-Lipschitz. Evaluating (5)
at the two maximizers bounds the differentiability remainder between 0
and ||x-x'||^2; thus F is everywhere finite, convex, differentiable,
and grad F=P_D. Its least gradient Lipschitz constant is exactly1:

    argmin F={0}, min F=0.                                    (8)

<x,v_j>=sigma x_j>0 and a sufficiently small positive multiple of

## 6. Exact true radius, terminal formula, and the width price
