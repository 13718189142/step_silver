# Coupled-coordinate instability on the recursive screening family

Status: unverified source-based derivation, not a new accepted fact.
Worker: max3. This is a companion to max3_recursive_channel_obstruction.md;
it is not a negative answer to the minimax target.

## Statement

Define c=13/5, a=c^(-17), b_r=a c^r and
H_0=(), H_m=(H_(m-1),b_(m-1),H_(m-1)). Its length is N_m=2^m-1 and
its sum is s_m=a(c^m-2^m)/(c-2).

There is a fixed integer r, independent of m, for which the explicit
Ye--Liu coupled-coordinate construction yields

    W_(N_m)(H_m) >= (98/81)^(2^(m-r-3)) / [2(1+s_r)]

for all m>=r+4. In particular this genuine worst-case error tends to infinity,
even though the restricted ray, quadratic, one-cap, and two-curvature functionals
in the companion candidate tend to zero. Both claims remain unverified here.

## External input, definitions, and normalization

The source is Yuhan Ye and Kaizhao Liu, Silver Rate Is (Almost) Optimal for
Gradient Descent, arXiv:2609.09152v2, Section3.1, Lemmas3.1--3.2 and the
pre-weakening construction for Lemma2.2; its proof is AppendixA.1--A.3.
The PDF and extracted text are in this worker's downloads directory. The
definitions, the two-coordinate proof, the global assembly, and the terminal
objective calculation have been read. A Matlas search for the local transfer
statement returned unrelated results, none used. The direct source is
https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09152 .

The exact input used is as follows. For epsilon,gamma>0 put
chi=sqrt(1+2gamma+epsilon^2). There exist finite constants A_YL and B_YL,
depending only on those two parameters, such that for every finite nonnegative
schedule and every chosen chronological checkpoint set with all checkpoint
sizes z_i>=B_YL, a finite-dimensional convex 1-smooth function with a minimizer
at zero and initial point e_1 has normalized objective at least

    [1/(2(1+S_tail))] product_i
           [z_i/(chi(A_YL+S_i)+epsilon z_i)]^2.

Here S_i is the sum of ordinary steps after the previous checkpoint and before
z_i, and S_tail is the sum after the last checkpoint. The normalization is
W=2(f(x_N)-min f)/||x_0||^2. Section3.1 computes unnormalized final gap
D^2/[4(1+S_tail)] at initial squared distance1; multiplying by2 gives the
displayed coefficient. The paper then deliberately weakens its statement by
dropping that factor2. No normalization inference from the label R_n is used.

Applicability details: each local function is a translated/scaled unit Moreau
envelope of the support function of a finite convex hull of gradient vectors
and zero. Its gradient is Euclidean projection onto that convex hull, hence
it is convex and 1-smooth. The prescribed two-coordinate trajectories have
nonnegative first-coordinate descent and second-coordinate ascent. Two exact
zero regions ensure that old components and future components are inactive
throughout the full GD trajectory. The global objective is one half the sum of
these adjacent-coordinate functions and a final one-sided Huber term. Each
coordinate appears in at most two terms, giving global smoothness constant1;
the local steps are therefore h_t/2. All component offsets are nonnegative,
so the origin is a common minimizer. These checks preserve arbitrary finite
nonnegative steps and the entire checkpoint sequence. Dimension is the number
of checkpoints plus one, finite at every fixed horizon.

The paper permits B_YL=2(1+epsilon^(-2)) and sufficiently large finite A_YL.
Only finiteness and these hypotheses are used below; neither parameter is sent
to zero with m, and no constant is hidden in an asymptotic notation.

## Exact checkpoint calculation

Choose epsilon=1/100 and gamma=2099/20000, so chi=11/10. Fix any permitted
finite A_YL. Choose r large enough that

    b_r>=B_YL=20002,   chi A_YL/b_r<=1/100.

Such a fixed r exists since b_r=a c^r tends to infinity. In H_m choose every
step whose level is at least r. All ordinary blocks between consecutive selected
steps, as well as the first and last ordinary blocks, are exact copies of H_r.
Thus every S_i and S_tail equals s_r. Set M=m-r.

The selected step sizes are b_r c^ell, with level ell occurring
2^(M-ell-1) times for ell=0,...,M-1. Since

    s_r/b_r=(1-(2/c)^r)/(c-2)<=5/3,

the fixed normalized denominator satisfies

    d=chi(A_YL+s_r)/b_r<=1/100+11/6=553/300<37/20.

Every transfer is at least

    G_ell=c^ell/[37/20+c^ell/100].

This expression increases with ell. Direct rational comparisons give

    G_0>=1/2, G_1>=4/3, G_2>=7/2, G_3>=8,

and G_ell>=1 for every ell>=4. For M>=4, the total transfer product is
therefore at least

    [(1/2)^8 (4/3)^4 (7/2)^2 8]^(2^(M-4))
       =(98/81)^(2^(M-4)).

Squaring and multiplying the exact terminal coefficient from the source gives

    W_(N_m)(H_m)>=(98/81)^(2^(M-3))/[2(1+s_r)],

which is the claimed bound. All constants r,A_YL,s_r are fixed independently
of m. The exponent equals (N_m+1)/2^(r+3), so the lower bound grows
exponentially in the horizon, despite its very small fixed growth constant.

## What this identifies and what it does not

This is a strict separation of mechanisms on a concrete schedule family. The
coupled-coordinate witness can undergo many shrinking transfers and then grow
through larger checkpoints, without having to revisit the previously defined
one-dimensional ray. In the binary level counts the first four transfer types
already have product greater than one. The verified ray construction cannot
execute even its first outward return on the same family. A two-curvature
homogeneous core becomes a scalar contraction after its two signs collapse or
interchange; that is also insufficient on these schedules.

This calculation is not a constant/profile lower bound for arbitrary schedules
and is not intended to reproduce the known silver-exponent theorem. Its use is
to locate a real geometric difference that a stronger exact construction must
retain. The local additive price A_YL and checkpoint threshold B_YL remain
fixed costs of the cited mechanism; nothing here removes them or shows that
they are negligible at the desired leading-constant scale.
