## 1. An exact adjacent criterion for a structured profile class

Write an actual prescribed schedule in chronological order as

    (block_0,b_0,block_1,...,b_(m-1),block_m),

where every block is a finite list of nonnegative steps, s_i is its sum,
and the b_i are checkpoints. Put

    T_i=s_i+1,    A_i=b_i-1,

and assume A_i>=T_(i+1) for 0<=i<m. Thus this part covers the explicit
regime b_i>=s_(i+1)+2; it is not a condition on the unrestricted problem.

Choose positive parameters beta_i with

    beta_i<=A_i/T_(i+1),
    R_i=A_i-T_(i+1) beta_i>=0,                            (1)
    beta_i beta_(i+1)<=1,
    R_i>=beta_i R_(i+1),             0<=i<m-1.            (2)

The last two inequalities are absent when m=1. Define

    q_i=sqrt(A_i beta_i/T_(i+1)),
    a_i=sqrt(T_(i+1) beta_i/A_i),
    alpha_0=1/T_0, alpha_(i+1)=q_i alpha_i,
    zeta_0=e_0,
    zeta_(i+1)=-a_i zeta_i+sqrt(1-a_i^2)e_(i+1),
    omega_i=alpha_i zeta_i.                               (3)

The e_i are an orthonormal basis of R^(m+1). Equations (1) ensure
0<a_i<=1, so every zeta_i is unit and these are genuine Euclidean data.
Fix the SINGLE polyhedral function and its SINGLE Moreau envelope

    J(z)=max(0,<omega_0,z>,...,<omega_m,z>),
    f(x)=min_z[J(z)+||x-z||^2/2].                         (4)

Start at X_0=T_0 omega_0=e_0, and set

    X_(i+1)=X_i-(s_i+b_i)omega_i,
    Z_i=X_i-T_i omega_i.                                 (5)

Then every query within ordinary block i, and the following checkpoint
query, has gradient omega_i. In particular all individual entries of each
ordinary block are executed; their order and their size need not be bounded.
At the endpoint after the final ordinary block, the exact normalized gap is

    V(h,beta)=1/T_0^2 product_(i=0)^(m-1)
                         [A_i beta_i/T_(i+1)].           (6)

The origin is an actual closest minimizer, with ||X_0||=1. Therefore (6)
is a lower bound for the true W_n(h), where n is the full supplied length.

If s_i>0 for 0<=i<=m-2, conditions (2) are also NECESSARY for the
prescribed data (3)--(5) to be supported by (4). This exactness concerns
this Markov, homogeneous-plane class, with A_i>=T_(i+1). It does not
claim that the three-profile state for general affine histories collapses.

### Full proof of all old and future comparisons

The Markov formula gives

    <zeta_i,zeta_j>=(-1)^(j-i) product_(l=i)^(j-1) a_l,
                                                        i<j.

Since T_(i+1)alpha_(i+1)=A_i alpha_i a_i, (5) yields

    Z_0=0,
    Z_(i+1)=Z_i-A_i alpha_i(zeta_i+a_i zeta_(i+1)).        (7)

Every summand at index l in (7) is orthogonal to zeta_j for j>=l+1.
Hence <Z_i,zeta_j>=0 for every j>=i. For j<i, taking the other
inner products in (7), and using a_l q_l=beta_l, gives exactly

    <omega_j,Z_i>=-alpha_j^2 H_(j,i),
    H_(j,i)=sum_(l=j)^(i-1) (-1)^(l-j) R_l
                              product_(v=j)^(l-1) beta_v. (8)

Every adjacent pair of summands, starting at an even offset from j, is
its nonnegative product weight times R_l-beta_l R_(l+1). If the number
of summands is odd, its last summand is nonnegative. Thus (2) proves
H_(j,i)>=0 for EVERY lag. No inherited plane has been discarded.

At a query after an ordinary cumulative sum u in [0,s_i], the proposed
proximal point is

    X_i-(u+1)omega_i=Z_i+d omega_i,  d=s_i-u>=0.

The current plane has value d alpha_i^2. For a future plane j>i its
value is

    d alpha_i^2 (-1)^(j-i) product_(l=i)^(j-1) beta_l.

At odd separation it is nonpositive; at even separation the product
is at most one by grouping the adjacent factors in (2). Thus EVERY
future plane is at most the current one, including arbitrarily distant
ones and the final plane.

For a past plane j<i, its residual (8) is nonpositive, and

    <omega_j,omega_i>/alpha_i^2
       =(-1)^(i-j) product_(l=j)^(i-1) [T_(l+1)/A_l].

At odd separation it is nonpositive; at even separation the product is
at most one because A_l>=T_(l+1). Consequently every past plane is
also at most the current plane. The zero plane is dominated since d>=0.

It follows that omega_i is an active-plane subgradient at the proposed
proximal point. Adding ||x-z||^2/2 to its subgradient inequality proves
that this point uniquely minimizes the proximal objective. This proves
every actual gradient and GD update in the supplied chronological schedule.

For completeness, the envelope in (4) is convex, finite, differentiable,
and 1-smooth. Convexity follows by taking the infimum over z of the jointly
convex defining expression. Existence and uniqueness of the proximal point
follow from coercivity and strict convexity. Adding two proximal
subgradient inequalities gives
||g(x)-g(y)||^2<=<g(x)-g(y),x-y>, with g=x-prox_J(x).
The two minimizing inequalities then identify the derivative as g, and
the displayed bound gives its 1-Lipschitz property.

At the final endpoint u=s_m, the proximal point is Z_m and J(Z_m)=0;
thus 2f(x_n)=alpha_m^2, yielding (6). The minimum of f is zero and its
minimizer set is exactly {J=0}. For any such minimizer z,
<omega_0,z><=0. Since X_0=T_0 omega_0=e_0,

    ||X_0-z||^2=1+||z||^2-2<X_0,z>>=1.

Origin itself is a minimizer, proving the exact normalization.

For necessity of (2), at the checkpoint proximal point Z_(i+2) the
current height is zero. Formula (8) at lag two forces
R_i-beta_i R_(i+1)>=0. At the beginning of ordinary block i, with
s_i>0, the future plane i+2 has value
s_i alpha_i^2 beta_i beta_(i+1), whereas the current value is
s_i alpha_i^2. This forces beta_i beta_(i+1)<=1. Conditions (1) are
the requirement a_i<=1 in the specified Euclidean realization. Together
these prove the asserted exact local criterion.

and computes the genuine closest-minimizer normalization. Formula (9)

This is a structured subclass of the geometric profile state, with
homogeneous planes and zero-height checkpoint proximal points. It does

## 6. Exact retained loss when the next-block condition fails

The proof above also identifies the complete additional constraint for
arbitrary A_i=b_i-1>0, including A_i<T_(i+1). Keep definitions (1),
(3)--(8), the Euclidean constraint 0<beta_i<=A_i/T_(i+1), and
T_i=s_i+1. Define the empty products to be one and, for j<i, put

    P_(j,i)=product_(l=j)^(i-1) beta_l,
    Q_(j,i)=product_(l=j)^(i-1) [A_l/T_(l+1)].

The exact old-plane comparison on the whole of ordinary block i is

    H_(j,i)>=s_i[(-1)^(i-j) P_(j,i)
                              -P_(j,i)Q_(j,i)]_+,
    H_(j,i)>=0.                                         (23)

Indeed at its proximal point Z_i+d omega_i the old plane minus the
current plane, divided by alpha_j^2, equals

    -H_(j,i)+d[(-1)^(i-j)P_(j,i)-P_(j,i)Q_(j,i)].

This is affine in d in [0,s_i], whose two endpoints are actual samples.
Thus (23) is necessary and sufficient. Future comparisons are still the
even-lag product constraints P_(i,j)<=1 whenever s_i>0. At odd lag the
sign already makes the future plane nonpositive. These are exact all-plane
conditions; in particular the positive-part term in (23) must be kept
when a product of A_l/T_(l+1) is less than one.

They give a concrete interval for appending a new block to a legal prefix.
Suppose block i has already been constructed and A=A_i>0 and the entire
next block, with T=T_(i+1)=s_(i+1)+1, are prescribed. Its only new scalar
is beta=beta_i. For every j<i with i-j odd, use the already retained
quantities

    p_j=P_(j,i)>0,  q_j=Q_(j,i)>0,
    c_j=H_(j,i)/p_j>=0.

The exact admissible interval is

    0<beta<=Upper,
    beta>=Lower,

    Upper=min{A/T,
                  min_(j<i, i-j odd, s_j>0) 1/p_j},
    Lower=max{0,
                  max_(j<i, i-j odd)
                 (A-c_j)/[T-s_(i+1)(1-A q_j/T)_+]}.     (24)

An empty inner minimum is +infinity and an empty inner maximum contributes
zero. Each denominator is at least T-s_(i+1)=1. A nonpositive candidate
for Lower is harmless. These formulas keep every old query and do not
assert that the interval is nonempty.

To check them, (8) gives, at the new even lag from j,

    H_(j,i+1)/p_j=c_j-A+T beta.

Substituting this and the new products p_j beta and q_j A/T into (23)
is exactly the lower bound in (24). If its numerator is nonpositive the
new residual is nonnegative automatically; otherwise the same bound also
implies that nonnegativity. At a new odd lag the old nonnegative residual
is increased by a nonnegative summand and the bracket in (23) is negative,
so there is no extra condition. The immediately preceding plane likewise
has residual A-T beta>=0. A future new plane at an old positive-height
query gives beta p_j<=1 precisely for the indicated even separations;
this proves Upper. Its comparisons at zero-height old proximal points
hold by the exact forward orthogonality in (7). This proves both necessity
and sufficiency of (24) for appending the supplied complete block.

For any sequence of accepted intervals, the same fixed-origin normalization
and product payoff (6) remain exact, with all directions fixed before the
actual GD run. Choosing the largest allowed beta maximizes the immediate
new squared gradient for that retained prefix; no greedy optimality for
the entire remaining schedule is claimed.

### A precise test of the omitted positive-height term

Take three one-step ordinary blocks, each (1), and two bridges each 2.
Thus the complete schedule is (1,2,1,2,1), T_0=T_1=T_2=2 and
A_0=A_1=1. The tempting parameters beta_0=beta_1=1/2 have
R_0=R_1=0, beta_0 beta_1=1/4, so every zero-residual test and
every future-plane test passes. But (23) at (j,i)=(0,2) requires

    0 >= 1*(1/4-1/16)=3/16,

which fails. Concretely their gradients are omega_0=e_0/2,
omega_1=-e_0/4, omega_2=e_0/8. At the initial query e_0/4 of the
last ordinary block, the intended proximal point is e_0/8. The old
plane omega_0 has value 1/16 there, while the proposed current plane
omega_2 has value 1/64. This is the exact old-at-new violation.

With beta_0=1/2 retained, (24) gives Lower=4/5>Upper=1/2,
so no choice of the next correlation fixes that prefix within this
specified geometry. Re-selecting the earlier parameter does: take

    beta_0=8/19, beta_1=1/2.

Now H_(0,2)=3/19 and the right side of (23) is also 3/19;
all the other comparisons hold. Formula (6) gives payoff 1/76.
This is even the maximum in this two-parameter Markov class: its only
nonautomatic old comparison is

    beta_0(3-5 beta_1/4)<=1,

with 0<beta_0,beta_1<=1/2. The objective beta_0 beta_1/16 increases
up to beta_1=1/2, where beta_0=8/19 is its largest feasible value.
This restricted calculation is not the true W_5 of that schedule.

Thus the arbitrary-schedule obstacle has an explicit additional term and
a concrete failed extension, rather than a presumed missing feasibility
theorem. The nonperiodic family of Sections 2--4 avoids that term by an
actual prescribed mass condition. A uniform policy that controls (23)--(24)
while attaining the complete B_n benchmark remains unresolved.

## 7. A positive terminal level removes an entire growing loss factor

This refinement is useful precisely when the zero-level score (11) is
asymptotically too small. It changes the terminal cell of the globally
fixed construction; it is not an unauthorized deletion of queried-prefix
constraints or an application of terminal completion to earlier samples.

Keep the schedule, ordinary blocks, and all parameters up to index 2k-1
from Section 2. Write rho=rho_(k-1) and A=rho T for the coefficient
at the last checkpoint. The queried prefix through that checkpoint is
kept exactly. Its gradient scale is

    alpha_(2k-1)^2=(1/[2T^2])
                       product_(j=0)^(k-2)(3rho_j/4).    (25)

For the final ordinary block, choose instead a terminal margin parameter

    T_terminal=2S+1=2T-1,
    beta_last=A/T_terminal,
    a_last=1,
    q_last=A/T_terminal.                                (26)

Thus the last direction is the negative of the preceding direction and
the final gradient scale is alpha_(2k)=q_last alpha_(2k-1). Define the
final residual point with the actual new margin,

    Z_(2k)=X_(2k)-T_terminal omega_(2k).

The same bridge update now gives Z_(2k)=Z_(2k-1), since the last
direction is exactly reversed and T_terminal alpha_(2k)=A alpha_(2k-1).
The last residual coefficient is zero.

All old planes and all old queries are still checked. The preceding
residual inequality is automatic because its right-hand side is zero.
The last adjacent future product is

    beta_(2k-2) beta_last=(rho T)/[2(2T-1)]<=1

when T>=4/3 and rho<=5/2. The last old-correlation factor is
T_terminal/A=(2T-1)/(rho T)<=1 because rho>=9/4. All earlier
factors and inequalities are unchanged. Consequently the pairing and
product arguments in the full proof of Section 1 still check every
old/future comparison. In the terminal block the proximal points are
Z_(2k)+(T_terminal-u-1)omega_(2k), 0<=u<=S, whose coefficients are
nonnegative. This directly checks its entire sequence of supplied queries.

At the final query the proximal value is now

    F_terminal=S alpha_(2k)^2>0,

and the exact objective numerator is

    2f_end=(2S+1)alpha_(2k)^2
           =A^2 alpha_(2k-1)^2/(2T-1).

The global function is again (4) with the revised final normal. The origin
remains its closest minimizer by the same omega_0 halfspace argument.
Thus the EXACT normalized score of this new instance is

    V_positive= [rho_(k-1)^2/(2(2T-1))]
                         product_(j=0)^(k-2)(3rho_j/4).  (27)

For comparison, the earlier zero-proximal-level instance on the IDENTICAL
schedule had exact score V_zero=T^(-2)product_(j<k)(3rho_j/4).
Their exact ratio is

    V_positive/V_zero=2rho_(k-1)T^2/[3(2T-1)],           (28)

which grows proportionally to T. This is a change in the actual normalized
objective of a legal instance, not just in an unnormalized gradient scale.

The scalar choice (26) has a simple exact optimization interpretation.
For an arbitrary terminal margin M>=S+1, keep the last direction reversed
and choose alpha_terminal=A alpha_(2k-1)/M. Whenever the old/future
checks hold, its score is

    A^2 alpha_(2k-1)^2 [2(M-S)-1]/M^2.

Its derivative has the sign of 2S+1-M, so its maximum occurs at
M=2S+1. We checked that this value satisfies the actual old/future tests;
no global optimality beyond this terminal family is asserted.

### A family for which the positive-level score reaches B_n and the old
### score does not

Now change only the prescribed horizon-dependent depth from (13) to

    r=k+18,
    N_k^+=16(2k+1)(2^(k+18)-1)+2k.                       (29)

All rho_j, ordinary blocks, and checkpoints remain exactly as specified
there. The old all-plane policy is legal at this depth too, but its
normalization cost T^2 is now substantial. Formula (27), using
rho_j>=9/4 and T<=(211/3)c^k, gives

    V_positive >= [3/(2(2T-1))](27/16)^k
                >=(3/(4T))(27/16)^k
                >=(9/844)(135/208)^k.                  (30)

Here all constants are independent of the horizon. Unlike (15), this
displayed lower bound tends to zero; comparison to the exact B_n sequence
is therefore substantive.

Put lambda_silver=1+sqrt(2). Its use in the following elementary bound
does not replace B_n by an asymptotic model. From the exact shifted
primitive join in Section 4,

    Omega(A,A)=lambda_silver A.

Induction over the actual balanced splits gives
C_(2^r-1)>=lambda_silver^r. A dominant split with this primitive left
part and empty right part gives

    B_(2^r)>=Gmass(lambda_silver^r,1)>=2lambda_silver^r+1.

Moreover B_n is increasing: the split with empty left part has
Gmass(1,B_(n-1))>=B_(n-1)+2. Since N_k^+>=2^r,

    B_(N_k^+)>=2lambda_silver^(k+18)+1.

Multiplying (30) by this bound yields

    V_positive B_(N_k^+)
       >=(9/422)lambda_silver^18
                         [135lambda_silver/208]^k>1    (31)

for EVERY k>=1. To check the strict inequality without a decimal value,
lambda_silver>12/5 implies 135lambda_silver/208>81/52>1,
and lambda_silver>2 makes (9/422)lambda_silver^18>1.
Thus this specified positive-level construction satisfies the complete
finite-horizon benchmark comparison

    W_(N_k^+)(h)>=V_positive>U_(N_k^+)^ZJ,
                                      for every k>=1.   (32)

On this SAME family the former zero-level score is below the benchmark:

    V_zero<= (15/8)^k/T^2,
    V_zero/U_(N_k^+)^ZJ ->0.                            (33)

Indeed T>=(40/3)a c^r, N_k^+<=48k2^r, and (22) bound the ratio by a
constant times k^(4/3)[(15/8)2^(4/3)/c^2]^k, plus a smaller term.
Since 2^(4/3)<c and (15/8)/c=75/104<1, it tends to zero.

The K and Q estimates (17) were proved for every r>=18, not just the
first depth choice. With r=k+18 the same calculation gives

    max(K_(N_k^+),Q_(N_k^+))/U_(N_k^+)^ZJ ->0.            (34)

For the last Q term use 2^(4/3)(2/3)^4<c*(16/81)<1;
the other terms contain (2^(4/3)/c)^r times polynomial factors in k.

Equations (28)--(34) identify a horizon-growing quantitative gain from
retaining a positive terminal level, with every old plane, every actual
prefix query, and the same closest-minimizer normalization preserved.
This closes the selected nonperiodic test family only. The arbitrary
schedule interval constraints (23)--(24), positive-level gluing beyond
this structured family, and the unrestricted uniform B_n comparison
remain open.

### Completing only the unqueried endpoint cannot supply this gain

This gives a quantitative use of accepted fact 1252b8823bb5a290, whose
maximal extension retains all genuinely queried prefix jets and a minimizer
sample. Apply it to the ZERO-level instance on (29), with the optimum
sample at origin. Its last actual step is a=c^(-17), since H_r begins
and ends with this step. Write omega for its terminal-block gradient and
v=||omega||^2=V_zero. The last queried jet has

    x_(n-1)=Z+(1+a)omega,
    g_(n-1)=omega,
    f_(n-1)=(a+1/2)v,
    x_n=Z+omega.

For ANY convex 1-smooth extension of those prefix jets, the descent
inequality at x_(n-1), obtained by integrating the 1-Lipschitz gradient
along the segment of length a||omega||, gives

    f(x_n)<=f_(n-1)-a v+(a^2/2)v=(1+a^2)v/2.            (35)

In particular it holds for the pointwise maximal prefix extension in
fact 1252b8823bb5a290. Its normalized error is at least V_zero because
the original function is one compatible extension, and (35) gives

    V_zero<=V_maximal_completion<=(1+a^2)V_zero.         (36)

The normalization has not changed. Indeed the retained initial jet is
x_0=e_0, g_0=e_0/T, f_0=(T-1/2)/T^2, and the extension has minimum
zero at origin. For any of its minimizers z, the necessary smooth-convex
interpolation inequality (proved in fact 1252b8823bb5a290) is

    f_0+<g_0,z-x_0>+||g_0||^2/2<=0.

Substitution gives <e_0,z><=0. Therefore every minimizer has squared
distance at least one from e_0, and origin attains one. This proves that
the actual closest-minimizer denominator in (36) is exactly one too.

By (33) and the fixed finite factor 1+a^2, the maximal-completion score
divided by U_(N_k^+)^ZJ still tends to zero. Thus the gain (28) comes
from constructing a different entire terminal block, with a positive
proximal endpoint value and rechecked old/new constraints. It cannot be
obtained merely by completing the genuinely unqueried last sample of the
old zero-level transcript. Every parameter is chosen before the GD run.
