## 2. Definitions, legality, and normalization reproduced from scratch

Fix an integer m>=8. Write

 lambda=1+sqrt(2), beta=1-1/m, c=lambda(1+1/m), a=beta/c,
 H_0=(), H_(j+1)=(H_j,1+a*c^j,H_j), 0<=j<m,
 n=2^m-1, N=2^m, h=H_m,
 r=2/c, Zgeom=sum_(j=0)^(m-1) r^j, w_j=r^j/Zgeom.

Every occurrence of a child word retains these SAME enclosing-m
parameters. Let S_t=sum_(i<t)h_i and S=S_n. Counting the two copies
at each join, or solving S_(j+1)=2S_j+1+a*c^j, gives

 1+S=2^m+(beta/c)(c^m-2^m)/(c-2)
     =(1-zeta)2^m+zeta*c^m,  zeta=beta/[c(c-2)].       (2.1)

Here 0<zeta<1: lambda(lambda-2)=1, c>lambda, and beta<1.

Use one orthonormal coordinate e_v for each binary word v of length
less than m. For a leaf s=(s_1,...,s_m), define

 a_s=sum_(j=0)^(m-1) sqrt(w_j)(-1)^(s_(j+1))e_(s_1...s_j),
 F(x)=log[N^(-1)sum_s exp(<a_s,x>)].                 (2.2)

The feature mean is zero, and every feature has squared norm one.
Different coordinate rows of the feature matrix are orthogonal. Rows
with disjoint supports have zero product. If one node is below the
other, the descendant row has equal positive and negative entries on
its support, on which the ancestor row is constant. Each row has
positive squared norm. Thus the matrix has row rank N-1.

Jensen's inequality applied to the exponential gives F>=0. Equality
requires all leaf logits to be equal; their sum is zero, so they must
all vanish. Full row rank then gives x=0. Consequently F has the UNIQUE
attained minimum zero at zero. The same rank argument shows that the
map from x to the N logits is onto their zero-sum subspace.

Differentiating this finite sum gives grad F=E_p a and Hess F=Cov_p(a),
where p_s=exp(<a_s,x>)/sum_t exp(<a_t,x>). To bound the Hessian, fix a
unit vector u and let A and B be the smallest and largest values of
<a_s,u>. For any random variable z in [A,B],

 E z^2 <=(A+B)E z-AB,
 Var(z)<=(B-A)^2/4-(E z-(A+B)/2)^2<=(B-A)^2/4.

The first inequality follows by averaging (z-A)(B-z)>=0.
If leaves first separate at level j, their squared distance is

             4w_j+2sum_(l>j)w_l.                   (2.3)

The quarter-distances strictly decrease with j, since the difference
between consecutive ones is w_j-w_(j+1)/2>0. The diameter bound above
therefore gives the globally valid constant

                  Ltree=(1+w_0)/2.                (2.4)

It is sharp. Choose a pair separating at the root. Prescribe equal
logits for that pair and logits smaller by T for every other leaf,
then subtract the common mean. Surjectivity of the logit map realizes
these logits at an actual finite x. As T tends to infinity the Gibbs
law tends to the equal law on that pair. Its covariance in the unit
feature-difference direction tends to the squared diameter divided by
four, namely Ltree. This establishes the global supremum.

Take t0=log(2) and the exact initial point

 x_0,(0^j)=t0/sqrt(w_j), 0<=j<m,
 x_0,v=0 on every other node.

The squared distance to the whole minimizer set is exactly

 R^2=t0^2 sum_(j=0)^(m-1)1/w_j
    =t0^2 r^(1-m) Zgeom^2.                          (2.5)

The last equality reverses the finite geometric sum. Run the actual
iteration x_(i+1)=x_i-(h_i/Ltree)grad F(x_i). For an arbitrary desired
smoothness L, the function (L/Ltree)F uses exactly steps h_i/L and has
the legal instance score 2F(x_n)/(Ltree R^2). All positions, values,
gradients, and the minimum are from this one finite static function.

## 3. Actual group probabilities and conditional fields

All coordinates off the all-left spine remain zero. At a point on
that spine, leaves with the same first right turn have equal logits.
A non-spine coordinate splits one such group into equal halves, so
its mean feature coordinate vanishes. Induction through every physical
step proves invariance.

Define y_(i,j)=sqrt(w_j)x_(i,0^j). For first exit k<m and the final
all-left leaf k=m, respectively, set

 q_k=2^(-k-1) (k<m), q_m=2^(-m),
 ell_(i,k)=sum_(j<k)y_(i,j)-y_(i,k) (k<m),
 ell_(i,m)=sum_(j<m)y_(i,j),
 P_(i,k)=q_k exp(ell_(i,k))/sum_(u=0)^m q_u exp(ell_(i,u)).  (3.1)

These are probabilities of the original leaves, aggregated only over
identical logits. In particular they are not free control variables.
Let

 Q_(i,j)=sum_(k=j)^m P_(i,k), 0<=j<=m,
 d_(i,j)=Q_(i,j+1)-P_(i,j)=2Q_(i,j+1)-Q_(i,j).

Then Q_(i,0)=1 and the exact coordinate update is

 y_(i+1,j)=y_(i,j)-(h_i w_j/Ltree)d_(i,j).           (3.2)

Initially the denominator in (3.1) is (m+4)/4, each side-group
unnormalized mass is 1/4, and the final unnormalized mass is 1. Thus

 P_(0,k)=1/(m+4) (k<m), P_(0,m)=4/(m+4),
 Q_(0,j)=(m-j+4)/(m+4),
 F(x_0)=log[(m+4)/4].                               (3.3)

For each time define conditional log-partitions recursively by

 A_(i,m)=0,
 A_(i,j)=log[(exp(-y_(i,j))+exp(y_(i,j)+A_(i,j+1)))/2],
 T_(i,j)=y_(i,j)+A_(i,j+1)/2.                       (3.4)

This is the conditional partition on the actual all-left depth-j
subtree with its original uniform conditional reference. The side
subtree contributes one because all its coordinates are zero. Direct
factorization gives

 A_(i,j)=A_(i,j+1)/2+log cosh T_(i,j),
 Q_(i,j+1)/Q_(i,j)=(1+tanh T_(i,j))/2,
 d_(i,j)=Q_(i,j)tanh T_(i,j),
 D_i:=F(x_i)=A_(i,0)=sum_(j=0)^(m-1)2^(-j)log cosh T_(i,j). (3.5)

Every A_(i,j) is nonnegative. In particular the objective is the FULL
sum in (3.5), rather than just its final two-leaf contribution.
The exact conditional-field update is

 T_(i+1,j)=T_(i,j)-alpha_(i,j)tanh T_(i,j)
                   +(A_(i+1,j+1)-A_(i,j+1))/2,
 alpha_(i,j)=h_i w_j Q_(i,j)/Ltree.                 (3.6)

Equation (3.6) retains the changing child partition. Dropping its last
term, or replacing alpha by a fresh copy of the original child word,
changes the actual process.

Since sum_k q_k ell_(i,k)=0, substitution in (3.1) also proves

 D_i=sum_k q_k log(q_k/P_(i,k)).                    (3.7)

The reference law is the FIRST argument of this relative entropy.

## 4. Exact cumulative subtree clocks

For any physical prefix length t and depth 0<=j<m, put

 Cclock_(t,j)=sum_(i<t)alpha_(i,j)
            =(w_j/Ltree)sum_(i<t)h_i Q_(i,j).

The complete history sum admits the exact identity

 Cclock_(t,j)=S_t/(Ltree Zgeom c^j)
               +sum_(l<j)c^(l-j)(t0-y_(t,l)).       (4.1)

To prove it, the relation d_(i,j)=2Q_(i,j+1)-Q_(i,j) and Q_(i,0)=1
give by finite induction

 Q_(i,j)=2^(-j)[1+sum_(l<j)2^l d_(i,l)].

Multiply by h_i and sum. Equation (3.2) gives

 sum_(i<t)h_i d_(i,l)=Ltree(t0-y_(t,l))/w_l.

Finally 2^l/w_l=Zgeom c^l and w_j 2^(-j)=1/(Zgeom c^j), proving (4.1).
There is also the mass version, valid through j=m:

 sum_(i<t)h_i Q_(i,j)
  =2^(-j)[S_t+Ltree Zgeom sum_(l<j)c^l(t0-y_(t,l))]. (4.2)

These formulas hold for every prefix, not just recursive block ends.
They do not assert that a nonlinear trajectory depends only on the
sum of its effective steps.

## 5. Actual entropy and radius payment identities

Let p_i^[j] be the conditional Gibbs law on the leaves of the depth-j
all-left subtree, and let u^[j] be the uniform conditional law there.
For probability vectors p and v with positive entries, define
KL(p||v)=sum_s p_s log(p_s/v_s). Set

 I_(i,j)=KL(p_i^[j]||u^[j]),
 Kstep_(i,j)=KL(p_i^[j]||p_(i+1)^[j]),
 E_(i,j)=sum_(l=j)^(m-1) w_l Q_(i,l)^2 tanh^2 T_(i,l),
 Rsub_(i,j)^2=sum_(l=j)^(m-1)y_(i,l)^2/w_l.

Every symbol uses the SAME actual iterate. Conditioned at depth j,
the subtree gradient has squared norm E_(i,j)/Q_(i,j)^2. Its actual
coordinate update has multiplier h_i Q_(i,j)/Ltree, because global
gradients in that subtree equal Q_(i,j) times conditional gradients.
It follows exactly that

 A_(i+1,j)-A_(i,j)
      =-h_i E_(i,j)/(Ltree Q_(i,j))+Kstep_(i,j),     (5.1)

 Rsub_(i+1,j)^2-Rsub_(i,j)^2
      =-2h_i Q_(i,j)(A_(i,j)+I_(i,j))/Ltree
                         +h_i^2 E_(i,j)/Ltree^2.   (5.2)

For completeness, the finite exponential-family calculation behind
(5.1) is as follows. If A is a conditional log-partition, g=grad A,
and z'=z-vg, then

 KL(p_z||p_z')=<g,z-z'>+A(z')-A(z)
              =v||g||^2+A(z')-A(z).

Here v=h_i Q_(i,j)/Ltree. Also the centered conditional feature mean
at the origin is zero, and

 <z,grad A(z)>=A(z)+KL(p_z||u^[j]).

Expanding ||z-vg||^2 proves (5.2). No limiting, continuous-time, or
independent-child approximation was taken. All probabilities are
strictly positive, so these expressions are finite. Nonnegativity of
each KL follows from -sum_s p_s log(v_s/p_s)>=sum_s(p_s-v_s)=0,
using log z<=z-1.

At the root, these give the exact telescopes

 D_n=D_0-(1/Ltree)sum_(i<n)h_i E_(i,0)
                                  +sum_(i<n)Kstep_(i,0),           (5.3)
 Rsub_(n,0)^2=R^2-(2/Ltree)sum_(i<n)h_i(D_i+I_(i,0))
                                  +(1/Ltree^2)sum_(i<n)h_i^2 E_(i,0). (5.4)

The positive Kstep terms in (5.1) and the squared-step terms in (5.2)
cannot be discarded to claim monotonicity or a terminal upper bound.
The forward conditional entropy has the additional exact decomposition

 I_(i,j)=sum_(l=j)^(m-1)[Q_(i,l)/Q_(i,j)]
          [T_(i,l)tanh T_(i,l)-log cosh T_(i,l)].    (5.5)

Indeed the conditional probability of reaching each spine node is
Q_(i,l)/Q_(i,j), and the divergence of its Bernoulli split from the
equal split is T tanh T-log cosh T. Side subtrees have zero conditional
divergence. This also records explicitly the different weights in
forward entropy (5.5) and reverse entropy (3.5). The same expansion
between consecutive conditional laws gives the further exact formula

 Kstep_(i,j)=sum_(l=j)^(m-1)[Q_(i,l)/Q_(i,j)]
   [log cosh T_(i+1,l)-log cosh T_(i,l)
      -(T_(i+1,l)-T_(i,l))tanh T_(i,l)].             (5.6)

For a single split its log probability is sigma*T-log(2 cosh T),
where sigma=+1 or -1. Taking the old-law expectation of the difference
between the old and new log probabilities gives the bracket in (5.6).
Multiplying by each node's old conditional reach probability and
summing proves the formula. Thus the payments in (5.1) have explicit
values in the same consecutive conditional fields; they are not free
nonnegative slack variables.

## 6. A quantitative mass bound without a concentration assumption

For every time i and depth 1<=j<=m, put u=2^j Q_(i,j) and
Phi(u)=u-1-log u. Then

                  Phi(2^j Q_(i,j))<=2^j D_i.        (6.1)

Split the sum in (3.7) between the subtree and its complement. The
conditional divergences are nonnegative, so D_i is at least the
binary divergence with reference mass p=2^(-j) and current mass Q:

 -p log(Q/p)-(1-p)log[(1-Q)/(1-p)].

Set u=Q/p. The inequality -log(1-z)>=z for z<1, applied to
z=p(u-1)/(1-p), bounds the second term below by p(u-1).
This proves (6.1), including Q<p. Further,

 Phi(u)-(sqrt(u)-1)^2=2[sqrt(u)-1-log sqrt(u)]>=0.

Consequently

 (1-sqrt(2^j D_i))_+^2 <=2^j Q_(i,j)
                         <=(1+sqrt(2^j D_i))^2,     (6.2)
 alpha_(i,j)<=h_i/(Ltree Zgeom c^j)
                         *(1+sqrt(2^j D_i))^2.     (6.3)

These are bounds from the actual entropy at the SAME time; terminal
small entropy alone supplies no bound for earlier clocks. The initial
state (3.3), for example, is very far from uniform at deep levels.

There is also a quantitative endpoint enclosure for (4.1). Define
U_l(D)=arcosh(exp(2^l D)), with arcosh z=log(z+sqrt(z^2-1)) for z>=1.
The nonnegative summands in (3.5) give

 |T_(t,l)|<=U_l(D_t),  0<=A_(t,l+1)/2<=2^l D_t,
 -U_l(D_t)-2^l D_t<=y_(t,l)<=U_l(D_t).

Inserting these into the exact clock formula yields

 Cbase_(t,j)-sum_(l<j)c^(l-j)U_l(D_t)
 <= Cclock_(t,j)
 <= Cbase_(t,j)+sum_(l<j)c^(l-j)[U_l(D_t)+2^l D_t], (6.4)

where Cbase_(t,j)=S_t/(Ltree Zgeom c^j)+t0 sum_(l<j)c^(l-j).
Unlike (6.3), this bounds an entire accumulated clock using the
CURRENT entropy and the elapsed physical step sum. It still does not
control the individual factors in the nonlinear update (3.6).

## 9. Two direct routes and what remains missing

Route A attempted to integrate the changing subtree clocks and use
the endpoint constraints to force residual entropy. Equations (4.1)
and (4.2) succeed exactly. The positive occupations (7.1)--(7.3) show
that their linear endpoint relaxation permits D_n=0. This is a scoped
failure of that relaxation, not evidence that the actual endpoint is
zero. The scalar test in Section7 separately defeats replacing
chronological nonlinear updates by their total effective time.

Route B attempted to obtain the threshold from survival of the deepest
initial field. The conditional decomposition is exact, and (6.1)
supplies a quantitative change-of-measure bound. The finite price in
Section8 shows that persistence alone cannot make the deepest-pair
certificate sufficient. Its amplification alternative needs an actual
conditional overstep and the intermediate entropy required by (8.6).
Neither amplification nor its absence has been established on H_m.
This route does not control the contribution of the other levels.

The remaining inequality for the completely specified trajectory is

 sum_(j=0)^(m-1)2^(m-j)log cosh T_(n,j)
             >= Ltree t0^2 r Zgeom^2 c^m/(2B_(2^m-1))              (9.1)

for every sufficiently large m, or a strict eventual reverse inequality
for this same object. T, A, and Q in (9.1) must be generated by
(3.1)--(3.6), with initial values (3.3) and the original physical word.
A next estimate must control the chronological conditional partition
changes or the inter-iterate conditional KL payments across the large
steps and their retained suffixes. A bound on final mass alone, a
uniform-reset clock, or a deepest-coordinate persistence statement
does not fill this gap. No global or trajectory-specific entropy
monotonicity has been assumed.
