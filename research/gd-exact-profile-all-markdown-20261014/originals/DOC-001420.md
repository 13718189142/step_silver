## 2. Static function, true radius, exact gap, and smoothness

Put A=D/2. The scalar function s_+^2/2 is convex and continuously
differentiable with derivative s_+. Thus

    F(x)=||(A x)_+||^2/2,
    grad F(x)=A^T(Ax)_+=D^T(Dx)_+/4.                    (6)

The elementary bound sum_i(x_i-x_(i+1))^2<=4 sum_i x_i^2 implies ||D||<=2;
the positive-part map is 1-Lipschitz. Formula (6) therefore proves that F is
convex, everywhere finite, continuously differentiable, and 1-smooth.

Its minimum is zero and its entire minimum set is span{1}. Indeed F(x)=0
means x_i<=x_(i+1) around the entire directed cycle, forcing equality everywhere.
Also 1^T grad F(x)=0, so the mean is preserved at every step, including a zero
step. Pairing i with N-1-i shows sum_i v_i=0. The identity cos^2 u=(1+cos 2u)/2
and the telescoping formula
2*sin(pi/N)*cos((2i+1)*pi/N)=sin((2i+2)*pi/N)-sin(2i*pi/N) give

    R^2=||v||^2=N/2.                                    (7)

There is no hidden choice of a more distant minimizer in this denominator.

For later use, the unweighted combinatorial Laplacian of a path on r vertices, divided by4,
has eigenvectors cos(k*pi*(i+1/2)/r), 0<=k<r, and eigenvalues
sin^2(k*pi/(2r)). This follows by substituting the cosine addition formula at
each interior vertex and the one-neighbor formula at each endpoint. Their
pairwise orthogonality follows by expressing products of cosines as sums and
using the finite geometric series; hence these r vectors form a basis.

The strict active edges of F at any x form a disjoint union of paths, since
all edges of a directed cycle cannot have strictly positive differences. If
H_x=(1/4)*sum_(i:(Dx)_i>0) (e_i-e_(i+1))(e_i-e_(i+1))^T, then

    grad F(x)=H_x x, 2F(x)=x^T H_x x.                    (8)

Every path component has at most N vertices. Its largest eigenvalue is at
most cos^2(pi/(2N))=1-mu. Along a segment, (6) is continuous and affine on
finitely many subsegments; on each its derivative is such a path Hessian.
Integrating along the segment proves that grad F is (1-mu)-Lipschitz.
At the strictly decreasing point v all path edges0,...,N-2 are active and the
closing edge is inactive. Perturbing v a sufficiently small amount in the top
path eigenvector keeps this strict sector. The derivative attains norm1-mu
there. Consequently the LEAST smoothness constant is exactly1-mu.

The exact radial energy gap on the mean-zero space is

    min_(x !=0, sum x_i=0) 2F(x)/||x||^2 = mu.           (9)

Here is a direct proof that does not confuse the gap with strong convexity.
Minimize 2F on the compact mean-zero unit sphere. Its minimum rho is strictly
positive because the only zeroes of F are constants. The multiplier equation
and sum grad F=0 give grad F(x)=rho*x at a minimizer: the multiplier on 1 is
zero, and taking the inner product with x uses <grad F(x),x>=2F(x).
By (8), rho is a positive eigenvalue of a path-component Hessian. The path
spectrum above puts every such positive eigenvalue at least mu. Conversely,
v is strictly decreasing and is the lowest positive cosine eigenvector of
the N-vertex path, so grad F(v)=mu*v and 2F(v)=mu*||v||^2.

Thus (9) is exact, but some sector Hessians have large kernels. In particular
no claim of globally mu-strong convexity is made.

A useful elementary upper value bound is

    sum_i ((Dx)_i)_+^2 <=2||Px||^2, hence 2F(x)<=||Px||^2/2. (10)

To prove it, replace x by Px and partition strict active edges into maximal
decreasing runs. On a run with endpoint values b>=e, the sum of the squared
nonnegative drops is at most (b-e)^2<=2(b^2+e^2). Different runs have disjoint
vertex sets, since at least one inactive edge separates them. Summing yields
(10). Isolated vertices add only nonnegative norm to the right side.

The smoothness constant stays near one while the relevant radial gap mu tends
to zero. Already T_t(v)=(1-t*mu)*v for 0<=t<1/mu shows that no strict norm
contraction independent of N can be transported from the triangle family.

## 7. Exact chronology for all depths, including ties and zero steps

For an arbitrary nonnegative step t let

    T_t(x)=x-(t/4)*D^T(Dx)_+,
    E_t(y)=y-(t/4)*D D^T y_+.

Thus in edge coordinates the exact recursion is

    y'_(i)=y_i-(t/4)*(2(y_i)_+-(y_(i-1))_+-(y_(i+1))_+). (23)

It preserves sum y_i=0. The formula uses positive parts at each ACTUAL state;
no simultaneous diagonalization or time-homogeneous linear semigroup is
asserted. At a tie the positive part is zero, and at t=0 the map is the identity;
the query and all interpolation rows remain present.

For fixed m define the maps for whole child words by

    Phi_0=identity, Phi_(j+1)=Phi_j o T_(t_j) o Phi_j,
    Psi_0=identity, Psi_(j+1)=Psi_j o E_(t_j) o Psi_j.    (24)

Composition is read from right to left. These expressions apply the first
child, then its central step, then the second child to the OUTPUT of the first
two pieces. They neither change m nor reset x or y at a boundary.

Consequently the original terminal state and its exact payoff are

    x_n=b_m*Phi_(m-1)(-v),
    Y_m=Psi_(m-1)(-Dv)=D Phi_(m-1)(-v),
    E_m=|| (Y_m)_+ ||^2,
    V_m=b_m^2*E_m/(2N).                                (25)

Here E_m denotes a scalar terminal edge energy, not the one-step map E_t.
Equation (25) is an exact all-depth formula for the actual trajectory. Merely
writing it down is not the missing terminal estimate.

One actual but nondecisive upper bound follows directly from (23). Put
M(y)=sum_i(y_i)_+ and

    P_m=product_(j=2)^(m-2) (t_j/2)^(2^(m-2-j)).

For0<=t<=2, (23) is a sum of nonnegative multiples of neighboring positive

This upper bound allows enormous growth. It proves neither a useful family

## 10. Two direct attempts and concrete shortcut failures

Attempt A: extend the scalar low-eigenvalue product through the right child.
This fails at its first gradient query. Formula (17) changes the active path
to the single closing edge; grad F(-v) is supported at two endpoints and is
not -mu*v. Equations (19)--(22) further show actual overlap and noncommutation.
Thus the continued polynomial product is not the selected function's GD.

Even replacing the product by a sum-of-steps diffusion law loses actual order.
Starting at X=-v, either first step t_0 or t_1 yields exactly the same strict
three-edge sector in (22). Both lie below2 and exceed8mu. Writing s=t_0,t=t_1,
the two two-step compositions have difference

    T_t T_s X - T_s T_t X=(t-s)*(H^(0)-H^(1))*X !=0.     (32)

Indeed expand (I-tH^(1))(I-sH^(0))X and the reversed expression; the st term
cancels. The two omitted edge differences of X both equal -4mu*z, so
(H^(0)-H^(1))X is nonzero; t_1>t_0. This comparison refutes an order-free
shortcut on the same static function. It is not a second proposed run or a
change to the specified word.

Attempt B: use the exact radial gap and small-step dissipation to retain the
post-central energy. Equation (23) has nonnegative positive-part coefficients
only when t<=2. The actual level2 step is t_2=1+lambda*(1-1/m^2)>3, and later
levels are larger. The positive-mass argument yields only the expanding
factor t/2 in (26); it cannot give the needed lower bound on J_m.

The absence of a lower norm bound from the small radial gap is concrete.
On this SAME directed cycle consider the mean-zero vector w=e_(N-1)-e_0.
Only its closing edge is positive, so grad F(w)=w/2. The actual level1 step
t_1=2-1/m therefore gives

    T_(t_1)(w)=w/(2m), F(T_(t_1)w)=F(w)/(4m^2).         (33)

Meanwhile the graph's exact gap mu tends to zero exponentially. Thus gap
information alone cannot prevent strong damping of a possible edge-localized
component. This test is a counterexample to the generic proposed inference,
not an assertion that the specified suffix equals w.

The telescoping identity also keeps a signed overstep contribution:

    ||X_(k+1)||^2-||X_k||^2
      =-4h_k F(X_k)+h_k^2||grad F(X_k)||^2.             (34)

For steps above2 the smoothness bound does not turn (34) into a usable
lower bound on terminal energy. Continuous-time diffusion results or a
stable explicit discretization cannot be substituted for these large steps.

No counterexample to the actual statement liminf J_m>0 has been obtained.
Attempts A and B fail as proof routes; the selected family remains open.
