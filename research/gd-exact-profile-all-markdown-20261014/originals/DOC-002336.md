## 2. An exact lower bound on the exponent

The identity lambda^3=7+5sqrt(2) and the strict bound sqrt(2)>7/5 give
lambda^3>14. Therefore

    lambda^15 > 14^5 = 537824 > 524288 = 2^19.

Since lambda=2^rho and logarithm is increasing,

    rho>19/15>1,   rho/2>19/30.                     (2.1)

These are exact integer and radical comparisons.

## 3. Primitive capacity power inequality

For t>0 define E(t)=(1+t)^rho-1 and q(z)=log E(exp(z)), where log is the
natural logarithm. With t=exp(z), direct differentiation gives

    q''(z)=rho*t*(1+t)^(rho-2)
       *[(1+t)^rho-1-rho*t]/[(1+t)^rho-1]^2 >=0.     (3.1)

The bracket vanishes at t=0 and its derivative for t>0 is
rho[(1+t)^(rho-1)-1]>0, because rho>1. Thus q is convex on the whole
real line. Applying convexity at z and -z yields

    E(t)E(1/t) >= E(1)^2
                 =(2^rho-1)^2=(lambda-1)^2=2.      (3.2)

For x,y>0 put A=x^(1/rho), F=y^(1/rho), and T=(A+F)^rho. Then

    T-x=x E(F/A),   T-y=y E(A/F).

By (3.2), (T-x)(T-y)>=2xy, equivalently

    T^2-(x+y)T-xy>=0.

T is positive, so it lies at least as high as the positive root of this
quadratic. That root is Omega(x,y). By continuity the same inequality
holds if x or y is zero. We have proved

    Omega(x,y) <= (x^(1/rho)+y^(1/rho))^rho
                                          for all x,y>=0. (3.3)

Omega is nondecreasing in both nonnegative arguments, directly from its
defining formula. The n=0 primitive claim in (1.1) is equality. If the
claim holds below n, each original split k+(n-k-1)=n-1 gives

    Omega(C_k,C_(n-k-1))
       <= Omega((k+1)^rho,(n-k)^rho)
       <= (n+1)^rho.

Taking the original maximum proves C_n<=(n+1)^rho for ALL n. No integer
split has been removed.

## 4. A sharper scalar power inequality

We prove

    (5/6)x^(2p)+(1-x)^p <=1
              for every x in[0,1] and p>=19/30.      (4.1)

For fixed x in[0,1], increasing p only decreases both summands. It
suffices to study the continuous function

    psi(x)=(5/6)x^(19/15)+(1-x)^(19/30).

Its endpoint values are psi(0)=1 and psi(1)=5/6. Every interior critical
point satisfies

    x^(4/15)(1-x)^(11/30)=3/5,
    x^8(1-x)^11=(3/5)^30.                           (4.2)

The function x^8(1-x)^11 is strictly decreasing for x>8/19: its
logarithmic derivative there is (8-19x)/(x(1-x)). For x>=2/3, therefore,

    x^8(1-x)^11 <= 2^8/3^19 < (3/5)^30.             (4.3)

The last strict inequality is exactly the integer comparison

    2^8*5^30 = 238418579101562500000000
           < 239299329230617529590083 = 3^49.

Thus EVERY interior critical point of psi lies below2/3.

At such a point the derivative equation in (4.2) gives

    psi(x)=(1-x/2)/(1-x)^(11/30)=:g(x).

Direct differentiation gives

    g'(x)/g(x)=(19x-8)/[30(1-x)(2-x)].               (4.4)

Consequently g decreases up to8/19 and then increases. Its maximum on
[0,2/3] is attained at an endpoint. At the endpoints,

    g(0)=1,
    g(2/3)^30=2^30/3^19<1,

where 2^30=1073741824<1162261467=3^19. Hence psi<=1 at every interior
critical point, and also at its two endpoints. The maximum of this
continuous function on[0,1] is attained; if interior it is a critical
point. This proves (4.1), including both boundary values.

## 5. Dominant capacity bound at every integer split

Set kappa=12/5. The formula for G gives

    sqrt(G(x,y))=(sqrt(y)+sqrt(y+8x))/2.

This is the positive root of z^2-sqrt(y)z-2x=0. G is nondecreasing in
both nonnegative arguments. The n=0 claim B_0<=kappa is immediate.
For any split at n>=1 put A=k+1, F=n-k and N=A+F=n+1. The induction
hypothesis and Section3 imply

    G(C_k,B_(n-k-1)) <= G(A^rho,kappa F^rho).

Let z=sqrt(kappa)*N^(rho/2). To place z at least at the positive root,
it is sufficient and necessary that

    z^2-sqrt(kappa F^rho)z-2A^rho >=0.

Dividing by kappa N^rho makes this inequality

    (2/kappa)(A/N)^rho+(F/N)^(rho/2)<=1.

It is (4.1) with x=A/N, p=rho/2>19/30 and 2/kappa=5/6. Thus EVERY
original split has value at most kappa(n+1)^rho. Taking the original
maximum proves the second statement of (1.1) for every integer n>=0.
This proof uses no numerical recurrence evaluation or saved endpoint.

## 6. The same-enclosing-m hierarchy and its exact total mass

Use exactly the parameters in Section1, with m>=1536. The physical
lengths are n_j=2^j-1. Put A_j=1+S_j. Summing the literal concatenation

    A_(j+1)=2A_j+a*c^j.

    A_j=(1-v)2^j+v*c^j.                              (6.1)

Since c>lambda>2 and lambda(lambda-2)=1, we have0<v<1. In particular
A_m>=v*c^m, and D_m=2A_m-1.

For the required elementary lower bound on v, write

    c(c-2)=1+2(lambda+1)epsilon+lambda^2 epsilon^2.

The inequalities lambda<5/2 and lambda^2=2lambda+1<6 imply

    v > (1-epsilon)/(1+7epsilon+6epsilon^2)>1-8epsilon.

For the second comparison, epsilon<1/8 and

    (1-8epsilon)(1+7epsilon+6epsilon^2)
       =1-epsilon-50epsilon^2-48epsilon^3<1-epsilon.

Thus v>1-2/m. The binomial expansion at integer m>=2 gives

    (1+1/(4m))^m
       >=1+1/4+(m-1)/(32m)=41/32-1/(32m).           (6.2)

Also lambda^m>2^m>m, so lambda^(-m)<1/m. Combining these inequalities,

    D_m/lambda^m

Division by the positive lower bound (6.3) proves
