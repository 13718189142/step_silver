# Exact global three-step primitive capacity, including arbitrary terminal credit

Status: complete self-contained candidate, NOT verifier-accepted. No
solver or fact_submit was used. The two polynomial identities and their
explicit coefficient sign proofs are included in full; no numerical
calculation or computational artifact is a proof premise.

The public max4 candidates 5b4359baa88bf3e4 and 500d209094028aef suggested
the mixed witness. Every ingredient used here is proved explicitly;
those unverified findings and the earlier max3 C32 are not premises.
The sole accepted predecessor is smooth-convex interpolation fact
1252b8823bb5a290.

## 1. Theorem and scope

For any nonnegative three-step schedule h=(u,v,w), put T=1+u+v+w.
For a legal convex differentiable 1-smooth GD run define

    V_h=2sum_(i<3)h_i(f_i-f_3)
        +sum_(i<3)h_i(1-h_i)||g_i||^2
        -(T-1)T||g_3||^2.

Call h primitive if V_h>=0 for every such finite-dimensional run with
a minimizer. Put t=sqrt(2), lambda=1+t and T_0=lambda^2=3+2t.

Then every primitive h satisfies

    T<=T_0.

Equality holds only for h=(t,2,t), and an explicit universally valid
interpolation certificate below proves attainment.

More strongly, if T>=T_0 and h!=(t,2,t), there is a legal finite-dimensional
run with V_h<0 and ALL Q_(i,3)=0, i=0,1,2. Consequently replacing V_h
by V_h minus ANY real linear combination of those three incoming-terminal
interpolation residuals cannot permit a larger capacity, or another
equality schedule. This is a necessity statement for arbitrary terminal
credit, not a sufficiency assertion for negative credit at Silver.

This proves the exact capacity at length three only. It does not bound
all-length primitive capacities and does not resolve the unrestricted
asymptotic normalized-objective target.

Sections2--4 first reduce any possible exception to

    t<u<7/4, 1<v<2, 1<w<t, T_0<=T<7,
    u+w>=2T/lambda-2.                              (R)

Section5 eliminates this entire remaining region.

## 2. Actual witnesses and all their legality conditions

For any legal run use

    Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2<=0,
    V_h=2sum_(i<3)h_i(f_i-f_3)
         +sum_(i<3)h_i(1-h_i)||g_i||^2
         -(T-1)T||g_3||^2.

For data with f_i=<g_i,x_i>-||g_i||^2/2 and x_3=g_3, set
y_i=x_i-g_i. Expansion gives

    Q_ij=<g_i-g_j,y_j>, Q_(i,3)=0,
    V_h=||x_0||^2-T^2||g_3||^2.                   (2)

All witnesses used below have these properties and include the legal
optimum jet (0,0,0). Besides the finite Q conditions, optimum legality
requires <g_i,y_i>>=0; the other direction is saturated by the value
formula. A feasible finite array is realized by the conjugate maximum
of quadratics in accepted 1252b8823bb5a290. Thus the arrays specify
fixed globally smooth convex functions with a minimizer.

The interval-projection witness at any selected step b, with ordinary
sums P,Q, has gradient 1 before b and -(b-1)/(Q+1) after it, starting
at P+1 and ending at its negative threshold, when b>1. Formula (2)
gives the necessary cut condition

    T(b-1)_+<=(P+1)(Q+1).                         (3)

This is a direct fixed clipped-derivative construction, including zero
ordinary steps. Apply it at the first and last step to obtain

    u,w<=M(T):=2T/(T+1)<2.                        (4)

For u,v,w>1 abbreviate

    a=u-1, A=u+1, r=v-1, D=w+1, eta=r/D;
    T=A+D+r.

The full-curvature quadratic f(x)=x^2/2, x_0=1, gives

    F_Q=1-T^2 a^2 r^2(w-1)^2>=0.                  (5)

The middle-cut interval, with x=(A,1,-r,-eta) and
g=(1,1,-eta,-eta), gives

    F_B=A^2-T^2 eta^2>=0, or Tr<=AD.              (6)

If r(w-1)<=1, the one-sided Huber derivative min(x,1), started at A,
has x=(A,1,-r,r(w-1)) and g=(1,1,-r,r(w-1)). It gives

    F_C=A^2-T^2 r^2(w-1)^2>=0.                   (7)

When eta a<1, a two-dimensional mixed witness gives

    F_N=a u F_B+(1+eta)u^2(2-u^2)>=0.             (8)

Here is its complete verification. Combine the middle-cut base array
from (6) with the auxiliary scalar array

    x_aux=(1,-a,ra,eta a),
    g_aux=(1,-a,eta a,eta a),
    f_aux,i=x_aux,i*g_aux,i-g_aux,i^2/2.

The auxiliary proximal points are (0,0,w eta a,0). Its only positive
finite residual is Q_aux,(0,2)=(1-eta a)w eta a. Every optimum
inequality is legal because g_aux,i*y_aux,i>=0. The base proximal
points are (u,0,-w eta,0); its Q_(0,2)=-(1+eta)w eta and all its
residuals are nonpositive. With

    epsilon=(1+eta)/[a(1-eta a)],

take the orthogonal sum of the base and sqrt(epsilon) times the
auxiliary points and gradients, with the summed values. Residuals add;
the sole positive auxiliary edge is exactly canceled and every other
edge is nonpositive. The energy equals

    F_B+epsilon(1-T^2 eta^2 a^2)
       =F_N/[a(1-eta a)].                         (9)

For the algebra use 1-a^2 A^2=u^2(2-u^2). This proves (8) with
all actual edges, rather than an assumed two-dimensional interpolant.

## 3. A uniform box where all four witnesses apply

Assume T>=T_0 and all the cut conditions (3). First v>1. Otherwise
T=1+u+v+w<=2+2M(T), so T^2-5T-2<=0. At T=T_0 this polynomial
equals 2t>0 and it increases thereafter, a contradiction.

Thus r>0 and the middle cut (6) applies. As T=A+D+r,

    T(T-A-D)<=AD<=(A+D)^2/4,
    T<=lambda(A+D)/2<=lambda(M(T)+1).

Consequently

    T^2+(1-3lambda)T-lambda<=0.                    (10)

Its left side increases for T>=T_0, and at T=7 equals
56-22lambda>0 because lambda<5/2. Hence T<7 and (4) gives
u,w<7/4.

Both u and w exceed1. If, for example, u<=1, then A<=2, D<11/4,
so r=T-A-D>T-19/4. But

    Tr>T(T-19/4)>=T_0(T_0-19/4)
       =11/4+5t/2>11/2>AD,

contradicting the middle cut. The argument for w is identical.

On this entire necessary box the extra hypotheses of (7) and (8) hold:

    r(w-1)<=A(w^2-1)/T<363/(64T_0)<1,
    eta a<=Aa/T=(u^2-1)/T<33/(16T_0)<1.           (11)

For the first strict numerical comparison, 363<64T_0 is equivalent
to 171<128sqrt(2), and squaring gives 29241<32768. Thus no local
neighborhood restriction is being silently used in this global screen.
The earlier inequality T<=lambda(A+D)/2 also gives

    u+w>=2T/lambda-2>=2t.                         (12)

## 4. Excluding three of the four endpoint orientations

Suppose u<=t. By (12), w>=t. Since T>=T_0 and w<2, the difference
between the two sides of the necessary inequality Tr(w-1)<=A is
at least

    T_0(T_0-t-w-2)(w-1)-(t+1)
       =T_0(w-t)(2-w)>=0.                         (13)

It is strictly positive unless u=w=t and T=T_0. In that exceptional
case v=2. Hence every other candidate must have u>t.

Suppose next that also w>=t. On the rectangle [t,M(T)]^2 consider

    q_T(u,w)=T(u-1)(w-1)(T-u-w-2).

This function is strictly concave in either endpoint with the other
fixed. Its minimum is attained at a corner. At T=T_0, M(T_0)=1+t/2,
the corner values are

    q_(T_0)(t,t)=1,
    q_(T_0)(t,M(T_0))=q_(T_0)(M(T_0),t)=lambda/2>1,
    q_(T_0)(M(T_0),M(T_0))=lambda/2>1.

Each corner value strictly increases with T>=T_0. All its positive
factors increase; in particular the derivatives of
T-t-M(T)-2 and T-2M(T)-2 are respectively
1-2/(T+1)^2 and 1-4/(T+1)^2, both positive.
Thus q_T>1 except at the single point T=T_0,u=w=t. This contradicts
the quadratic condition (5). Every nonexceptional candidate therefore
satisfies u>t>w.

Finally its middle step is less than2. For u>t the expression F_N in
(8), with u,w held fixed, strictly decreases as r>0 increases: the
negative squared term in F_B decreases, while u^2(2-u^2)<0.
At r=1 a direct expansion gives

    D^2 F_N/u
       =P_u(D):=u^2 D^2+(4-2u^2-u^3)D-(u-1)(u+2)^2.

This polynomial has one positive and one negative root, since its
leading coefficient is positive and its constant term negative. Also

    P_u(lambda)=-(lambda+1)u^3-2u^2+4(lambda+1)<0

for u>t: the right side vanishes at t and has strictly negative
derivative for u>0. Its positive root is therefore beyond lambda,
and P_u(D)<0 for 0<D<=lambda. Since w<t gives D<lambda, F_N<0
at r=1 and at every r>=1. Necessarily r<1, proving the preliminary reduction (R).

The remaining constraints can be written without a mixed-energy
abbreviation. In the region (R), a primitive candidate must satisfy

    Tr<=AD,
    T a r(w-1)<=1,
    u^2+u-1+u(2-u^2)eta-(u-1)T^2 eta^2>=0.        (14)

The last expression is F_N/u after expansion. Section5 proves these
necessary inequalities are incompatible with the remaining endpoint cut
throughout (R).



## 5. Exact elimination of the remaining asymmetric region

Assume (R), the endpoint cut u<=2T/(T+1), and F_N,F_Q>=0.
Continue to write a=u-1, A=u+1, D=w+1, and put K=T-A.
Thus r=K-D and eta=(K-D)/D.

For fixed u,D in (R), F_N strictly decreases with T>=T_0:
its negative term -a u T^2 eta^2 strictly decreases, and its other
T-dependent term has coefficient u^2(2-u^2)<0.
The quantity

    T a (T-A-D)(D-2)

strictly increases with T there. The endpoint cut says
T>=u/(2-u). Therefore any putative feasible T induces feasible necessary
inequalities at the smaller mass

    T_min=max{T_0,u/(2-u)}.                        (15)

Here and below D is unchanged. At this smaller mass K-D>0 still holds,
because T_min>=T_0, u<7/4, D<lambda, and
T_0-11/4-lambda=t-3/4>0.

For the remainder of this section set T=T_min and K=T-A. Define

    B=u^2+u-1,
    N(D)=a(A^2-T^2)D^2
          +K[2aT^2+u(2-u^2)]D-aT^2K^2,
    Q(D)=T a(K-D)(D-2)-1.                         (16)

Thus N(D)=D^2 F_N/u>=0 and Q(D)<=0. Let

    kappa=(T^2-A^2)/T>0,
    ell=aT^2(K-2)+KB+2aA^2>0,
    c_0=-aK[T^2(K-2)+2A^2]+(T^2-A^2)/T.

The positivity of ell follows from K>2, a>0, B>0. Subtraction of the
quadratic coefficients gives the exact identity

    N(D)-kappa Q(D)=ell D+c_0.                    (17)

Hence D>=D_L:=-c_0/ell. Moreover Q is strictly increasing for every
D<=lambda, since

    Q'(D)=T a(K+2-2D),
    K+2-2lambda>=T_0-u+1-2lambda=2-u>0.

It is therefore enough to prove Q(D_L)>0: then D>=D_L and D<lambda
would imply Q(D)>0, a contradiction.

The switch in (15) is exactly at

    M_0=2T_0/(T_0+1)=1+t/2.

### 5.1 First interval: t<u<=M_0, T=T_0

Clearing the positive denominator ell^2 gives

    ell^2 Q(D_L)
       =-T_0 a c_0^2-T_0 a(K+2)c_0 ell
          -(2T_0 a K+1)ell^2
       =(u-t) P_8(u).                             (18)

Here P_8 is the following degree-eight polynomial, with coefficients
listed in ascending order of powers of u:

    13696+9680t,
    -44720-31632t,
    42848+30300t,
    -4028-2832t,
    -13912-9832t,
    7640+5396t,

Identity (18) follows by substitution of ell,c_0 and t^2=2; all
quantities in it have been specified explicitly.

For 0<=z<=1 put u=t+(1-t/2)z. Write
B_(d,k)(z)=binomial(d,k) z^k(1-z)^(d-k). Direct expansion gives

    P_8(t+(1-t/2)z)=sum_(k=0)^8 beta_k B_(8,k)(z),

    beta_0=768+544t,
    beta_1=762+539t,
    beta_2=(5144+3632t)/7,
    beta_3=9573/14+13539t/28,

As u>t, equation (18) proves Q(D_L)>0 on this first interval.

### 5.2 Second interval: M_0<=u<7/4, T=u/(2-u)

Put p=u, q=2-u>0, and K_num=p-Aq. Define the explicit polynomials

    ell_num=a p^2(K_num-2q)+K_num B q^2+2aA^2 q^3,
    c_num=-a p K_num[p^2(K_num-2q)+2A^2 q^3]
            +(p^2-A^2 q^2)q^3,
    X=-c_num, Y=p q ell_num.

These are just cleared versions of the same ell and c_0:

    ell=ell_num/q^3,
    c_0=c_num/(p q^4),
    D_L=X/Y, Y>0.

Substitution into Q gives the second exact identity

    q^2 Y^2 Q(D_L)
      =p a(K_num Y-qX)(X-2Y)-q^2 Y^2
      =u(u-2)^3(u^2-2)^2 P_13(u),                 (19)

where

       +248u^7+480u^6-160u^5-656u^4+256u^3

All denominator factors are positive; (19) is an ordinary polynomial
identity, checkable by multiplying the displayed polynomials.

First,

    P_13(M_0)=(-1807+1277t)/64<0,

    29229255 P_13'(5/3+z/3)

    -822569770,

    -40950087840,
    -56318574240,
    -76541675760,
    -102886977600.

Every b_k is strictly negative, and 29229255>0. Thus P_13 decreases
strictly on [5/3,2], which contains [M_0,7/4]. The asserted negative
sign follows from its value at M_0.

In (19), u>0, (u-2)^3<0, (u^2-2)^2>0, and P_13(u)<0. Therefore
Q(D_L)>0 on the whole second interval too.

Both alternatives in (15) now contradict (16)--(17). This eliminates
(R), so the only possible primitive schedule with T>=T_0 is Silver.

## 6. Attainment and the signed-terminal extension

At h=(t,2,t), the following nonnegative multipliers, with every
unlisted multiplier zero, give an exact identity:

    lambda_01=2,
    lambda_10=2lambda,
    lambda_21=2t lambda,
    lambda_31=lambda_13=2lambda,
    lambda_23=2lambda^2+2t lambda,
    lambda_32=2lambda^3,

    V_h+sum_(i!=j) lambda_ij Q_ij=0.               (20)

Substitute x_(i+1)=x_i-h_i g_i and t^2=2 to verify (20) by matching
each function coefficient and each gradient inner product. For example,
the finite value divergences are (-2t,-4,-2t,4+4t), exactly canceling
the four coefficients of V_h. The remaining gradient coefficients all
vanish. Since Q_ij<=0 and every multiplier is nonnegative, (20) proves
V_h>=0 in every finite dimension, establishing attainment.

If T>=T_0 and h is not Silver, the preceding contradiction shows that
at least one of the explicitly constructed cut, quadratic, one-sided
Huber, or two-dimensional mixed witnesses has strictly negative energy.
All those witnesses have x_3=g_3 and the saturated value formula, so
Q_(i,3)=0 for every i<3. Thus for any real numbers beta_0,beta_1,beta_2,
that same witness satisfies

    V_h-sum_(i<3) beta_i Q_(i,3)=V_h<0.

It also rules out a full PSD intrinsic identity whose only possibly
negative interpolation coefficients are on these incoming-terminal
edges: the signed edges vanish, while every other nonnegative
coefficient multiplies a nonpositive Q. This proves the stronger
necessity statement without a dual-attainment assumption.

## 7. What remains open

The theorem gives exactly

    max_{primitive h of length3} (1+sum h)=(1+sqrt(2))^2,

with a unique maximizer, and the same necessary capacity/equality
restriction under arbitrary incoming-terminal credit. It does not
give the required all-length capacity induction, arbitrary-tail
certificate re-selection, or a constant-preserving connection from
primitive energy to the original normalized objective problem.
