## 3. The actual function and the complete incidence identity

Fix N>=3 and let T_N be all three-element subsets of {0,...,N-1}.
For each tau choose one of its two cyclic orientations, independently of
the trajectory, and let E_tau be the resulting three directed edges.
A concrete choice for tau={u<v<w} is u->v, v->w, w->u.
Repeated edges in different triangles remain separately indexed.
Put

 w_tri=3/[N(N-2)],
 E_N={x in R^N: sum_u x_u=0},
 P_N=I-11^T/N,
 F_N(x)=(w_tri/6)*sum_(tau in T_N) sum_(u->v in E_tau)
                          (x_u-x_v)_+^2.                (3)

This finite formula is fixed at every physical query. No set or coefficient
is selected in response to a gradient. Write P_tau for the orthogonal
projection onto the vectors supported on tau whose three coordinates sum
to zero. The sum of the three unoriented edge outer products on tau is
3P_tau. Every unordered edge occurs in N-2 triangles, while
sum_(u<v)(e_u-e_v)(e_u-e_v)^T=N P_N: its diagonal entries are N-1 and
its off-diagonal entries are -1. Consequently

 sum_tau P_tau = N(N-2)P_N/3,
 sum_tau w_tri P_tau=P_N.                                (4)

Let A_tri have one row sqrt(w_tri/3)*(e_u-e_v)^T for every separately
indexed directed edge u->v in every triangle. Then

 A_tri^T A_tri=P_N,
 F_N(x)=|| (A_tri x)_+ ||^2/2,
 grad F_N(x)=A_tri^T (A_tri x)_+.                         (5)

In particular A_tri is an isometry on E_N and has operator norm1 on R^N.
Its range projection is Pi_tri=A_tri A_tri^T: indeed Pi_tri is symmetric,
and Pi_tri^2=A_tri P_N A_tri^T=Pi_tri.

Each scalar function t_+^2/2 is convex and continuously differentiable,
with derivative t_+. Thus F_N is convex and differentiable. Since the
componentwise positive-part map is 1-Lipschitz, (5) gives

 ||grad F_N(x)-grad F_N(y)|| <= ||x-y||.

Hence F_N is an everywhere-defined convex 1-smooth function. No assertion
that this smoothness upper bound is the least possible constant for every
orientation and N is needed or made. The normalization in (3) is fixed.

## 4. Every directed row, the minimum, physical GD and true distance

For arbitrary real t,u direct consideration of their four sign pairs gives

 u_+^2/2-t_+^2/2-t_+*(u-t)
       =(u_+-t_+)^2/2+t_+*(-u)_+.                        (6)

For x,y put r=(A_tri x)_+, s=(A_tri y)_+, d=r-s. Summing (6), and using
grad F_N(x)=A_tri^T r, gives

 F_N(y)-F_N(x)-<grad F_N(x),y-x>
       =||d||^2/2+sum_e r_e*(-(A_tri y)_e)_+.

Because Pi_tri is the range projection,
||A_tri^T d||^2=||Pi_tri d||^2 and
||d||^2=||Pi_tri d||^2+||(I-Pi_tri)d||^2. Therefore the exact residual is

 Q(x,y):=F_N(x)-F_N(y)+<grad F_N(x),y-x>
                       +||grad F_N(x)-grad F_N(y)||^2/2
 =-sum_e (A_tri x)_+,e*(-(A_tri y)_e)_+
  -||(I-Pi_tri)((A_tri x)_+-(A_tri y)_+)||^2/2 <=0.        (8)

The orientation here agrees exactly with (2). It holds for EVERY x,y,
so all ordered original trajectory pairs and every nonlocal cycle pass.
It includes equal points at zero steps and both directions involving any
minimizer. No finite collection of adjacent checks is substituted for (8).

Clearly F_N>=0. Equality forces every directed difference in every triangle
to be nonpositive. Around a directed triangle the three differences sum
to zero, so all three must be zero. Every two vertices occur in some
triangle; all coordinates are therefore equal. Conversely constant
vectors give value0. Hence

 argmin F_N=span{1}, min F_N=0,
 x_star=(sum_u x_0,u/N)*1,
 R_tri=dist(x_0,argmin F_N)=||P_N x_0||.                  (9)

We always take x_0 outside the minimum set, so R_tri>0. The mean coordinate
is unchanged by GD because every edge vector has coordinate sum0.

For ANY finite nonnegative word h=(h_0,...,h_(n-1)), including zero steps,

## 6. Exact triangle radial bounds and its sharp one-step norm factor

First work on one triangle's mean-zero plane, with
f_triangle(z)=[(z_0-z_1)_+^2+(z_1-z_2)_+^2+(z_2-z_0)_+^2]/6.
For an oriented triangle tau, f_tau denotes this same sum-of-three-edges
formula on the plane range(P_tau), with that triangle's vertex labels.
Reversing the cyclic orientation is obtained by replacing z by -z,
so the following bounds hold for either orientation.

There are two sector types, with their boundaries allowed. In a two-positive
sector, after a cyclic relabelling, let the positive successive gaps be
u=z_0-z_1>=0 and v=z_1-z_2>=0. The centered coordinates give

 ||z||^2=2(u^2+uv+v^2)/3,
 2f_triangle(z)=(u^2+v^2)/3.

In a one-positive sector put p=z_0-z_1>0 and let
s=z_2-z_1 in [0,p], so the other differences are -s and -(p-s). Then

 ||z||^2=2(p^2-ps+s^2)/3,
 2f_triangle(z)=p^2/3.

It follows in both cases, including z=0, that

 ||z||^2/3 <= 2f_triangle(z) <= 2||z||^2/3.               (18)

For a nonnegative step t define the piecewise scalar

 q_tri(t)=1-t/3,                         0<=t<=3/2;
          sqrt(1-t+t^2/3),               3/2<=t<=3;
          2t/3-1,                       t>=3.          (19)

We claim that its value is EXACTLY the maximum of
||z-t grad f_triangle(z)||/||z|| over nonzero triangle-plane vectors z.

In a two-positive sector the Hessian is the path Laplacian divided by3.
Its eigenvectors (1,0,-1) and (1,-2,1) on the centered plane have
eigenvalues1/3 and1 respectively. Writing b=(u-v)/(u+v) in [-1,1]
gives the exact squared ratio

 [3(1-t/3)^2+b^2(1-t)^2]/(3+b^2).                       (20)

Its maximum is (1-t/3)^2 for t<=3/2, because
(1-t)^2-(1-t/3)^2=4t(2t-3)/9<=0.
For t>=3/2 its maximum is the value at b^2=1,
[(1-t)^2+3(1-t/3)^2]/4=1-t+t^2/3.

In a one-positive sector the Hessian has eigenvalue2/3 in the edge
direction (1,-1,0) and eigenvalue0 in the other centered direction.
Writing b=(p-2s)/p in [-1,1] gives the exact squared ratio

 [3(1-2t/3)^2+b^2]/(3+b^2).                             (21)

Its maximum is 1-t+t^2/3 for 0<=t<=3, and (1-2t/3)^2 for t>=3.
Comparing the maxima of (20),(21), using
(1-t/3)^2-(1-t+t^2/3)=t(3-2t)/9 and
(1-2t/3)^2-(1-t+t^2/3)=t(t-3)/9, proves (19).
Each maximum is attained at b=0 or |b|=1 in its stated sector, so the
factor is sharp for one triangle. This is an exact geometric maximization
on paper, not a numerical evaluation or scan.

## 7. Why overlaps and arbitrary dimension do not escape the factor

Restrict to E_N and let the linear map J_tri send x to the tuple
(sqrt(w_tri)*P_tau x)_tau, in the orthogonal direct sum of triangle planes.
By (4) it is an isometry. Its adjoint therefore has norm1.
The triangle gradient lies in its own triangle plane, and (3) gives

 grad F_N(x)=sum_tau w_tri grad f_tau(P_tau x).

For every nonnegative t, including large oversteps, (4) therefore gives

 x-t grad F_N(x)
   =J_tri^* [ (sqrt(w_tri)*(P_tau x-t grad f_tau(P_tau x)))_tau ].

Taking squared norms, using the norm of J_tri^*, and applying (19)
SEPARATELY to each current triangle state gives

 ||x-t grad F_N(x)||^2
   <=sum_tau w_tri ||P_tau x-t grad f_tau(P_tau x)||^2
   <=q_tri(t)^2*sum_tau w_tri ||P_tau x||^2
   =q_tri(t)^2*||x||^2.                                (22)

The triangle states need not be independent and are not evolved separately.
They all come from the SAME x. Their overlaps are exactly what (4) and the
isometry of J_tri account for. The update on the left is the original
coupled update, not independent triangle GD followed by a reset.

Likewise summing (18) and using (4) yields

 ||x||^2/3 <= 2F_N(x) <= 2||x||^2/3, x in E_N.           (23)

For a general start replace x_i by x_i-x_star, with its actual constant
mean x_star from (9). Mean invariance and (22),(23) give the explicit
whole-word bound, for EVERY h>=0,

 0<=V_tri(h,x_0)<=(2/3)*product_(i<n) q_tri(h_i)^2.        (24)

This proof applies verbatim to any finite positive triangle weighting on
a Euclidean working subspace for which sum_tau w_tau P_tau=I on that
subspace, with each triangle plane contained in the subspace. Thus arbitrary
orientations and all other such exact identity decompositions are covered.
No result is asserted after changing this normalization or its hypotheses.
