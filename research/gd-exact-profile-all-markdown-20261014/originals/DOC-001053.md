## 1. Exact operator form on the original formal space

Fix n>=1, N=n+1, h=(h_0,...,h_(n-1)), and normalize L=1 and the
optimum sample to x_star=g_star=f_star=0. The N+1 vectors
x_0,g_0,...,g_n remain independent. Put

    x_i=x_0-sum_(t<i) h_t g_t,
    Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2.

For actual smooth-convex samples Q_ij<=0, by fact1252b8823bb5a290.
Let lambda_ij>=0 denote the coefficient of Q_ij. A full upper certificate
at level q has all finite value coefficients zero and is the PSD form

    M(q)=q||x_0||^2-2f_n+sum_(i!=j)lambda_ij Q_ij.       (1)

Define the strictly lower triangular N by N matrix T by
T_it=h_t for t<i and T_it=0 otherwise, and put V=I+T. For a real
matrix B, Sym(B)=(B+B^T)/2. Let e_n be the last coordinate vector
and 1 the vector with every coordinate equal to1.

Here is an exact operator parameterization useful for specifying structured
arrays. Choose an N by N real matrix A satisfying

    A_ij<=0 for i!=j,
    b=A1>=0,
    a=A^T1-2e_n>=0,                                  (2)

where inequalities on vectors are coordinatewise. Set

    lambda_ij=-A_ij  (finite distinct i,j),
    lambda_(i,star)=b_i,  lambda_(star,i)=a_i.          (3)

All actual coefficients are nonnegative. With r_i=sum_(j!=i)lambda_ij,
c_i=sum_(j!=i)lambda_ji, one has A_ii=r_i+b_i and
a_i=r_i+b_i-c_i-2*1_(i=n). Thus the value coefficient at i in (1) is
r_i+b_i-c_i-a_i-2*1_(i=n)=0, including at the terminal.

Expanding every quadratic term in (1) gives the complete matrix

    M(q) = [x_0;g]^T [[q, -b^T/2],[-b/2, G]] [x_0;g],
    G=Sym(A V)-e_n e_n^T.                            (4)

The notation means sums of Euclidean inner products when the formal vectors
have dimension greater than1. To check (4), write K_ij=lambda_ij for i!=j
and K_ii=0. The finite position terms equal
g^T[diag(r)-K]Tg. The star position terms equal
-<x_0,sum b_i g_i>+g^T diag(b)Tg. The squared-gradient terms equal
g^T[diag(r+b)-Sym(K)-e_n e_n^T]g, using the just-checked value flow.
Adding them proves (4). Conversely a finite nonnegative array with the
required value flow gives (2)--(4). This identity alone is a reformulation,
not progress on unrestricted PSD; the restricted cone studied next is
specified independently of that unknown PSD inequality.

## 2. A genuine bulk cone: directed binary-coordinate operators

Take N=2^m, m>=1, n=N-1, and index the finite samples in chronological
binary order. At each bit k choose numbers

    u_k>=0, v_k>=0, 0<=u_k-v_k<=2, kappa_k>=0,
    A_k=[[u_k,-v_k],[-u_k,v_k+2]].                   (5)

The two column sums of A_k are0 and2; its two row sums are
u_k-v_k and2+v_k-u_k, both nonnegative. Embed A_k at bit k with
identity matrices on the other bits. Choose arbitrary delta_i>=0 and put

    A=sum_(k=1)^m kappa_k (I tensor ... tensor A_k tensor ... tensor I)
                   +diag(delta_0,...,delta_n).       (6)

An off-diagonal entry in (6) is nonzero only between indices differing in
one bit. For each setting of the other m-1 bits, the edge0->1 at bit k
has coefficient kappa_k v_k, and the reverse edge has coefficient
kappa_k u_k. This is a scalable bulk organization with m*2^(m-1)
interior cross-block pairs. It is not the endpoint-fan join. The same
two-node factor is used in every context at a given bit; that equality
of contexts is a real restriction.

Let s=sum kappa_k. Then b=A1>=0. At a finite index i with binary bits i_k,

    a_i=2sum_k kappa_k i_k+delta_i-2*1_(i=n).

Consequently (2), hence all actual signs and all value coefficients, hold
if and only if

    t:=2s+delta_n >=2.                              (7)

There is no prohibition on outgoing optimum edges: all these a_i are
retained. The delta_i permit arbitrary additional equal weights on
Q_(i,star) and Q_(star,i). The initial coefficient q is free. Incoming
terminal edges are allowed; no terminal or queried pair is deleted.

## 3. Full one-step calibration

The cone contains a valid one-step seed throughout 0<H<=sqrt(2). Put
M=1+H, take physical step h_0=H, and choose

    A_H=[[2,-2/M],[-2,2+2/M]], delta=0, kappa=1.

Its actual coefficients are

    lambda_01=2/M, lambda_10=2,
    lambda_(0,star)=2H/M, lambda_(1,star)=2/M,
    lambda_(star,0)=lambda_(star,1)=0.

Direct expansion proves

    (1/M)||x_0||^2-2f_1+sum lambda_ij Q_ij
      = [||x_0-Hg_0-g_1||^2
             +(2-H^2)||g_0-g_1||^2+M(M-1)||g_1||^2]/M.       (8)

The value coefficient at0 is2/M-2+2H/M=0, and that at1,
including -2f_1, is2-2/M+2/M-2=0. Expanding the three squares verifies
all gradient and initial-distance coefficients. Every square coefficient
in (8) is nonnegative. In particular H=sqrt(2) gives the full undeformed
Silver seed, and the actual critical h_0=1+a_m also lies in this interval
for m>=128, as checked below. The next obstruction therefore concerns
the proposed tensor extension, rather than an invalid local seed.

## 4. Exact constant lower barrier for the entire cone (5)--(7)

Claim. If h_0=H>=7/5, no array (5)--(7), at ANY depth m, can make the
full matrix (4) PSD with q<=1/8. All remaining steps may be arbitrary
nonnegative numbers. All u_k,v_k,kappa_k and delta_i may depend on the
horizon. The local factors need not be calibrated as in Section3.

Proof. Define the nonnegative numbers

    U=sum kappa_k u_k,
    R=sum kappa_k v_k,
    B=U-R>=0,
    z=B+delta_0>=0,
    t=2sum kappa_k+delta_n>=2.                       (9)

The symbol R in this section is only a scalar coefficient sum, not an
objective or a gradient remainder. At the all-zero binary index0,

    b_0=z,
    G_00=U+delta_0-HR=z-(H-1)R.                    (10)

For (10), every off-diagonal neighbor of index0 is later than0,
T_j0=H at every such neighbor, and the total outgoing coefficient is R.
At the all-one index n,

    b_n=t-B,
    G_nn=t+R-1.                                    (11)

For (11), column n of V has only its diagonal entry1, and
A_nn=sum kappa_k(v_k+2)+delta_n=t+R.

The source condition gives sum b_i-sum a_i=2. If q=0 and the full
matrix were PSD, every entry in its initial row would be zero, hence
b=0, contradicting this condition and a>=0. A negative q also violates
PSD. Thus suppose 0<q<=1/8.

The principal matrix in x_0,g_0 must be PSD. By (10), its determinant
and lower diagonal require

    z^2 <=4q[z-(H-1)R],
    z-(H-1)R>=0.                                   (12)

In particular z<=4q and B<=z<=4q. Rearranging (12) as

    (z-2q)^2+4q(H-1)R<=4q^2

also gives R<=q/(H-1). Since H>=7/5 and q<=1/8,

    B<=1/2,  R<=5/16.                              (13)

The other principal matrix, in x_0,g_n, has positive lower diagonal
t+R-1>=1. By (11), its determinant requires

    q >= (t-B)^2/[4(t+R-1)].                        (14)

Using (13), t>=2, and t+R-1<t gives

    q > (t-1/2)^2/(4t)
       >= (3t/4)^2/(4t)
       =9t/64 >=9/32.

The middle inequality uses t-1/2>=3t/4, which follows from t>=2.
This contradicts q<=1/8, completing the proof. No Schur complement
of an omitted subspace, no Huber equality, and no positivity restricted
to legal transcripts were used: these are necessary principal tests
on the full original formal space.

## 6. A separate exact screen for symmetric bulk multiplier arrays

This section concerns ANY array with lambda_ij=lambda_ji for all finite
distinct i,j, including arbitrary complete bipartite, dyadic hierarchical,
or Walsh-diagonal nonnegative kernels. Optimum incidence may be arbitrary
as long as the value flow holds. It is a different restriction from (6).

Identify R^2 with C, with <u,v>=Re(conj(u)v). Set zeta=(1+i)/2 and
define a formal trajectory

    p_0=1, p_(i+1)=(1-h_i zeta)p_i,
    x_i=p_i, g_i=zeta p_i, f_i=|p_i|^2/4.           (15)

It obeys the physical GD equations exactly and has ||x_0||=1. Since
Re(zeta)=|zeta|^2=1/2, direct expansion gives

    Q_(i,star)=Q_(star,i)=0,
    Q_ij=(1/2)Im(conj(p_i)p_j)=-Q_ji.              (16)

The individual finite Q need not be nonpositive. These are formal test
vectors, not asserted to be a legal smooth-convex transcript. On (15),
the quadratic form of a symmetric finite array equals

    M(q)=q-|p_n|^2/2.

Full PSD therefore requires q>=|p_n|^2/2, irrespective of every optimum
edge. For the recursive word with h-levels1+a c^k, define

    T_j=|p_(2^j-1)|^2,
    d_k=(1+a^2 c^(2k))/2.

Then T_0=1 and T_(j+1)=d_j T_j^2, since the word contains two
identical child products and the single bridge. Both undeformed Silver
(a=1/lambda,c=lambda) and the actual critical word for m>=128 satisfy
a>2/5 and c>12/5. Consequently

    d_0>4/7, d_1>19/20, d_2>3, d_3>15,
    T_4=d_0^8 d_1^4 d_2^2 d_3
         >(4/7)^8(19/20)^4*135 >6/5.                (17)

The four rational inequalities in (17) follow by evaluating
(1+(2/5)^2(12/5)^(2k))/2 at k=0,1,2,3. For the final comparison,
the left rational product reduces to900778752/720600125; its numerator

It follows inductively that for every j>=4,

    T_j>(6/5)^(2^(j-4)),
    q>(1/2)(6/5)^(2^(j-4)).                        (18)

Thus the symmetric multiplier organization fails even the undeformed

For a general directed finite array, the same computation yields instead

Equation (19) identifies an actual contribution that is lost on replacing

## 7. Exact tensor contraction for the remaining directed dense family

## 9. Dense tensor kernels and arbitrary nonnegative mixtures: the same barrier

The coordinate sum in (6) is not essential. Here is a strictly larger,
genuinely dense coefficient cone, with independent cross-block pairs
differing in arbitrarily many bits. Its assumptions are explicit; no
arbitrary unknown full matrix is being hidden inside its definition.

At every bit k choose a two by two matrix

    P_k=[[1-v_k,u_k],[v_k,c_k-u_k]],
    0<=u_k<=v_k<=1,  u_k<=c_k<=1-v_k+u_k.           (23)

All entries are nonnegative, all row and column sums are at most1, and
the first column sum is exactly1. These properties follow directly:
the row sums are1-v_k+u_k and c_k+v_k-u_k; the column sums are1,c_k.
Conversely every nonnegative two by two matrix with these row/column
properties has the representation (23).

Take any finite number of products, with factors P_(ell,k) satisfying
(23), and choose eta_ell>=0 and delta_i>=0. Define

    P_ell=P_(ell,m) tensor ... tensor P_(ell,1),
    A=sum_ell eta_ell(I-P_ell)+diag(delta).          (24)

Thus EVERY actual finite multiplier is the concrete nonnegative sum

    lambda_ij=sum_ell eta_ell product_k P_(ell,k)[i_k,j_k], i!=j.   (25)

The diagonal of P is not an interpolation edge. The definition of A
sets the star coefficients via (3), rather than treating a diagonal
as a free nonnegative interpolation constraint. Its row sums b are
nonnegative because each P_ell has row sums at most1. Every column
sum of A is nonnegative. The only additional requirement for a>=0 is

    t=sum_ell eta_ell(1-product_k c_(ell,k))+delta_n>=2.          (26)

At all other indices the subtraction2e_n in (2) is zero. Equations
(24)--(26) therefore check every actual sign and all value coefficients.
The number of mixture terms and every parameter may depend on m; no
bounded-rank or fixed-library assumption is imposed.

This cone contains the valid seed of Section3. For any0<H<=sqrt(2),
put M=1+H, eta=4, and take a single factor

    P_H=[[1/2,1/(2M)],[1/2,1/2-1/(2M)]].           (27)

It has u=1/(2M),v=c=1/2, satisfies (23), and
4(I-P_H)=A_H. Equation (8) is its full one-step calibration. More
generally the cone contains all coordinate sums (6), including their
nonnegative limits: write a local two-node A_k as eta(I-P_k) with eta
large enough to keep both diagonal entries of P_k nonnegative, and
put identity factors at every other bit. Its row/column bounds follow
from those of A_k. The delta remains unchanged.

We now prove that every full PSD certificate from (24)--(26), on any
schedule with h_0>=7/5, still requires q>1/8.

For one product, temporarily omit the mixture label and put

    D0=product_k(1-v_k), L0=product_k(1-v_k+u_k),
    Dn=product_k(c_k-u_k), Cn=product_k c_k,
    Ln=product_k(c_k+v_k-u_k).

These are respectively the first diagonal, first row sum, last diagonal,
last column sum, and last row sum of P. The first column sum is1.
All are nonnegative and the row and column sums are at most1.

Two elementary product inequalities are decisive:

    0<=Ln-Cn<=1-L0,
    0<=Cn-Dn<=L0-D0.                                (28)

To prove them, for nonnegative vectors s,d define
F_d(s)=product_k(s_k+d_k)-product_k s_k. Its expansion is the sum
over nonempty subsets J of product_(k in J)d_k times
product_(k not in J)s_k. Each coefficient is nonnegative, so F_d
is coordinatewise nondecreasing in s.
For the first inequality of (28), set d_k=v_k-u_k>=0.
Then c_k<=1-d_k by (23), and

    Ln-Cn=F_d(c)<=F_d(1-d)=1-product_k(1-d_k)=1-L0.

For the second, set d_k=u_k. Since c_k-u_k<=1-v_k,

    Cn-Dn=F_u(c-u)<=F_u(1-v)=L0-D0.

Now restore the mixture label and define

    B_L=sum_ell eta_ell(1-L0_ell),
    R_L=sum_ell eta_ell(L0_ell-D0_ell),
    B_R=sum_ell eta_ell(Ln_ell-Cn_ell),
    R_R=sum_ell eta_ell(Cn_ell-Dn_ell).

Every number is nonnegative. By (28),

    B_R<=B_L, R_R<=R_L.                             (29)

The actual first and last coefficients of the full matrix (4) are

    z=b_0=B_L+delta_0,
    G_00=z-(h_0-1)R_L,
    b_n=t-B_R,
    G_nn=t+R_R-1.                                   (30)

For the first diagonal, the finite outgoing row sum at0 is R_L and
each such target j>0 has T_j0=h_0. For the last diagonal use
A_nn=sum eta(1-Dn)+delta_n=t+R_R and column n of V as before.
These prove (30) on the original formal space.

If0<q<=1/8 and the matrix were PSD, its x_0,g_0 principal submatrix
would imply exactly as in (12)

    B_L<=z<=4q<=1/2,
    R_L<=q/(h_0-1)<=5/16.

Equations (29)--(30) and the x_0,g_n principal submatrix would then
give, using t>=2,

    q >=(t-B_R)^2/[4(t+R_R-1)]
       >(t-1/2)^2/(4t)>=9/32,

a contradiction. Levels q<=0 are excluded by the same source/initial-row
argument in Section4. This proves q>1/8 for the entire dense mixture cone.

The critical and undeformed applications in Section5 follow unchanged.
The obstruction allows all directions of optimum incidence, all actual
interior pairs, arbitrarily many mixture terms, and arbitrary diagonal
optimum storage. It rules out this normalized tensor CONE, not all
nonnegative tensor-product arrays: a factor violating its first-column
normalization or its row/column bounds can leave the cone. Nor does it
cover a correction with negative mixture weights even if the final edge
array is nonnegative. The product inequalities (28), not a mere change
of basis or an assumed Huber kernel, are the mechanism behind the bound.

## 10. General first-query budget and required context variation

For context and a possible next mutation, the first part of the preceding
test is universal. In ANY nonnegative full certificate with h_0=H>1,
write b_0=lambda_(0,star) and r_0=sum_(j=1)^n lambda_0j. The x_0,g_0
principal submatrix is

    [[q,-b_0/2],[-b_0/2,b_0-(H-1)r_0]].             (31)

This follows directly from the general expansion (4), with no restrictions
on incoming terminal edges, optimum-source edges, or the rest of the array.
Since q>0, its determinant implies

    b_0<=4q, r_0<=q/(H-1).                          (32)

The proof is the identity
(b_0-2q)^2+4q(H-1)r_0<=4q^2. The earlier arbitrary-q queried-cut
draft367cf22cf1bd54cc retains more variables and all target-position
moments; (31)--(32) are compatible with that larger interface. They are
rederived here jointly, not imported from an unverified draft.

If a contextual extension of the binary-coordinate array has first
totals B_L,R_L and terminal totals B_R,R_R as in (30), t>=2, and
obeys B_R<=K B_L and R_R<=K R_L for some K>=1, then the same proof
shows

    q>1/(8K), provided h_0>=7/5.                    (33)

Indeed q<=1/(8K) would imply B_R<=1/2,R_R<=5/16 and also q<=1/8,
which reproduces the contradiction. Thus a useful q=O(1/M_m) in this
contextual extension requires K at least a constant times M_m. A bounded
change of context weights cannot repair the cone. This quantitative
condition is necessary only; it does not assert that larger variation
will make the full matrix PSD or that every possible certificate admits
this contextual parameterization.
