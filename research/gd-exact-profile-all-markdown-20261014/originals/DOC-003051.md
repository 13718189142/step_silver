## 1. General actual function and four-step trajectory

Let h=(a,b,c,d), with a>1, b>1, c>1, d>=0. Define

 B=b-1, C=c-1, D=d+1, s=a+b+c+d, A=1+s.

Choose u>0 and z>0 satisfying

 u>=B,
 z^2 <= (u+1)((a-1)u-1),
 z^2 >= C d(D-BC)/D^2.                                  (1)

The second inequality implies (a-1)u-1>0. The third imposes
no additional restriction if its right side is negative. Put

 p=(u,0), q=(-1,z), r=(-BC/D,0), K=conv{p,q,r},
 F(x)=max_(g in K) {<x,g>-||g||^2/2}.                      (2)

This nondegenerate compact triangle contains0 in the relative interior
of its horizontal base. Completing the square shows that the unique
maximizer in (2) is the Euclidean projection P_K(x).

For completeness, if p_x=P_K(x), p_y=P_K(y), the two projection
normal inequalities give

 ||p_x-p_y||^2 <= <p_x-p_y,x-y>.

Hence P_K is1-Lipschitz. The maximizing formula gives

 0<=F(x+t)-F(x)-<p_x,t>
       <=<P_K(x+t)-p_x,t><=||t||^2,

so F is differentiable with gradient P_K; it is convex as a supremum
of affine functions. Since0 belongs to K, F>=0 and F(0)=0. Moreover
P_K(x)=0 exactly when x is in the normal cone of K at0. Pairing x
with both signs of the horizontal base forces its first coordinate
to vanish; pairing it with q then forces its second coordinate<=0.
Thus argmin F={(0,t):t<=0}.

The proposed ORIGINAL iterates and gradients are

 x0=(a u-1,z),       g0=(u,0),
 x1=(-1,z),         g1=(-1,z),
 x2=(B,-Bz),        g2=(B,0),
 x3=(-BC,-Bz),      g3=(-BC/D,0),
 x4=(-BC/D,-Bz),    g4=(-BC/D,0).                        (3)

The four equations x_(i+1)=x_i-h_i g_i follow by subtraction.
The initial point has positive second coordinate, so its closest
minimizer is0 and its squared distance is(a u-1)^2+z^2.

We check every required projection normal, including both terminal
orientations implicitly through the single actual function.

At x0, the residual y0=x0-g0=((a-1)u-1,z) has

 <q-p,y0>=z^2-(u+1)((a-1)u-1)<=0,
 <r-p,y0>=(-BC/D-u)((a-1)u-1)<0.

At x1 the residual is0. At x2, g2 is on the base because
-BC/D<0<B<=u, and y2=x2-g2=(0,-Bz) has zero pairing with
both base directions and pairing-Bz^2<0 with q-g2.

At x3 the residual is y3=(-BCd/D,-Bz). Its base comparison
with p-r is nonpositive, and

 <q-r,y3>=B{C d(D-BC)/D^2-z^2}<=0.

At x4 the residual y4=(0,-Bz) has zero pairing with the base
and pairing-Bz^2<0 with q-r. These are all vertices of K,
so (3) gives the actual gradient of the same F at every query.

For all five indices define f_i=F(x_i)=<x_i,g_i>-||g_i||^2/2.
Every original ordered interpolation residual has the exact form

 Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2

## 2. Exact intrinsic energy and a necessary family of inequalities

Use the energy from accepted04219554e41d9750, Section10:

 V_h=2sum_(i=0)^3 h_i(f_i-f4)
       +sum_(i=0)^3 h_i(1-h_i)||g_i||^2-s(s+1)||g4||^2.

The identity

 ||x_i||^2-||x_(i+1)||^2
     =2h_i f_i+h_i(1-h_i)||g_i||^2

follows from (3) and f_i=<x_i,g_i>-||g_i||^2/2.
Summing it gives

 V_h=||x0||^2-||x4||^2-2s f4-s(s+1)||g4||^2.

Here f4=||g4||^2/2, ||x4||^2=(BC/D)^2+B^2z^2, and therefore

 V_h=(a u-1)^2+(1-B^2)z^2-A^2(BC/D)^2.                 (5)

Consequently the original full primitive cone must satisfy

 (a u-1)^2+(1-B^2)z^2 >= A^2(BC/D)^2                  (6)

for EVERY u,z obeying(1). Indeed its accepted exact identity is

This is a necessary family, not a sufficient condition. The imposed

# C82: exact elimination of the C81 witness parameters

Status: UNVERIFIED joint extension. For the complete construction and
proof, concatenate the C81 proof with this file. C81 is not an accepted
fact; its geometry is part of the joint proof, not an imported premise.
There are no new word proposals, optimizer calls or numerical replays.

Let h=(a,b,c,d), a,b,c>1,d>=0, and put

 alpha=a-1, B=b-1, C=c-1, D=d+1, A=1+a+b+c+d,
 H(u)=(u+1)(alpha u-1),
 ell=max{0,C d(D-BC)/D^2},
 R=[2-a+sqrt(a^2+4alpha ell)]/(2alpha),
 U=max{B,R}, K0=A^2(BC/D)^2.

Define three scalar coefficients

 q2=a^2+(1-B^2)alpha,
 q1=-2a+(1-B^2)(a-2),
 q0=B^2-K0,

and q(u)=q2 u^2+q1 u+q0.

## 1. Feasible parameter domain

The C81 triangle has parameters u>=B,z>0 with

 ell<=z^2<=H(u).

Since alpha>0, H has its only nonnegative root at1/alpha;
H'(u)=2alpha u+a-2>=a>0 for u>=1/alpha.
Solving H(u)=ell therefore gives R>=1/alpha exactly as above.
The closure of the admissible parameter domain is

 u>=U, ell<=z^2<=H(u).                                  (1)

Every boundary point in(1) with z=0 is a limit of points with
u>U,z>0 obeying the original normal inequalities. In particular
H(u)>ell for u>U. Taking such a limit in the explicit polynomial
energy is justified by continuity; no assertion about strict normals
at the limiting point is needed.

## 2. Closed formula for the infimum over this entire witness family

The energy is

 E(u,z)=(a u-1)^2+(1-B^2)z^2-K0.

If0<B<=1, its smallest value at fixed u uses z^2=ell.
Moreover a u-1>0 throughout u>=U>=1/alpha, so that value is
strictly increasing in u. Its infimum over the family is

 E_min=(a U-1)^2+(1-B^2)ell-K0.                         (2)

If B>1, its smallest value at fixed u uses z^2=H(u).
Expanding the resulting expression gives exactly q(u). Hence:

 * If q2<0, or q2=0 and q1<0, then E_min=-infinity.
 * If q2=0 and q1>=0, then E_min=q(U).
 * If q2>0, set u_opt=max{U,-q1/(2q2)}. Then
   E_min=q(u_opt).                                      (3)

These statements follow respectively from the leading term, from
linearity on the half-line, and from completing the square
q(u)=q2(u+q1/(2q2))^2+q0-q1^2/(4q2).

Every original full primitive certificate with a,b,c>1,d>=0
therefore must satisfy E_min>=0, with E_min defined by(2)--(3).
If E_min<0, an actual finite u and z>0 give a negative energy:
for an attained strict negative value this follows directly or by
continuity from a zero-z boundary; for E_min=-infinity take a
sufficiently large finite u. The C81 normals then give an actual
convex1-smooth function with a minimizer. This is a completely
parameter-eliminated necessary test for this particular family.

## 3. A broad monotone regime

Suppose1<a<=2 and1<b<=1+sqrt(3). If B<=1, formula(2)
already applies. If1<B<=sqrt(3), then

 q2>=a^2-2(a-1)=(a-1)^2+1>0,
 q'(1/alpha)=a[2/alpha+1-B^2]>=0,

because alpha<=1 and B^2<=3. Since q2>0, q' increases;
thus q is nondecreasing throughout u>=U>=1/alpha.
Consequently no stationary-point alternative is needed in this
regime: E_min=q(U) if B>1. If additionally R>=B, then H(U)=ell
and BOTH cases reduce to the same explicit radical inequality

 (a R-1)^2+(1-B^2)ell >= A^2(BC/D)^2.                    (4)

At B=1 it specializes to Section4 of C81. These are conditional
parameter statements. No unverified global-domain reduction is used
to claim that an arbitrary four-step seed lies in this regime.

## 4. Exact scope of the remaining gap

Eliminating the witnesses is not eliminating the original twenty
nonnegative multipliers. The displayed tests are necessary because
each negative case yields an actual trajectory; their nonnegative
cases certify only that this particular triangle family cannot reject
the schedule. No converse to the full flow/PSD interface is proved.
In particular failure to derive a contradiction from(2)--(4) does not
give a seed, and a negative saved optimization score does not give a
global impossibility theorem.
