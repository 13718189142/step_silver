# Active analytic interface: retain a squared gradient-aggregate residual

Status: self-contained unverified certificate algebra and two exact tests. This is an analytic mechanism after the600-evaluation discovery cap, not a claimed improved schedule family. No numerical scan extension or new verifier submission.

Work at L=1. For a nonnegative schedule h of length n, use x_(i+1)=x_i-h_i g_i, g_i=grad f(x_i), f_i=f(x_i). Let u=(u_0,...,u_n) be nonnegative proof weights with D=sum u_i>0. These are certificate coefficients, not algorithmic state or additional GD updates. Define
U=sum_i u_i g_i, d=x_0-x_n, e=U-d,
E_h(u)=2sum_i u_i(f_i-f_n)+sum_i u_i||g_i||^2+2sum_i u_i<g_i,x_0-x_i>-||U||^2.

For any minimizer x_star, direct expansion gives
||x_0-x_star||^2+2sum_i u_i Q_(i,star)-2D(f_n-f_star)
=||x_n-x_star-e||^2+E_h(u),
where Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2<=0. Hence any bound
E_h(u)>=nu||e-kappa g_n||^2, nu>=0,                 (A)
implies W_n(h)<=1/D. Unlike a saturated primitive certificate, D is independent of the physical sum and can be strictly below2sum(h)+1. The square preserves vector information about the difference between the certificate gradient aggregate and actual GD displacement.

For completeness Q_ij<=0 follows by applying the smooth descent inequality to F(z)=f(z)-<g_i,z>: x_i minimizes F, and F(x_j-grad F(x_j))<=F(x_j)-||grad F(x_j)||^2/2. This gives the displayed interpolation inequality; the smooth descent inequality follows by integrating the Lipschitz gradient along a segment.

1. Exact tested one-step family.
Let t=1+r with1/2<=r<=1 and take h=[t], u_0=1/r, u_1=(1-r)/r^2. Then D=1/r^2 and direct coefficient comparison gives
E_h(u)=(2/r)(-Q_10)+((2r-1)/r^4)||r g_0+g_1||^2.
Both coefficients are nonnegative. Moreover
e-g_1=((1-r-r^2)/r^2)(r g_0+g_1).
If1-r-r^2!=0, (A) therefore holds with
kappa=1, nu=(2r-1)/(1-r-r^2)^2.
If1-r-r^2=0, e-g_1 vanishes identically, so any finite nu>=0 is allowed in (A). The objective bound is W_1(t)<=r^2, attained by f(x)=x^2/2. Thus this is a real unsaturated objective certificate rather than a proposed potential with no examples.

A convenient rational seed is t=5/3, u=(3/2,3/4), D=9/4, kappa=1, nu=27. Its exact identity is
E_[5/3](u)=3(-Q_10)+(27/16)||(2/3)g_0+g_1||^2
          =3(-Q_10)+27||e-g_1||^2.

2. Exact one-step transport as a3-by3 matrix condition.
Suppose (A) is available for a block. Append a predetermined step t>=0. Put g=g_n, q=g_(n+1). Keep the old weights except increase u_n by alpha, and append the new weight beta. Require
u_n+alpha>=0, beta>=0, D'=D+alpha+beta>0,
where nu_n in this line means the old terminal weight u_n, not the coefficient nu. Equivalently, the first condition is simply u_n+alpha>=0. Define
u'>=0, kappa' real, e'=e+(alpha-t)g+beta q.
Choose mu>=0 with 2(D+alpha)+mu>=0.

Let M be the symmetric3-by3 coefficient matrix in the vector variables(e,g,q) with entries
M_ee=nu-nu',
M_eg=-nu kappa-alpha-nu'(alpha-t),
M_eq=-beta-nu'(beta-kappa'),
M_gg=nu kappa^2+D+alpha+alpha(1-alpha)-nu'(alpha-t)^2-mu(t-1),
M_qq=D+alpha+beta(1-beta)-nu'(beta-kappa')^2+mu,
M_gq=(D+alpha)(t-1)+beta(t-alpha)-nu'(alpha-t)(beta-kappa')-(mu/2)(2-t).

If M is positive semidefinite, the extended schedule satisfies
E_(h,t)(u')>=nu'||e'-kappa' q||^2.
This is an explicit sufficient matrix test with fixed dimension. Its identity is
E_(h,t)(u')-nu'||e'-kappa'q||^2
=E_h(u)-nu||e-kappa g||^2
 +[2(D+alpha)+mu](-Q_(n+1,n))+mu(-Q_(n,n+1))
 +(e,g,q)^T M(e,g,q).
The matrix quadratic form means the same coefficients paired with Euclidean inner products, so PSD suffices in every dimension.

To check the identity before imposing positivity, the exact energy increment is
E_(h,t)(u')-E_h(u)
=2(D+alpha)(f_n-f_(n+1))+alpha(1-alpha)||g||^2+beta(1-beta)||q||^2
 +2beta(t-alpha)<g,q>-2<e,alpha g+beta q>.
Substituting the two interpolation expressions gives the displayed matrix. No unknown interpolation theorem or numerical feasibility claim is hidden in this transport rule.

3. A nontrivial exact transport test.
For the rational seed in part1, append t=1 and choose alpha=0,beta=1,nu'=27,kappa'=28/27,mu=0. Then
M=diag(0,9/4,239/108)>=0.
Thus h=(5/3,1) with proof weights(3/2,3/4,1), D'=13/4, has the exact bound
E_h(u')>=27||e'-(28/27)g_2||^2,
so W_2(5/3,1)<=4/13. This bound is deliberately reported at its actual value; it is weaker than the project benchmark and supplies no negative answer to(P). Its role is to test that the new residual state transports with a nonzero coefficient and nonnegative interpolation multipliers.

4. Necessary tests and the unresolved propagation issue.
For an all-affine Huber trajectory with constant sampled gradient and s=sum h,
E_h(u)=D(2s+1-D)||g||^2, e=(D-s)g.
Thus (A) must obey
nu(D-s-kappa)^2<=D(2s+1-D).
For f(x)=x^2/2, x_0=1, define p_i=product_(j<i)(1-h_j), p=p_n and w=sum_i u_i p_i. Then it must also obey
1-Dp^2-(1-w)^2>=nu[w-1+(1-kappa)p]^2.
These give immediate exact hostile tests for any proposed coefficient recursion.

The one-step matrix has M_ee=nu-nu', so this particular adjacent-interpolation update cannot increase nu. A proposed long-block rule must address this restriction, rather than claim that repeated application already gives the desired rate. No growth law for D has been proved. In particular no D_n>B_n=2S_bullet(n)+1 has been obtained.

A concrete extension to investigate is discounting old proof weights and retaining the full displacement as a vector coordinate. For0<=eta<=1,
E_h(eta u)=eta E_h(u)+eta(1-eta)||U||^2.
If new weights are u'=(eta u+alpha at the old terminal sample,beta at the new sample), then
E_(h,t)(u')=eta E_h(u)+eta(1-eta)||U||^2
 +2(eta D+alpha)(f_n-f_(n+1))+alpha(1-alpha)||g||^2+beta(1-beta)||q||^2
 +2beta(t-alpha)<g,q>+2<d-eta U,alpha g+beta q>.
The retained coordinates(d,U,g) transform linearly to(d+tg,eta U+alpha g+beta q,q). This gives a concrete next matrix interface; it is not a proved accelerated closure. Nonlocal interpolation links within concatenated blocks may be necessary to regain sufficient residual control. A complete candidate still needs a feasible infinite recursion, an actual objective upper bound, and an exact comparison with the true B_n profile.
