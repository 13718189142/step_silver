## 3. Every cross-copy inequality, in both directions

For every integer k>=0 prescribe scaled core data

    X_(k,i)=R^k x_i,       G_(k,i)=R^k g_i,
    V_(k,i)=R^(2k) f_i,       i=0,1.

Add the optimum (0,0,0). Within one copy, all Q residuals are R^(2k)
times the corresponding core residuals. Now take two distinct copies
with absolute scale factors L>s>0; their signs can be either positive or
negative. Since |R|>400, we have L>=400s.

Define the positive optimum margins of any jet by

    delta_i^+=f_i-||g_i||^2/2,
    delta_i^-=<x_i,g_i>-f_i-||g_i||^2/2.

Both core margins are at least delta by(2), and scaling multiplies them
by the squared scale. The exact cross residual for a large jet u and
small jet v is

    Q_uv=-delta_u^- -delta_v^+ +<G_u,X_v-G_v>.

Consequently, regardless of signs and chosen core indices,

    Q_uv<=-delta(L^2+s^2)+2Ls,
    Q_vu<=-delta(L^2+s^2)+2Ls.                           (3)

No old/future plane or cross-copy inequality has been dropped.

We will need strong convexity as well. For any sampled jets define

    G_i'=(G_i-mu X_i)/(1-mu),
    V_i'=(V_i-mu||X_i||^2/2)/(1-mu).

Direct expansion of the interpolation residual gives the exact identity

    (1-mu)^2 Q_ij'
       =(1-mu)Q_ij+(mu/2)||(X_i-G_i)-(X_j-G_j)||^2.       (4)

Within one nonzero copy the right side is at most

    [-eta(1-mu)+8mu] L^2<0,

because the difference of the two core y vectors has norm at most4.
Against the optimum it is at most[-delta(1-mu)+2mu] L^2<0. For two
distinct copies, ||L y_i-(+/-s)y_j||^2<=8(L^2+s^2), so(3),(4) give

    (1-mu)^2 Q_uv'
      <=-[delta(1-mu)-4mu](L^2+s^2)+2Ls
      <=-(27/2000)(L^2+s^2)+2Ls<0.                       (5)

Here delta(1-mu)-4mu=.0135986>27/2000=.0135, and
2Ls<=L^2/200 because L>=400s. The same estimate holds for Q_vu'.
This proves ALL transformed interpolation inequalities for the entire
countably infinite set of scaled jets and the optimum.

## 4. One global interpolant for the infinite set

For each transformed sample put

    y_i'=X_i-G_i',          d_i'=V_i'-||G_i'||^2/2,
    H_i'(v)=||v||^2/2+<y_i',v>-d_i'.

The optimum anchor is H_star'(v)=||v||^2/2. The transformed strict optimum
residuals proved above imply d_i'>0 on the two unscaled core jets. For the
kth copy, y_i' scales by R^k and d_i' by R^(2k). Hence, for each fixed v,

    <R^k y_i',v>-R^(2k)d_i' -> -infinity.

The same convergence is uniform on any bounded set of v. Therefore

    H'(v)=sup_i H_i'(v),       F'(x)=sup_v(<x,v>-H'(v))     (6)

defines an everywhere finite continuous1-strongly convex H'. Locally it
is the maximum of finitely many anchors. Moreover H'(v)>=||v||^2/2,
H'(0)=0, and H' is minimized at0.

For each x the conjugate supremum has a unique maximizer g(x): existence
follows from the quadratic lower bound on H', and uniqueness from its
strong convexity. The optimality inequalities for two points x,y imply
<g(x)-g(y),x-y>>=||g(x)-g(y)||^2, hence ||g(x)-g(y)||<=||x-y||.
The two supremum inequalities also bound the remainder in
F'(x+d)-F'(x)-<g(x),d> between0 and
<g(x+d)-g(x),d>, which is at most||d||^2. Thus F' is differentiable
with gradient g and1-Lipschitz gradient. It is convex and has minimum0 at0.

All interpolation inequalities Q_ij'<=0 say that at v=G_i' the i-th
anchor attains the supremum in(6). Its gradient there is X_i. Thus
X_i belongs to the subgradient of H' at G_i'; conjugate equality gives
F'(X_i)=V_i' and grad F'(X_i)=G_i'. This checks every sample, including
the infinite collection. Finally set

    F(x)=(1-mu)F'(x)+mu||x||^2/2.                        (7)

It interpolates all ORIGINAL scaled jets, is mu-strongly convex, and has
gradient Lipschitz constant at most(1-mu)+mu=1. It is one fixed function,
independent of the horizon at which the trajectory is inspected.

## 7. What this proves and what it does not

The proof gives a concrete way to glue an infinite family of legal scaled
copies while retaining every crossing constraint and a common strong
convexity modulus. Strict optimum margins pay for the cross terms when
radial scale ratios are large. This is a sufficient condition with an actual
fixed realization; it is not lossless gluing of arbitrary boundary jets.

The alternating schedule already has huge quadratic error, for example
its pair multiplier at scalar curvature1/2 is
(1-h/2)(1-b/2)=-(999/2000)(999/2), whose magnitude exceeds one.
Therefore this example does not establish a new family where the quadratic
channel misses the benchmark. Its role is to make the positive elliptic
copy mechanism concrete. For the geometric ruler family at a dyadic
resonance, the still-missing step is a legal expanding finite core and
quantitative control of its rotated copies.
