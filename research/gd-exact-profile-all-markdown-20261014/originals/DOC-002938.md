## 10. What the thin-inactive-group law actually reduces to

For any fixed word h with all h_i>1 define the scalar symmetric Huber
profile, on the real line and with its own minimum0, by

    V_Hub(h)=sup_(s!=0) Phi(Psi_h(s))/s^2,                   (35)

where Psi_h is the actual chronological composition of T_(h_i). This is
one fixed convex C1 1-smooth function Hub, varying only its initial point.
The elementary constant-slope example s=1+2S gives V_Hub>=H_base.
No accepted fact has identified V_Hub with K_n or the alternating-ray
channel; no such identification is assumed here.

For an instance of either law with z_0!=0, remove the nonnegative zeta
in the denominator of (24) to obtain the weighted-average bound

    E <= [ (S+R)^2 E_car(R) + kappa*z_0^2 E_sc ] /
                       [ (S+R)^2 + kappa*z_0^2 ],
    E_car(R)=(2R-1)/(S+R)^2 <=1/(1+2S),
    E_sc=Phi(t)/z_0^2 <=V_Hub(h).                            (36)

The carrier bound follows from
(S+R)^2-(1+2S)(2R-1)=(R-S-1)^2. If z_0=0, then t=0 and the
same upper bound follows directly. Consequently every Law II instance
satisfies E<=V_Hub(h), for every word in its h_i>1 domain.

The converse SUPREMUM relation needs attention to inverse branches and
strict guards. For one scalar step h=1+d, the smallest possible magnitude
of an input with output magnitude u>=0 is

    rho_d(u)=u/d if 0<=u<=d, and u+h if u>d.                 (37)

To prove this, inputs of magnitude at most1 give output magnitude d times
their magnitude. For inputs of magnitude r>=1, the output magnitude is
|r-h|. When u<=d the outer inverse h-u is at least1 and the core inverse
u/d is at most1. When u>d, the only admissible outer inverse is h+u.
All alternatives are included; oddness removes the sign ambiguity.

The function rho_d is increasing and left-continuous. Therefore composing
(37) backward minimizes the initial magnitude among ALL scalar trajectories
having a given terminal magnitude. Indeed a larger intermediate magnitude
cannot lower the earlier minimum, by monotonicity at each preceding step.
Denote the resulting finite composition by r_h(u).

Our strict inverse (15) agrees with these minimal magnitudes except when an
intermediate u equals d. Those equality choices are not silently admitted:
(15) uses its outer branch there so that every declared support is strict.
However (37) and its finite composition are left-continuous and strictly
increasing. For any terminal u>0, choose positive u_l increasing to u and
avoiding the finitely many threshold preimages of this finite piecewise
affine composition. The strict construction then has

    |z_0(u_l)|=r_h(u_l) ->r_h(u), Phi(u_l)->Phi(u).            (38)

Only finite real instances are used at each l. No trajectory limit is
offered as an attained witness. Thus the supremum of E_sc over the strict
law is exactly V_Hub(h): replacing any scalar prehistory by the minimal
one cannot reduce Phi(u)/initial^2, and (38) approximates boundary cases.

Now choose such a strict scalar history, any fixed R>max(1,u), and Z=0.
With q=1 and r arbitrarily large, kappa=2r+1 tends to infinity and (24)

    sup_(ALL finite Law II instances) E(F,h)=V_Hub(h).       (39)

This is an equality of suprema. It does not assert finite attainment when

    kappa > (S+R)^2[eta-E_car(R)] /
