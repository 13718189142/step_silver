The formal gradient quadratic is

    R=2<g_n,sumh_i g_i>+||g_n||^2-M||p||^2
      +sum_ij mu_ij<B_jG,G_j-G_i>,
    B_jG=g_j+sum_(k<j)h_k g_k, B_pG=M p.

At a physical node i<n, define forward mass a_i=sum_(j>i,j<=n)mu_ij and backward mass b_i=sum_(j<i)mu_ij. Its diagonal coefficient is

    c_ii=w_i+b_i-(h_i-1)a_i.                       (1)

This follows from incoming physical rows contributing in_i, each forward outgoing row contributing -h_i mu_ij, and full divergence at i equal zero. The p diagonal is M and the p--i matrix entry is -Mw_i/2. Testing only g_i and p therefore forces

    c_ii>=Mw_i^2/4>=0.                            (2)

## 3. Exact parameterization on an increasing physical tree

Consider any physical tree rooted at n, with parent pi(v)>v, both edge directions, and all absorption edges. For a non-root subtree rooted at v, let A_v be its total PHYSICAL step mass and W_v its total absorption mass. Let L_v=t_parent-t_v>0.

Summing physical divergences over the subtree gives

    mu_(parent,v)=mu_(v,parent)+W_v.

Summing clock drift over the same subtree, and telescoping every internal edge, gives

    mu_(v,parent)L_v+W_v(M-t_v)=2A_v.

The telescoping identity used here is
sum_(internal child edges u) W_u(t_parent(u)-t_u)
 =sum_(subtree nodes u)w_u(t_v-t_u).
Thus both edge weights are uniquely determined:

    mu_(v,parent)=[2A_v-W_v(M-t_v)]/L_v,
    mu_(parent,v)=[2A_v-W_v(M-t_parent)]/L_v.       (6)

This does not prescribe absorption or canonical weights.
