# The optimal three-step objective schedule needs more than endpoint proof-weight corrections

Status: complete unverified counterexample to a proposed certificate template. This is not a counterexample to the schedule, which is a standard optimal recursive objective schedule. No verifier call.

For a schedule h of length n and sum S, consider the restrictive proof-weight template

  u_0=h_0+a,
  u_i=h_i for1<=i<n,
  u_n=b.

Here a,b are real, and actual weights must be nonnegative. If the weight mass is the Huber-tight value D=2S+1, then b=S+1-a. Write d=x_0-x_n, U=sum_i u_i g_i, e=U-d. This template has the exact linear relation

  e=a g_0+b g_n.                                    (1)

The intrinsic objective certificate is E_h(u)>=0, where

  E_h(u)=2sum_i u_i(f_i-f_n)+sum_i u_i||g_i||^2
          +2sum_i u_i<g_i,x_0-x_i>-||U||^2.

For L=1 and minimizer0 it satisfies the exact identity

  E_h(u)=||x_0||^2+2sum_i u_i Q_(i,star)
           -2D f_n-||x_n-e||^2.                    (2)

The template is attractive because a one-step optimal schedule h=(3/2) has weights(2,2), hence a=1/2,b=2, and E=4(-Q_10)>=0. But it already fails on a basic three-step objective schedule.

Set t=sqrt(2), lambda=1+t, and

  h=(t,lambda,3/2),
  S=5/2+2t,
  D=2S+1=6+4t=2lambda^2.

This schedule is ConPD((sqrt(2)),(3/2)). The bridge is lambda, by direct substitution in psi. The argument below only needs the explicit steps and two legal equality examples; it does not assume its optimality among all schedules.

## Quadratic equality fixes the sole free endpoint parameter

Use f(x)=x^2/2, x_0=1. All Q_(i,star)=0, and

  x_3=p=(1-t)(1-lambda)(1-3/2)
       =-1/(2+t)=-1/sqrt(D).

Thus2D f_3=Dp^2=1. From(1)–(2),

  E_h(u)=-[p-a-bp]^2.

Any valid nonnegative intrinsic energy must therefore have a+(b-1)p=0. Substituting b=S+1-a gives the unique value

  a=-Sp/(1-p)=S/(sqrt(D)+1)=lambda/2,
  b=S+1-a=(3/2)(2+t).                              (3)

The associated weights are

  u=((1+3t)/2,lambda,3/2,(3/2)(2+t)),

and all are positive.

## The middle-checkpoint Huber equality contradicts those weights

Put c=1/lambda and q=t/(4lambda). Define the one-dimensional convex1-smooth function

  f(x)=x^2/2 for -q<=x<=c,
  f(x)=cx-c^2/2 for x>=c,
  f(x)=-qx-q^2/2 for x<=-q.

Its derivative is x clipped to[-q,c], which is nondecreasing and1-Lipschitz; f is differentiable, f(0)=min f=0. Starting at x_0=1, the specified GD steps give

  x_1=c,
  x_2=c-1=-t/lambda=-4q,
  x_3=-4q+(3/2)q=-(5/2)q,
  (g_0,g_1,g_2,g_3)=(c,c,-q,-q).

Every sampled point is on an outer affine piece or its boundary. Therefore Q_(i,star)=f_i-g_i x_i+g_i^2/2=0 at all four samples. Also

  f_3=(5/2)q^2-q^2/2=2q^2,
  2f_3=4q^2=1/(2lambda^2)=1/D.

This is the middle-cut equality construction from accepted facts b98bb807d514e80d and1fca3e5dd555cb4a, with prefix sum t, checkpoint lambda, and tail sum3/2; all trajectory details are included here.

For the only possible template weights(3),

  U=(u_0+u_1)c-(u_2+u_3)q
    =(6+11t)/(8lambda),
  U-1=(3t-2)/(8lambda)>0.

Since e=U-(1-x_3), one has x_3-e=1-U. Equation(2) now yields

  E_h(u)=-[(3sqrt(2)-2)/(8(1+sqrt(2)))]^2<0.

Thus no choice of a,b in the endpoint-only template can certify the Huber-tight denominator D for this schedule: the quadratic forces(3), and a second legal equality witness refutes it. The original schedule remains valid with a different weight vector. In the usual ConPD construction, one may use the nonnegative weights

  (sqrt(2),2+sqrt(2),2+sqrt(2),2+sqrt(2)),

which have mass D and adjust internal indices as well as endpoints.

The scope is precise. This rules out modifying only the initial and terminal entries of the physical-step weight vector. It does not exclude all fixed-dimensional potentials, all gradient-aggregate residuals, or another certificate with internal anchor weights. It explains why retaining a single initial-gradient correction while insisting every internal weight equal the physical step is too narrow for the upper-construction program.
