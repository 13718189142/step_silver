# Selected complex-polynomial bridge and lens obstruction

Status: verifier accepted as fact dfde2fa326c908bf. The fact graph is authoritative; this is the submitted proof artifact.

## Statement

Complex-polynomial transcripts, an explicit legal spectral lens, and a quantitative limit of that lens.

For each integer n>=1 and each real nonnegative schedule h=(h_0,...,h_{n-1}), define Poly_0(z)=1 and Poly_i(z)=product_{t=0}^{i-1}(1-h_t z) for 1<=i<=n. Identify the complex plane with the real Euclidean plane, with inner product <u,v>=Re(conj(u)v). For z=alpha+i beta set xi_i=Poly_i(z), gamma_i=z Poly_i(z) for 0<=i<=n, and xi_*=gamma_*=0. Put kappa=alpha-|z|^2 and I_n={*,0,...,n}. For each ordered pair define
q_ij=<gamma_j,xi_i-xi_j>+||gamma_i-gamma_j||^2/2,
regarded as the weight on the directed edge j->i. For each directed simple cycle C in I_n define
Area_C=sum_{j->i in C} Im(conj(xi_j)xi_i),
Energy_C=sum_{j->i in C}|xi_i-xi_j|^2.
Define D_h to be the set of z satisfying kappa Energy_C>=2|beta Area_C| for every such cycle.

(a) The data (xi_i,gamma_i), including the optimum sample *, are interpolable by a finite everywhere-defined convex differentiable function f:R^2->R with 1-Lipschitz gradient and grad f(0)=0 if and only if z belongs to D_h. For z in D_h let ell_i be the largest q-weight of a directed path i->*, allowing the empty path at *, and put v_i=-ell_i. The maxima exist over simple paths and ell_*=0. There is such an f with f(xi_i)=v_i for every i. These values are simultaneously maximal among all interpolating f satisfying f(0)=0. Its exact GD recursion for h starts at xi_0=1, and its normalized terminal error is
Gap_h(z):=-2ell_n.
In particular
|z|^2|Poly_n(z)|^2 <= Gap_h(z) <= (2alpha-|z|^2)|Poly_n(z)|^2.

(b) The parameter lens
Lens_n={alpha+i beta: alpha-alpha^2-beta^2>=|beta|cot(pi/(n+2))}
is contained in D_h for every h.

(c) Let Cheb_j be the polynomials defined by Cheb_0(x)=1, Cheb_1(x)=x and Cheb_{j+1}(x)=2xCheb_j(x)-Cheb_{j-1}(x). For every n>=2 define the positive schedule Chi_n, in any permutation, by the n entries
sec^2((2k-1)pi/(2(2n+1))), k=1,...,n.
Its terminal polynomial is (-1)^n Cheb_{2n+1}(sqrt(z))/((2n+1)sqrt(z)), interpreted as a polynomial including z=0, and its sum is s(Chi_n)=2n(n+1)/3. For all z in Lens_n,
Gap_{Chi_n}(z) <= 2cosh^2(2)/(2n+1)^2.

For an arbitrary schedule of length n>=1 define
V_lens(h)=max{sup_{z in Lens_n} Gap_h(z), 1/(2s(h)+1)}.
Let W_n(h) be the project worst-case normalized objective error, namely the supremum of 2(f(x_n)-f_star)/(L||x_0-x_star||^2) over every finite dimension, convex differentiable f with L-Lipschitz gradient and a minimizer x_star, and x_0!=x_star, using x_{t+1}=x_t-(h_t/L)grad f(x_t). Here L>0 is fixed and s(h)=sum h_t. Then V_lens(h)<=W_n(h). For the exact benchmark U_n^ZJ=1/(2S_bullet(n)+1) defined by the recursions in predecessor b98bb807d514e80d, and p=log_2 3 from that predecessor, the explicit schedules satisfy
0<=V_lens(Chi_n)/U_n^ZJ
 <= 2cosh^2(2)(1+4n^p)/n^2 ->0.
Thus the universal-lens witnesses, even augmented by the canonical sum-based Huber witness, cannot prove the required uniform matching lower bound. This is a limitation of that witness family only, not an upper bound on W_n(Chi_n) and not a decision of the unrestricted minimax target.

## Proof

1. Necessary smooth-convex inequality.
For any convex differentiable 1-smooth f and any x,y, put k(u)=f(u)-<grad f(y),u>. Convexity and grad k(y)=0 imply k(y)<=k(u). Integrating the Lipschitz gradient along a segment gives k(u+d)<=k(u)+<grad k(u),d>+||d||^2/2. Take d=-grad k(u) and combine these inequalities to obtain
f(x)>=f(y)+<grad f(y),x-y>+||grad f(x)-grad f(y)||^2/2. (1)
Applied to any proposed interpolant, (1) says its values obey f_i-f_j>=q_ij.

2. Cycle identity and necessity.
For complex u,v, <u,v>=Re(conj(u)v). Expand
<z xi_j,xi_i-xi_j>
=alpha Re(conj(xi_j)(xi_i-xi_j))+beta Im(conj(xi_j)xi_i).
The real part is (|xi_i|^2-|xi_j|^2-|xi_i-xi_j|^2)/2. Also
||gamma_i-gamma_j||^2=|z|^2|xi_i-xi_j|^2.
Therefore
q_ij=(alpha/2)(|xi_i|^2-|xi_j|^2)+beta Im(conj(xi_j)xi_i)
      -(kappa/2)|xi_i-xi_j|^2. (2)
The norm-difference terms telescope on a cycle, whose weight is beta Area_C-kappa Energy_C/2. Both orientations are available, and their areas have opposite signs but equal energies. Consequently nonpositivity of every cycle is equivalent to the definition of D_h. Summing (1) on cycles proves the necessary implication in (a). The cycle *->0->* also forces kappa>=0.

3. Values from paths.
Assume z is in D_h. Every closed walk is a sum of simple directed cycles, after splitting at repeated vertices; thus every closed walk has nonpositive weight. Any path to * can have repeated vertices deleted, and deleting the intervening nonpositive cycle cannot reduce the weight. There are finitely many simple paths on I_n, so ell_i is finite and is a maximum over them. At * the empty path has weight zero and every closed walk has weight at most zero, so ell_*=0.
Concatenate edge j->i with a maximum path i->*, then delete cycles if needed. It follows that ell_j>=q_ij+ell_i. Hence v_i=-ell_i satisfies v_i-v_j>=q_ij. For any other feasible values F_i with F_*=0, summing the inequalities along a path i->* yields -F_i at least the weight of that path. Thus F_i<=-ell_i=v_i for every i.

4. A finite-dimensional global interpolant.
Define on the real plane
H(g)=max_{i in I_n}{<gamma_i,xi_i>-v_i+<xi_i,g-gamma_i>+||g-gamma_i||^2/2}.
This is the maximum of finitely many quadratics with Hessian equal to the identity. It is finite, continuous and 1-strongly convex. The inequality v_j-v_i>=<gamma_i,xi_j-xi_i>+||gamma_j-gamma_i||^2/2 is exactly the assertion that at g=gamma_i the j-th quadratic is at most the i-th quadratic. Thus
H(gamma_i)=<gamma_i,xi_i>-v_i.
The i-th quadratic is active and has derivative xi_i at gamma_i. Its supporting affine function is below H, so xi_i belongs to the subdifferential of H at gamma_i.

Define f(x)=sup_g{<x,g>-H(g)}. For each fixed x, the maximizer g(x) exists and is unique: H(g)-<x,g> is continuous, strongly convex, and coercive because even any one term of the defining maximum has leading term ||g||^2/2. Optimality says x belongs to the subdifferential of H at g(x). Strong convexity implies that for all x,y,
<x-y,g(x)-g(y)>>=||g(x)-g(y)||^2.
To justify this implication, strong convexity and x in partial H(u) imply H(v)>=H(u)+<x,v-u>+||v-u||^2/2: combine H((1-t)u+tv)>=H(u)+t<x,v-u> with the strong-convexity upper bound along the segment, divide by t>0, and let t decrease to zero. Write this inequality for u=g(x),v=g(y) and in the reverse direction, then add. Cauchy--Schwarz gives ||g(x)-g(y)||<=||x-y||.
The definition of f yields, for every displacement d,
<g(x),d><=f(x+d)-f(x)<=<g(x+d),d>.
It follows that the difference between f(x+d)-f(x) and <g(x),d> lies in [0,||d||^2]. Therefore f is differentiable with gradient g(x), and that gradient is 1-Lipschitz. It is finite by existence of the maximizers and convex as a supremum of affine functions. The subgradient condition xi_i in partial H(gamma_i) gives g(xi_i)=gamma_i and f(xi_i)=v_i. At the optimum sample this gives f(0)=0 and grad f(0)=0, so convexity makes 0 a minimizer.

The prefix-polynomial identity Poly_{i+1}=(1-h_i z)Poly_i gives xi_{i+1}=xi_i-h_i gamma_i. Thus the interpolation is a legal GD trajectory, with ||xi_0||=1. Apply (1) in both directions between n and *:
v_n>=||gamma_n||^2/2,
v_n<=<gamma_n,xi_n>-||gamma_n||^2/2.
Since <gamma_n,xi_n>=alpha|Poly_n(z)|^2, these are the two bounds in (a).

5. A discrete area-energy inequality.
Consider any r>=2 complex numbers w_0,...,w_{r-1}, with w_r=w_0. Put
d_k=(1/r)sum_{j=0}^{r-1}w_j exp(-2pi i k j/r), 0<=k<r.
The finite geometric-sum identity reconstructs w_j=sum_k d_k exp(2pi i k j/r). The same identity, on expanding the quadratic sums, gives
sum_j Im(conj(w_j)w_{j+1})=r sum_k |d_k|^2 sin(2pi k/r),
sum_j |w_{j+1}-w_j|^2=4r sum_k |d_k|^2 sin^2(pi k/r).
The k=0 terms vanish. For 1<=k<r, the ratio of the absolute sine coefficient in the first sum to the coefficient in the second is (1/2)|cot(pi k/r)|<=cot(pi/r)/2. The triangle inequality proves
|sum_j Im(conj(w_j)w_{j+1})| <= (cot(pi/r)/2)sum_j |w_{j+1}-w_j|^2. (3)
This also covers r=2, where the left side and cot(pi/2) are zero.
Every simple cycle on I_n has length r<=n+2. The function cot(pi/r) increases as the integer r>=2 increases. From (3) and the defining inequality for Lens_n one gets kappa Energy_C>=2|beta Area_C|. This proves (b).

6. Explicit real positive Chebyshev steps and their sum.
The recurrence for Cheb_j, the cosine addition formula, and induction give Cheb_j(cos t)=cos(jt). Its parity is the parity of j and its degree is j. Put m=2n+1. The odd polynomial Cheb_m(x) is divisible by x; hence (-1)^n Cheb_m(sqrt(z))/(m sqrt(z)) is a real polynomial of degree n. Differentiating Cheb_m(cos t)=cos(mt) at t=pi/2 gives Cheb_m'(0)=(-1)^n m, so its value at z=0 is 1.
The positive roots of Cheb_m are cos((2k-1)pi/(2m)), k=1,...,n. Squaring them gives exactly the distinct roots lambda_k=cos^2((2k-1)pi/(2m)) in (0,1) of the degree-n polynomial. It follows from the value at zero that the polynomial equals product_k(1-z/lambda_k). Thus its reciprocal roots are precisely the stated positive schedule, regardless of their order.

Twice differentiating the cosine identity and eliminating the angle gives the polynomial identity
(1-x^2)Cheb_m''(x)-xCheb_m'(x)+m^2 Cheb_m(x)=0.
Differentiation at x=0 gives Cheb_m'''(0)=-(m^2-1)Cheb_m'(0). The coefficient of z in (-1)^n Cheb_m(sqrt(z))/(m sqrt(z)) is consequently -(m^2-1)/6. The coefficient of z in the product of schedule factors is minus their sum. Hence s(Chi_n)=(m^2-1)/6=2n(n+1)/3.

7. The lens lies in an ellipse of small width.
Let n>=2, N=n+2, theta=pi/N, t=tan(theta)>0, and u=asinh(t/2)>0. For z=alpha+i beta in Lens_n,
0<=alpha<=1 and |beta|<=t alpha(1-alpha),
because alpha-alpha^2-beta^2>=0 and dropping beta^2 only weakens the defining inequality. Put s=alpha(1-alpha), so 0<=s<=1/4. Since sinh(u)=t/2 and cosh(u)>=1,
(2alpha-1)^2/cosh^2(u)+(2beta)^2/sinh^2(u)
 <=1-4s+16s^2<=1. (4)

For completeness (4) implies there is a complex w with |Im w|<=u and z=(1+cos w)/2. If beta=0, take a real w with cos w=2alpha-1. If beta!=0, the continuous function
v -> (2alpha-1)^2/cosh^2(v)+(2beta)^2/sinh^2(v)
decreases from infinity as v increases from zero and is at most 1 at v=u. Choose v in (0,u] where its value is 1, and choose a real angle a with
cos a=(2alpha-1)/cosh(v), sin a=-2beta/sinh(v).
Then w=a+i v has cos w=2alpha-1+2i beta.
A square root of z is cos(w/2). The cosine identity for Cheb_m holds for complex arguments as well, because it follows from polynomial recurrence and the complex cosine addition formula. Thus
|z| |Poly_n(z)|^2
 =|Cheb_m(sqrt(z))|^2/m^2
 =|cos(mw/2)|^2/m^2
 <=cosh^2(m u/2)/m^2. (5)
The polynomial interpretation covers z=0 as well; both sides of the equality before the bound are zero there.

The inequality asinh(t/2)<=t/2 follows by integrating its derivative, which is at most 1 on the nonnegative line. The function tan is convex on [0,pi/4], so it lies below its chord and tan(theta)<=4theta/pi=4/N. Therefore m u/2<=m t/4<=m/N<2. Combining (5) with (a) and alpha<=|z| proves
Gap_{Chi_n}(z)<=(2alpha-|z|^2)|Poly_n(z)|^2
 <=2|z||Poly_n(z)|^2<=2cosh^2(2)/(2n+1)^2.

8. Canonical Huber witness and the exact-profile comparison.
For any h>=0 let S=s(h) and delta=1/(2S+1). In dimension one define
f_delta(x)=x^2/2 for |x|<=delta, and delta|x|-delta^2/2 otherwise.
The matching derivative is x clipped to [-delta,delta], hence is continuous, nondecreasing and 1-Lipschitz. The function is convex, differentiable, 1-smooth and minimized at 0. Starting from x_0=1, every partial sum of h lies in [0,S]. Induction gives x_i=1-delta sum_{t<i}h_t>=1-delta S=(S+1)/(2S+1)>=delta. Thus every gradient equals delta and
2f_delta(x_n)=2delta(1-delta S)-delta^2=1/(2S+1).
Multiplying either this function or the interpolant from part 4 by L produces the same normalized error for the specified arbitrary L>0. Consequently each term defining V_lens(h) is a legal lower-bound witness for W_n(h), proving V_lens(h)<=W_n(h).

For Chi_n the Huber term is
1/(1+4n(n+1)/3)<=3/(4n^2).
Part 7 bounds its lens term by 2cosh^2(2)/(2n+1)^2. Since 2cosh^2(2)>=2>3/4, both terms are at most 2cosh^2(2)/n^2.
Verified predecessor b98bb807d514e80d states, for exactly the project phi/psi recursions and the project normalization, that B_n=2S_bullet(n)+1<=1+4n^p with p=log_2 3<2. Multiply the preceding bound on V_lens by B_n to obtain the displayed ratio estimate. It tends to zero because n^{-2}->0 and n^{p-2}->0. This uses no assumed limit for S_bullet(n)/n^rho and no numerical estimate of the profile constant.

Only the explicitly restricted witness family was upper bounded. None of the calculations gives an upper bound for the unrestricted W_n on the displayed schedules.
