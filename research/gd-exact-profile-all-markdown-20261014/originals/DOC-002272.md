## 1. A fixed terminal law at plus/minus 2 cannot support a standalone all-word theorem

The R9 report specifies a greedy inverse candidate starting from the mean-zero terminal law Y=+2 or -2 with equal aggregate probabilities. It correctly leaves its original-variance estimate unproved. The following calculation shows that a standalone estimate V0<=3B_n/(1-o(1)) for that fixed terminal law is FALSE on unit-step words. This does not refute its inherited-H_m performance, a portfolio including standard Huber witnesses, or the general inverse construction with a different terminal law.

For any convex1-smooth function with minimizer x_star, unit-step GD obeys

\[
 \|x_i-x_\star\|^2-\|x_{i+1}-x_\star\|^2
 =2\langle g_i,x_i-x_\star\rangle-\|g_i\|^2
 \ge2(f_i-f_\star).
\]

The smooth-convex interpolation inequality at x_i,x_(i+1), together with x_i-x_(i+1)=g_i, gives

\[
 f_i-f_{i+1}\ge
 \langle g_{i+1},g_i\rangle+\tfrac12\|g_i-g_{i+1}\|^2
 =\tfrac12(\|g_i\|^2+\|g_{i+1}\|^2).
\]

Cocoercivity also gives

\[
 \|g_i-g_{i+1}\|^2\le\langle g_i-g_{i+1},g_i\rangle,
 \qquad\|g_{i+1}\|^2\le\|g_i\|^2.
\]

Consequently f_i-f_n>=(n-i)||g_n||^2. Summing the distance inequalities and then these value inequalities proves the universal terminal bound

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \|x_0-x_\star\|^2\ge
 \|x_n-x_\star\|^2+2n(f_n-f_\star)+n(n+1)\|g_n\|^2.
 \quad}                                                     \tag{1}
\]

For the fixed probability-simplex terminal law Y=+/-2, the unique minimum is0, ||Y||^2=4, its projection is g_Y=+/-1, and F_K(Y)=3/2. Refining atoms with identical terminal values changes none of these quantities. Thus EVERY admissible preimage trajectory with that same terminal law, not merely the greedy one, satisfies

\[
 V_0\ge4+3n+n(n+1)=(n+2)^2.
\]

Equality is achieved by the two-atom noncrossing Huber trajectory starting at +/-(n+2), taking n unit steps and ending at +/-2. Its exact best normalized payoff under the fixed terminal requirement is therefore

\[
 \frac3{(n+2)^2}.
\]

Accepted fact931b9653ce0f7201 gives B_n<=(12/5)(n+1)^rho, rho=log_2(1+sqrt2)<2. Hence

\[
 B_n\frac3{(n+2)^2}
 \le\frac{36}{5}\frac{(n+1)^\rho}{(n+2)^2}\longrightarrow0.
\]

The fixed-terminal standalone estimate should therefore be marked **REFUTED on unit-step words**, rather than left as a conjectural all-word bound. Standard Huber already handles those words, so this does not reject a combined adversarial portfolio on words where that baseline is deficient.

## 2. A benchmark-scaled terminal law covers the entire low-mass case exactly

For a horizon n define

\[
 Q_n=(B_n+1)/2\ge1,
 \qquad \bar S_n=(B_n-1)/2=S_\bullet(n).
\]

Use the terminal law +/-Q_n with equal probabilities in the same two-atom projection simplex. Its exact terminal twice-value is B_n. For ANY prescribed nonnegative word of total mass S, the actual initial law +/-(Q_n+S) stays on its two affine Huber rays at every physical query and ends at +/-Q_n. The original variance is (Q_n+S)^2. If S<=barS_n, then

\[
 (Q_n+S)^2\le B_n^2,
 \qquad W_n(h)\ge\frac{B_n}{(Q_n+S)^2}\ge\frac1{B_n}.
\tag{2}
\]

All steps, including zero steps, are retained; no radius or minimizer is reset. Thus this choice of terminal scale covers the entire low-total-mass case. A natural sufficient target for a further inverse construction on the remaining words is original variance at most (1+o(1))*B_n^2 while preserving terminal twice-value B_n. No such high-mass estimate is proved here, and the two-point terminal shape is not assumed sufficient for every word. It may be changed if a genuine construction needs more terminal geometry.

## 3. One-step inverse optimization must account for the preceding prefix cost

This is a conditional attained identity within the same finite-law construction. It is a possible starting point for a further direct proof, not a composition theorem.

For a finite mean-zero next law Y, h>1, d=h-1, choose a_*>=max(d,maxY), t=a_*-h, Z=a_*-Y, and a conditional right probability omega satisfying E[omega Z]=d. The actual inverse values are X_left=t-Z and X_right=t+Z/d. Let J=E[omega Z^2]. Then

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 V(X)&=V(Y)+2h a_*-h^2+(d^{-2}-1)J,\\
 F_K(X)&=a_*-h+\tfrac12+J/(2d^2),\\
 \|P_KX\|^2&=J/d^2-1.
 \end{aligned}                                               \tag{3}
\]

Fix local nonnegative cost coefficients A,B, distinct from the benchmark B_n, and define

\[
 \mathcal C_{A,B}(X)=V(X)+2A F_K(X)+B\|P_KX\|^2.
\]

Substitution into (3) gives the EXACT full cost

\[
 \mathcal C_{A,B}(X)
 =V(Y)+2(h+A)a_*-h^2-2Ah+A-B
       +\left(\frac{1+A+B}{d^2}-1\right)J.
\tag{4}
\]

In particular, minimizing only V(X) at the latest reverse step can choose the wrong branch for the cost imposed by earlier physical steps. The sign deciding which tail to allocate is now 1+A+B-d^2, not merely 1-d^2.

Suppose

\[
 C_0=1+A+B>d^2,\quad T=h+A,\quad w=C_0/d^2-1>0,
 \quad\gamma=d+2T/w.
\]

Choose the unique z with E(Y-z)_+=gamma, select all upper-tail atoms above z and optionally some boundary mass at z, and let p be the total selected mass. The actual threshold parameter is

\[
 a_*=z+(d+\gamma)/p.
\]

Its support guard is p(maxY-z)<=d+gamma. Mean zero also gives z>=-gamma, so a_*>=d. When that REAL guard holds, the construction is admissible and

\[
 J=(d-\gamma)(a_*-z)+\mathbb E(Y-z)_+^2.
\]

The coefficient of a_* in (4) then cancels. This is the slope-zero condition for the corresponding upper-tail fractional-knapsack optimization; if a boundary atom supplies an interval of equally minimizing thresholds, its mass may affect earlier-step costs even when (4) is unchanged.

With U_gamma(Y)=2gamma F_K(Y/gamma), the attained cost is

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \mathcal C_{A,B}(X)
 =V(Y)+T U_\gamma(Y)+B_\gamma\|P_K(Y/\gamma)\|^2,
 \quad B_\gamma=w\gamma^2-T\gamma=\gamma(wd+T)>0.
 \quad}                                                       \tag{5}
\]

To verify the constant term, E(Y-z)_+^2=gamma^2(||P_K(Y/gamma)||^2+1), and w gamma=wd+2T. The leftover constant in (4) is

    -h^2-2Ah+A-B + w*gamma*d = 0,

because w*gamma*d=C_0-d^2+2Td and h=d+1. This proves (5) by exact algebra.

For A=B=0 and 1<h<2, gamma=h(h-1)/(2-h), B_gamma=h^2/(2-h), recovering the R9 native identity. At h=sqrt2 it reduces to

    V(X)=V(Y)+2sqrt2 F_K(Y)+(2+sqrt2)||P_KY||^2

under the corresponding real support guard. This is the constructive connection to the primitive terminal potential.

The evaluations at Y/gamma in (5) are auxiliary evaluations of the SAME function on the SAME refined finite probability space. They are not permission to run the remaining physical word from Y/gamma, change its steps, or replace its body. The resulting auxiliary-threshold cost is not automatically closed under another application of (4). Propagating the actual tail guards, boundary masses, and such cost data through the full word remains unproved.

### The primitive concatenation root appears in this attained cost algebra

Take the local cost coefficients A=C_L-1 and B=(C_L-1)C_L, so C_0=C_L^2. For a physical bridge h=1+d with 0<d<C_L, formula (5) gives

\[
 T=C_L+d,\qquad
 \gamma=\frac{d(C_L+d)}{C_L-d},\qquad
 B_\gamma=\frac{C_L(C_L+d)^2}{C_L-d}.
\]

If d is the Zhang--Jiang primitive joining root

\[
 d(C_L+C_R+d)=C_L C_R,
\]

then EXACTLY

\[
 \gamma=C_R,\qquad
 B_\gamma=(C_L+d)(C_L+C_R+d).
\]

Indeed the root equation is d(C_L+d)=C_R(C_L-d). Substitution proves both identities. For balanced capacities C_L=C_R=C, d=(sqrt2-1)C; the support guard in (5) becomes p(maxY-z)/C<=sqrt2 at the actual stop-loss level C. Thus the primitive joining root, not merely its leaf value, appears in the conditional actual inverse-cost calculation.

This identifies the scalar coefficients under a REAL tail guard. It does not prove that an earlier arbitrary prefix has cost exactly C_(A,B) for every endpoint law, or that the same law remains attainable through the two complete children. Those joint attainability and concentration requirements are still unproved. The displayed root identity is not an all-word converse to the Zhang--Jiang theorem.
