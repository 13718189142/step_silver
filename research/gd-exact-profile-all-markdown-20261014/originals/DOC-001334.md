## 2. Why simply adding independent coordinates fails

The scalar asymmetric Huber function

    phi_q(x)=x^2/2                     if x>=-q,
             -q x-q^2/2               if x<=-q          (4)

has continuous nondecreasing 1-Lipschitz derivative, unique minimizer0,
and is an instance of the accepted single-cut construction. From x_0=1,
the original first step ell sends it to -a. Its remaining gradients are
all -q and its final value has doubled gap a q=a^2/(2T+1).

On two different tail samples i<j, both ordered scalar interpolation
residuals are zero because that branch is affine. If one replaces a scalar
gradient z_i by (sqrt(1-epsilon)z_i,sqrt(epsilon)|z_i|e_i), uses the actual
same-step positions, and simply scales all values by 1-epsilon, the reverse
residual on such a pair becomes epsilon q^2>0. Thus the naive full-support
lift is NOT interpolable. This is an exact original-row obstruction to
that shortcut, for every epsilon>0.

The next sections supply and price the missing strict slack before adding
the independent coordinates.

## 3. A strict scalar function on the actual schedule

Define one scalar function, fixed before the run,

    f_mu(x)=(1-2mu)phi_q(x)+mu x^2/2.                  (5)

It is convex, differentiable, and has derivative Lipschitz constant
1-mu<1. Its curvature where defined is either mu or 1-mu. Its unique
minimizer is0. Write x_i for its actual GD trajectory from x_0=1 and
z_i=f_mu'(x_i). The first actual block is the physical leaf ell:

    z_0=1-mu,
    x_1=1-ell(1-mu)=-a+mu ell.                        (6)

For comparison define the unmodified Huber tail locations

    x_i^0=-a+q sum_(t=1)^(i-1)h_t, 1<=i<=n.

They lie in [-a,-(a+q)/2], so are at most -a/2. We now prove that all
actual x_i, i>=1, remain on the NEGATIVE FLAT branch of phi_q. Although
phi_q is flat in its derivative there, f_mu has the small positive
curvature mu, which is essential.

Put D_mu=2mu(1+S)=q/[32(1+S)]<q/32. As long as x_i<=-q, the true recursion is

    z_i=-(1-2mu)q+mu x_i,
    x_(i+1)=(1-mu h_i)x_i+(1-2mu)q h_i.              (7)

Writing d_i=x_i-x_i^0, it becomes

    d_1=mu ell,
    d_(i+1)=(1-mu h_i)d_i-mu h_i(2q+x_i^0).          (8)

All coefficients 1-mu h_i are nonnegative. Also
|2q+x_i^0|<=a+2q<1. Induction in (8) therefore gives
|d_i|<=mu(ell+sum_(1<=t<i)h_t)<=mu S<D_mu. Its own branch assumption is
preserved, since

    x_i<=-a/2+D_mu<-a/2+q/32
                       <=-47a/96<-q.                (9)

This proves (7)-(9) for every original sample, including n; it is not a
calculation on a reset or compressed tail. The closed formula, if desired,
is x_i=D+(x_1-D)product_(t=1)^(i-1)(1-mu h_t), where
D=(1-2mu)q/mu. Equations (6)-(7) specify all data without an optimization.

For i>=1 the gradients z_i are negative. Their magnitudes A_i=|z_i|
satisfy q/2<=A_i<=2q<1, using (2), (9), and mu<q. Also A_0=1-mu<=1.
Every tail step increases x_i by at least q/2 because h_i>1.
Consequently every pair of distinct sampled scalar positions, INCLUDING
the optimum0, is separated by at least q/2. For pairs involving the
optimum use |x_i|>=47a/96>q for i>=1 and x_0=1; for a pair involving
x_0 and a tail position use their opposite signs.

The actual doubled terminal value retains a quantitative fraction of the
known scalar payoff. Since both x_n and x_n^0 lie in the branch (4),

    2f_mu(x_n)>=(1-2mu)[a q-2q D_mu]
               >=(1-2mu)[a q-q^2/16]
               >=(31/32)(47/48)a q>(3/4)a q.         (10)

This explicitly prices the regularization and the change in trajectory.

## 4. Uniform strict slack for EVERY ordered scalar pair

For any scalar arguments x,y, put d=y-x and e=phi_q'(y)-phi_q'(x),
and set alpha=1-2mu. The necessary inequality in accepted
`1252b8823bb5a290` applied to phi_q says

    phi_q(y)-phi_q(x)-phi_q'(x)d>=e^2/2.

For f_mu, its gradient difference is alpha e+mu d. Expanding its signed
ordered residual gives

    Q_mu(x,y)
      =f_mu(x)-f_mu(y)+f_mu'(x)d
                            +[f_mu'(x)-f_mu'(y)]^2/2
      <=-alpha e^2/2-mu d^2/2+(alpha e+mu d)^2/2
       =-alpha mu(e-d/2)^2-mu d^2/4
      <=-mu d^2/4.                                    (11)

This holds in BOTH orientations, for every x,y, including zero.
Combining the actual sample separation with (11), define

    nu=mu q^2/16>0,
    epsilon=nu/[4(1+nu)] in (0,1/4).                  (12)

Every original scalar residual between distinct samples in
{star,0,...,n}, with star=(0,0,0), is at most -nu. Thus the smallest actual
nonempty block (6), its optimum incidences, both directions of every later
pair, and every terminal pair have a common explicit negative margin.

The displacement in (11) is the actual scalar displacement, equivalently
d=-sum h_t z_t between the corresponding chronological samples. The
signed contributions from the initial positive gradient and the later
negative gradients have not been changed or deleted.

## 5. Dense vector lift with all original pairs

Work initially in the Euclidean space R direct-sum R^N. Let e_i,
0<=i<=n, be its standard orthonormal vectors in the second summand.
Prescribe the full data by

    G_i=(sqrt(1-epsilon) z_i, sqrt(epsilon) A_i e_i),
    X_0=(sqrt(1-epsilon),0),
    X_i=X_0-sum_(t<i)h_t G_t,
    f_i=(1-epsilon)f_mu(x_i),                         (13)

and include the optimum jet (0,0,0). The first coordinate of X_i is
sqrt(1-epsilon)x_i. Its second coordinate is
sqrt(epsilon)Z_i, where Z_i=-sum_(t<i)h_t A_t e_t.

The original full residual splits EXACTLY, with the full signed scalar
displacement intact, as

    Q_ij=(1-epsilon)Q_mu(x_i,x_j)+epsilon R_ij.        (14)

For i<j finite, the added-coordinate residuals are

    R_ij=(1/2-h_i)A_i^2+A_j^2/2,
    R_ji=(A_i^2+A_j^2)/2.                            (15)

For an optimum pair, R_(i,star)=R_(star,i)=A_i^2/2,
since the i-th coordinate of Z_i is zero. As every A_i<=1 and h_i>=0,
ALL these residuals are at most one. Thus (11)-(12) prove the uniform
strict original-pair bound

    Q_ij<=-(1-epsilon)nu+epsilon=-3nu/4<0             (16)

on every distinct ordered pair of {star,0,...,n}. In particular (15) at
indices0,1 resolves the very first original physical block with its
genuinely negative correlation, before any appeal to a larger block.

This is a full pairwise calculation, not a covariance feasibility test.
The Gram and anchor products are

    <G_i,G_j>=(1-epsilon)z_i z_j+epsilon A_i^2 1_(i=j),
    <X_0,G_i>=(1-epsilon)z_i, ||X_0||^2=1-epsilon.    (17)

For all i>=1, <G_0,G_i><0. For every distinct pair, the product in (17)
is nonzero. Accordingly there is no orthogonal queried pair and no

## 6. An explicit positive finite character law and chronological labels

The same geometry is also a literal finite character construction.
Use d=m+1 bits and D_law=2^d=2N. Give physical sample i the label

    lambda_0=(0,binary(0)),
    lambda_i=(1,binary(i)), 1<=i<=n.                  (18)

The first bit records the sign, the other m bits record the original
physical index. The labels are distinct. They do not reorder or duplicate
any sample or insert any step.

Let s range over {0,1}^d, write e_sig for the vector with its first bit1
and all other bits0, and define

    chi_s(lambda)=(-1)^(s dot lambda),
    w_s=(1-epsilon)1_(s=e_sig)+epsilon/D_law.           (19)

Every weight is strictly positive and their sum is one. Define
u_i=(sqrt(w_s)chi_s(lambda_i))_s, and let eta_0=1, eta_i=-1 for i>=1.
Finite character orthogonality gives, with no positivity inference from
finite-field algebra,

    <u_i,u_j>=(1-epsilon)eta_i eta_j+epsilon 1_(i=j).   (20)

Therefore the actual gradients g_i=A_i u_i have exactly (17). This law
has dense correlation support: for every nonzero binary label difference
the kernel is (1-epsilon)(-1)^(its first bit), never zero.

For an entirely explicit anchor, including its true norm, put

    D_0=epsilon+(1-epsilon)N,
    lambda_extra=(0,binary(1)),
    v_s=chi_s(lambda_extra)/(D_law sqrt(w_s)),
    v_unit=v/||v||,
    Xhat_0=(1-epsilon)/D_0 sum_(i=0)^n eta_i u_i
                 +sqrt(epsilon(1-epsilon)/D_0) v_unit. (21)

The label lambda_extra is not one of (18). Pairing a differing bit proves
<v,u_i>=0 for every i. Also
sum_i eta_i<u_i,u_j>=D_0 eta_j. Equations (20)-(21) give

    <Xhat_0,g_i>=(1-epsilon)z_i,
    ||Xhat_0||^2=(1-epsilon)^2 N/D_0
                         +epsilon(1-epsilon)/D_0
                   =1-epsilon.                      (22)

Thus the anchor and gradients in (21) have the identical FULL Gram data
of (13), including the initial point. Set
Xhat_i=Xhat_0-sum_(t<i)h_t g_t and retain the values f_i in (13).
All original residuals (14)-(16) hold verbatim. The term involving v_unit
is an explicitly priced orthogonal component of the anchor, not an
unrecorded radius change. The dimension increase changes no time index.

## 7. ONE static function, exact values, unique minimizer, and true radius

Use either the direct-sum realization (13) or the character realization
(18)-(22); write X_i,g_i for the chosen positions and gradients. Define

    y_i=X_i-g_i, delta_i=f_i-||g_i||^2/2,
    H_dense(v)=||v||^2/2
                    +max{0,max_(0<=i<=n)(<y_i,v>-delta_i)},
    F_dense(X)=sup_v(<X,v>-H_dense(v)).                (23)

The optimum row of (16) gives delta_i>=3nu/4>0. By accepted
`1252b8823bb5a290`, the complete pair system (16) and formula (23) produce
an everywhere finite convex differentiable 1-smooth function satisfying
F_dense(X_i)=f_i and grad F_dense(X_i)=g_i at EVERY supplied sample.
Its actual GD is therefore exactly the original physical trajectory.
This uses one fixed function for the whole horizon, not changing
functions at a bridge.

Since every delta_i is strictly positive, H_dense(v)=||v||^2/2 in some
neighborhood of zero. It is everywhere at least ||v||^2/2, so
F_dense(0)=0. For any X!=0 choose a sufficiently small 0<t<1; then
H_dense(tX)=t^2||X||^2/2, implying
F_dense(X)>=(t-t^2/2)||X||^2>0. Hence

    argmin F_dense={0},
    R_dense^2=||X_0||^2=1-epsilon,
    V_dense=2(1-epsilon)f_mu(x_n)/(1-epsilon)
                =2f_mu(x_n).                         (24)

Thus the dense character lift preserves the strict scalar instance's
normalized payoff EXACTLY. The only loss from the known Huber calibration
is the already bounded scalar strictification (10). This proves the first
inequality in (3), with actual values, minimizer and radius supplied.

For original smoothness L, replace F_dense by L F_dense and use physical
GD steps h_i/L. Scaling X by R_dense yields initial distance one if desired

## 8. Comparison with the ORIGINAL exact benchmark

Here B_n is precisely the accepted Zhang--Jiang denominator
2S_bullet(n)+1, not 1+sum h or a newly defined profile. Recall from
accepted `b98bb807d514e80d` the original mass recurrences

    C_0=B_0=1,
    C_n=max_(0<=k<n)Omega(C_k,C_(n-k-1)),
    B_n=max_(0<=k<n)G(C_k,B_(n-k-1)),
    Omega(u,v)=[u+v+sqrt((u+v)^2+4uv)]/2,
    G(u,v)=2u+v/2+sqrt(v^2+8uv)/2.                    (25)

Both joins are increasing. Also G(u,v)>=Omega(u,v): after cancelling
u+v from twice both expressions, it suffices to square the nonnegative
quantities 3u+sqrt(v^2+8uv) and sqrt(u^2+6uv+v^2). The first square minus
the second is 8u^2+2uv+6u sqrt(v^2+8uv)>=0. Induction in (25) therefore
gives B_n>=C_n. Choosing the balanced split repeatedly, and using
Omega(u,u)=sigma u, gives

    B_(2^m-1)>=C_(2^m-1)>=sigma^m.                    (26)

The sum of the ACTUAL word has the exact closed form

    S=N-1+a_chr(c_chr^m-N)/(c_chr-2)
     =N-1+r_sum(c_chr^m-N),
    r_sum=delta/[c_chr(c_chr-2)].                      (27)

Indeed there are 2^(m-k-1) occurrences of level k. Summing their finite
geometric series yields (27), without changing any enclosing parameter.
Since c_chr>sigma, c_chr^2-2c_chr>1>delta, whence 0<r_sum<1 and

    S<c_chr^m-1,
    2T+1<2c_chr^m,
    c_chr^m=sigma^m(1+1/m)^m<3sigma^m.                (28)

For the last inequality expand the binomial: each coefficient after
division by m^k is at most 1/k!, and sum_(k>=0)1/k!<3 because
k!>=2^(k-1) for k>=1, with strict inequality for some k.

Combining (10), (24), (26)-(28), and a>6/25 gives

    B_n V_dense>(3/4) a^2/6=a^2/8
                  >9/1250>1/150.                    (29)

For a>6/25, use a=(sqrt(2)-1)(m-1)/(m+1),
(m-1)/(m+1)>=3/5, and sqrt(2)>7/5, all valid for m>=4.

This proves a positive original-B payoff for the specified dense signed
law family on every original enclosing horizon. It does not prove a
matching factor, and it says nothing comparable for arbitrary h solely
