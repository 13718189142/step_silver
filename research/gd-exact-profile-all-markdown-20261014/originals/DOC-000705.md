# Why creating penultimate flow first does not solve the stationary case

Status: unverified failure analysis of a specific proposed extension. The valid transfer and descent identities remain intact.

Consider a full matching stationary tuple (h,q,lambda,G,f) with q>0, h_{n-1}>0, all primal interpolation inequalities, dual PSD, complementarity, and the stepsize stationarity equations. Put p=g_{n-1}, g=g_n, delta=f_{n-1}-||p||^2/2.

## 1. Last-step stationarity selects an orthogonal terminal pair

For the SAME legal primal G selected by the full stationary tuple, one has

    <p,g>=0.                                               (1)

Here is a proof that does not assume a C2 function or differentiability of W. Realize the legal finite data by a convex 1-smooth interpolating function. Change only the final step b by a small real u. Then x_n(u)=x_n-u p, g_n(u)=g_n+O(u), and

    f_n(u)=f_n-u<p,g>+o(u).

Let D be the derivative of the full certificate matrix with respect to b, holding q and lambda fixed. The old matrix annihilates the old vector realization by PSD complementarity. Therefore its evaluation on the changed gradient vectors is O(u^2), and the full polynomial identity gives

    sum lambda_ij Q_ij(u)
       =u[<D,G>-2<p,g>]+o(u).

The left side is nonpositive for both signs of u and vanishes at zero. Consequently <D,G>=2<p,g>. The full stationarity equation <D,G>=0 proves (1). This argument keeps the same G; it does not substitute an arbitrary active hard function for the stationary one.

## 2. A positive margin in the penultimate unit-step residual

The interpolation inequality Q_{n,n-1}<=0, together with (1), gives

    f_{n-1}-||p||^2/2 >= f_n+||g||^2/2,
    delta >= q/2+||g||^2/2 > q/2.                          (2)

The strictness holds because q=2f_n>0 implies g_n!=0. If B=prod_t(1+h_t) is the elementary terminal radius bound for normalized initial distance, convexity further gives

    ||g||^2>=q^2/(4B^2),
    delta>=q/2+q^2/(8B^2).                                 (3)

This is a constraint on the actual full stationary primal, including the case q>tau.

## 3. Exact obstruction to certificate inflation followed by final-step blending

Suppose one first changes only the certificate at the SAME h, perhaps increasing its level from q to q', to create a positive new penultimate star-outgoing multiplier nu'. Every such feasible certificate must satisfy, on the old matching legal primal,

    q' >= q+nu' delta.                                     (4)

Indeed its PSD identity gives q'>=2f_n+nu' delta after the other nonnegative interpolation contributions are dropped.

The largest permitted final-step convex blend then certifies the value 2q'/(2+nu'). But (2) and (4) imply

    2q'/(2+nu')
       >= q + nu'||g_n||^2/(2+nu') > q.                    (5)

A smaller blend parameter gives no better certified level. Therefore the two-stage procedure

    raise or alter the certificate at fixed h to create nu'>0,
    then use the proved final-step convex-blend bound,

cannot prove a descent from a full stationary tuple. This is an exact finite inequality, not only a first-order obstruction, and it applies regardless of q-tau.

The original penultimate-flow descent still detects genuinely nonoptimal data, and the transport operation still preserves full feasibility. Equation (5) explains why those operations cannot be bootstrapped into a generic q>tau contradiction after full last-coordinate stationarity has already been reached.

## 4. Implication for the next mechanism

The surviving problem necessarily involves an earlier stepsize change, a nonlinear suffix response, or a quantitative global consequence of the full matrix beyond terminal coordinate optimality. The exact suffix counterexample in research/max2_earlier_reset_obstruction.md forbids carrying final-step convexity backward without additional structure.

The current terminal operations are complete useful constraints, but they do not prove Huber tightness of globally optimal schedules. Further work should not keep rediscovering last-coordinate descent under different notation.
