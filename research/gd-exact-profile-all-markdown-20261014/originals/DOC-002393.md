## 3. Target class: global properties and degenerate polytopes

For a nonempty compact convex K, define

    d(U) = min_(Y in K) ||U-Y||^2 / 2,
    P(U) = P_K(U).

Projection exists because K is compact, and is unique because the squared norm is strictly convex in Y. The normal condition is

    <U-P(U), Z-P(U)> <= 0  for all Z in K.

Writing this condition once for U with Z=P(V), and once for V with Z=P(U), then adding, gives

    <P(U)-P(V), U-V> >= ||P(U)-P(V)||^2.

Expanding the square of the residual difference proves

    ||(U-P(U))-(V-P(V))|| <= ||U-V||.

The trial point P(U) gives

    d(U+v)-d(U) <= <U-P(U),v> + ||v||^2/2.

The trial point P(U+v), used to bound d(U), gives

    d(U+v)-d(U) >= <U+v-P(U+v),v> - ||v||^2/2.

The Lipschitz residual makes the difference between either linear term and `<U-P(U),v>` of order at most `||v||^2`. Therefore d is differentiable with gradient U-P(U). This closes the minor normalization wording issue without invoking an external interpolation theorem.

Completing the square in the actual objective yields

    Phi(X) = <lambda,X> - ||lambda||^2/2 + d(X-lambda),
    grad Phi(X) = X-P_K(X-lambda).

Its gradient difference is a residual difference at inputs separated by the same vector, so its Lipschitz constant is at most one globally. Convexity follows from joint convexity of `<lambda,Y>+||X-Y||^2/2` and the trial point formed from convex combinations of minimizers. This argument has the correct inequality direction for partial minimization.

The exposing assumption gives `<lambda,Y> >= 0` for every Y in K, with equality only at Y=0. The norm term is also nonnegative. Since the minimum over K is attained, Phi(X)=0 forces its minimizing Y to be zero and X=Y. Conversely Phi(0)=0 by the trial point Y=0. Hence the complete minimizer set is exactly `{0}`.

None of these arguments needs K to have interior, full affine dimension, distinct listed generators, or simplex geometry. If K={0}, then Phi(X)=||X||^2/2; the exposing assumption is vacuous and the minimum remains unique. Lambda may be zero in this degenerate class member. The admissible subclass is nonempty, for example by taking K={0}, lambda=0, and any nonzero initial point in a positive finite dimension.

Every restricted instance is consequently an admissible original instance, with original minimizer zero and exactly the denominator `||X_0||^2`. Initial orthogonality is only an additional restriction. This proves the direction `W_aff <= W` without any interpolation or limiting argument.

## 4. Strict source: ordered interpolation, signs, and degeneracies

Fix the strict source used in Section 3 of the draft: mu-strong convexity with mu>0, smoothness bound ell with 0<ell<1, unique minimum at zero, and f(0)=0. For fixed i, the function

    q(x) = f(x)-<g_i,x>

is convex and ell-smooth, and its gradient vanishes at x_i. Thus x_i is a global minimizer of q. Applying the smooth descent inequality at x_j, with step 1/ell, gives

    f_j-f_i-<g_i,x_j-x_i> >= ||g_j-g_i||^2/(2 ell).

After rearrangement this is exactly the draft's orientation

    Q_ij <= -(1/ell-1)||g_i-g_j||^2/2.

The expression using y and delta is also correct: expanding

    delta_i-delta_j+<g_i,y_j-y_i>

leaves `f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2`. Thus the slack corresponds to the outgoing normal at row i, not its opposite orientation.

The optimum label is a legitimate sample of the same source. At the row `i=*`, `j` arbitrary, one obtains `Q_(*,j)=-delta_j`, hence

    delta_j >= gamma ||g_j||^2,  gamma=(1/ell-1)/2>0.

Strong convexity implies

    <g_i-g_j,x_i-x_j> >= mu ||x_i-x_j||^2.

Consequently equal source gradients force equal source points. Equal points then force equal values, y-vectors, and delta-heights. In particular a zero source gradient forces x_i=0, f_i=0, y_i=0, delta_i=0. Conversely delta_i=0 forces g_i=0 through the strict slack estimate. These implications justify every zero-denominator exclusion in M.

Repeated gradients do **not** imply equal lifted iterates: the scalars z_i may differ on a later revisit. The draft does not need that false implication. It only needs equality of Y_i and Y_j, which does follow from equality of the source gradients. Then the corresponding normal direction and its correction vanish exactly regardless of z_i or e_i. This is the correct treatment of repetitions and zero steps.

## 5. Static lift: every normal and a simultaneous finite B

For the fixed finite source trajectory, the recurrence for z consists of finitely many scalar affine operations on finite quantities. All z_i and e_i are finite and independent of B. No bounded-step or stability assumption is required for this statement.

In the lifted construction,

    <lambda_B,Y_i> = delta_i.

Every nonzero listed Y_i has positive delta_i. A convex combination of listed generators with zero height can assign positive weight only to zero generators, and is therefore zero. This establishes strict exposure on the entire polytope, not only on its generating list. The polytope is compact and has at most n+2 vertices; repetitions and redundant generators only decrease the vertex count.

Independently expanding the projection normal gives

    <G_i-lambda_B,Y_j-Y_i>
      = <g_i,y_j-y_i> + (e_i/B-B)(delta_j-delta_i)/B
      = Q_ij + e_i(delta_j-delta_i)/B^2.

The sign of both the `-lambda_B` term and the height difference matches the required projection condition for `X_i-lambda_B` at Y_i.

If g_i differs from g_j, the denominator `gamma||g_i-g_j||^2` is strictly positive. There are only finitely many such ordered pairs, including j=*. Every ratio in M is finite; its maximum is finite, even if some ratios are very large. If there are no distinct-gradient pairs, the stipulated value M=0 is harmless. For `B^2 >= max(1,2M)`, each positive correction is bounded by half of the corresponding strict interpolation margin. Negative corrections only help. Therefore every distinct-gradient normal is negative, and every equal-gradient normal is zero.

The index set covers i=0,...,n and j=*,0,...,n. It includes terminal row n, terminal comparison point n, every reversed ordered sample pair, and every sample-to-optimum row. Since the tested points generate K_B, linearity in the comparison point extends the inequality to every point of K_B.

The missing-looking optimum row is not actually missing: at the actual lifted optimum X=0, Y=0, the normal against any generator is

    <-lambda_B,Y_j> = -delta_j <= 0.

Hence `P_K(-lambda_B)=0` independently of the dynamic z recurrence. It is neither necessary nor appropriate to assign a dynamic z-value to the optimum label.

The most delicate edge case is a zero source gradient while the lifted vertical coordinate is nonzero. Here x_i=0 and Y_i=0, but X_i=(0,z_i/B) and G_i=(0,z_i/B). Against a nonzero generator the exact normal is

This is precisely a distinct-gradient pair already controlled by M. Against a zero generator the normal is zero. Thus source arrival at its minimum does not incorrectly force the lifted run to arrive at its own minimum, and its subsequent vertical motion remains legal. Later large steps can magnify z, but all finitely many resulting rows, including the terminal row, were included before B was selected.

There is no circular dependence: source samples and z/e are fixed first; M is then a finite number; B is chosen; K_B and lambda_B define one static function. ## 6. Exact lifted recursion, objective, and radius

Once the simultaneous normal tests hold, the unique projection at each lifted sample is Y_i. Therefore the gradient is G_i, and evaluating the actual minimization objective at its actual minimizer gives

    Phi_B(X_i) = delta_i + ||G_i||^2/2
               = f_i + e_i^2/(2B^2).

There is no unspecified affine constant, no omitted vertical norm, and no replacement of the terminal by a more convenient point. The formula holds for the actual terminal i=n because that row was included in the same normal argument.

The horizontal update is the original source update; the vertical update is exactly

    z_i - h_i e_i = (1-h_i)z_i+h_i delta_i = z_(i+1).

Induction from X_0 then shows that these are the actual iterates of the one static Phi_B under the same original GD word. For h_i=0 both horizontal and vertical coordinates are unchanged. Later repeated horizontal points with different vertical coordinates are already handled by the repeated-gradient normal analysis.

Since z_0=0,

    X_0=(x_0,0),  <lambda_B,X_0>=0,  ||X_0||^2=||x_0||^2>0.

The target function has unique minimizer zero. Thus this norm is the true closest-minimum distance, not merely a distance to a selected convenient minimizer. The size of lambda_B contributes neither to that radius nor to the point X_0.

The terminal payoff is consequently

    2 Phi_B(X_n)/||X_0||^2
       = 2 f(x_n)/||x_0||^2 + e_n^2/(B^2||x_0||^2).

The additional term is nonnegative. Its coefficient is fixed when the strict source and word are fixed, so the stated O(B^-2) description is valid at precisely that scope. Domination itself uses one finite B and does not need a limit in B.

## 7. General sources, selected minimizers, and supremum quantifiers

The original problem permits any selected minimizer x_star in the denominator. Translating that selected minimizer to zero and subtracting f_star leaves f>=0 and grad f(0)=0, even if other minima exist. For 0<epsilon<1/2,

    f_epsilon = (1-2epsilon)f + (epsilon/2)||x||^2

is epsilon-strongly convex. Its gradient Lipschitz bound is

    (1-2epsilon) + epsilon = 1-epsilon < 1.

The coefficients have the required signs. Zero is its unique minimizer with value zero. It therefore satisfies all strict-source hypotheses, including injectivity needed for duplicate-gradient rows.

For each fixed finite word and fixed source, the finite induction in the draft is sufficient. The induction hypothesis `x_i^epsilon -> x_i` gives boundedness of the points near epsilon=0 and, by continuity of grad f,

    (1-2epsilon) grad f(x_i^epsilon) + epsilon x_i^epsilon
        -> grad f(x_i).

Multiplication by the fixed finite h_i preserves convergence. After finitely many such updates, continuity of f and boundedness of the terminal points give convergence of the terminal regularized objective to the original objective. No continuous dependence over an unbounded number of updates is being asserted.

The denominator remains exactly `||x_0-x_star||^2` after translation. For a nonunique-minimum source it need not have been the source's closest-minimum distance, and the proof does not require that: the original definition itself allows every selected minimizer. The constructed target's denominator is nevertheless its true closest-minimum distance because its minimum is unique. In particular, source starts at another minimizer are not excluded; their original payoff is zero and the same approximation argument remains valid.

The quantifier order is

    fixed n,h -> arbitrary original source and selected minimizer
      -> each epsilon>0 -> its actual finite source run
      -> a finite B depending on that run -> a restricted instance.

For each epsilon the restricted supremum is at least the corresponding regularized source payoff. Since the left side is the same supremum for all epsilon, taking the scalar limit yields the original source payoff as a lower bound for it. Taking the supremum over original sources then proves `W_aff >= W` at the candidate's scope. There is no exchange of a horizon limit with a supremum and no requirement that the constructed instances, B-values, or polytopes converge.

Both original and restricted suprema are meaningful. For example, the elementary bounds `||grad f(x)||<=||x||`, `f(x)<=||x||^2/2`, and `||x-h grad f(x)||<=(1+h)||x||` show a finite word has a finite original supremum. A finiteness estimate is not needed to rescue the limit argument, but confirms that no ambiguity such as subtracting infinite quantities is hidden here.

For advertised smoothness L>0, the replacement F=f/L has smoothness at most one; its gradient update with h is exactly the original update with h/L on f. Its normalized payoff is `2(F(x_n)-F_star)/||x_0-x_star||^2`, equal to the original W normalization. Conversely multiplying a normalized constructed target by L gives an admissible L-smooth original instance. The relation W=2R is retained throughout.

Every source dimension d stays finite, and the construction uses d+1 dimensions. It claims neither a fixed universal dimension nor a simplex. Empty words n=0 still have samples {*,0}, at most two generators, and no update to check. Zero steps, revisits, hitting the source optimum, and arbitrarily large but finite nonnegative steps introduce no missing case.
