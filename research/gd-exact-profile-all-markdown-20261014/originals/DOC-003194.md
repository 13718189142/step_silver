## 3. Limit word and continuity of its chronological work

Directly summing the multiplicity of every step gives

 S=2^m-2-a+a(c^m-2^m)/(c-2),      S/lambda^m -> e,
 d_(m-1-j)/S -> delta lambda^(-j),
 delta=1-2/lambda=3-2sqrt(2).                            (10)

Here a->1/lambda, (1+1/m)^m->e, and lambda(lambda-2)=1. The largest
normalized step has limit delta. The normalized limiting word consists
of a central step delta, two copies with central step delta/lambda,
four copies with central step delta/lambda^2, and so on, with their
recursive chronology retained. The total mass at level j is
delta(2/lambda)^j; the total mass is one.

Its truncation after N largest levels is the finite word obtained from
H <- (H,d,H), starting empty and using, in ascending order,

 d=delta/lambda^(N-1), ..., delta/lambda, delta.

The omitted mass is exactly epsilon_N=(2/lambda)^N. This definition
does not introduce any new physical terminal step.

For the four modal numbers

 zeta_1, conjugate(zeta_1), zeta_2, conjugate(zeta_2),

every normalized factor satisfies |1-d zeta|<=1 whenever 0<=d<=1/5:
expanding the square reduces this to d<=2Re(zeta)/|zeta|^2, whose
minimum here is 16/17. All limiting steps and, eventually, all actual
normalized steps satisfy the stronger bound d<1/5.

Deleting steps of total mass tau from such a chronological word changes
any modal prefix product by at most |zeta|tau. To see this, telescope
the deletion one factor at a time; all other factors have modulus<=1,
and the deleted factor differs from one by d zeta.

For any two of the four modal numbers A,B define the scalar work moment

 W_word(A,B)=sum_t d_t P_t(A)P_t(B)[1-d_t(A+B)/2].        (11)

The bracket is the average of 1-d_t A and 1-d_t B, so its modulus is
at most one. Deleted terms have total modulus<=tau. At retained terms,
the change in the two-product is at most (|A|+|B|)tau. Since the total
step mass is at most one,

 |W_word-W_deleted| <= (1+|A|+|B|)tau.                  (12)

After multiplying by AB this is <23tau, because |A|,|B|<=sqrt(17)/2
<21/10 and |AB|<=17/4. Expressing the three real coordinates J in these
four complex modes uses rows with absolute coefficient sums 2,1,1.
Consequently deleting mass tau changes any entry of the real work
matrix by less than 92tau. We use the simpler uniform bound 100tau.
The normal work terms have error less than 23tau.

These inequalities prove existence of the infinite-word moments. They
also prove convergence from the ACTUAL words in (10). Indeed, retain
only their N largest levels. The resulting finite word converges to
the displayed N-level truncation, including its chronological order.
For fixed N the deleted normalized mass converges to epsilon_N:
the normalized total mass is exactly one and the retained masses
converge individually. Apply (12), first let m tend to infinity, and
then let N tend to infinity. The removed original d_0 is among the
deleted levels when m>N, so this argument also handles it explicitly.
We obtain E_m->E and convergence of the two normal work coefficients.

The left-half product and bridge/endpoint products are

 L(zeta)=product_(j>=1)(1-delta lambda^(-j)zeta)^(2^(j-1)),
 p_l=L(zeta_l),   v_l=(1-delta zeta_l)p_l,
 t_l=p_l v_l.                                          (13)

For the actual words, the corresponding products are exactly
v_l,m=(1-d_(m-1)z_l)p_l,m and
t_l,m=(1-d_(m-1)z_l)p_l,m^2/(1-d_0 z_l).
The last denominator tends to one. Deletion of a small total mass and
the same product bound prove convergence to (13). Thus K_m and D_l,m
converge entrywise to the quantities K,D_l given by (7)--(8) and the
limiting work moments.

 k_-=u_q y_q+(u_--u_q)y_-,

        23/100000 < k_- < 24/100000,

## 9. A self-contained benchmark envelope sufficient for the full metric

This proof needs only the following weaker scalar comparison, which
is established here directly:

             G(x,(5/2)y) <= (5/2)Omega(x,y), x,y>=0.    (29)

Let k=5/2 and p=Omega(x,y). The plane
R=(16/15)x+(5/3)y bounds p from above. Indeed R>=max(x,y), and

 (R-x)(R-y)-2xy
  =(16x^2-40xy+250y^2)/225
  =[16(x-5y/4)^2+225y^2]/225 >=0.

Since p is the larger root of (p-x)(p-y)=2xy, this proves p<=R.
The number G(x,ky) is the larger root of
t^2-(4x+ky)t+4x^2=0. Substituting t=kp and using
p^2=(x+y)p+xy gives

 (kp)^2-(4x+ky)kp+4x^2
        =x[4x+(25/4)y-(15/4)p] >=0.

Moreover p>=x+y, so kp>=2x+(k/2)y, the vertex of the quadratic.
It follows that kp is at least its larger root, proving (29).
The reasoning includes x=0 or y=0; these cases also follow directly.

The function G is nondecreasing in its second nonnegative argument,
