# Exact cost of a joint certificate perturbation above the Huber level

Status: unverified quantitative derivation, following the updated q>tau assignment. The historical GS interface is not re-proved. The initial verification allowance is already used.

## 1. Definitions and the exact finite-perturbation inequality

Normalize L=1, x_*=g_*=f_*=0. For a fixed positive length-n schedule h, let (q,lambda,G,f) be a full stationary primal-dual tuple. Explicitly:

* lambda_ij>=0 for distinct i,j in I={*,0,...,n}, with out_i-in_i=2*1_{i=n};
* M(q,lambda,h)>=0 is the matrix of q||x_0||^2-2f_n+sum lambda_ij Q_ij after GD substitution;
* G>=0 is the Gram matrix of (x_0,g_0,...,g_n), G_00=1, and Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2<=0;
* M G=0 and lambda_ij Q_ij=0;
* <g_t,z_t(lambda)>=0 for t<n, where
  z_t(b)=sum_{i,j} b_ij(1_{t<i}-1_{t<j})g_i.
  The indicator is zero for the star, and g_*=0.

All objects in these conditions refer to the SAME full legal transcript. No subset of these conditions is used as a replacement for the full system.

Set sigma_ij=-Q_ij>=0. Consider ANY second feasible upper certificate (q',lambda',h'), where h'>=0 and lambda' has the same flow. The new certificate need not be optimal. Put a=h'-h and b=lambda'-lambda. Define the explicit bilinear form

    B_G(a,b)=sum_{t<n} a_t <g_t,z_t(b)>.

Because M is affine separately in h and lambda, its exact finite expansion, paired with the original G, is

    <M(q',lambda',h'),G>
      = (q'-q)-sum_{i,j}lambda'_ij sigma_ij+B_G(a,b).           (1)

To verify the formula, the first-order h term vanishes by stationarity. The lambda term is sum b_ij T_ij(G), with T_ij=Q_ij-(f_i-f_j). Flow equality cancels the f differences, while sum lambda_ij Q_ij=0 changes sum b_ij Q_ij to -sum lambda'_ij sigma_ij. The only remaining mixed term is B_G(a,b). The q coefficient is G_00=1. There are no higher-order terms.

Both matrices in the left side are PSD. Therefore EVERY new feasible certificate satisfies

    q'-q >= sum lambda'_ij sigma_ij - B_G(h'-h,lambda'-lambda). (2)

This is a concrete quantitative obstruction to a proposed joint descent. A descent by Delta=q-q'>0 must obey

    B_G(h'-h,lambda'-lambda)
       >= Delta+sum lambda'_ij sigma_ij.                       (3)

In particular:

* Holding h fixed cannot lower q, as expected from matching primal feasibility.
* Holding lambda fixed cannot lower q for ANY new h, not just for a small change: then both terms on the right of (2) vanish.
* The gain from simultaneous motion has to overcome the exact cost of placing multiplier mass on interpolation inequalities that are strict for the old stationary primal.

Let R_g=max_i ||g_i||. Direct Cauchy--Schwarz bounds give

    |B_G(a,b)|<=R_g^2 ||a||_1 ||b||_1,
    Delta+sum lambda'_ij sigma_ij
       <=R_g^2 ||h'-h||_1 ||lambda'-lambda||_1                 (4)

for every feasible descent. Thus joint movement, not a single-block update, is essential. This is a necessary inequality, not a proof that a descent exists when q exceeds tau.

For a differentiable path through a stationary tuple, any first-order newly positive multiplier on an edge with sigma_ij>0 costs first order, while the useful bilinear term starts at second order. Hence a path with q'(0)=0 must have zero first derivative on every such inactive edge. This conclusion also follows directly from (2) by dividing by the path parameter.

## 2. One-step q>tau certificates with unavoidable star-outgoing flow

The full one-step system provides a useful hostile test of attempts to remove lambda_{*,i} without moving the schedule.

Take h_0=1+a with a>=1. The true worst-case value is q=a^2, certified by

    lambda_{0,*}=2a^2,
    lambda_{*,0}=2a^2-2,
    lambda_{1,0}=2,

and all other multipliers zero. The flow equations hold. Its entire PSD identity is

    q||x_0||^2-2f_1+sum lambda_ij Q_ij
      =a^2||x_0-g_0||^2+||a g_0+g_1||^2.                    (5)

The quadratic f(x)=x^2/2 from x_0=1 gives g_0=1, g_1=-a, f_1=a^2/2 and makes the right side zero. Thus primal and dual match and W_1(1+a)=a^2. This value is strictly above tau=1/(2a+3) for a>=1.

For a>1, every tight certificate at this very same schedule needs nonzero star-outgoing flow. To prove this, suppose nu_i=lambda_{*,i}=0. Then mu_0+mu_1=2, where mu_i=lambda_{i,*}. Pairing the optimal PSD slack with the matching quadratic vector (1,1,-a) gives M(1,1,-a)^T=0. Its x_0 row yields

    mu_0-a mu_1=2a^2.

Combining with mu_0+mu_1=2 gives

    mu_1=2(1-a)<0,

a contradiction. Thus a general-q proof cannot simply remove all star-outgoing multipliers while holding h and q fixed.

There IS an actual full feasible descent in this example. For any 0<epsilon<a-1, replace a by a-epsilon in all displayed variables. The nonnegative multipliers, both squares in (5), the chronological GD equation and the matching quadratic primal all remain valid. The objective drops by

    a^2-(a-epsilon)^2=2a epsilon-epsilon^2>0.

This verifies a schedule-and-flow motion with the full cone and the same legal function, rather than just a formal linearized descent. These tuples are not stationary, so the example does not decide the hypothetical global-minimizer question.

For completeness, the path continues below a=1. For 1/2<=a<=1 take

    lambda_{0,*}=2a, lambda_{1,*}=2(1-a), lambda_{1,0}=2a,

all other multipliers zero, and q=a^2. The gradient block is [[2a,1],[1,1]], while mu=(2a,2(1-a)). Its Schur complement is

    Z=(2a-1) [[1,1/a],[1/a,1/a^2]] >=0.

The quadratic again annihilates the full slack and attains q. Decreasing a keeps all constraints feasible until a=1/2, at which point q=tau=1/4. Thus the full one-step non-Huber region has an explicit piecewise-algebraic descent to the Huber-tight optimum. The change of formula at a=1 is necessary to preserve nonnegative multipliers.

## 3. Why the general-q residual alone has not supplied the descent

For an arbitrary full feasible certificate, the earlier exact Huber evaluation is

    eta=(1,tau e)^T M(1,tau e)
       =q-tau-tau sum_i nu_i[1-tau(P_i+1)]>=0,
    tau=1/(2S+1), S=sum h_t, P_i=sum_{t<i}h_t.

Its residual estimates control M applied to this one explicit Huber vector. A feasible descent from a stationary certificate also has to control the ENTIRE nullspace of M and the cost of activating strict primal interpolation inequalities. Formula (2) makes the missing interaction concrete: a positive value of q-tau by itself neither gives an allowed first-order multiplier support nor a positive value of B_G large enough to pay the required costs.

Two attempted shortcuts therefore fail for explicit reasons:

1. Freeze the optimal multipliers and use the Huber slack to move only h: equation (2) proves that q cannot decrease for any h'.
2. Remove star-outgoing flow at fixed h and q: the exact a>1 example proves such a tight certificate need not exist.

These are restrictions on the indicated operations. They do not refute a general simultaneous feasible descent theorem at global minimizers. The old full stationary tuple must remain attached to (2); using source/cut/energy identities with a nonlegal Gram would invalidate the inequality.

The current open task is to build a simultaneous variation with a favorable explicit B_G and PSD feasibility from the specific GD structure, or obtain a different quantitative consequence from the complete matrix. No Huber-tightness or asymptotic profile comparison has been proved here.
