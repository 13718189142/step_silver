## 3. Legal initializers, actual objective, and true normalization

\[

\tag{3.1}

\tag{3.2}

A point \(v\in D_0\) is the projection iff
\(\langle z-v,w-v\rangle\le0\) for every generating vertex \(w\), including
zero. Necessity follows from segment derivatives of squared distance;
sufficiency follows by expanding squared distance. Adding two such normal
inequalities gives firm nonexpansiveness, and hence the 1-Lipschitz
property of the projection. The two maximizing affine functions bound
the first-order remainder of (3.2) between zero and
\(\|\Pi_{D_0}(z+h)-\Pi_{D_0}z\|\|h\|\le\|h\|^2\).
Thus \(\Phi\) is finite, convex, differentiable, and 1-smooth, with gradient
\(\Pi_{D_0}\).

\[

\tag{3.3}
\]

\[

\tag{3.4}
\]

\[

\tag{3.5}
\]

available. If instead \(\langle z,v_k\rangle>0\), a sufficiently small

Changing variables \(d=V^T(z_0-z_\star)\) gives the exact closest-minimum

\[

\[
