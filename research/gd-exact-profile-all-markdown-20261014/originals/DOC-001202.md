# C57: nonlinear ordered repairs have exactly the Huber leading scale

Status: **complete scoped proof candidate; not verifier-accepted**.
No optimization, installation, or fact submission is allocated. The only
accepted mathematical predecessor used below is `1252b8823bb5a290`, for
the necessity and sufficiency of smooth-convex interpolation inequalities
and their explicit conjugate interpolant. Everything specific to the
trajectory, the repair, and its quantitative bound is proved here.

This continues the actual-primal lane. It enlarges the previous single
monotone quadratic auxiliary coordinate to arbitrarily many nonlinear
coordinates, and even to vectors ordered by any fixed acute cone. It gives
an explicit all-pair legal repair and identifies the sharp leading payoff
of the whole stated class. It does not upper-bound unrestricted W_n(H_m).

## 1. The actual schedule and the scalar data that are kept

Fix an integer m>=128, and define

    epsilon=1/m, lambda=1+sqrt(2),
    c=lambda(1+epsilon), a=(1-epsilon)/c,
    d_j=a c^j,
    H_0=(), H_(j+1)=(H_j,1+d_j,H_j).

Let h=H_m, n=2^m-1, S=sum_(t<n)h_t, M=S+1. Define

    b0=1+a, b1=1+d_1=2-epsilon.

The rightmost three steps are (b0,b1,b0). Every step exceeds one.
In particular S>=n, so S tends to infinity with m. The elementary bounds
needed below are

    1<b0<sqrt(2), 1<b1<2, 7/3<c<5/2, d_1+c>3.       (1)

Indeed a<(1+sqrt(2))^(-1)=sqrt(2)-1; d_1=1-epsilon;
c>1+sqrt(2)>12/5; and, using sqrt(2)<10/7,
c<(17/7)(129/128)<5/2. Since epsilon<=1/128, these also
give d_1+c>3.

Here is a complete definition of the scalar formal transcript. Put

    M_2=1+2b0+b1, k_2=M_2+6epsilon,
    k_(j+1)=-k_j^2/d_j, j>=2.

For an H_2 block with terminal scalar gradient v, use successive gradients

    ((2-c+6epsilon/b0)v, v, c v, v),                 (2)

start at k_2 v, and use the three actual GD steps (b0,b1,b0).
The last entry in (2) is the block's endpoint gradient. Its endpoint is v,
because

    b0(2-c+6epsilon/b0)+b1+b0 c+1=k_2.

For H_(j+1), give the right H_j child terminal gradient v and the left
child terminal gradient -k_j v/d_j; start the parent at k_(j+1)v.
The left endpoint is -k_j v/d_j. The bridge 1+d_j moves it to k_j v,
the right child's required start. The right endpoint is v. This verifies
the full chronological GD recursion inductively.

Choose root terminal gradient 1. Denote the resulting scalar positions
by x_i and gradients by g_i, 0<=i<=n. Thus

    x_0=k_m, x_n=g_n=1,
    x_(i+1)=x_i-h_i g_i.

Set y_i=x_i-g_i; in particular y_n=0. No legality is asserted for these
scalar data. We keep x_i for all i, and g_i for the QUERIED indices i<n,
when adding auxiliary coordinates. The final gradient and value may be
chosen freely by a legal interpolating function.

The two final queried indices r=n-2 and s=n-1 satisfy

    g_r=1, g_s=c, x_r-x_s=b1, x_s=1+b0 c.

For the normalized interpolation residual

    Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<G_i,X_j-X_i>+||G_i-G_j||^2/2,

their scalar contribution to Q_rs+Q_sr is, for EVERY choice of values,

    D=(c-1)^2+b1(c-1)=(c-1)(d_1+c)>4.              (3)

This pair is inside the queried prefix. Terminal completion cannot
delete it.

## 2. The ordered auxiliary class, including its distance convention

Work in any finite auxiliary Euclidean dimension q>=1, which may depend
arbitrarily on m. Let K be a convex cone that is acute:

    <u,v>>=0 for every u,v in K.

No assertion is made that every cone has this property. The nonnegative
orthant is an example. Consider data

    X_i=(x_i,z_i), 0<=i<=n,
    G_i=(g_i,p_i), 0<=i<n,
    z_(i+1)=z_i-h_i p_i.

Require

    z_i in K for 0<=i<=n,
    p_i in K for 0<=i<n,
    p_t-p_i in K for every 0<=t<i<n.                (4)

These are constraints on the auxiliary coordinates of one JOINT
trajectory. They do not require a separable objective, independent
one-dimensional auxiliary functions, a fixed rank, a quadratic model,
or prescribed function values. For the orthant, (4) means nonnegative
positions and nonnegative, coordinatewise nonincreasing queried gradients.

Assume these queried jets arise from ONE convex differentiable 1-smooth
function F with F(0)=0=min F. Initially write

    R^2=||X_0||^2=k_m^2+||z_0||^2,
    P=2F(X_n)/R^2.

The bound below always holds with this specified-minimizer normalization.
For a statement about the true closest-minimizer distance, explicitly
require that 0 is a closest minimizer to X_0. The constructions in
Sections 4 and 5 have a UNIQUE minimizer 0, so their R is exactly the
true distance. No automatic closest-minimizer claim is made for arbitrary
other interpolants.

The final gradient is unrestricted. In particular the class allows the
pointwise maximal unqueried-terminal completion of any legal prefix
that satisfies (4), whenever the stated normalization condition holds.

## 3. A uniform upper bound for the entire class

Put

    N=S-b0-b1=sum_(t<r)h_t,
    C=1+2b0-b0^2,
    A=2c+(b0^2-1)c^2,
    D0=2S+1-b0^2.

All of N,C,D0 are positive, and 0<A<12 by (1).

First add the two necessary interpolation inequalities for r and s.
Write Delta=p_r-p_s. The actual step has auxiliary displacement
z_r-z_s=b1 p_r. Combining its contribution with (3) gives

    D<=b1<p_r,Delta>-||Delta||^2
       =b1^2||p_r||^2/4-||Delta-(b1/2)p_r||^2.

Consequently

    ||p_r||^2>=4D/b1^2.                             (5)

This necessary bound uses no ordering assumptions and no function values.
The ordering assumption next makes the cost accumulate over the actual
earlier steps:

    z_0-N p_r=z_r+sum_(t<r)h_t(p_t-p_r) in K.

Since p_r is in K and K is acute, expansion of the squared norm yields

    ||z_0||^2>=N^2||p_r||^2,
    R^2>=4D N^2/b1^2>4N^2.                         (6)

For the objective, interpolation at the last query with the optimum and
smoothness on the actual last step give, respectively,

    F(X_s)<=<G_s,X_s>-||G_s||^2/2,
    F(X_n)<=F(X_s)-b0(1-b0/2)||G_s||^2.

These are necessary for every legal completion, with no terminal gradient
assumed. Adding and inserting the displayed scalar last-query data gives

    2F(X_n)<=A+2<p_s,z_s>-C||p_s||^2.               (7)

For t<s, acuteness and p_t-p_s in K imply
<p_t,p_s>>=||p_s||^2. Therefore

    <z_s,p_s>
      =<z_0,p_s>-sum_(t<s)h_t<p_t,p_s>
      <=<z_0,p_s>-(S-b0)||p_s||^2.

Equation (7) becomes

    2F(X_n)<=A+2<z_0,p_s>-D0||p_s||^2
             <=A+||z_0||^2/D0,                     (8)

where the final inequality is the exact completion of the square in p_s.
Dividing by R^2 and using (6) proves the uniform bound

    P <= 1/(2S+1-b0^2) + A b1^2/(4D N^2)
      < 1/(2S+1-b0^2) + 3/(S-b0-b1)^2.             (9)

Since b0,b1 stay bounded and M=S+1, it follows that

    limsup_(m->infinity) M P <= 1/2                 (10)

uniformly over all dimensions, acute cones, jointly legal function values,
and final completions in this class, with the distance convention stated
in Section 2. This is stronger in scope than a fixed-curvature analysis:
there is no curvature parameter in (5)--(10).

## 4. An explicit nonlinear repair of EVERY interpolation pair

The class in Section 2 is nonempty for the actual scalar formal transcript.
Here is an analytic construction with a closed rule and a closed initial
distance formula. It is not a nearest-feasible-point optimization.

Define finite scalars

    Gmax=max(1,max_(0<=i<=n)|g_i|),
    w_l=Gmax|y_(l+1)-y_l|+1,
    u_l=sqrt(2w_l/(h_l-1)),       0<=l<n.

Use n auxiliary coordinates, one for each chronological cut l. In
coordinate l define the 1/h_l-smooth, convex, differentiable function

    psi_l(t)=0                              if t<=0,
             t^2/(2h_l)                    if 0<=t<=h_l u_l,
             u_l t-h_l u_l^2/2              if t>=h_l u_l.

Its two joining values and derivatives agree. Its derivative is
nondecreasing and is Lipschitz with constant 1/h_l<1. Prescribe

    z_i^(l)=u_l sum_(v=i)^l h_v, p_i^(l)=u_l,   if i<=l,
    z_i^(l)=0,                  p_i^(l)=0,     if i>=l+1.

The auxiliary GD recursion holds at every step, including the extinction
step l: its position before that step is h_l u_l and its position after
the step is zero. Earlier sample positions lie in the affine branch of
psi_l. The positions and gradients lie in the nonnegative orthant, and
each gradient decreases exactly once, to zero. Thus (4) holds.

Now prescribe full-vector jets and values, including the terminal,

    X_i=(x_i,z_i), G_i=(g_i,p_i),
    f_i=g_i^2/2+sum_(l<n)psi_l(z_i^(l)), 0<=i<=n,
    X_star=G_star=0, f_star=0.                      (11)

The scalar expression g_i^2/2 is not asserted to come from a legal
one-dimensional function. The final function will be a joint interpolant.

Here is the complete interpolation law. The scalar part of Q_ij is

    g_i(y_j-y_i).

For two finite indices i<j, an auxiliary coordinate with l<i or l>=j
contributes zero in BOTH directions. A cut i<=l<j contributes

    Q_ij^(l)=-w_l,
    Q_ji^(l)=-w_l-u_l^2 sum_(v=i)^(l-1)h_v.        (12)

To verify (12), the earlier point has gradient u_l and value
u_l z_i^(l)-h_l u_l^2/2, whereas the later point has zero position,
gradient, and value. Direct substitution into Q gives the two formulas;
the sum in the second is empty if l=i.

The triangle inequality supplies

    |g_i(y_j-y_i)|, |g_j(y_i-y_j)|
       <=Gmax sum_(l=i)^(j-1)|y_(l+1)-y_l|.

By the definition of w_l, (12) therefore makes BOTH full residuals at
most -(j-i)<0. This checks every distinct finite pair, including pairs
with the terminal; it is not only an adjacent-pair check.

For the optimum pair and i<n, the scalar contributions are

    Q_(i,star)^scalar=-g_i y_i,
    Q_(star,i)^scalar=0.

Because y_n=0, |g_i y_i|<=Gmax sum_(l=i)^(n-1)|y_(l+1)-y_l|.
In each l>=i the forward auxiliary contribution is -w_l, and the reverse
one is -w_l-u_l^2 sum_(v=i)^(l-1)h_v. All l<i contributions are zero.
Thus both optimum residuals are strictly negative. At the terminal,
X_n=G_n=(1,0,...,0), f_n=1/2, and the two optimum residuals are zero.
All ordered interpolation inequalities for (11) have now been checked.

For completeness the actual function is explicit. Let

    Y_i=X_i-G_i, delta_i=f_i-||G_i||^2/2,
    H(v)=||v||^2/2+max_(i in {star,0,...,n})
                            [<Y_i,v>-delta_i],
    F_base(X)=sup_v[<X,v>-H(v)].                    (13)

By accepted fact `1252b8823bb5a290`, this is an everywhere finite convex
differentiable 1-smooth function with all the prescribed jets and the
same actual GD trajectory. It has a UNIQUE minimizer 0, as can also be
checked from (13): for i<n, the l=i term contributes w_i>0 to delta_i,
and every other term is nonnegative. Hence delta_i>0. The star and terminal
have Y=0 and delta=0. In a neighborhood of v=0 their zero affine function
strictly dominates every nonzero queried affine function. Thus H(v)
equals ||v||^2/2 in that neighborhood and its subgradient at 0 is {0}.
The maximizing-vector characterization of (13), proved in the accepted
predecessor, identifies the minimizers of F_base with this subgradient.

Consequently the true squared initial distance is exactly

    R_base^2=k_m^2+
        sum_(l=0)^(n-1) [2w_l/(h_l-1)]
                              [sum_(v=0)^l h_v]^2. (14)

The exact normalized objective is 1/R_base^2. This pure extinction repair
loses the formal large payoff: (6) yields 1/R_base^2<1/(4N^2).
Nevertheless it is a fully specified actual legal object with one common
function, all values, all gradients, and the true initial distance.

## 5. The coefficient 1/2 is sharp for the whole ordered repair class

The uniform upper bound (10) cannot be replaced by a smaller leading
constant for this class. Add one further coordinate to F_base, using the
symmetric Huber function with threshold tau>0,

    Huber_tau(t)=t^2/2                     if |t|<=tau,
                 tau|t|-tau^2/2            if |t|>=tau.

This function is convex and differentiable, its derivative is 1-Lipschitz,
and its minimizer is uniquely 0. Choose any amplitude T>0 and put

    tau=T/(2S+1), z_i^H=T-tau sum_(t<i)h_t.

All sampled positions, including the terminal, exceed tau because
z_n^H=T(S+1)/(2S+1)>tau. All its queried gradients therefore equal tau.
The actual recursion and the nonnegative ordering requirements hold.

On the enlarged space use the direct sum

    F_total(X,t)=F_base(X)+Huber_tau(t).

It is convex and 1-smooth in the product Euclidean norm, as follows by
squaring and adding the separate gradient Lipschitz inequalities. Its
minimizer is uniquely 0, and its first-coordinate queried transcript is
unchanged. Its exact normalized payoff and true distance are

    P_T=[1+T^2/(2S+1)]/[R_base^2+T^2].              (15)

For fixed m, letting T tend to infinity yields 1/(2S+1). For an explicit
horizon-dependent choice take T^2=m R_base^2, which gives

    P_T>= [m/(m+1)]/(2S+1).

Let V_m denote the supremum of the true-distance-normalized payoff over
the ordered repair class of Section 2 with 0 a closest minimizer. The
explicit unique-minimizer examples above and (9) give

    1/(2S+1) <= V_m
       <= 1/(2S+1-b0^2)+3/(S-b0-b1)^2,

and therefore the exact class asymptotic

    lim_(m->infinity) M V_m = 1/2.                 (16)

The lower endpoint is a supremum assertion; no attainment at finite T is
claimed. The explicit T choice already suffices for the limiting lower
bound. This calibrates the obstruction against an actual legal function.

## 6. What a non-ordered repair would have to change

There are exact identities that expose the assumptions used above.
For arbitrary auxiliary positions and gradients obeying GD, define

    J=sum_(t<s)h_t <p_t-p_s,p_s>.

Equation (7), with no ordering assumption, gives

    2F(X_n)<=A+2<z_0,p_s>-D0||p_s||^2-2J
             <=A+||z_0||^2/D0-2J.                 (17)

For the earlier queried gradient define

    B=<z_r,p_r>+sum_(t<r)h_t<p_t-p_r,p_r>.

The exact GD identity is

    <z_0,p_r>=N||p_r||^2+B.                        (18)

In the ordered class J>=0 and B>=0. Equations (17)--(18) hold without
that class restriction, whereas the conclusions J>=0 and B>=0 do not.
For example, retaining the unit formal terminal payoff with R^2 of order
M would require, by (5),(18) and Cauchy--Schwarz,

    B <= R||p_r||-N||p_r||^2,

which is negative for all sufficiently large m whenever R=O(sqrt(M)).
Thus such a normalization-saving repair must supply genuine cancellation
in its cumulative positions/gradients, or modify the scalar queried data.
Similarly, if R^2/M tends to infinity and a family has M P>=1+eta for
some fixed eta>0, then (17) forces

    limsup [M J/R^2] <= -1/4-eta/2.                 (19)

To check (19), divide (17) by R^2, use ||z_0||^2<=R^2,
M/D0->1/2, and M A/R^2->0. This statement is a necessary condition,
not an existence claim for an escaping legal repair.

diagnoses this fully specified repair class on this critical positive

gradients, changed first-coordinate queried trajectories, all possible
minimizer locations, or unrestricted smooth convex adversaries. It does
not replace the original zero-step or large-step quantifiers, and is not
a theorem about W_n(H_m). All new conclusions here remain unverified
