# A uniform tail transfer for core-ended projection defects, and its serial limit

Status: **complete joint proof draft, NOT verifier-accepted**, cumulative round
17. This file is one self-contained candidate package. It does not use another
unverified finding as a lemma. There is no new optimization, numerical replay,
installation, or fact_submit. The 16-evaluation discovery allocation remains
closed at 98.02878378597961 measured seconds.

The performance W_n and exact benchmark B_n=2S_bullet(n)+1 are those in accepted
facts b98bb807d514e80d and 1252b8823bb5a290. All additional constructions and
estimates are proved below. The initial norm used here is proved to equal the
distance to the ENTIRE minimizer set, not just to an arbitrarily chosen optimum.

The new conclusion has two parts. A fixed core-ended projection defect can
survive an arbitrarily long objective tail with a fixed relative surplus. But
iterating the specified constant-coordinate transfer across balanced primitive
joins necessarily dilutes that surplus. Neither conclusion settles the original
all-schedule minimax problem.

## 1. Projection functions and the meaning of a core endpoint

For a compact convex set K containing 0, put

    F_K(x)=max_(g in K) {<x,g>-||g||^2/2}.

Completing the square shows that the unique maximizer is P_K(x), the Euclidean
projection of x onto K. A vector g in K is this projection exactly when

    <v-g,x-g> <= 0                    for every v in K.             (1)

For polytopes it suffices to check every listed vertex. The two projection
inequalities for x,x' imply

    ||P_K(x')-P_K(x)||^2 <= <x'-x,P_K(x')-P_K(x)>.

Thus the projection is 1-Lipschitz. Evaluating the two maxima at each other's
maximizers gives

    0 <= F_K(x')-F_K(x)-<P_K(x),x'-x>
      <= <P_K(x')-P_K(x),x'-x> <= ||x'-x||^2.

Consequently F_K is finite, convex, differentiable, and has 1-Lipschitz gradient
P_K. Furthermore

    argmin F_K = K^polar := {z : <z,g><=0 for every g in K}.          (2)

Indeed the displayed polar inequalities imply F_K(z)=0. Conversely, if
F_K(z)=0, apply its defining inequality to epsilon*g in K and let epsilon
decrease to zero to obtain <z,g><=0.

Fix a finite nonnegative prefix p=(p_0,...,p_(ell-1)) and actual projection
data x_i,g_i for i=0,...,ell, including 0 as an optimum sample. Thus
x_(i+1)=x_i-p_i*g_i, and (1) holds against 0 and every g_j. It suffices to
use K_0=conv(0,g_0,...,g_ell). Assume

    x_ell = g_ell = w != 0.                                       (3)

This is the *core endpoint* condition. Define

    A=1+sum p_i,  R_i=sum_(j=i)^(ell-1) p_j,
    y_i=x_i-g_i,  r_init^2=||x_0||^2,
    Delta=A^2||w||^2-r_init^2.

The last quantity is the particular squared-distance defect studied here.
It is not asserted to represent every possible failure of primitive validity.

## 2. One finite buffer works for EVERY negative terminal direction

Choose a nonnegative number beta satisfying

    beta >= max_i (-<g_i,w>)_+,
    beta >= max_(i:R_i>0) (-<w,y_i>)_+/R_i.                        (4)

An empty maximum is zero and (u)_+=max(u,0). These are maxima of finitely many
explicit numbers, so beta is finite. For R_i=0, nonnegativity of all remaining
steps gives x_i=x_ell=w. Uniqueness of projection gives g_i=w and y_i=0; those
indices require no division by zero.

For any tau>0 lift the old gradients and positions into one extra coordinate:

    G_i=(g_i,sqrt(beta)),
    X_i=(x_i,(1+R_i)*sqrt(beta)),
    k=-tau*G_ell,
    K=conv(0,G_0,...,G_ell,k).                                    (5)

Every old sample is still a projection sample for this single K. Against an
old vertex G_j the extra-coordinate difference vanishes, so its normal is
exactly the original normal in (1). Against 0 the new normal is

    -<g_i,y_i>-beta*R_i <= 0.

Here <g_i,y_i>>=0 follows by using the old zero vertex. Against k the normal is

    <-tau*w-g_i,y_i>-(1+tau)*beta*R_i <= 0,                        (6)

because -<g_i,y_i><=0 and -<w,y_i><=beta*R_i. The R_i=0 case is zero.

At every point u*k with u>=1, the projection is k. The zero vertex has
nonpositive normal. An old vertex has

    <G_i-k,k>
      =-tau*(<g_i,w>+beta)-tau^2*(||w||^2+beta) <= 0               (7)

by (4); multiply this by u-1. The k vertex has zero normal. Equations
(6)-(7) check every old/new ordered pair through one common projection body.

The lifted initial point has the exact positive-cone representation

    X_0=G_ell+sum_(i<ell) p_i*G_i.                               (8)

For every z in K^polar, (8) gives <X_0,z><=0. Hence
||X_0-z||^2>=||X_0||^2, with equality at z=0. The true initial distance is

    dist(X_0,argmin F_K)^2 = r_init^2+A^2*beta.                    (9)

This includes duplicated gradients and zero physical steps. The construction
does not impose any restriction on upper-certificate support or PSD rank.

## 3. Arbitrary objective tails and a surplus independent of their length

Let z=(z_0,...,z_(m-1)) be ANY finite nonnegative tail, with total T and
D=1+2T. Choose any bridge 1+d with d>0, and use (5) with tau=d/D. After
the prefix, X_ell=G_ell. The bridge sends this point to -d*G_ell=D*k.
At a tail partial sum t, the iterate is (D-t)*k. Since 0<=t<=T,
D-t>=T+1>=1. Section 2 proves that its gradient is always k. In particular
the final point is (T+1)*k, and

    2F_K(X_final)=D*||k||^2=(d^2/D)*(||w||^2+beta).

Combining with (9) proves the exact attained normalized payoff

    E(p,d,z;beta)
       = (d^2/D)*(||w||^2+beta)/(r_init^2+A^2*beta).               (10)

This is an actual lower witness for W_(ell+1+m)(p,1+d,z). It uses the exact
chronological tail; no optimization over its ordering or gradients was made.

In particular choose the source ConPD bridge

    d=(sqrt(D^2+8*A*D)-D)/4,
    2d^2+Dd=AD.                                                  (11)

This equals psi(sum p_i,T)-1 from the problem's exact recurrence. Its total
physical mass is

    B_concat=2A+2d+D=(D+2d)^2/D,
    d^2/(D*A^2)=1/B_concat.                                     (12)

Therefore (10) becomes

    E=(1+delta)/B_concat,
    delta=Delta/(r_init^2+A^2*beta).                             (13)

If Delta>0, this is a strict positive relative surplus INDEPENDENT of the
tail length, total T, ordering, and values. A single beta from (4) works for
all of them. This strengthens a finite strict-defect implication only for
the explicitly stated projection/core class; it does not normalize an
arbitrary smooth-convex defect into that class.

For any L>0 multiply F_K by L. The physical updates h_i/L then produce the
same normalized payoff. Scaling the argument and objective simultaneously
can normalize the true distance in (9) to one.

## 4. The detected dense direction has an explicit core defect

This section rederives the needed geometry; the round16 planar draft is not
used as a correctness premise. Put

    s=sqrt(2), p0=(16-11s)/3, q0=(8s-10)/3, m0=10s-14=q0-2p0.

For 0<=t<=1/50 set

    a=s-p0*t, b=2+q0*t, zeta=b-1,
    prefix p(t)=(a,b,a), A=1+2a+b.

This is exactly the three-step primitive-child part of the balanced dense
redistribution tested in the closed batch. Define

    U=1/(a+1)^2,
    kappa=-[zeta^2+(a+1)/2]/[zeta*(a+2)],
    u=(1/(a+1),0),
    v=(kappa,sqrt(1/2-kappa^2))/(a+1),
    w=-zeta*u-a*v,
    N=zeta^2*(2-a)+a*(a^2-2)/2, J=N/(a+2).

Expansion gives

    ||u||^2=U, ||v||^2=U/2, <u,v>=kappa*U, ||w||^2=J*U,
    r_u:=<u,w>/U=[a(a+1)/2-2zeta^2]/[zeta(a+2)],
    r_v:=<v,w>/U=[zeta^2+(1-a-a^2)/2]/(a+2),
    <w-v,-zeta*u-v>=0.                                         (14)

All following terminating decimals denote exact rationals. Squaring endpoints
gives 1.4142<s<1.4143 and consequently

    .147<p0<.149, .437<q0<.439, .140<m0<.145,
    1.411<=a<=1.415, 1<=zeta<=1.01,
    -.654<kappa<-.639, .578<N<.603,
    .169<J<.177, -.1<r_u<0, -.062<r_v<0.                         (15)

Here -kappa has numerator between 2.2055 and 2.2276 and denominator between
3.411 and 3.44915. The polynomial a(a^2-2)/2 is increasing on this interval,
so bounding it and zeta^2(2-a) separately proves the N bounds. For r_u the
numerator is at least 1.411*2.411/2-2*1.01^2=-.3392395 and is negative;
its denominator is at least 3.411. For r_v the numerator is at least
1+(1-1.415-1.415^2)/2=-.2086125 and is negative; its denominator is at
least 3.411. These prove the last two bounds. Also .654^2<1/2, so v is real
and u,v are independent.

Use K_0=conv(0,u,v,w) and the prefix data

    x_0=(a+1)u=(1,0), g_0=u;
    x_1=u,             g_1=u;
    x_2=-zeta*u,       g_2=v;
    x_3=w,             g_3=w.                                  (16)

The recursion for (a,b,a) holds exactly. At samples 1 and 3 the residual is
zero. At sample 0 it is a*u; (15) checks its normal against all four vertices.
At sample 2, put y=-zeta*u-v. The normals against 0 and u, in units of U,
are zeta*kappa+1/2<0 and

    -zeta+(zeta-1)*kappa+1/2 <= -1/2.

The w normal is zero by (14), and the v normal is zero by definition. Thus
(16) is an actual prefix trajectory with a core endpoint.

The buffer beta=U/8 satisfies (4). Indeed the only negative endpoint
correlations in (14) have magnitude less than U/10. For the residual test,
sample 0 contributes a*(-r_u)*U/(2a+b)<U/10, sample 1 contributes zero,
and <w,y_2>=<v,y_2>=(-zeta*kappa-1/2)U>0. Sample 3 has no remaining
steps and zero residual.

To bound its defect, set

    R_prim(t)=A^2*J/(a+1)^2.

At t=0, A=3+2s=(s+1)^2 and J=(s-1)^2=1/A, so R_prim(0)=1.
Differentiation, with A'=m0>0, gives

    (log R_prim)'=2m0/A+2p0/(a+1)+N'/N+p0/(a+2),
    N'=2zeta*(2-a)*q0-p0[-zeta^2+(3a^2-2)/2].                   (17)

The first product in N' is greater than 1.17*.437; the square bracket is
less than 1.004. Hence N'>1.17*.437-1.004*.149>.361. From (15),

    (log R_prim)' > 294/2415+361/603+147/3415 > 3/4.

The last inequality follows by multiplication by positive integer
denominators. Integrating and using exp(u)>=1+u gives

    R_prim(t)>=1+3t/4.

Also A<=5.84 and a+1>=2.411, so

    1+A^2*U/8 <= 1+5.84^2/(8*2.411^2) < 7/4.

Consequently the surplus in (13) obeys

    delta(t)=[R_prim(t)-1]/[1+A^2*U/8] >= 3t/7.                 (18)

For t>0 the inequalities are strict where indicated. Every bound is uniform
over the full stated parameter interval and has no numerical solver premise.

## 5. A named unbounded-horizon family is strictly worse than the benchmark

Fix any t in (0,1/50]. For each m>=0 take ANY nonnegative m-step suffix z
whose sum T is at most S_bullet(m). Use prefix p(t) from section 4 and bridge
(11). This is a specified family of physical schedules of length n=m+4.
It allows arbitrary tail order, zero steps, and complete dual re-selection.

Since d<A<6, (12) gives

    B_concat < D+4A < B_m+24 <= B_n+24.

The final comparison follows directly from monotonicity of the exact dominant
recurrence: the empty-prefix split gives
G(1,B_j)=2+B_j/2+sqrt(B_j^2+8B_j)/2>B_j. Also B_n>=2n+1 by the same
recurrence, since G(1,D)>=D+2 and B_0=1.

Equations (13) and (18) prove

    B_n*W_n(p(t),1+d,z)
      >= (1+3t/7)*B_n/(B_n+24).                                (19)

In particular the limit inferior, uniformly over this entire suffix class,
is at least 1+3t/7. Explicitly, for n>=ceil(56/t+12), the right side is at
least 1+3t/14. Thus the fixed prefix defect need not disappear when the
objective tail grows without bound.

This is a LOWER bound showing a particular family is too poor. It is not
an improved upper schedule, not an all-horizon minimax converse, and not
an inference from a finite objective value. It does not assume equality
between a Silver subsequence and the exact B_n benchmark.

## 6. What happens when this transfer is repeated at primitive joins

The same projection calculation has a core-ending version. Append any
nonnegative block of capacity C=1+sum(block)>1 to a core prefix of capacity
A, with bridge 1+r satisfying

    r*(A+C+r)=A*C.

Write A_next=A+C+r and tau=r/C=A/A_next in (5). The bridge reaches C*k;
the block's constant gradient k leaves final point k. The complete normal
audit in section 2 applies because every point in this block is u*k with
u>=1. The capacity and initial norm identities give

    A_next^2||k||^2=A^2(||w||^2+beta),
    r_init,next^2=r_init^2+A^2*beta,
    Delta_next=Delta.                                           (20)

Now iterate this SPECIFIC construction. At level j let the capacity be A_j,
the core endpoint w_j, and initial squared distance R_j^2. The jth join has
tau_j=A_j/A_(j+1) in (0,1). It adds one constant coordinate sqrt(beta_j)
to every old gradient, retains all old vertices, and has

    w_(j+1)=-tau_j*(w_j,sqrt(beta_j)).

Allow ANY beta_j>=0 for which the actual construction is legal. It need not
satisfy the sufficient all-tau bound (4). Let N_j=A_j^2||w_j||^2=R_j^2+Delta,
where (20) makes Delta>0 independent of j.

For j>=1, the old prefix contains the previously queried vertex

    v_old=-w_j/tau_(j-1).

At the first point of the new appended block, the residual is
(C_j-1)*w_(j+1). Since C_j>1, its normal against the lifted v_old forces

    beta_j >= [(1/tau_(j-1)-tau_j)/(1+tau_j)]*||w_j||^2.          (21)

This is an ORIGINAL normal inequality, not a condition imposed for
convenience. To see it, expand
<(v_old,sqrt(beta_j))-w_(j+1),w_(j+1)><=0 and divide by tau_j>0.

It follows from (20)-(21) that

    N_(j+1) >= [(1+1/tau_(j-1))/(1+tau_j)]*N_j.

For r>=1 the factors telescope exactly:

    N_r >= N_1 * (1+tau_0)/(1+tau_(r-1)) * A_(r-1)/A_0.         (22)

Thus Delta/R_r^2 tends to zero whenever A_(r-1) tends to infinity. This
permits arbitrary nonempty appended blocks and arbitrarily retuned legal
buffers in the specified construction. The C_j>1 assumption is essential
to this particular normal argument: if the appended block has zero mass,
its first point is already core and that normal provides no restriction.

For balanced joins C_j=A_j, put lambda=1+sqrt(2). Then
A_(j+1)=lambda*A_j, tau_j=1/lambda, and (21) reads
beta_j>=sqrt(2)*||w_j||^2. Consequently

    N_r >= lambda^(r-1)*N_1,
    Delta/R_r^2 <= Delta/[lambda^(r-1)*N_1-Delta].                (23)

If the appended physical block is an identical copy of the current prefix,
its length obeys ell_r+1=2^r*(ell_0+1). Hence the relative defect carried
by this single-branch construction is O((ell_r+1)^(-log_2(lambda))). This
remains true if a fixed modified three-step seed occurs in a positive
fraction of the physical word: the constructed trajectory uses a constant
gradient on each appended copy and does not exploit that copy's own defect.

An eventual objective-tail transfer can only reduce this relative surplus,
since (13) divides Delta by R_r^2+A_r^2*beta_final>=R_r^2. Therefore serial
constant-coordinate transfers do not automatically turn a fixed finite
defect into a fixed relative obstruction on repeated-copy dense variations.

This is a limit of the specified witness construction. It is NOT an upper
bound on W, a restriction on full PEP ranks or multipliers, or an assertion
that no different joint multi-block construction can retain a fixed gap.

There is an explicit counterexample to deleting C_j>1 from the claim about
arbitrarily selected legal buffers. Start with the one-step prefix (2),
K_0=[-1,1], x_0=g_0=1 and x_1=g_1=-1. Its capacity is A_0=3 and
Delta=8. Append only EMPTY right blocks, C_j=1, and the exact primitive
bridges 1+r_j, where r_j*(A_j+1+r_j)=A_j. Choose every beta_j=0.
Then 0<r_j<1, all sampled points stay in [-1,1], and the same function
F_( [-1,1] ) has gradient equal to the point throughout the trajectory.
Every endpoint is core. The identities A_(j+1)*r_j=A_j and
w_(j+1)=-r_j*w_j imply A_j^2*w_j^2=9, while R_j^2=1.
Also A_(j+1)>A_j+1, so A_j tends to infinity. Thus Delta/R_j^2=8
does NOT decay in this zero-mass-right-block case. Every original normal
is valid because its residual is zero. This example pinpoints exactly why
the nonzero appended mass is needed in (21).

## 7. Scope of the advance

The finite full-model data suggested retaining a nontrivial core-ended
prefix, instead of testing only the global Huber and quadratic trajectories.
The new formula (13) gives a tail-uniform quantitative use of such a prefix.
Equation (19) makes it an actual unbounded-horizon obstruction for a named
class. The separate original-pair inequality (21) identifies precisely why
the obvious recursive extension loses its fixed relative strength.

The remaining bridge is a construction coupling defects in a growing set
of modified child blocks, with all original normals and the total initial
distance retained. No such bridge or improving full upper certificate has
been established here. The original target remains unresolved.
