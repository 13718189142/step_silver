# Active candidate: reset seeds with retained boundary gradients

Status: exploratory plan, not an upper-bound theorem. The explicit hostile tests below forbid treating the seed as primitive or assuming arbitrary dominant seeds admit balanced doubling.

New active negative candidate: a unit-step reset of a silver objective block, followed by balanced sum-based doubling, with a richer certificate that retains the last two sampled gradients. This lies outside the accepted terminal-seed washout theorem and outside the weakened scalar-potential recursion. No upper bound for the recursive family is claimed. A simpler unreset variant is explicitly killed by a full-curvature quadratic at length 15, and the reset seed's primitive certificate is explicitly killed by an asymmetric Huber instance. Thus only a genuinely richer last-iterate objective certificate could make the active candidate work.

Fix lambda=1+sqrt(2) and sigma=lambda^(-1)=sqrt(2)-1. Let pi^(0) be empty and pi^(k+1)=[pi^(k),1+lambda^(k-1),pi^(k)]. These are the standard silver primitive blocks, of length 2^k-1 and shifted sum lambda^k, as follows algebraically from the exact ConPP recurrence.

Actual reset seed and candidate family:
beta_k=(3+sqrt(8lambda^k+1))/4,
q^(k,0)=[pi^(k),beta_k,1],
A_(k,0)=1+sum q^(k,0)=lambda^k+beta_k+1.
For j>=0 define
q^(k,j+1)=[q^(k,j),1+sigma A_(k,j),q^(k,j)],
A_(k,j+1)=lambda A_(k,j).
Lengths are n_(k,j)+1=2^j(2^k+2). Every step is predetermined and positive, and no extra state is used by GD. The final unit step in the seed makes the polynomial on f(x)=x^2/2 vanish, so every doubled schedule also vanishes on that quadratic. This is only one hostile-instance test, not a full-class guarantee.

Important failed propagation test:
Starting instead from h^(0)=[3/2] and applying the same balanced mass rule produces A_j=(5/2)lambda^j. If p_j is the final multiplier on f(x)=x^2/2 and gamma_j=A_j|p_j|, then gamma_0=5/4 and gamma_(j+1)=gamma_j^2, since sigma lambda=1. Hence gamma_j=(5/4)^(2^j). At j=3 the length is 15, A_3=(35+25sqrt(2))/2 lies strictly between 35 and 36, and gamma_3^2=(5/4)^16>32. Its normalized quadratic error is gamma_3^2/A_3^2>32/36^2=2/81, whereas the conjectured Huber-tight upper bound 1/(2A_3-1)<1/69. Thus balanced sum-based doubling of an arbitrary dominant seed is false, even if one guesses that an endpoint parameter shrinks. The reset changes a specific failing mechanism; it is not an assertion that all other obstructions disappear.

The reset seed itself is not primitive when lambda^k>3:
Write A=lambda^k, B=beta_k-1, so A=2B^2+B. Its shifted sum is R=A+B+2. For B>1 set a=B/2 and consider the asymmetric Huber function with derivative clip(x,-a,1) and minimum zero at x=0. Start at x_0=A. The whole silver prefix stays on the right affine ray with gradient 1, arriving at 1. The beta_k step arrives at -B. The final unit step arrives at -B+a=-a. At all sampled points Q_{i,star}=f_i-<g_i,x_i>+||g_i||^2/2=0. At the final point g=-a and f=a^2/2, so the saturated primitive potential would require A>=Ra=RB/2. But
RB-2A=B(A+B+2)-2A=2B^2(B-1)>0.
Therefore the candidate must not be treated as a primitive seed. This exact failure does not rule out a weaker last-iterate objective certificate for the doubled family.

A tested richer certificate interface:
For a one-step schedule t in (0,3/2], put D=2t+1. Choose a nonnegative history weight u and final weight v=D-u. Define
J_(t,u)=2u(f_0-f_1)+u(1-u)||g_0||^2+2v(t-u)<g_0,g_1>+v(1-v)||g_1||^2.
With Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2 and x_1=x_0-tg_0, direct coefficient comparison gives
J_(t,u)=(2D-2u)(-Q_10)+(2D-4u)(-Q_01)
+[-u^2+(4t-2)u-2(t-1)D]||g_0-g_1||^2.
Thus this is a genuine exact certificate whenever all three displayed coefficients and u,v are nonnegative. For t=1, u=3/2, it gives J=3(-Q_10)+(3/4)||g_0-g_1||^2. For t=3/2, u=2, it gives J=4(-Q_10).
The certificate implies the objective bound W_1(t)<=1/(2t+1), by adding u Q_(0,star)+v Q_(1,star) and completing the square ||x_0-x_star-u g_0-v g_1||^2. It retains information lost in a scalar endpoint-gradient coefficient.

The concrete finite algebra obligation for the reset candidate is to propagate a rank-one dominance certificate whose coefficient vector equals kappa times the steps in the interior but has independent coefficients on the last two sampled gradients and the final gradient. The original primitive-plus-beta_k objective block already has the explicit vector [pi^(k),A+1,2beta_k-1]. After the appended unit step, its bridge-gradient coefficient is of order A, whereas the physical bridge step is of order sqrt(A); this explains why a single common multiplier on all past steps loses necessary information. Derive the finite boundary-variable quadratic identity for composing two such objects; do not merely posit its feasibility. If closing the certificate class would require retaining every internal coefficient with no quantitative recursion, record that finite-state proposal as BLOCKED rather than presenting a restatement of full PEP as progress.

For a negative proof, a successful certificate still must yield a nonvanishing separation relative to the exact B_(n_(k,j))=2S_bullet(n_(k,j))+1, or must supply a way to insert the improved blocks into an objective construction with an exact profile comparison. The algebraic mass formula alone does neither. No new verifier submission is authorized for this exploratory package.
