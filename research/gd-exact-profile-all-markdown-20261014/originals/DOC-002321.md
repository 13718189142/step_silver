## 2. Projection gradient and exact minimum cone

For a nonempty compact CONVEX set `D`, completing the square shows that the
unique maximizer defining `F_D(x)` is the Euclidean projection `P_D(x)`.
For `v in D`, the exact characterization is

    P_D(x)=v iff <x-v,w-v><=0 for every w in D.           (2.1)

Necessity follows by differentiating squared distance along segments in `D`.
For sufficiency expand `||x-w||^2` about `v`. When `D` is a finite convex
hull it suffices to test every generator, since the normal expression is
affine in `w`.

Adding the two projection inequalities at `x,x'` gives

    <x-x',P_D(x)-P_D(x')>>=||P_D(x)-P_D(x')||^2.

Therefore `P_D` is 1-Lipschitz. Evaluating each affine maximum at the other's
optimizer gives

    0<=F_D(x')-F_D(x)-<P_D(x),x'-x>
      <=<P_D(x')-P_D(x),x'-x><=||x'-x||^2.

This proves differentiability with gradient `P_D`, and hence global
1-smoothness. Convexity follows from the maximum of affine functions.

When `0 in D`, the exact minimum value is zero and

    C_D={a:<a,g><=0 for every g in D}.                  (2.2)

If `a` obeys these inequalities, every defining term is nonpositive and the
zero generator attains zero. Conversely, if `<a,g>>0`, convexity and `0 in D`
allow `theta*g in D` for arbitrarily small positive `theta`; its objective
term is positive for sufficiently small `theta`. Thus no other point is a
minimizer. `C_D` is the closed convex POLAR CONE, with threshold zero.

Every `x` has a closest point in `C_D`: a distance-minimizing sequence can be
confined to a bounded ball, then compactness and closedness give a minimizer;
strict convexity gives uniqueness. Throughout this audit the exact radius is

    r_D(x)=dist(x,C_D),

not distance to the origin, not a body polar with threshold one, and not
distance to a single selected minimizer unless that point is actually closest.
Convexity is essential to the projection-gradient and minimum-cone claims;
the task's definition of `U_d` makes sense for any compact set, but its
smooth-function application here is to compact convex sets, especially
simplices. No claim about arbitrary nonconvex compact sets is being added.

## 3. Strict interpolation rows and degeneracies

Take a convex differentiable `ell`-smooth source with `0<ell<1`, translated
so that `f(0)=min f=0`. Retain its ACTUAL samples `i=0,...,n`, with

    x_(i+1)=x_i-h_i g_i,      g_i=grad f(x_i),
    f_i=f(x_i),              (x_*,g_*,f_*)=(0,0,0).

Define, with this orientation,

    Q_ji=f_j-f_i+<g_j,x_i-x_j>+||g_j-g_i||^2/2,
    gamma=(1-ell)/(2ell)>0.

To check the sign independently, apply the `ell`-smooth descent inequality
to `k(x)=f(x)-<g_j,x>` from `x_i` with displacement
`-(g_i-g_j)/ell`. Convexity and `grad k(x_j)=0` give

    k(x_j)<=k(x_i-(g_i-g_j)/ell)
           <=k(x_i)-||g_i-g_j||^2/(2ell),

so

    Q_ji<=-gamma||g_j-g_i||^2.                         (3.1)

This holds for every ordered pair, including both directions involving the
optimum and the original terminal sample. Put

    eta_i=<x_i,g_i>-f_i-||g_i||^2/2,   eta_*=0,
    H_i=sum_(t<i) h_t eta_t.

The two optimum rows are, distinctly,

    Q_(i,*)=-eta_i,
    Q_(*,i)=-f_i+||g_i||^2/2.

Consequently

    eta_i>=gamma||g_i||^2,
    f_i-||g_i||^2/2>=gamma||g_i||^2.                    (3.2)

Every nonzero gradient therefore has positive height. If `g_i=0`, convexity
makes `x_i` a minimizer, so `f_i=eta_i=0`; subsequent positions remain at
that possibly nonzero minimizer and all subsequent gradients are zero.

If `g_i=g_j=g`, the two ordinary convexity inequalities yield
`f_i-f_j=<g,x_i-x_j>`. Thus `eta_i=eta_j` and `Q_ji=0`. Equal gradients
need NOT imply equal positions. Identifying their vertex label is valid;
discarding their distinct physical time rows would not be valid. This check
does not use strong convexity or injectivity of the source gradient.

## 4. Moving-coordinate lift and all strict normal margins

For a finite positive `R`, set

    v_i=(g_i,eta_i/R),       v_*=0,
    z_i=(x_i,-R-H_i/R),     a_R=(0,-R),
    D_R=conv{0,v_0,...,v_n}.

Direct expansion, without changing either ordered index, gives

    <z_i-v_i,v_j-v_i>
      =<x_i-g_i,g_j-g_i>-(eta_j-eta_i)
         -(H_i+eta_i)(eta_j-eta_i)/R^2
      =Q_ji-(H_i+eta_i)(eta_j-eta_i)/R^2.             (4.1)

This includes the reference zero vertex. The sign of the added term is
not automatically favorable, so a simultaneous finite guard is required.
For different gradients define

    A=max_(0<=i<=n, j in {*,0,...,n}, g_i!=g_j)
          (H_i+eta_i)(eta_i-eta_j)_+/(-Q_ji),

and set the maximum of an empty set to zero. Nonnegative steps give
`H_i+eta_i>=0`; all included denominators are strictly positive by (3.1).
There are finitely many finite rows, so `A` is finite.

For `R^2>=A`, all normals in (4.1) are nonpositive. Equal-gradient rows
are identically zero, including repeated zero gradients. Thus
`P_(D_R)(z_i)=v_i` for EVERY physical index, and

    z_(i+1)=z_i-h_i v_i

holds exactly because `H_(i+1)=H_i+h_i eta_i`. These are actual iterates
of one static function, not a sequence of independently selected faces.
The vertical coordinate moves; freezing it would invalidate this proof.

The selected nearby minimizer is legal:

    <a_R,v_i>=-eta_i<=0,
    ||z_0-a_R||=||x_0||.

Evaluation at the actual maximizing vertex gives

    F_(D_R)(z_i)=f_i-C_i/R^2,
    C_i=eta_i H_i+eta_i^2/2>=0.                        (4.2)

For the simplex step, assume some sampled gradient is nonzero. List the
distinct nonzero gradients as `g^1,...,g^m`, use their common heights
`eta^1,...,eta^m`, and set `g^0=eta^0=0`. Let `a(i)` denote each sample's
label and put

    delta=min_(0<=a<b<=m)||g^a-g^b||>0,
    v^b=(g^b,eta^b/R).

Choose `R^2>=max(1,2A)`. The possible positive correction in (4.1) is at
most half of `-Q_ji`; if its sign is favorable this estimate remains true.
For every physical `i` and every different label `b`, therefore,

    N_ib:=<z_i-v^(a(i)),v^b-v^(a(i))>
         <=-gamma*delta^2/2.                          (4.3)

For the same label the row is exactly zero. Choosing any representative of
label `b` is legitimate because the normal expression depends only on its
gradient and height; ALL physical source indices `i` are still retained.
For `a(i)=0,b!=0`, the correction in (4.1) is `-H_i eta^b/R^2<=0`.
Thus zero-gradient rows introduce no unprotected equality against another
vertex. In particular strictness has not been inferred from genericity.

## 5. Nonnegative chronological perturbation and simplex geometry

perturbation. Every repeated nonzero gradient receives one fixed coordinate,

    Z_0=(x_0,-R,0),
    Z_(i+1)=Z_i-h_i w^(a(i)).                          (5.1)

With `a=a(i)`, the exact new normal is

    <Z_i-w^a,w^b-w^a>
      =N_ib-epsilon^2<b_i+e_a,e_b-e_a>.                (5.2)

There is no linear-in-`epsilon` term. Write `b_(i,a)=<b_i,e_a>` for
`a>0` and `b_(i,0)=0`. The coefficient of `epsilon^2` in (5.2) is

    0                       for a=b;
    b_(i,a)-b_(i,b)+1        for a,b>0 and a!=b;
    b_(i,a)+1               for a>0, b=0;
    -b_(i,b)                for a=0, b>0.

Every `b_(i,a)` is between zero and `S`. Thus this coefficient is at most
`S+1`. This is where nonnegative steps protect the chronological estimate,
and the sample-to-zero row is explicitly among the potentially positive
perturbations. If

    0<epsilon^2<=gamma*delta^2/[4(S+1)],                (5.3)

every different-label normal is at most `-gamma*delta^2/4`, whereas every
same-label normal remains exactly zero. The projection test proves

    P_D(Z_i)=w^(a(i)) for every i=0,...,n.

Together with (5.1), this proves the actual GD trajectory of the single
fixed `F_D`, including its terminal value. It retains the entire original
horizontal strict-source trajectory before compression.

The vectors `w^1,...,w^m` are independent: a relation projected to the
new coordinate block has coefficients `epsilon` times the original ones.
Hence the body is an `m`-simplex in its span, with zero a vertex. No claim
that the unperturbed `v^b` are independent was used.

The minimizer and value formulas are exact:

    <A_R,w^b>=-eta^b<=0,
    ||Z_0-A_R||=||x_0||,
    F_D(Z_i)=f_i-C_i/R^2-epsilon^2 beta_i,

If `a(i)=0`, then `beta_i=C_i=0`; otherwise `0<beta_i<=S+1/2`.
For any positive desired value loss `tau`, the simultaneous choices

    R^2>=max(1,2A,2C_n/tau),

## 6. Compression and the exact polar-minimum radius

Let `E=span{w^1,...,w^m}`. The body lies in `E`, so for every ambient `Z`

    F_D(Z)=F_D(P_E Z),
    P_D(Z)=P_D(P_E Z),

by orthogonal decomposition in both the inner product and squared distance.
Set `q_i=P_E Z_i` and `a=P_E A_R`. Projection of the exact updates gives
the actual same-word trajectory in `E`, with identical gradients and values.
An ORTHONORMAL identification of `E` with `R^m` makes `D` a full-dimensional
simplex without changing any Euclidean quantity. An arbitrary affine
identification with a standard simplex would not suffice.

Let

    C_E={a in E:<a,w^b><=0 for every b}.

The FULL ambient minimum cone is exactly `C_E+E^perp`: the constraints
depend only on the `E` component. Consequently

    dist(Z_0,C_E+E^perp)^2
      =inf_(a in C_E,u in E^perp)
          {||P_E Z_0-a||^2+||P_(E^perp)Z_0-u||^2}
      =dist(q_0,C_E)^2.                               (6.1)

This is the exact closest-minimum radius before and after compression.
The explicit selected minimizer satisfies

    <a,w^b>=-eta^b<=0,
    ||q_0-a||=||P_E(Z_0-A_R)||<=||x_0||.               (6.2)

Suppose `tau<f_n`. The resulting terminal value is positive. A start in
the minimum cone has zero projection gradient and stays a minimizer for
every step, so such a positive terminal value forces

    r:=dist(q_0,C_E)>0.

Equations (6.1)-(6.2) show `r<=||x_0||`. Therefore

    2F_D(q_n)/r^2>=2(f_n-tau)/||x_0||^2.               (6.3)

The lower numerator is positive BEFORE the smaller denominator is used.
The source does not assert `r=||x_0||`, or convergence of `r` to that
number. Neither is required. Ignoring the polar cone or using the large
coordinate `R` in the denominator would destroy the intended transfer.

Finally, at most `p+1` positions are sampled: each zero step leaves the
position unchanged and each positive step adds at most one position.
The number `m` of distinct nonzero sampled gradients therefore satisfies
`m<=p+1`, including the terminal gradient. The simplex has exactly `m+1`
vertices and dimension exactly `m`. The old large ambient source dimension
disappears under this orthogonal compression.

## 9. Elementary d=1 endpoint-to-full-W bridge

For a fixed compact convex `D` containing zero and any scalar `d>0`, define

    U_d^D(x)=max_(g in D){2<x,g>-d||g||^2}.

The superscript is kept in this section to emphasize that ONE body is used
for both the actual trajectory and both sides of any proposed inequality.
The maximization is attained; `U_d^D>=0`, and

    U_1^D(x)=2F_D(x).                                   (9.1)

Let `a` be the actual closest point to `x` in `C_D`. For EVERY `g in D`,
`<a,g><=0`, and completion of a square gives

    2<x,g>-d||g||^2
      <=2<x-a,g>-d||g||^2
      =||x-a||^2/d-d||g-(x-a)/d||^2
      <=dist(x,C_D)^2/d.

Thus

    U_d^D(x)<=dist(x,argmin F_D)^2/d.                   (9.2)

This is an exact elementary upper bound, not an assertion that every `D,x`
attains it. It uses the complete actual polar minimum cone. In particular,
it does not require `0` to be the closest minimizer.

For a fixed word `H` of length `n`, let

    T_H^D=(I-h_(n-1)P_D) composed with ...
                         composed with (I-h_0 P_D),
    S_H=sum H,           Delta_H=1+2S_H.

The empty composition is the identity. Assume, as an UNPROVED HYPOTHESIS,
that a finite constant `C>=0`, independent of the simplex and start, obeys

    for every full-dimensional origin-vertex simplex D in R^k,
      1<=k<=p(H)+1, and every actual start x in R^k,
    U_1^D(T_H^D x)<=C U_(Delta_H)^D(x).                (E_H)

All allowed simplex shapes and all starts must be included. For `x` outside
`C_D`, (9.1)-(9.2) give

    2F_D(T_H^D x)/dist(x,C_D)^2<=C/Delta_H.

For starts in `C_D`, the terminal value is zero. For any selected minimizer
the denominator can only be larger than the closest-distance denominator.
Taking the restricted supremum, then using (R), proves the CONDITIONAL
full-function estimate

    W_n(H)<=C/(1+2S_H).                                 (9.3)

Thus YES: the `d=1` endpoint hypothesis suffices. It is enough to prove
`(E_H)` on precisely the dimensions required by (R); a uniform all-dimension
simplex theorem would also suffice. The lack of a uniform lift condition
number is harmless only because `(E_H)` is quantified over ALL those bodies
and starts BEFORE taking their supremum.

The corresponding stronger all-`d` propagation conjecture would require,
on the same actual composition,

    U_d^D(T_H^D x)<=C U_(d+2S_H)^D(x)
    for every d>=1, every permitted D, and every x.     (P_H)

This includes `(E_H)` by setting `d=1`. The bridge above does not prove
`(P_H)`, does not recover other `d` from `(E_H)`, and does not require them.
For a recursive proof one may WANT control of trailing parameters at
internal nodes, but that is a method-specific stronger burden, not a
logical prerequisite for the final full-`W` implication. If an induction
uses factors at child nodes, its final accumulated factor must still
satisfy `(E_H)`; no per-level loss is silently discarded here.

This audit attempted neither `(E_H)` nor `(P_H)` for the all-depth family.
They remain missing quantitative inequalities, not consequences of (R).
