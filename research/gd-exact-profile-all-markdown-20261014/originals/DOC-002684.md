# Positive-value periodic Markov relay with signed history
Status: complete explicit construction, UNVERIFIED. No new verifier call.
This tests the specific repair suggested after the minimum-reset and residual-sign obstructions: allow positive proximal function values, and check actual past-plane deficits even when the signed residual is positive.

## 1. A parameterized model
Fix a in(0,1), chi>=1, T>=2, and kappa in[0,T-2]. Assume
T>=2a chi,                                               (1)
(T-2-kappa)-(T-kappa)chi^2+T chi/a>=0.                     (2)
For any number m>=1 of blocks, in R^(m+1) set
zeta_0=e_0,
zeta_(i+1)=-a zeta_i+sqrt(1-a^2)e_(i+1).
Define alpha_i=chi for even i and alpha_i=1 for odd i, omega_i=alpha_i zeta_i. Thus
<zeta_i,zeta_j>=(-a)^|i-j|
and all omega_i are independent.

Each real block consists of one unit step followed by
b_i=T-1+(T/a)alpha_(i+1)/alpha_i.
Take the ONE finite max-affine function
J(z)=max{0,max_(0<=i<=m)(<omega_i,z>-kappa alpha_i^2)}
and f=env_1 J. Start at X_0=T alpha_0 zeta_0.
We prove that block i queries X_i and X_i-omega_i with gradient omega_i at both, and that
X_(i+1)=X_i-(1+b_i)omega_i.
The terminal point is X_m with gradient omega_m.

The function f is legal globally: J is finite nonnegative convex with J(0)=0, and its envelope is finite convex differentiable1-smooth. For precision, the proximal minimization has a unique minimizer by the quadratic term; its subgradient equation implies grad f(x)=x-prox_J(x), and monotonicity gives
||grad f(x)-grad f(y)||^2<=<grad f(x)-grad f(y),x-y>.
At any point where plane i is active at x-omega_i, that subgradient equation yields the claimed gradient and value
f(x)=<omega_i,x>-alpha_i^2/2-kappa alpha_i^2.
Thus it remains to compare every affine plane at the indicated proximal points.

## 2. Exact signed residuals
Let Z_i=X_i-T omega_i and put
B_i=(T/a)alpha_(i+1), D_i=B_i(1-a^2).
The update gives
Z_0=0,
Z_(i+1)=Z_i-B_i(zeta_i+a zeta_(i+1)).
Direct multiplication of Markov correlations proves
<Z_i,zeta_j>=0 for j>=i,
<Z_i,zeta_j>=-S_(j,i) for j<i,
where
S_(j,i)=sum_(l=j)^(i-1)D_l(-a)^(l-j).                     (3)
Indeed zeta_l+a zeta_(l+1) is orthogonal to all zeta_j with j>=l+1, and its inner product with earlier zeta_j is(1-a^2)(-a)^(l-j). This proves(3) by summing the update; no sign of S is assumed.

At a real query x=X_i-u omega_i with u=0 or1, its proposed proximal point is
Z_i+d omega_i, d=T-u-1>=T-2.
The terminal query has d=T-1 and obeys the same lower bound.
The current affine plane has value(d-kappa)alpha_i^2>=0.
The current-minus-j-th plane difference is
d alpha_i(alpha_i-C_ij alpha_j)
 +alpha_j S_(j,i)-kappa alpha_i^2+kappa alpha_j^2          (4)
for j<i, with C_ij=(-a)^(i-j); for j>i the same formula has S=0.
Every coefficient of d is positive: at even separation the amplitudes agree and C<1, while at odd separation C<0. Hence it suffices to check d=T-2.

Future planes are already controlled. At even separation their offsets agree and their deficit is d alpha_i^2(1-C)>=0. At odd separation the deficit is
(d-kappa)alpha_i^2+kappa alpha_j^2+d a^|i-j| alpha_i alpha_j>=0.
The substantive point is the full past history.

## 3. All past planes, including positive residuals
For even separation i-j=2q, q>=1, let z=alpha_j=alpha_i and y be the opposite amplitude. Formula(3) is the exact geometric sum
S_(j,i)=(T/a)(y-a z)(1-a^(2q)).
Substituting into(4) gives, at d=T-2,
(1-a^(2q)) z[-2z+(T/a)y]>=0
by(1), since z/y is either chi or1/chi.

For odd separation i-j=2q+1, q>=0, let z=alpha_j and y=alpha_i be the opposite amplitude. Now
S_(j,i)=(T/a)[y-a z+a^(2q)(a z-a^2 y)].
Substitution into(4), still at d=T-2, gives
F_infty(y,z)+a^(2q)[T z^2-2a yz],                       (5)
where
F_infty(y,z)=(T-2-kappa)y^2-(T-kappa)z^2+(T/a)yz.
The second term in(5) is nonnegative by(1).
Among the two choices(y,z)=(1,chi),(chi,1), the first has smaller F_infty: the difference is
2(T-1-kappa)(chi^2-1)>=0.
Its nonnegativity is precisely(2). This proves every old-plane comparison, at every lag, with the signed residual retained. Together with the future and zero-plane checks it proves the entire trajectory in Section1.

The exact sampled values are
f(X_i-u omega_i)=(T-u-1/2-kappa)alpha_i^2.
At a checkpoint query u=1, the nonsmooth proximal value is
J(X_i-2omega_i)=(T-2-kappa)alpha_i^2.                    (6)
It is strictly positive whenever kappa<T-2. Thus these checkpoints are not minimum resets.

## 4. Closest minimizer and payoff
The minimizer set of f is exactly
P_min={z:<omega_i,z><=kappa alpha_i^2 for every i}.
The point y_proj=kappa alpha_0 zeta_0 lies in P_min: for even i the amplitude equals alpha_0 and the correlation is at most1; for odd i the correlation is negative. Constraint0 is active, and
X_0-y_proj=(T-kappa)omega_0
is a positive multiple of its normal. For any z in P_min this gives
<X_0-y_proj,z-y_proj><=0,
so squared-distance expansion proves that y_proj is the unique closest minimizer and
dist(X_0,P_min)^2=(T-kappa)^2 alpha_0^2.
The normalized terminal value of this one instance is therefore exactly
2(T-kappa-1/2)alpha_m^2 / [(T-kappa)^2 alpha_0^2].        (7)
For even m, alpha_m=alpha_0 and this is
[2(T-kappa)-1]/(T-kappa)^2.
All smooth-convex interpolation inequalities, including the optimum sample, hold because all samples have been checked on this one explicit envelope.

## 5. Rational parameters, quadratic stability, and a strict failure of the old test
Choose
a=9/10, chi=6/5, T=23/10, kappa=1/10.
Condition(1) has positive margin
T-2a chi=7/50.
The left side of(2) is exactly37/375>0.
The alternating checkpoint sizes are

both in[3,5]. Therefore for m=2r and every integer r>=1 the length4r schedule
(1,463/135,1,131/30) repeated r times
has the explicitly realized normalized error
W_(4r)(h)>=85/121.                                     (8)
At every checkpoint its proximal value is alpha_i^2/5>0.

This model violates the earlier nonpositive-residual criterion. For any even j with j+2<=m,
S_(j,j+2)=(T/a)(1-a^2)(1-a chi)<0
because a chi=27/25>1. Thus
<Z_(j+2),zeta_j> >0,
and D_j>=a D_(j+1) is false at every such pair. Nevertheless the exact plane comparisons above hold. This is an explicit repair, not an assumption that local correlation checks suffice.

For comparison, each quadratic block multiplier is(1-lambda)(1-b_i lambda).
For0<=lambda<=1/5 it is nonnegative and at most exp(-lambda).
For1/5<=lambda<=1, its positive value is at most8/25, since b_i>=3; the magnitude of its negative minimum is at most4/5, since b_i<=5 and max_(b<=5,b>=3)(b-1)^2/(4b)=4/5.
Hence the full finite-dimensional quadratic normalized objective after m blocks is at most
max{1/(2 e m),(4/5)^(2m)} ->0.
The nonlinear persistent value is not a large quadratic product.

The omega_i are independent, adjacent ones have negative inner product, and their nonnegative-offset max-affine system has isolated zero/plane hinges. At such hinges the envelope has rank-one Hessian projectors onto omega_i. Adjacent projectors do not commute, proving that this function is nonseparable in every fixed orthonormal basis. Independence permits exposing each hinge by solving for one affine value0 and all others strictly negative. ## 6. Scope
This is a parameterized, fully signed-history construction with positive checkpoint values and a concrete rational periodic test outside the earlier residual-sign interface. It does not claim that(1)--(2), or a generalization of them, can realize an arbitrary schedule at the benchmark profile. The endpoint values of the exact all-past comparison now show where positive residuals can be paid for by real face margins and offsets. A future extension must solve those inequalities quantitatively for nonperiodic data, rather than imposing S>=0 again or assuming feasibility at target strength.
