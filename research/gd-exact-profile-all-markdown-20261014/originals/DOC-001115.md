## 2. A definite function-realization proposal: tilted squared distance

For the chosen mechanism define

                 y_i=x_i-R_i=u-s_i v+sum_j(d_j-1)p_ij w_j.

Choose the shared values

                    f_i=||R_i||^2/2+<v,x_i>-||v||^2/2.       (6)

At the optimum set R_star=-v and y_star=v; formula(6) still gives f_star=0
at x_star=0. Substituting (6) into every original Q_ij gives the exact law

                   Q_ij=<R_i,y_j-y_i><=0              (7)

for ALL ordered pairs i,j in {star,0,...,q}. In particular the original
optimum constraints are retained, rather than imposed separately on modes.

When (7) holds, let C=conv{v,y_0,...,y_q}. For any query i and any c in C,
<R_i,c-y_i><=0. Since x_i=y_i+R_i,

 ||x_i-c||^2=||R_i||^2+||c-y_i||^2-2<R_i,c-y_i>
                                                >=||R_i||^2.

Thus the Euclidean projection of x_i onto C is y_i. The star condition
likewise says that the projection of0 onto C is v. Consequently the actual
function

             F_C(x)=dist(x,C)^2/2+<v,x>-||v||^2/2       (8)

interpolates ALL the prescribed jets and has minimum zero at zero.

For completeness, Euclidean projections onto a closed convex set obey
<P_Cx-P_Cz,x-z>>=||P_Cx-P_Cz||^2, obtained by adding the two projection
normal inequalities. Therefore I-P_C is1-Lipschitz. Comparing the minimum
squared-distance expressions at x and x+d gives differentiability of
dist(x,C)^2/2 with gradient x-P_Cx. This function is convex, since joint
convexity of ||x-c||^2 and convexity of C give the convexity inequality after
taking minima over c. Addition of the linear term preserves convexity and
smoothness. Its gradient at0 is-v+v=0, proving the minimum assertion.

The actual minimizer set is also explicit. The star inequalities give
<v,c>>=||v||^2 for every c in C. Completing the square gives

 F_C(x)=min_(c in C){||x-(c-v)||^2/2+<v,c>-||v||^2}.

Hence argmin F_C={c-v:c in C,<v,c>=||v||^2}. One may require this face to be
the singleton{v}, or require0 to be the closest point in this set to x_0,
when normalizing by the TRUE closest-minimizer distance. The upper result
below holds with any designated minimizer0 and ||x_0||=1, and hence also
with that stronger true-distance convention. The lower calibration has a
unique minimizer, so its denominator is exactly the true distance.

The same obstruction applies to ANY convex1-smooth interpolant whose queried

necessary all-pair law (7) is used in its proof.
