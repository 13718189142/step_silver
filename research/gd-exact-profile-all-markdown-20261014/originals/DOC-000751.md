# Quantitative tests of the verified alternating-ray functional

Status: new analytic candidate findings based on verified fact
1fca3e5dd555cb4a; not independently verifier-accepted. Worker: max3.

## A precise obstruction to the ray functional alone

For every n>=1 define h_t=(t+1)/2 for 0<=t<n. Then the alternating-ray
functional F_ray_n of fact 1fca3e5dd555cb4a equals the single-cut functional
K_n: no selected chain with two or more checkpoints is feasible.
For n>=2,

\[
F_{\rm ray,n}(h)=K_n(h)\leq16/n^2,
\qquad
\frac{F_{\rm ray,n}(h)}{U_n^{ZJ}}
\leq16(1+4n^{\log_2 3})/n^2\longrightarrow0.
\]

This refutes the exact uniform-profile comparison for F_ray_n alone. It does
not refute the same comparison after adding quadratic witnesses, and it is
not a negative solution of the minimax problem.

### General first-return obstruction

For any selected first two checkpoints at original indices i<j, let
P=sum_{t<i}h_t and M=sum_{i<t<j}h_t. The first two magnitudes in the verified
recurrence, if the second is positive, are

\[
m_0=1/(1+P),\quad
m_1=(h_i-1)/[(1+P)(1+M)],\quad O_1=P/(1+P).
\]

The available distance after the second checkpoint, before dividing by the
next ordinary-block mass, is therefore

\[
(h_j-1)m_1-O_1
=\frac{(h_i-1)(h_j-1)/(1+M)-P}{1+P}.
\tag{1}
\]

Every feasible chain with at least two checkpoints must have both h_i>1
and h_j>1. If the second checkpoint is nonterminal, otherwise m_1 or m_2
would be nonpositive. If it is terminal, its feasible final magnitude must
be at least m_0>0, forcing the same strict positivity.

If

\[
(h_i-1)(h_j-1)\leq P(1+M),
\tag{2}
\]

then (1) is nonpositive. This contradicts either the required positive m_2
when more checkpoints follow, or the terminal lower bound
z_term>=(1+s_2)m_0>0 when these are the only two checkpoints.
Consequently, if (2) holds for every eligible pair i<j, all feasible chains
have at most one checkpoint.

### The half-linear schedule satisfies every pair inequality

For h_t=(t+1)/2, an eligible i has i>=2. Write j=i+1+d with integer d>=0.
Then P=i(i+1)/4, M>=d(i+2)/2, and

\[
4(h_i-1)(h_j-1)=(i-1)(i+d).
\]

On the other hand

\[
4P(1+M)\geq i(i+1)[1+d(i+2)/2].
\]

At d=0, the right side minus the left is2i>0. Its coefficient of d is
i(i+1)(i+2)/2-(i-1)>0 for i>=2. Thus (2) holds strictly for all eligible
pairs, proving F_ray_n=K_n.

### Uniform bound on K_n for this family

The total step sum is n(n+1)/4, so the global Huber term is at most2/n^2.
For a cut j write P_j=j(j+1)/4 and
T_j=[n(n+1)-(j+1)(j+2)]/4. If j>=n/2, then
(h_j-1)_+<=j/2 and P_j>=j^2/4, so the cut term is at most4/j^2<=16/n^2.
If j<n/2, the inequalities
j+1<=(n+1)/2 and j+2<=(n+3)/2 give

\[
1+2T_j\geq(3n^2+5)/8\geq n^2/4.
\]

Also (h_j-1)_+<=1+P_j. Thus this cut term is at most4/n^2. Combining
the cases proves the stated16/n^2 estimate. Verified fact b98bb807d514e80d
supplies the exact-recursion estimate 1/U_n^ZJ<=1+4n^(log_2 3), which gives
the vanishing comparison without importing an asymptotic numerical constant.

## What the obstruction says about the mechanism

The alternating-ray construction demands that the second crossing can return
beyond the already visited positive-ray record. Inequality (2) prevents even
that first return, regardless of any later selected checkpoints. A genuine
GD trajectory can first return inside its old range and later expand. The
verified construction does not describe that behavior. Quadratic witnesses
are one way to capture it; arbitrary inner returns would require a new legal
extension of the state, rather than deleting the ray-feasibility conditions.

No bound on the full W_n of the half-linear schedules is claimed. Their
quadratic behavior may be large, so this construction is not evidence against
the combined functional max(F_ray_n,Q_n), where Q_n is the exact quadratic
worst-case polynomial functional.

## A second exact necessary condition for any adjacent checkpoint pair

For consecutive selected checkpoints b_i,b_{i+1}, the verified recurrence and
O_{i+1}=O_i+s_i m_i imply that a feasible next same-ray magnitude at least
m_i requires

\[
(b_i-1)(b_{i+1}-1)\geq
(1+s_{i+1})(1+s_i+s_{i+2})
 +\frac{b_{i+1}+s_{i+1}}{m_i}O_i.
\tag{3}
\]

For a nonterminal next visit this follows by multiplying its recurrence by
1+s_{i+1}, substituting m_{i+1}, and using m_{i+2}>=m_i. For a terminal
next visit, replace m_{i+2} by its allowed upper endpoint z_term/(1+s_{i+2});
the same feasibility inequality follows. Every quantity retained on the right
is nonnegative. Equation (3) is a useful local obstruction, not a sufficient
condition for a complete legal chain and not a profile theorem.
