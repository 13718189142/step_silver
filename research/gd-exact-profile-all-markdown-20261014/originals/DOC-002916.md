    ell=a^2*b*(1-b*t), z=a*b*v*t,
    R=ell*N^2+mu-C^2*a^2*b*beta*t^2,
    D=N^2*b^2-A^2.

Let e_i be the five original standard columns, indexed0 through4,
e=sum e_i, and P_i=sum_(j<i)h_j. Define

    T_ii=1, T_ij=h_j for j<i, T_ij=0 for j>i,
    omega=(-2h0,-2h1,-2h2,-2h3,2s)^T,
    Dmat=diag(h0(2-h0),h1(2-h1),h2(2-h2),h3(2-h3),-s(s+2)),
    L=diag(Lambda e)-Lambda, M=Dmat+(LT+T^T L^T)/2.

The hypotheses are ALL twenty signs Lambda_ij>=0 for i!=j, zero
diagonal, L^T e=omega, and M>=0 on ALL of R^5. There is no support,
rank, symmetry, reversal, or duplicated-gradient restriction.

The accepted identity is

    V_h+sum_(i!=j)Lambda_ij Q_ij=sum_(i,j)M_ij<g_i,g_j>,
    Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2,
    V_h=2sum_(i<4)h_i(f_i-f4)+sum_(i<4)h_i(1-h_i)||g_i||^2
                                                 -s(s+1)||g4||^2, (1)

on formal data with x_(i+1)=x_i-h_i*g_i. Formal substitutions below
are used as polynomial identities; no unproved interpolation is assumed.

Te=e+P and the flow give e^TMe=0. Indeed e^TDmat e=-s^2-sum h_i^2
and omega^T(e+P)=s^2+sum h_i^2. Applying PSD to e+r*y for both
signs of the real number r gives Me=0. The same argument proves

    |<x,y>_M|^2<=||x||_M^2*||y||_M^2,
    ||x||_M^2=0 implies Mx=0,                           (2)

where <x,y>_M=x^TMy and ||x||_M^2=x^TMx. Under the flow, the
individual kernel equations are

    sum_j Lambda_ij(P_j-P_i)=2h_i(1+P_i), i<4,
    sum_(j<4)Lambda_4j(s-P_j)=2sC.                       (3)

These follow by expanding 2Me=2Dmat e+L(e+P)+T^T omega.

Put

    E=e0, J=e0+e1, B=e3+e4,
    m=||E||_M^2, S=||J||_M^2, Tcut=||B||_M^2, rJB=<J,B>_M,
    B0=Lambda30+Lambda40, B1=Lambda31+Lambda41,
    Avec=u*E-a*beta*J-a*b*mu*B,
    c03old=u*mu*z, c23old=a^2*b*beta*mu*v*t.

The three exact identities needed here are

    R=ell*mu^2*Tcut+mu*||Avec||_M^2
         +ell*mu*(Hpre*B0+beta*B1)+c03old*Lambda03+c23old*Lambda23, (4)

    D=beta*((b*w*H0pre-u)*B0+b^2*w*B1-2*b*w*rJB-beta*S
                     -u*Lambda20-b*v*c*(Lambda04+Lambda14)),       (5)

    2<E,B>_M=a*B0-Lambda03-(1+v)*Lambda04.                         (6)

To prove (4), use saturated values f_i=x_i*g_i-g_i^2/2 on

    x_base=(N,2+b,1,-c,-t), g_base=(1,1,1,-t,-t),
    x_aux =(1,-a,ab,-abc,-abt), g_aux=(1,-a,ab,-abt,-abt).

For these values Q_ij=(g_i-g_j)(x_j-g_j). In ell times the base
plus mu times the auxiliary, the complete nonzero residual list is

    Q30=Q40=-ell*mu*Hpre, Q31=Q41=-ell*mu*beta,
    Q03=-c03old, Q23=-c23old.

Every other ordered residual is zero. In particular Q13 cancels
because ell=a^2*b*(1-bt); at Q03 use ell+ab(1+abt)=ab*u, and
at Q23 use ell+a^2*b^2*mu=a^2*b*beta. Telescoping (1) gives
R; the two gradient columns modulo e are -mu*B and Avec, proving (4).

For (5), start the scalar two-step cut at x0=A with gradients
(1,1,-b), then extend FORMALLY with constant gradient -b and affine
values through the last two steps. Its energy is -D and its column
modulo e is beta*J. Its negative residuals are
Q20=Q30=Q40=-u*beta, and its positive residuals are
Q03=Q13=w*b*beta, Q04=Q14=(w+v)*b*beta. All others vanish.
Thus (1) gives

 D=b*beta*(w*(Lambda03+Lambda13)+(w+v)*(Lambda04+Lambda14))
                        -beta^2*S-u*beta*(Lambda20+B0).

Besides (6), direct multiplication of T gives

    2<e1,B>_M=beta*B0+b*B1-Lambda13-(1+v)*Lambda14.

Adding these rows, and using (w+v)-w(1+v)=-vc, gives (5).

The additional ORIGINAL row used in this derivation is

    2<E,J+B>_M=-a*Lambda20+Lambda02+w*Lambda03+w*Lambda04.           (7)

In fact e2=e-J-B and
2M02=a*Lambda20-Lambda02-w*(Lambda03+Lambda04). This proves (7)
without any PSD quotient or new support assumption.


### Definition needed for the original positive root

For k>0 and real theta, set

    F(k,theta)=b^2*w^2-z*theta

This definition and the original row identities just proved suffice for

## 3. The unrestricted root and the new full-row remainder

Assume v>0 for now. Then ell>0, z>0 and 1-bt>0. Set

    k0=b^2*w/(ell*mu), kold=b^2*w^2/(ell*mu^2).

The minimum of F(k,theta) over all real theta is attained at

    theta(k)=2*z*k*mu/(1+a^2*k*mu).

It is strictly decreasing as a function of k: at each fixed theta,
partial F/partial k=-ell*mu^2-theta^2/(4*k^2*mu)<0, and the minimum
is attained continuously. At k0 the minimum divided by mu^2 is
b^2*(gamma-1)*(gamma+a^2*k0*mu)/(1+a^2*k0*mu)>0,
where gamma=w/mu>1. At kold it is negative, since F(kold,0)=0 and
the negative linear term improves it for small positive theta.
There is therefore a unique root k in (k0,kold).

Here is an explicit definition requiring no root search. Define

    Hbar=(2*b*w-beta
          +sqrt((beta-2*b*w)^2+4*b*w^2*(1-b*t)/mu))/(2*(1-b*t)),
    k=Hbar/(a^2*mu), theta=2*z*k*mu/(1+Hbar),
    x=b*w-a*theta/2=b*(w+Hbar*mu)/(1+Hbar), rho=x/beta,
    hcoef=theta/(2*k*mu), jcoef=a^2*theta/2,
    rstar=u*theta/2.

The positive Hbar solves

    (1-bt)*Hbar^2+(beta-2*bw)*Hbar-b*w^2/mu=0,

which follows by substituting theta(k) into F=0. Also

    0<rho<1, hcoef+jcoef=z,
    a*x-a*b*mu=hcoef, k*ell*mu^2+theta^2/(4*k*mu)
                                            =2*b*w*x-x^2.         (8)

The inequalities use mu<w and bw<=beta, with strictness supplied
by Hbar>0. The identities follow by expansion and F=0.

Full original feasibility itself forces v<2. Indeed the terminal
diagonal is n=||e4||_M^2=sum_(j<4)Lambda4j-s(s+2)>=0.
Every weight in the last row of (3) is at least v. Hence

    v*n<=s*(2C-(C+1)*v), so v<=2C/(C+1)<2.                       (9)

We now work on 0<v<2; v>=2 was already excluded by this original row.
Define the nonnegative correction

    qcorr=max(0,((1+v)*rstar-beta*b*v*c)
                         /(w+(1+v)*(1-rho))),
    rshift=rstar-qcorr*(1-rho),
    X=beta*J+x*B.

The EXACT original-array remainder is

    eps:=k*R-D
       =(1+Hbar)*||X||_M^2
         -2*(k*mu*u*a+qcorr/beta)*<E,X>_M+k*mu*u^2*m
         +cB*B0+c1*B1+c20*Lambda20+qcorr*Lambda02
         +c03*Lambda03+c04*Lambda04+c14*Lambda14+c23*Lambda23,      (10)

where

    cB=k*ell*mu*Hpre-beta*(b*w*H0pre-u)+a*rshift,
    c1=beta*(k*ell*mu-b^2*w), c20=beta*u-a*qcorr,
    c03=k*c03old-rshift+w*qcorr,
    c04=beta*b*v*c-(1+v)*rshift+w*qcorr,
    c14=beta*b*v*c, c23=k*c23old.

To check the identity, first combine (4) and (5). Its Gram part is

 beta^2*||J||_M^2+2*beta*b*w*<J,B>_M+k*ell*mu^2*Tcut
                                                 +k*mu*||Avec||_M^2.

Subtract 2*rshift*<E,B>_M+2*qcorr*<E,J+B>_M from that Gram part.
Restore them using (6) and (7); this gives precisely all costs in
(10). Since rshift=rstar-qcorr*(1-rho), the subtraction differs
from 2*rstar*<E,B> by 2*qcorr*<E,X>/beta. F=0 and (8) turn the
uncorrected Gram into ||X||^2+k*mu*||uE-aX||^2. This proves (10).

Every displayed cost is nonnegative; cB,c1,c20,c03,c14,c23 are
STRICTLY positive. Here is the full sign and PSD check.

Let

    qmax=beta*u*(sqrt(k*mu*(1+Hbar))-a*k*mu).

Then

    0<=qcorr<qmax<beta*u/(2a).                                  (11)

If qcorr=0 this follows from Hbar>0. Otherwise put
tau=sqrt(Hbar/(1+Hbar)) in (0,1), Vprod=vbc, and
Sden=2+b+c+v. Direct substitution gives

    qcorr=(beta/a)*Vprod*(u*tau^2-a*beta)
                              /(Sden-Vprod+Vprod*tau^2),
    qmax=(beta*u/a)*tau/(1+tau).

The numerator of qcorr is positive in this case. The inequality
qcorr<qmax is equivalent to

    Vprod*(1+tau)*(u*tau-a*beta)<u*tau*Sden.

Its left side is smaller than 2*Vprod*u*tau. Moreover
2*Vprod<=2v<2+b+c+v=Sden, using bc<=1 and v<2. This proves the
strict inequality. The upper bound on qmax follows from
tau/(1+tau)<1/2.

Also rshift>0: when qcorr>0 its numerator is less than
(1+v)*rstar, while its denominator is strictly larger than
(1+v)*(1-rho). Thus qcorr<rstar/(1-rho).
At k=k0 the part of cB before a*rshift equals u*(1-bc)>=0;
it strictly increases with k. Hence cB>0. Since k>k0, c1>0.
Equation (11) gives c20>beta*u/2>0. Finally
rstar<k*u*mu*z=k*c03old and rshift<=rstar, proving c03>0.
The definition of qcorr gives c04>=0, and c14,c23>0.

Set

    alpha=(k*mu*u*a+qcorr/beta)/(1+Hbar), Y=X-alpha*E,
    sigma=k*mu*u^2-(k*mu*u*a+qcorr/beta)^2/(1+Hbar)>0.

The positivity of sigma is exactly qcorr<qmax. Thus (10) is

    eps=sigma*m+(1+Hbar)*||Y||_M^2+the nonnegative costs in (10).  (12)

In particular every full original certificate obeys D<=kR. This
necessity argument uses the actual original rows. Its comparison with
the complete older conditions is supplied by Sections1--9 of the
present joint text, not by a claim about an auxiliary optimizer.

## 4. The actual quadratic column and joint first-row budget

Define

    pquad=(1,-a,ab,-abc,abc*(v-1))^T,
    Q=1-C^2*a^2*b^2*c^2*(v-1)^2,
    delta=u^2-2, W0=1+bc*(v-1).

Tp=e and Le=0. Telescoping h_i(2-h_i)p_i^2=p_i^2-p_(i+1)^2 gives

    ||pquad||_M^2=Q>=0,
    2<E,pquad>_M=u*(m-delta)
             +a^2*(beta*Lambda20+(1-bc)*Lambda30+W0*Lambda40).    (13)

For the row formula, let O0=sum_(j>0)Lambda0j and
I0=Lambda20+Lambda30+Lambda40. Then m=1-a^2-aO0 and node0 flow
gives Lambda10=O0+2u-I0. With Te0=u e-a e0 and
Mp=Dmat pquad+T^T L^T pquad/2 one obtains

 pquad^T L e0=uO0+2au-a*(beta*Lambda20+(1-bc)*Lambda30+W0*Lambda40),
 2<E,pquad>_M=2(1-a^2)-a*pquad^T L e0,

which gives (13). Both incoming weights 1-bc and W0 are nonnegative,
and 1-bc<=W0, including v<1.

By (12), eps>=sigma*m+c20*Lambda20+cB*B0. Define

    xi_cap=min(c20/(a^2*beta), cB/(a^2*W0)),

omitting the second term if W0=0. For every real xi with
xi<=xi_cap and xi<sigma/u,

    eps>=xi*u*delta-xi^2*Q/(sigma-xi*u).                         (14)

Subtract xi*u*delta using (13). All remaining multiplier coefficients
are nonnegative: for xi>=0 this is the definition of xi_cap and the
order of the incoming weights; for xi<0 the coefficients increase.
The remaining Gram expression after adding xi^2Q/(sigma-xi*u) is

    ||sqrt(sigma-xi*u)*E+xi*pquad/sqrt(sigma-xi*u)||_M^2>=0.

Thus this is the same ORIGINAL matrix and column, not independent
energy selections. Let Qplus=Q+u^2*delta. Sending xi to -infinity
shows Qplus>=0, and

    Qplus=a^2*(A^2-C^2*b^2*c^2*(v-1)^2).

That prerequisite is the old scalar-return condition and is not
counted as an independent improvement.

For completeness, when Q,Qplus>0 the free maximizing xi is
xi_free=(sigma/u)*(1-sqrt(Q/Qplus)). If xi_free<=xi_cap, (14)
has supremum

    Bcal=(sigma/u^2)*(sqrt(Qplus)-sqrt(Q))^2.

Otherwise its supremum is

    Bcal=xi_cap*u*delta-xi_cap^2*Q/(sigma-xi_cap*u).

The latter case automatically has xi_cap<sigma/u. For Q>0,Qplus=0,
the supremum is sigma*Q/u^2. For Q=0,Qplus>=0 it is
u*delta*min(xi_cap,sigma/u), and it is zero when Q=delta=0.
These statements follow on putting y=sigma-xi*u>0: the objective is

 sigma*delta+2*sigma*Q/u^2-(delta+Q/u^2)*y-Q*sigma^2/(u^2*y).

The earlier first-row budget is also retained, on its stated domain
0<a<1 and a*eta<1, where

    eta=b/(1+w+v), nu=(1+eta)*(1-a*eta)/a,
    Erow=a+3+1/a-(u*delta/a)*eta-C^2*eta^2.

Its exact identity is

 Erow=nu*(m+a*(Lambda20+B0))+(1+eta)^2*M11
                 +(1+eta)*eta*((w+v)*Lambda12+v*Lambda13).        (15)

Here is a direct derivation. For orthonormal physical vectors U,V set
gfirst=-eta*U+sqrt(nu)*V and use saturated values on

 Xphys=(U+u*gfirst,U,-b*U,-(1+v)*eta*U,-eta*U),
 Gphys=(gfirst,U,-eta*U,-eta*U,-eta*U).

The nonzero residuals are Q20=Q30=Q40=-a*nu,
Q12=-(1+eta)*(b-eta), and Q13=-(1+eta)*v*eta. Every other finite
residual is zero. Use ||gfirst||^2=[1+(1-a)*eta]/a and
<U,gfirst>+a||gfirst||^2=1-a*eta to check the target0 residuals.
The energy telescope gives Erow and the two gradient columns modulo
e are sqrt(nu)*E and (1+eta)*e1, proving (15).

In particular, for EVERY lambda>=0, (14) holds with its left side
eps+lambda*Erow, sigma replaced by sigma+lambda*nu, and cap replaced
by

 min((c20+lambda*nu*a)/(a^2*beta),
                         (cB+lambda*nu*a)/(a^2*W0)).              (16)

This follows by adding the TWO exact budgets before using (13), so
the same m and original incoming coefficients must pay for both.
It does not replace any of the older2948 conditions. For example,
putting Wrow=max(beta,W0), Krow=max(u,a*Wrow) in (13),(15),(2) gives

 u*delta<=Krow*Erow/nu+2*sqrt(Q*Erow/nu).

For delta>0, v>1, ab<=1 and c(v-1)<=1, Krow=u and Q<1. These imply
Erow>nu*delta^2/u^2 and

    b<(a+3)*(C-a-2)/((a+1)*(C+a+2)).

To see the latter equivalence, the direct factorization is

 u^2*(Erow-nu*delta^2/u^2)
    =((a+3)-(delta+u*C)*eta)*((a+3)+(u*C-delta)*eta).

The second factor is positive since C>A and u*A>delta. Substituting
eta=b/(C-A-b) in the positive first factor gives the displayed
bound. All its hypotheses remain.

## 5. A further matched full-column inequality near equality

In this section v>0 and all constants are the ones already defined.
Let

    dphys=a*b*c*v=(1+v)*z,
    nbar=s*(2C-(C+1)*v)/v>=0,
    rcoef=ell*mu^2+mu*hcoef^2>0,
    Lnorm=sqrt((u-a*alpha)^2/sigma+a^2/(1+Hbar)),
    Lterm=sqrt((alpha-beta)^2/sigma+1/(1+Hbar)),
    Kterm=(dphys/x)*Lterm*sqrt(nbar), Jfee=a/(2*x),
    Pcost=max(ell*mu*Hpre/cB,ell*mu*beta/c1,c03old/c03,1/k),
    Gcost=max(mu*Lnorm^2,Pcost).

All denominators are strictly positive. If nbar<0, the original
terminal budget (9) already excludes the word; it is never square-rooted.

Every full original array satisfies the following explicit scalar test.
Write y=sqrt(eps), which requires eps>=0, and set

 tau_max(y)=(sqrt(Q)+Lnorm*y
       +sqrt((sqrt(Q)+Lnorm*y)^2+4*jcoef*(Kterm*y+Jfee*y^2)))
                                                            /(2*jcoef),
 Psi(y)=rcoef*tau_max(y)^2
            +2*mu*hcoef*Lnorm*tau_max(y)*y+Gcost*y^2.

Then

                              R<=Psi(sqrt(eps)).                 (17)

Here is the full derivation retaining the actual quadratic column.
The vector identity, checked in all five coordinates, is

    pquad=uE-aX-jcoef*B+dphys*e4+ab*e.

The original terminal rows give

    2<E,e4>_M=a*Lambda40-Lambda04,
    2<J,e4>_M=2<E,e4>_M+beta*Lambda40+b*Lambda41-Lambda14.

Since X=Y+alpha E, they imply

 x<B,e4>_M=(alpha-beta)<E,e4>_M+<Y,e4>_M
                              -beta*(beta*Lambda40+b*Lambda41-Lambda14)/2.

Taking the inner product of pquad with B therefore gives

 <B,pquad>_M=(u-a*alpha)<E,B>_M-a<Y,B>_M-jcoef*Tcut
       +(dphys/x)*((alpha-beta)<E,e4>_M+<Y,e4>_M)
       -dphys*beta*(beta*Lambda40+b*Lambda41-Lambda14)/(2*x).       (18)

Let eps_G=sigma*m+(1+Hbar)||Y||_M^2 and let eps_C be the cost sum
in (12). Thus eps=eps_G+eps_C with both nonnegative. Weighted
Cauchy--Schwarz gives

 ||(u-a*alpha)E-aY||_M<=Lnorm*sqrt(eps_G),
 ||(alpha-beta)E+Y||_M<=Lterm*sqrt(eps_G).

Also ||e4||_M^2<=nbar by (9), and
c14=beta*dphys/a. Drop the negative Lambda40,Lambda41 terms in
(18), use Lambda14<=eps_C/c14, and use
<B,pquad>_M>=-sqrt(Tcut*Q). The result is

 jcoef*Tcut<=sqrt(Tcut*Q)
        +(Lnorm*sqrt(Tcut)+Kterm)*sqrt(eps_G)+Jfee*eps_C.          (19)

For the other direction (4) and Avec=(u-a*alpha)E-aY+hcoef*B give

 R<=rcoef*Tcut+2*mu*hcoef*Lnorm*sqrt(Tcut*eps_G)
                                    +mu*Lnorm^2*eps_G+Pcost*eps_C
  <=rcoef*Tcut+2*mu*hcoef*Lnorm*sqrt(Tcut*eps)+Gcost*eps.           (20)

The cost bound follows by comparing each cost in (4) to the cost of
the SAME multiplier in (12); their maximum ratio is precisely Pcost.
Thus costs and energies have not been chosen from different arrays.
Replacing eps_G,eps_C separately by eps in (19) gives, for
tcut=sqrt(Tcut)>=0,

 jcoef*tcut^2-(sqrt(Q)+Lnorm*y)*tcut-(Kterm*y+Jfee*y^2)<=0.

Its nonnegative root is tau_max(y). The right side of (20) increases
with tcut>=0, which proves (17).

This supplies a quantified positive-gap exclusion test. Psi is
continuous and strictly increasing on [0,infinity), tends to infinity,
and

    Psi(0)=rcoef*Q/jcoef^2.

Consequently, if R>rcoef*Q/jcoef^2, there is a unique y0>0 with
Psi(y0)=R, and every full array must have

                           eps>=y0^2>0.                         (21)

Strict increase follows from the explicit nondecreasing positive
coefficients in tau_max and Gcost>0. This is an algebraic definition
and proof; no numerical root finding is used. In particular Q=0,R>0
forces a positive eps, even when delta=0 and the first-row bound
(14) alone is zero. This prices a further original cross column.
No uniform lower bound as v,ell,R,Q or other factors vanish is claimed.
