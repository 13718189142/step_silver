## 1. Objects, scope, and the new distinction

Use smoothness 1. A supplied word h=(h_0,...,h_(n-1)) generates

    X_(i+1)=X_i-h_i g_i,  g_i=grad F(X_i),
    f_i=F(X_i)-min F,  q_i=||g_i||^2,
    V=2 f_n / R_init^2,  R_init=dist(X_0,argmin F)>0.

The included optimum is star, with X_star=g_star=f_star=0. The ORIGINAL
directed residual is

    Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,X_j-X_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2.       (1)

For a nonzero queried gradient put

    A_i=sqrt(q_i), u_i=g_i/A_i,
    eta_i=<X_i,g_i>/q_i,
    delta_i=f_i-q_i/2,
    z_i=<X_i,g_i>-f_i-q_i/2.

The two optimum rows imply

    eta_i>=1,  delta_i>=0,  z_i>=0,
    delta_i+z_i=(eta_i-1)q_i.                         (2)

The affine value reserve is delta_i and the optimum upper-row slack is
z_i. They are different quantities. The constructions below make both
strictly positive. The class bounds allow either to be zero and do not
prescribe any queried value within the legal interval (2).

Three conclusions are proved here.

* Arbitrary slack does not rescue an aligned-anchor Markov trajectory
  satisfying 1<=eta_i<h_i. Its amplitude contraction is already forced
  by its support geometry, independently of all values and remote rows.
* A genuinely future-correlated anchor does escape that contraction.
  Sections 4--6 provide an exact finite family and a bounded rule for
  every word with min h>1, with all pairs, one static function, a true
  radius, a payoff and a paid continuation law.
* Sparse future correlations still cannot supply the original benchmark
  on the original enclosing-m recursive words. Section 8 gives an exact
  weighted bound for arbitrary locations and arbitrary values, a free
  terminal, and even arbitrary signs at the exceptional transitions.
  A nonvanishing benchmark payoff requires order n/m nonzero future
  anchor innovations under the stated support restriction.

The last conclusion does NOT exclude a dense bounded-rule anchor, a
different dependence law, or trajectories whose support ratios cross
the physical steps. It is not an upper bound on unrestricted W.

## 2. The finite sign law and the actual cost of a correlated anchor

For queried indices 0,...,n-1, suppose the unit directions have Markov
product inner products

    <u_i,u_j>=product_(k=i)^(j-1)rho_k, i<j, |rho_k|<=1. (3)

For strictly interior rho_k, these are the moments of a literal finite
two-state sign law. Let S_0 be fair and independently let the bond signs
J_sign_k have P(J_sign_k=1)=(1+rho_k)/2. Set S_(k+1)=S_k J_sign_k. Then

    p(s_0,...,s_(n-1))=2^(-n) product_(k<n-1)
                             (1+rho_k s_k s_(k+1)).   (4)

Successive summation over the last sign normalizes (4); independence
of the bonds gives (3). Real coordinates are explicit:

    u_0=e_0,
    u_(i+1)=rho_i u_i+sqrt(1-rho_i^2)e_(i+1),            (5)

where the e_i are orthonormal. Hence PSD is not an assumption about a
formal Gram matrix. Formula (5) realizes it. The formula also allows
rho_i=+/-1, in which case no new direction is needed at that transition.

Conversely, from (3), when |rho_i|<1 the vector

    e_(i+1)=(u_(i+1)-rho_i u_i)/sqrt(1-rho_i^2)

has unit norm and is orthogonal to every u_j, j<=i. This follows by
substituting <u_j,u_(i+1)>=rho_i<u_j,u_i>. It is consequently orthogonal
to all earlier nondegenerate innovations. Thus (5) describes the
canonical real innovations of any such product-correlation data.

Write w_(i+1)=<X_0,e_(i+1)>. Earlier gradients are orthogonal to
e_(i+1), so actual GD, not an assigned support matrix, gives

    eta_(i+1) A_(i+1)
       =rho_i(eta_i-h_i) A_i+sqrt(1-rho_i^2)w_(i+1).    (6)

The initial e_0 coefficient is eta_0 A_0. Orthogonal decomposition gives
the EXACT identity

    ||X_0||^2 = eta_0^2 q_0
       +sum_(i:|rho_i|<1)
          [eta_(i+1)A_(i+1)-rho_i(eta_i-h_i)A_i]^2
                                  /(1-rho_i^2)
       +||X_perp||^2,                              (7)

where X_perp is the component perpendicular to all queried gradients.
For a degenerate transition the numerator must be zero and there is
no summand. For prescribed A_i, eta_i and rho_i, (7) is the minimal
squared norm realizing those support data when X_perp=0. This is only
a geometric realization statement; it is NOT interpolation sufficiency.

If 0 is not the closest minimizer, (7) is not the true initial radius.
The explicit constructions below have unique minimizer 0. For the
whole-class bounds we instead use

    A_0<=R_init,                                       (8)

which follows from 1-Lipschitzness of grad F between X_0 and ANY
minimizer, followed by taking the infimum of the distances. Thus no
nonclosest minimizer is silently used as the normalization.

## 3. Slack cannot repair the unforced support contraction

Assume every queried eta_i lies in [1,h_i). If w_(i+1)=0, (6) forces
rho_i<0 for nonzero consecutive gradients, and

    A_(i+1)=|rho_i|(h_i-eta_i)A_i/eta_(i+1)
            <=(h_i-1)A_i.                          (9)

For a radial initial point X_0=eta_0 g_0 all future coefficients w_i
are zero. The sharper product before the last inequality in (9) is

    A_j/A_i <= product_(k=i)^(j-1)
                                  (h_k-eta_k)/eta_(k+1). (10)

No f_i or z_i appears. In particular arbitrary optimum slack, even
horizon-dependent and approaching the whole allowed reserve, cannot
change this support obstruction. A zero queried gradient means its
point minimizes F and every later GD iterate stays there; its terminal
error is zero and is already covered separately.

For later use there is also a universal one-step bound, without (3),
any support restriction, or any condition on the initial anchor. Adding
the two adjacent rows (1) yields

    ||g_i-g_(i+1)||^2 <= h_i<g_i-g_(i+1),g_i>.

Completing the square gives the entire necessary disk

    ||g_(i+1)-(1-h_i/2)g_i|| <= (h_i/2)||g_i||.

For h_i>1 this implies

    A_(i+1)<=max{1,h_i-1}A_i.                      (11)

If the adjacent inner product is nonpositive, a stronger bound holds.
Write d=h_i-1>0 and t=-<g_i,g_(i+1)>/q_i>=0.
The same inequality and Cauchy--Schwarz give

    q_(i+1)/q_i <= d+(d-1)t,  t^2<=q_(i+1)/q_i.

Thus (t+1)(t-d)<=0 and 0<=t<=d. Maximizing the affine expression over
that interval gives

    q_(i+1)<=d max{1,d} q_i.                       (12)

These are necessary consequences of the original rows, not claims
that adjacent rows suffice for a legal trajectory.

## 4. An actual correlated-anchor escape: all rows displayed

Take the supplied physical word h=(3,10). For

    8/5<=k<4,  0<epsilon<1/2,

define in R^3

    g_0=e_0,
    g_1=(-e_0+e_1)/2,
    g_2=(k/2)e_0-(k/2)e_1+sqrt(k(4-k)/2)e_2,
    X_0=2e_0+e_1,
    X_1=-e_0+e_1,
    X_2=4e_0-4e_1.

The points obey the supplied GD updates. Their squared gradient norms
are q=(1,1/2,2k), and <X_i,g_i>=2q_i. Prescribe

    f=(3/2,3/4,3k)-epsilon,
    delta_i=q_i-epsilon>0,  z_i=epsilon>0.          (13)

The normalized directions have product correlations with

    rho_0=-1/sqrt(2), rho_1=-sqrt(k)/2.

The projection coefficient of g_2 on g_1 is -k, and its e_2 component
is precisely the positive innovation needed for (5). Also
<u_0,u_2>=rho_0 rho_1, so this is the actual Markov law, not only its
adjacent correlations. Choosing k=8/5 makes rho_0 and rho_1 different.

All six non-optimum residuals are

    Q_01=-1,             Q_10=-1,
    Q_02=4-5k/2,         Q_20=-1,
    Q_12=-4-3k/2,        Q_21=-(1+k)/2.             (14)

For example <g_0,X_2-X_0>=2 and
||g_0-g_2||^2=1+k, giving Q_02=4-5k/2.
Also <g_2,X_0-X_2>=-7k/2, giving Q_20=-1.
The adjacent expansions use <g_0,g_1>=-1/2 and
<g_1,g_2>=-k/2, giving the remaining entries of (14).
Subtracting the same epsilon from all finite values changes none of
these six residuals. The optimum rows are exactly

    Q_(i,star)=-epsilon,  Q_(star,i)=epsilon-q_i<0. (15)

Equations (14)--(15) cover every directed pair. Section 6 constructs
one fixed smooth function realizing all these jets and proves its
unique minimizer is 0. Its true squared initial radius is 5 and its
payoff is

    V=(6k-2epsilon)/5.                             (16)

For the definite choice k=8/5, epsilon=1/8, the terminal value is
187/40 and V=187/100. The second physical displacement has e_1
component -5. Both the positive affine values and the transverse

This legal example falsifies any extension of the ALIGNED estimate
(10) to a free initial anchor: eta_0=eta_1=2, h_0=3 would give
q_1<=q_0/4 in the aligned case, but here q_1=q_0/2. The paid initial

This finite example is not a target-scale improvement. In fact every

It certifies a real escape from a scoped hypothesis, not a stronger

## 5. A bounded nonstationary rule with a paid initial pulse

Here is a complete construction for EVERY supplied word of length
n>=2 with min_i h_i>1. The function is planned for the full word before
GD begins. Choose

    1<eta<min_i h_i,  0<theta<1,

for example eta=(1+min_i h_i)/2 and theta=1/2. Set the virtual terminal
entry h_n=eta. For i=0,...,n-1 put

    t_i=theta min{1,(h_i-eta)/h_(i+1)}.

Let s=t_0, q_0=1, and set

    q_1=(h_0-1)s/eta,
    v=sqrt(q_1-s^2),
    w=(eta-1)s/v,
    g_0=e_0, g_1=-s e_0+v e_1,
    X_0=eta e_0+w e_1.                             (17)

The quantity v is strictly positive: s<(h_0-eta)/h_1<
(h_0-1)/eta. Thus all coordinates in (17) are real and w>0.
For i>=1 define

    b_i=(h_i-eta)/eta,
    rho_i=-sqrt(t_i/b_i),
    A_i=sqrt(q_i), u_i=g_i/A_i,
    u_(i+1)=rho_i u_i+sqrt(1-rho_i^2)e_(i+1),
    q_(i+1)=t_i b_i q_i, g_(i+1)=sqrt(q_(i+1))u_(i+1). (18)

The initial transition is rho_0=-s/sqrt(q_1), whose square is
s eta/(h_0-1)<1. For i>=1, t_i/b_i<=theta eta/h_(i+1)<=theta<1.
Thus all rho_i lie strictly between -1 and 0, and (4)--(5), now including terminal index n, give
the full law and literal real realization. Every parameter comes from
the supplied word and the two scalars; there is no free Gram matrix
or collection of independently adjustable modes.

Set X_i=X_0-sum_(j<i)h_j g_j. The first support is eta, and

    <X_1,g_1>=(h_0-eta)s+wv=(h_0-1)s=eta q_1.

For i>=1 all future innovations are orthogonal to X_i. Formula (18)
therefore gives <X_i,g_i>=eta q_i by induction using actual GD.

Choose one common slack epsilon with

    0<epsilon<(eta-1)min_(0<=i<=n)q_i,
    f_i=(eta-1/2)q_i-epsilon.                       (19)

For example half the right-hand minimum fixes epsilon from the full
word. Both delta_i=(eta-1)q_i-epsilon and z_i=epsilon are positive.
This slack is not set anew at each join.

We prove EVERY non-optimum pair next. Common epsilon cancels from
each such residual. For 1<=i<=j<=n put

    P_ij=product_(k=i)^(j-1)(-t_k),
    E_ij=product_(k=i)^(j-1)(t_k b_k)=q_j/q_i,
    B_ij=sum_(k=i)^(j-1)h_k P_ik+P_ij,
    P_ii=E_ii=B_ii=1.

The guards imply

    0<t_i<1,
    h_i-t_i h_(i+1)>=eta,
    h_i-t_i>=eta.                                  (20)

For i<j, pairing alternating terms in B_ij proves B_ij>=eta.
For even j-i, group physical terms as
P_(i,i+2r)[h_(i+2r)-t_(i+2r)h_(i+2r+1)], followed by the
positive P_ij. The first group alone is at least eta and all groups
are nonnegative. For odd j-i, the last term after the analogous
pairs is P_(i,j-1)[h_(j-1)-t_(j-1)]; if there are no earlier pairs
this is at least eta, and otherwise the first pair is already at
least eta. Also

    B_ij=h_i-t_i B_(i+1,j)<=h_i,                    (21)

because B_(i+1,j)>0, including B_jj=1. Thus
eta<=B_ij<=h_i for every i<j with i>=1.

Inner products from (18) give <g_i,g_j>=q_i P_ij and
<X_i,g_j>=eta<g_i,g_j> for i>=1. Expanding the ORIGINAL (1) gives

    Q_(j,i)=(eta-1)q_i(P_ij-1)<0,
    Q_(i,j)=q_i[eta-B_ij-(eta-1)E_ij]<0.            (22)

The first inequality uses the alternating sign and |P_ij|<1; the
second uses B_ij>=eta. No history is discarded in B_ij.

It remains to check every pair involving the original query 0.
Let y_0=X_0-g_0. Equation (17) gives <y_0,g_1>=0. Every later
g_j is a scalar multiple of g_1 plus directions e_2,...,e_j, so
<y_0,g_j>=0 for ALL j>=1. The exact original reverse rows are

    Q_(j,0)=-(eta-1), j>=1.                         (23)

For the other orientation, <g_0,g_j>=-s P_(1,j) and direct GD
substitution gives

    Q_(0,j)=eta-h_0+s B_(1,j)-(eta-1)q_j, j>=1.    (24)

For j=1 use B_11=1<=h_1. For j>1 use (21).
Since s h_1<=theta(h_0-eta)<h_0-eta, (24) is strictly negative.
The optimum rows are

    Q_(i,star)=-epsilon,
    Q_(star,i)=epsilon-(eta-1)q_i<0.                (25)

Equations (22)--(25) include the terminal and every chronological
cross pair, in both directions. In particular the argument does not
claim that local tests or a PSD correlation law suffice.

## 6. One static function, exact radius, payoff and continuation

For either construction in Sections 4--5 define, using ALL its jets,

    H(v)=||v||^2/2+max{0,max_(0<=i<=n)
                                 (<X_i-g_i,v>-delta_i)},
    F(X)=sup_v(<X,v>-H(v)).                         (26)

This is exactly the full-data construction in accepted
`1252b8823bb5a290`: expanding its ith quadratic gives
||v||^2/2+<X_i-g_i,v>-delta_i. The proved all-pair rows therefore
give one everywhere finite convex differentiable 1-smooth F with
F(X_i)=f_i, grad F(X_i)=g_i and F(0)=0=min F. It is fixed before GD.

Because all delta_i>0, the maximum in (26) is zero in a neighborhood
of v=0. If X!=0, choose 0<a<1 sufficiently small that aX belongs
to that neighborhood. Then

    F(X)>=(a-a^2/2)||X||^2>0.

Thus argmin F={0}. Section 4 has exact squared radius 5. Section 5
has exact squared radius eta^2+w^2, and its exact payoff is

    q_n=[(h_0-1)s/eta] product_(i=1)^(n-1)(t_i b_i),
    V=[(2eta-1)q_n-2epsilon]/(eta^2+w^2).           (27)

In Section 5 the second GD displacement has nonzero e_1 component
-h_1 v. Subsequent queried innovations also have nonzero coefficients
in the actual displacement. Hence these are paid transverse changes,
including their full cost in the radius, the supports and the values.

The state transfer for every subsequent transition i>=1 is

    q_(i+1)=t_i b_i q_i,
    f_(i+1)+epsilon=(t_i b_i)(f_i+epsilon),
    delta_(i+1)+epsilon=(t_i b_i)(delta_i+epsilon),
    X_(i+1)=X_i-h_i g_i,                            (28)

with u_(i+1) propagated by (18) in the SAME growing coordinate system.
For a literal append to already fixed jets through index k>=2, retain
eta, epsilon, w, all old jets and all old transitions. A sufficient
boundary condition for the first new physical step h_k is

    h_k>eta,  t_(k-1)h_k<=h_(k-1)-eta.              (29)

Choose later transitions by (18), and require
epsilon<(eta-1)q_i at every new sample. These conditions preserve
all old guards, add the missing boundary guard, and keep every affine
reserve positive. The SAME proofs (21)--(25) then cover all cross
pairs of the extended history. This is an existence statement for
one interpolant of the jointly planned complete word. It does not
change an already running objective at a join, or assert that a
shorter instance's particular F already has the desired extension.

Replacing a shorter word's virtual terminal step by a physical step
does not come free: (29) and the retained epsilon reserve can fail.
The pulse w is part of X_0, not energy added during the run.

For arbitrary L>0 multiply F by L and use steps h_i/L. Both the
trajectory and normalized W=2R payoff formulas remain the same.

## 7. Entire sparse-anchor class: exact weighted necessary bound

Consider ANY legal nonzero queried trajectory with (3) and

    1<=eta_i<h_i for every queried 0<=i<n.          (30)

Allow arbitrary initial anchors, arbitrary dimensions, all values
permitted by (2), and a completely FREE terminal gradient and value
generated by the same function. No construction guard from Section 5
is imposed. In the canonical innovations define

    I={i in {0,...,n-2}: |rho_i|<1 and w_(i+1)!=0},
    E_anchor=sum_(i in I) log_+(1/(h_i-1)),
    K_anchor=cardinality(I), log_+(x)=max{0,log x}.         (31)

These are the actual support and initial coefficients relative to the
included minimizer 0. Additional components X_perp are allowed.
No claim that 0 is closest is needed, by (8).

Outside I, (9) applies. Inside I, (11) applies. Multiplying the exact
per-step upper bounds gives

    q_(n-1)/q_0 <= [product_(i=0)^(n-2)(h_i-1)]^2
                                             exp(2 E_anchor). (32)

If EVERY adjacent queried inner product is nonpositive, use (12)
inside I instead; the stronger version is

    q_(n-1)/q_0 <= [product_(i=0)^(n-2)(h_i-1)]^2
                                                    exp(E_anchor).  (33)

In (32), nonzero anchor coefficients may cause positive transitions.
Thus (32) does not silently retain an all-negative sign restriction.
The weights in E_anchor show that correlations after steps h_i>=2 receive
no improvement factor at all in this estimate.

For a final physical step ell=h_(n-1) in (1,2), integrating the
1-Lipschitz gradient along that step gives

    2 f_n<=2 f_(n-1)-ell(2-ell)q_(n-1).

The actual optimum upper row and (30) imply
f_(n-1)<=(eta_(n-1)-1/2)q_(n-1), so

    2 f_n < [(ell-1)^2+2(ell-1)]q_(n-1).           (34)

This step leaves the terminal gradient entirely unrestricted. Combined
with (8), equations (32)--(34) are true-distance bounds, even for
functions with a nonunique minimizer and arbitrary optimum slack.

## 8. Uniform comparison on the ORIGINAL enclosing-m words

For m>=4 fix throughout the entire word

    N=2^m, n=N-1, sigma=1+sqrt(2), beta=1-1/m,
    c=sigma(1+1/m), a=beta/c,
    H_0=(), H_(j+1)=(H_j,1+a c^j,H_j), 0<=j<m.

Set h=H_m with these SAME enclosing-m parameters in every child.
The smallest and last step is ell=1+a, and

    1/5<a<1/2.

Indeed a=(m-1)/[(m+1)sigma]>=3/(5sigma)>1/5, since sigma<3,
and a<1/sigma<1/2, since sigma>2.

Let D be the product of ALL physical offsets h_i-1. The recursion
D_(j+1)=D_j^2 a c^j with D_0=1 proves by induction

    D=a^(N-1)c^(N-m-1)=beta^n c^(-m).              (35)

The product in (32) is D/a, since only the last physical step is
omitted. Use gamma=2 for the general-sign estimate (32), or gamma=1
under the all-nonpositive adjacent hypothesis of (33). Equations
(8), (32)--(35) prove the whole-class upper bound

    V <= (1+2/a) beta^(2n)c^(-2m) exp(gamma E_anchor).     (36)

This includes arbitrary horizon-dependent values, support ratios,
correlations, pulse positions and pulse magnitudes satisfying the
specified class hypotheses. The aligned class has E_anchor=0, so (36)
already closes arbitrary-slack repair and is stronger than the
previous tight-row estimate. Section 5 has only one possible queried
future coefficient, w_1, so it is covered as well.

For the ORIGINAL all-split benchmark, accepted `b98bb807d514e80d`
gives B_n<=1+4 n^(log_2 3)<=1+4*3^m. Therefore

    B_n V <= (1+2/a)(1+4*3^m)c^(-2m)
                             exp(-2n/m+gamma E_anchor).   (37)

We used log(1-1/m)<=-1/m, not an asymptotic replacement of the word
or benchmark. If E_anchor=o(n/m), (37) tends to zero. More quantitatively,
E_anchor<=2n/(gamma m) already forces a zero limit, because c>=sigma and
sigma^2=3+2sqrt(2)>3, while 1+2/a<11.

The exact weights identify where an exception could help. Offset
level 0 is a, level 1 is beta<1, and every level >=2 has offset at
least beta c=sigma(1-1/m^2)>1. Consequently, if K_0 and K_1 count
indices in I following physical levels 0 and 1, then

    E_anchor=K_0 log(1/a)+K_1 log(1/beta)
       <=K_anchor log(1/a).                              (38)

Correlations at higher physical levels contribute zero to this bound.

Suppose, along this sequence of ORIGINAL words, a family from Section
7 has liminf B_n V>0. Take a constant v_0>0 with B_n V>=v_0 for all
sufficiently large m. From the sharper (36) one MUST have

    gamma E_anchor >= 2n log(1/beta)+2m log c
                  -log B_n-log(1+2/a)+log v_0.     (39)

Here m log(1/beta)->1, log(1/a)->log sigma, and all terms of order m
are negligible relative to n/m, since m^2/(2^m-1)->0. Equations
(38)--(39) therefore imply the precise necessary count condition

    liminf_(m->infinity) K_anchor m/n >= 2/(gamma log sigma). (40)

In particular K_anchor=o(n/m), including any fixed number of arbitrarily
located anchor pulses, is excluded. The stronger constant 2/log sigma
applies when all adjacent correlations are nonpositive; the constant
1/log sigma applies when the exceptional transitions have free signs.

Neither (36) nor (40) asserts sufficiency. They do not bound the
unrestricted W_n(H_m), and they do not exclude a finite-state rule
which prepares many nonzero anchor coordinates from the full word.

## 9. Remaining interface, failed extensions and literature audit

The slack-only escape is closed in the complete class (30) with a
radial anchor; it does not require tight optimum rows or a floor on
the positive reserve. Retuning eta_i inside [1,h_i) cannot evade it.
The initial-pulse construction proves that a future-correlated anchor
is a real legal freedom. The finite family (13)--(16) disproves an
unqualified extension of the aligned contraction to that freedom.
The weighted bound then closes sparse-pulse repair on the original
words, even allowing arbitrary pulse locations, values and terminal.

What remains is a bounded rule preparing sufficiently many effective
future correlations, or support ratios crossing the physical steps,
with every chronological interpolation row and the true radius paid.
The identity (7) alone is not such a rule and is not presented as one.
It prices any proposed rule. Likewise (39) is only necessary; enough
nonzero entries does not imply that their amplitudes, cross rows or
actual payoff work. No arbitrary-h matching lower bound follows.

for their full domains: consecutive first-occurrence ruler blocks,
