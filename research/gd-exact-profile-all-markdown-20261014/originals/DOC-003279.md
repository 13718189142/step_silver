# Whole-field nonregularity for every positive nonuniform point metric

Status: JOINT UNVERIFIED appendix to max2 round61. No fact promotion,
formal gate or computation. This gives an all-weight argument in addition
to the explicit two-step metric demonstration in87225e1b68c94b70.
It is not used as a separately established premise.

Let p_wm be a prime, and put N=p_wm^2+p_wm+1. Points of the projective
plane over Z/p_wm Z are nonzero triples modulo nonzero scalars, and
lines are nonzero forms modulo scalars, with incidence by annihilation.
There are N points and N lines because each nonzero class has p_wm-1
representatives. A nonzero form has a two-dimensional kernel, giving
p_wm+1 points per line. Distinct line forms have a one-dimensional
common kernel, hence one intersection point. Dually each point is on
p_wm+1 lines and two distinct points share one line. All counts follow
by eliminating one or two coordinates with nonzero coefficients.

Let B be the real point-by-line incidence matrix. With I the identity,
J_ff the all-ones matrix and 1 the all-ones column, the point-side
counts give

    B B^T=p_wm I+J_ff, B1=(p_wm+1)1.                  (A1)

B is invertible since for nonzero z the quadratic form of B B^T is
p_wm||z||^2+(sum z)^2>0.

Give EVERY point P an arbitrary STRICTLY POSITIVE real weight omega_P.
Let W=diag(omega_P), Omega_w=sum_P omega_P, omega_max=max_P omega_P,
s_P=sqrt(omega_P), and P_w=I-s*s^T/Omega_w. Define every line column

    A_L=P_w W^(1/2)1_L/sqrt(p_wm*omega_max),
    A=(A_L)_L, G_wm=A^T A.                           (A2)

Thus the exact weighted Euclidean Gram has entries

    (G_wm)_LM=[sum_(P in L intersection M)omega_P
          -(sum_(P in L)omega_P)(sum_(P in M)omega_P)/Omega_w]
                                               /(p_wm*omega_max).

Using(A1), P_w*s=0 and W^(1/2)J_ff W^(1/2)=s*s^T gives the complete
field frame identity

    A A^T=P_w W P_w/omega_max.                       (A3)

Also A1=0. Conversely A c=0 implies Bc is constant, since
P_w W^(1/2)Bc=0 and the kernel of P_w is span(s). Invertibility of
B and B1=(p_wm+1)1 imply c is constant. Therefore

    ker A=span(1), range A=s perpendicular.           (A4)

Claim: the WHOLE-FIELD Gram G_wm is the Gram of a regular simplex
if and only if all omega_P are equal. Here regular simplex means

    G_wm=kappa_reg*(I-11^T/N) for some kappa_reg>0.   (A5)

This definition is equivalent to constant diagonal/off-diagonal entries
for the rank N-1 Gram with null column1: the row-sum condition forces
diagonal+(N-1)*off-diagonal=0, giving exactly(A5).

First suppose(A5). By A1=0,
A A^T A=A G_wm=kappa_reg A. By(A4), A A^T is multiplication by
kappa_reg on s perpendicular and zero on span(s); consequently
A A^T=kappa_reg P_w. Equation(A3) yields

    P_w(W-c_reg I)P_w=0, c_reg=kappa_reg*omega_max>0. (A6)

For any two distinct points i,j let
z_ij=e_i/s_i-e_j/s_j, where e_i,e_j are their coordinate unit vectors.
Then z_ij is perpendicular to s. Its quadratic form in(A6) is

    (omega_i-c_reg)/omega_i
                   +(omega_j-c_reg)/omega_j=0.      (A7)

Put zeta_i^wm=(omega_i-c_reg)/omega_i. Every distinct pair satisfies
zeta_i^wm+zeta_j^wm=0. Choose any three distinct indices, possible since N>=7.
The first two pair equalities give zeta_j^wm=zeta_k^wm=-zeta_i^wm; the third gives
-2zeta_i^wm=0. Thus zeta_i^wm=zeta_j^wm=zeta_k^wm=0. Pairing any other index with i gives
that index's zeta-value0 as well. Hence omega_i=c_reg for EVERY point.

Conversely if all omega_P equal c>0, then omega_max=c,
P_w=I-11^T/N and A=P_w B/sqrt(p_wm). The line-side counts give
B^T B=p_wm I+J_ff, and B^T1=(p_wm+1)1. Therefore

    G_wm=(1/p_wm)[B^T B-((p_wm+1)^2/N)11^T]
         =I-11^T/N,                                 (A8)

since N=p_wm^2+p_wm+1 makes 1-(p_wm+1)^2/N=-p_wm/N. This proves
the converse and identifies the normalization without a spectral
or finite-field-to-real shortcut.

Apply this argument to the round61 weights

    omega_P=1+1_(P=P0)
                +sum_(r<floor(k/2))b_r 1_(P in L_r)/(1+S),
    S=sum_(r<k)b_r, b_r>0, k>=2.

The sum over the first half is less than S, so every point other than
P0 has weight<2 whereas P0 has weight>=2. They are nonuniform.
Thus the complete real field Gram is NOT a regular simplex for EVERY
word to which the paired law applies, not only the written p_wm=2
calibration. This strengthens the geometry check, but changes none
of the three coefficient laws or their already joint-derived failures.
It gives no all-word lower witness, full-class upper bound or accepted
new fact. There is no extra mathematical program or formal submission.
