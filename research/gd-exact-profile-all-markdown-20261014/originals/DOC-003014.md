## 4. Eliminate every terminal clipping branch and alpha

    x4/t <= alpha_B*(z-1)
          <=[(v-1)/(w+1)]*(z-1)

upper terminal cap or its boundary. For completeness, without this box

    V=E0(t)-(x4+s)^2-(t-1)R alpha^2+(t-1)s,

but its domain x4>=t has empty intersection with (3) under (1).

The lower/core terminal boundary is the positive number

    alpha_A(t)=B/(R+t),
    0<alpha_A<alpha_B,                               (14)

because R=w+z>w. On 0<=alpha<=alpha_A, x4<=-t alpha and
g4=-alpha. Formula (11) becomes

    V=E0(t)-(B+H alpha)^2+(t-1)H alpha^2
      =E0(t)-B^2-2BH alpha-H(H+1-t)alpha^2.          (15)

Since H+1-t=u+1+B>0 and B,H>0, (15) strictly decreases with alpha.
Its minimum on this whole branch is at alpha_A. At alpha=0 it is

    V=H*(u+1+t-v)>0,

because u+1+t-v>=u+2-v>1/2.

On alpha_A<=alpha<=alpha_B, the terminal gradient is x4/t and

    V=E0(t)-(t-1)R alpha^2-q(t)(R alpha-B)^2.         (16)

This is a concave quadratic in alpha, since q(t)>0 and R>0.
For any point of the interval the defining concavity inequality bounds
its value below by the linear interpolation of its endpoint values;
therefore its minimum is at one of the two endpoints. Formulas (15)
and (16) agree at alpha_A because g4=-alpha_A there. They also remain
valid at alpha_B, where x3=-t alpha_B is exactly the boundary between

## 5. Eliminate curvature at the full-tail endpoint

    q(t)alpha(t)^2(z-t)^2

    V_B(t)>=V_B(1)*(d-r)^2/d^2
       +r*[2+u-v+r+P^2*(2/d-r/d^2)-R alpha(t)^2].   (21)

    2+u-v+r>1/2,

    R alpha(t)^2<(9/2)*(3/4)^2=81/32.

P>2,d<4/5. The third uses R=w+z<9/2 and
alpha(t)<=alpha(1)<3/4. Hence the bracket exceeds 95/32. In particular,

    V_B(t)>=V_B(1)*((z-t)/(z-1))^2

that every t in this branch has nonnegative energy, strictly positive

### 6b. Terminal point on the negative ray: z<=t<v
