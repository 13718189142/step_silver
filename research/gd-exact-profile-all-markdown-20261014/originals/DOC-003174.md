## 1. Exact schedule and all quadratic curvatures, including arbitrary b

Fix an integer m>=2. Within this document write

    lambda=1+sqrt(2), c=lambda(1+1/m), a=(1-1/m)/c,
    d_j=1+a c^j, H_0=(), H_(j+1)=(H_j,d_j,H_j),
    n=2^m-1, S_j=sum H_j, M_j=1+S_j.

The parameters c,a remain fixed for this entire horizon. They are not
recomputed when j changes. The symbol H_j here names a schedule, not the
conjugate jet quadratic from the project glossary. Put r=c-2 and A_mass=a/r.
Counting the occurrences of each level gives, for 0<=j<=m,

    S_j=2^j-1+a(c^j-2^j)/(c-2),
    M_j=A_mass c^j+(1-A_mass)2^j.                       (1)

There are 2^(m-j-1) occurrences of d_j in H_m. Its scalar multiplier at
curvature t in [0,1] is exactly

    P_m(t)=product_(j=0)^(m-1)(1-d_j t)^(2^(m-j-1)).     (2)

Let p_m be H_m without its final d_0 and let h^(m,b)=(p_m,b), b>=0. Then

    sum h^(m,b)=S_m-d_0+b,
    R_(m,b)(t)=(1-bt) product_(j=0)^(m-1)(1-d_j t)^N_j,
    N_0=2^(m-1)-1, N_j=2^(m-j-1) for 1<=j<m.           (3)

The exact worst normalized quadratic objective over ALL finite dimensions
is

    Q_m(b)=max_(0<=t<=1) t R_(m,b)(t)^2.                (4)

Indeed diagonalizing a positive semidefinite Hessian bounded by the identity
makes the normalized error a weighted average of the displayed scalar
quantities. A scalar quadratic attains the maximum. Zero eigenvalues have
zero objective contribution; affine translations to a minimizer change
neither calculation. Thus this is a full quadratic-class computation.

For an exact finite characterization of (4), subdivide [0,1] at all its
distinct interior roots 1/d_j and, when b>0, 1/b. On each root-free open
interval the logarithmic derivative of the nonzero objective is

    L(t)=1/t-2b/(1-bt)-2sum_j N_j d_j/(1-d_j t),
    L'(t)=-1/t^2-2b^2/(1-bt)^2
                    -2sum_j N_j d_j^2/(1-d_j t)^2<0.   (5)

Consequently there is exactly one interior maximizer between consecutive
roots, also between zero and the first root. On the last interval the
maximum is either its unique root of L or t=1. Values at roots are zero.
Coincident roots are combined, and b=0 simply removes its term. Equations
(3)--(5) specify the complete spectrum without a numerical sweep.

The unit-curvature values alone are

    P_m(1)=-(ac)^(2^m-1)c^(-m),
    R_(m,b)(1)=(1-b)(ac)^(2^m-2)c^(1-m).                (6)

They are not used as a replacement for (4).

## 2. Exact single-cut screen for every appended b

For a cut with step d, preceding mass P and following mass T, the accepted
cut value is (d-1)_+^2/[(1+P)^2(1+2T)]. For a fixed d and total schedule
mass S, its reciprocal denominator is

    D(P)=(1+P)^2[1+2(S-d-P)].

Its derivative has the sign of 2(S-d)-3P. Therefore its minimum on any
closed interval is attained at an endpoint. Among all occurrences of a
given level it suffices to check the first and last occurrence.

For h^(m,b), all levels 1<=j<m remain present. Their first and last
preceding masses are, respectively,

    P_j^first=S_j, P_j^last=S_m-d_j-S_j.                (7)

Their following masses are S_m-d_0+b-d_j-P. At level zero, after deleting
the original last occurrence, the endpoint preceding masses are

    P_0^first=0, P_0^last=S_m-2d_0-d_1,                 (8)

with following masses S_m-2d_0+b and d_1+b. For m=2 the two occurrences
in (8) refer to the same remaining index. The appended b contributes

    (b-1)_+^2/(1+S_m-d_0)^2.                           (9)

Taking the maximum of the cuts in (7)--(9) and
1/[1+2(S_m-d_0+b)] is EXACTLY K_n(h^(m,b)). No omitted internal cut can
be larger, by the denominator argument. In particular the accepted final
cut already proves W_n(h^(m,b))>=1/B_n whenever

    b>=1+(1+S_m-d_0)/sqrt(B_n).                        (10)

The global Huber term proves the same comparison whenever
b<=(B_n-1)/2-(S_m-d_0), if that right side is nonnegative. These tests use
the actual B_n, not a guessed dyadic constant.

## 6. Retained two-block geometry and all constant tails in its cap range

Here is the precise mechanism tested next. For ANY two selected cuts i<j
of an arbitrary schedule, let P be the mass before i, S the mass strictly
between i and j, T the mass after j, and define

    M_0=1+P, M_1=1+S,
    A=(h_i-1)/M_0, epsilon=(h_j-1)/M_1, sigma=2T+1.

Assume h_i,h_j>1 and choose the cap-range angle

    0<kappa<=A/(1+epsilon).                            (25)

Use the retained prefix gradients and proximal point

    p=(1,0)/M_0,
    q=(-kappa,sqrt(kappa(A-kappa)))/M_1,
    X=(-A,0), y=X-M_1 q, d=-M_1 q.                    (26)

These prefix data are actual jets of the single Moreau envelope of
J(z)=max(0,<p,z>,<q,z>), starting at (1,0). Before the first checkpoint
the proximal points are (1-(u+1)/M_0,0), 0<=u<=P, so the p-plane is
active. That checkpoint, of size1+A M_0, sends its input p to X. In
the second block, the proposed proximal points X-tq, 1<=t<=M_1, have

    <q,X-tq>=(A kappa/M_1)(1-t/M_1)>=0,
    <q-p,X-tq>
      =A kappa/M_1+A/M_0
          -t[A kappa/M_1^2+kappa/(M_0 M_1)]
      >=(A-kappa)/M_0>0.

This verifies every actual old query, even if individual ordinary steps
are large. At the second checkpoint its jet is

    x_j=y+q, g_j=q, f_j=||q||^2/2.

For completeness, the envelope minimizes J(z)+||x-z||^2/2. This has a
unique minimizer, and an active-plane slope g at z proves optimality at
x=z+g. At two proximal points, monotonicity of the subgradients gives
||g-g'||^2<=<g-g',x-x'>, hence a 1-Lipschitz gradient. The two minimizing
inequalities identify the derivative as g; joint convexity proves
convexity of the envelope. Thus the claimed function has the required
global regularity, not only valid formal data.

The second checkpoint, of size1+M_1 epsilon, sends the iterate to
X_tail=y+epsilon d. The optimum jet is (0,0,0). Every interpolant of
this prefix has origin as an actual closest minimizer to (1,0): the
retained first checkpoint jet (p,p,||p||^2/2), interpolated with any
minimizer z, forces <p,z><=0. Its squared distance from (1,0) is
therefore at least1, attained at origin. This normalization persists
for all extensions below.

Allow ANY legal continuation with a single common gradient g at every
tail query, including the terminal sample; allow affine intercepts,
positive values and extra dimensions, and retain every prefix jet.
Its endpoint is y+epsilon d-Tg. The accepted interpolation inequality
from this endpoint to the old jet (y+q,q,||q||^2/2) gives

    2f_end<=2epsilon<d,g>-sigma||g||^2
                         <=epsilon^2||d||^2/sigma
                         =A kappa epsilon^2/sigma.

This uses a retained old query, not a reset to a new problem. From (25),
EVERY such constant-tail continuation obeys the exact universal cap

    2f_end<=U_ij:=A^2 epsilon^2/[(1+epsilon)(1+2T)].     (27)

In particular it covers all angles and all optimized tail planes of the
previous explicit two-checkpoint cap policy. It does not cover angles
kappa>A/(1+epsilon), different two-block prefixes, or variable-gradient
tails. No claim that (27) is attained for every pair is needed below.

## 11. A quantitative requirement for a genuinely varying tail

Keep ANY angle 0<=kappa<=A in the exact retained prefix (26), but now
permit a varying tail. Denote its physical steps by t_l>=0, its gradients
at those queries by g_l, its terminal gradient by g_end, and put

    T=sum_l t_l, sigma=2T+1,
    Delta_tail=sum_l t_l(g_end-g_l).

This notation changes neither the prescribed steps nor the retained jets.
Its terminal point is y+epsilon d-sum_l t_l g_l. The outgoing
interpolation inequality to the retained jet (y+q,q,||q||^2/2) is

    2f_end<=2epsilon<d,g_end>-sigma||g_end||^2
                                  +2<g_end,Delta_tail>.

Completing the square gives the more useful exact bound

    2f_end<=A kappa epsilon^2/sigma
       +2<g_end,Delta_tail>
       -sigma||g_end-epsilon d/sigma||^2.             (54)

For the H_m geometry, the first term is bounded by gamma K, where
gamma=5949/6088 and K=1/(2S_m+1), by the complete calculation (45)--(51).
Therefore any such witness reaching 2f_end>=1/B_n must satisfy

    2<g_end,Delta_tail>
       >=1/B_n-gamma K
                   +sigma||g_end-epsilon d/sigma||^2. (55)

The true closest-minimizer normalization is still1 by the first retained
checkpoint jet. In particular, (24) implies the quantitative necessary
condition

    liminf B_n * 2<g_end,Delta_tail> >=1-2gamma/e>0     (56)

for any unbounded sequence of successful witnesses of this form.
This requirement keeps every parameter loss. Merely introducing new
gradient directions is insufficient: if
<g_end,g_l>>=||g_end||^2 at every positive tail step, the left side
of (55) is nonpositive. It includes all collinear contracting tails
g_l=c_l g_end with c_l>=1, and all changes g_l=g_end+w_l with
<g_end,w_l>=0. Neither class can escape the obstruction.

When T>0 and g_end!=0, Cauchy--Schwarz additionally gives

    sum_l t_l||g_l-g_end||^2
      >= [ (1/B_n-gamma K)_+ ]^2/[4T||g_end||^2]        (57)

for any successful witness. Equation (55) is stronger because it
retains the sign of the required terminal correlation and the square
loss. No sufficient construction with the required positive correlation
has been established. This is a necessary quantitative interface, not a
new lower bound on W_n and not an assertion of feasibility.
