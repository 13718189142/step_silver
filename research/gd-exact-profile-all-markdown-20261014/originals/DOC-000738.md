# An explicit ordering defeating quadratics plus all single-cut Huber witnesses

Status: complete candidate derivation, unverified. The initial-wave verification
allowance is already used by fact b98bb807d514e80d. This strengthening is for
main-agent prioritization. Worker: max3.

## Statement and scope

Let K_n(h) be the verified single-cut functional in fact b98bb807d514e80d.
For a schedule h define its exact quadratic-class worst case

\[
Q_n(h)=\max_{0\leq\lambda\leq1}
\lambda\prod_{t=0}^{n-1}(1-h_t\lambda)^2,
\qquad J_n(h)=\max\{K_n(h),Q_n(h)\}.
\]

Here Q_n is exactly the normalized worst case over convex quadratics in any
finite dimension with gradient Lipschitz constant at most L, after scaling
L=1. Indeed diagonalizing their symmetric Hessian makes the normalized error
a weighted average of the displayed scalar expression, and each scalar value
is attained by a one-dimensional quadratic. Thus J_n(h)<=W_n(h).

For every integer n>=3 put theta=pi/(2n+1) and

\[
a_k=\frac1{\sin^2(k\theta)}\quad(1\leq k\leq n).
\]

Define h^(n) by listing a_k with even indices k in decreasing index order,
followed by a_k with odd indices k in increasing index order. For example the
index order at n=7 is (6,4,2,1,3,5,7). This is a fully explicit positive,
finite, predetermined schedule. Then

\[
Q_n(h^{(n)})=\frac1{(2n+1)^2},\qquad
K_n(h^{(n)})\leq72\sin^2\theta
\leq\frac{72\pi^2}{(2n+1)^2}.
\]

Using B_n=1/U_n^ZJ<=1+4n^p with p=log_2 3<2 from verified fact
b98bb807d514e80d, it follows that

\[
\frac{J_n(h^{(n)})}{U_n^{ZJ}}
\leq\frac{72\pi^2(1+4n^p)}{(2n+1)^2}\longrightarrow0.
\]

This rules out proving the target by taking only the maximum of all quadratic
witnesses, the global Huber witness, and the single-cut witnesses in K_n.
It does not give any upper bound on W_n(h^(n)); a nonlinear history-dependent
witness could yield a much larger error.

## Exact quadratic calculation

Define polynomials T_0(x)=1, T_1(x)=x and
T_{r+1}(x)=2xT_r(x)-T_{r-1}(x) for integers r>=1. The cosine addition formula
and induction give T_r(cos u)=cos(ru). The recurrence also shows that T_r
has degree r and parity r. Write M=2n+1. Then T_M is odd and
T_M'(0)=M sin(M pi/2)=(-1)^n M, by differentiating the cosine identity
on -1<x<1. Therefore

\[
R_n(\lambda)=\frac{(-1)^nT_M(\sqrt\lambda)}{M\sqrt\lambda}
\]

extends to a polynomial of degree n with R_n(0)=1. For each k=1,...,n,
T_M(sin(k theta))=cos(M pi/2-k pi)=0. These give n distinct positive
roots of R_n, hence

\[
R_n(\lambda)=\prod_{k=1}^n\left(1-\frac\lambda{\sin^2(k\theta)}\right).
\]

For lambda in [0,1], |T_M(sqrt(lambda))|<=1. It follows that
lambda R_n(lambda)^2<=1/M^2. At lambda=1, T_M(1)=1 and equality holds.
Permutation of the step factors leaves the product unchanged, proving Q_n.

## Bounds on neighboring step sizes in the stated ordering

All a_k are at least1 and decrease with k. The function sin u/u decreases
on 0<u<=pi/2, since its derivative has numerator u cos u-sin u and the
derivative of that numerator is -u sin u<=0. Hence whenever k+2<=n,

\[
\frac{a_k}{a_{k+2}}
=\frac{\sin^2((k+2)\theta)}{\sin^2(k\theta)}
\leq\left(\frac{k+2}{k}\right)^2.
\tag{1}
\]

The first even-indexed step and the last odd-indexed step have index at
least n-1. Since n>=3 implies (n-1)/(2n+1)>=1/4, both are at most2.
Also a_2>=a_1/4 and a_3>=a_1/9, by the same decreasing-sinc argument
(or the double-angle identity for a_2).

## Every single-cut term remains small

For a selected position j, write b=h_j, P=P_j, T=T_j as in the verified
definition of K_n. Replace (b-1)_+ by b when an upper estimate is needed.

1. A step before the central largest step a_1 has even index. If it is the
first step, b/(P+1)<=2. Otherwise its preceding step is a_{k+2}; by (1),
b<=4a_{k+2}<=4P. Thus b/(P+1)<=4 in either case. Its suffix contains a_1,
so its cut term is at most16/(1+2a_1)<=8/a_1.

2. At a_1 the prefix contains a_2>=a_1/4 and the suffix contains
a_3>=a_1/9. Therefore its cut term is at most

\[
\frac{a_1^2}{(a_1/4)^2(2a_1/9)}=\frac{72}{a_1}.
\]

3. After a_1, the prefix always has P>=a_1. For a step a_k that is not
the last step, k is odd and k>=3, and its suffix contains a_{k+2}.
By (1), a_{k+2}>=a_k/4. Its cut term is at most

\[
\frac{a_k^2}{a_1^2(2a_k/4)}
=\frac{2a_k}{a_1^2}\leq\frac2{a_1}.
\]

At the last step b<=2, hence (b-1)^2<=1 and the cut term is at most
1/a_1^2<=1/a_1.

Finally the global Huber term is at most1/(1+2a_1)<=1/(2a_1).
The maximum of these bounds is72/a_1=72 sin^2 theta. Since sin(theta)<=theta,
this proves the claimed estimate, entirely analytically for every n>=3.

## Source grounding

The standard trigonometric definition of T_r is documented at NIST DLMF
18.5.1, https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.5. The polynomial identities used here are
derived above from their recurrence, so no external theorem is used as a
black-box proof step. Matlas was queried for the weighted Chebyshev product
identity and did not return a useful exact hit.

## A legal two-curvature witness exposes the missing interaction

The schedules above are severely unstable on an explicit globally legal
one-dimensional function. This calculation is part of the same unverified
candidate package and does not claim that every schedule is unstable.

For n>=3 keep M=2n+1 and theta=pi/M and put
mu=sin^2(5 theta/2). Define

\[
f_n(x)=\begin{cases}
\mu x^2/2,&x\geq0,\\
x^2/2,&x\leq0.
\end{cases}
\]

The derivative equals mu x on the nonnegative side and x on the nonpositive
side. It is continuous, nondecreasing, and 1-Lipschitz: within each half-line
this holds by its slope, while for x<=0<=y its increment mu y-x is between0
and y-x. Hence f_n is convex, differentiable, 1-smooth, and minimized at0.
Start x_0=1.

The chosen angle gives sin^2(2theta)<mu<sin^2(3theta). Along the even-index
prefix, every index k>=4 has 1-a_k mu>0, so the iterates stay positive.
At the last even step a_2, the factor is negative, so the next point is
negative. The following step a_1 therefore uses curvature1; because a_1>1,
it changes the sign back to positive. Every remaining odd-index step has
k>=3 and 1-a_k mu>0, so all later iterates stay positive. No factor is zero.

Relative to the ordinary quadratic product R_n(mu), exactly one factor,
at a_1, is replaced. Consequently

\[
x_n=\frac{1-a_1}{1-a_1\mu}R_n(\mu).
\]

Since arcsin(sqrt(mu))=5theta/2, the trigonometric identity proved above gives
R_n(mu)=sin(5pi/2)/(M sqrt(mu))=1/(M sqrt(mu)). Thus the exact normalized
error on this legal instance is

\[
2 f_n(x_n)=\mu x_n^2
=\frac{\cos^4\theta}
{M^2[\sin^2(5\theta/2)-\sin^2\theta]^2}.
\tag{3}
\]

For every n>=3, theta<=pi/7<pi/4, so cos^4(theta)>=1/4, and the square
bracket in (3) is positive and at most(25/4)theta^2. Therefore

\[
W_n(h^{(n)})\geq\frac{4(2n+1)^2}{625\pi^4}.
\]

More precisely the displayed instance's error divided by M^2 tends to
16/(441 pi^4), since sin u/u tends to1 and cos u tends to1. This is a direct
analytic GD trajectory, not an interpolation relaxation or numerical test.

The mechanism is the ordered pair a_2,a_1: the first crosses into the unit
curvature side, the second amplifies there and returns to the low-curvature
side. A next all-schedule candidate can therefore retain a fixed pair of
one-sided curvatures and its chronological sign transition rule, rather than
only the terminal quadratic polynomial or one selected crossing.
