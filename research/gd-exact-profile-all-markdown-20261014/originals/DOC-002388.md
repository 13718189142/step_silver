## 3. Arc, exact vertical extremum, and every supporting normal

For this report only, abbreviate theta_sp by theta_*, c_sp by c,
R_sp(theta) by R(theta), and the vectors e_sp,t_sp,n_sp by e,t,n.
These are scoped aliases, not changes to the project glossary. Set

    theta_*=arctan(1/epsilon),
    c=sqrt(1+epsilon^2) exp(kappa theta_*),
    e(theta)=(cos theta,-sin theta),
    t(theta)=(-sin theta,-cos theta),
    v(theta)=R(theta)e(theta), R(theta)=c exp(-kappa theta),
    n(theta)=e(theta)+kappa t(theta),
    K=conv({0} union {v(theta):0<=theta<=theta_*}).

Thus v(0)=(c,0), v(theta_*)=(epsilon,-1), and K is compact and convex.
Writing v=(p,-q), differentiation gives

    p'=-R(kappa cos theta+sin theta)<0,
    q'=R(cos theta-kappa sin theta).

Consequently epsilon<=p<=c, q>=0, and q/p=tan theta<=1/epsilon.
If kappa<=epsilon then theta_*<=arctan(1/kappa), so q attains its
maximum1 at theta_*. Otherwise its sole interior maximum is at
theta_c=arctan(1/kappa), and evaluation gives exactly

    qmax_sp=sqrt((1+epsilon^2)/(1+kappa^2))
              exp(kappa[theta_*-arctan(1/kappa)]).

At kappa=epsilon this expression equals1, so the piecewise definition
matches at equality. In the overshoot case define g(epsilon)=log qmax_sp
with kappa fixed. Then g(kappa)=0 and
g'(epsilon)=(epsilon-kappa)/(1+epsilon^2). Integration gives

    log qmax_sp=integral_epsilon^kappa (kappa-t)/(1+t^2) dt.

It is strictly positive, bounded below by
(kappa-epsilon)^2/[2(1+kappa^2)] and above by
(kappa-epsilon)^2/2. No step later silently replaces qmax_sp by1.

For delta=phi-theta the normalized support pairing is

    <n(theta),v(phi)>/R(theta)
      =exp(-kappa delta)(cos delta+kappa sin delta).

Its derivative is -(1+kappa^2)exp(-kappa delta)sin delta. Since
|delta|<=theta_*<pi/2, the expression increases up to delta0 and
decreases thereafter, with maximum1. The origin has strictly smaller
pairing0. Thus n(theta) supports the entire K, not just nearby arc
points. At theta0 the vector (0,1) also supports K because q>=0.

The horizontal component cos theta-kappa sin theta has derivative
-sin theta-kappa cos theta<0. It becomes negative precisely after
theta_c when theta_c<theta_*. The vertical component of n is
-sin theta-kappa cos theta<0. These statements are valid for all
positive kappa, even when the arc dips below vertical height-1.
They prove the full claimed support geometry without importing the
published circular geometry.

## 4. Actual backward projections, including all boundary cases

Let alpha_1,...,alpha_m be the ordered nonnegative ordinary steps and
r_j=(u_j,-y_j). Start at r_m=v(theta_*)=(epsilon,-1). At an uncapped
stage retain

    r_j=R(theta_j)[(1+t_j)e(theta_j)+kappa t_j t(theta_j)], t_j>=0,
    u_j>=epsilon, y_j>=1, y_j/u_j<=1/epsilon.             (A1)

Here the scalar t_j is the normal coefficient, not the vector t(theta).
The endpoint starts with t_m=0. Assume the induction at j and put
alpha=alpha_j.

If y_j<=kappa[u_j+(alpha-1)c], put mu=u_j+(alpha-1)c. Then
mu>=y_j/kappa>0, and at r_old=r_j+alpha(c,0),

    r_old-(c,0)=mu n(0)+(0,kappa mu-y_j).

Both terms belong to supporting normal rays at (c,0). Their sum
certifies its Euclidean projection: its inner product with every
v-(c,0), v in K, is nonpositive. Also
y_old<=kappa(u_old-c). Increasing u_old by any further alpha c preserves
the cap inequality for every alpha>=0. The cap is permanent backward,
including equality in the cap test; there is no unexplained endpoint
switch.

Otherwise the current point cannot already be capped. Define

    F_j(theta)=<r_j,e(theta)-kappa^(-1)t(theta)>/R(theta).

Equation(A1) gives F_j(theta_j)=1. The failed cap test gives
F_j(0)=(u_j-y_j/kappa)/c<1-alpha. Direct differentiation gives

    F_j'(theta)=(kappa+kappa^(-1))
                  (u_j cos theta+y_j sin theta)/R(theta)>0.

Therefore precisely one theta in (0,theta_j] satisfies F_j(theta)=1-alpha.
This is a mathematical existence argument, with no executed root search.
Since u_j,y_j>0, the clockwise polar angle of r_j lies in (0,pi/2).
In(A1) the radial coefficient is positive and the tangent coefficient
nonnegative, so that polar angle is at least theta_j. It follows that
<r_j,t(theta)>=0 for the selected theta<=theta_j.

Let w=r_j+(alpha-1)v(theta). The root equation says
<w,e(theta)>=kappa^(-1)<w,t(theta)>, while
<w,t(theta)>=0. Hence w is a nonnegative multiple of n(theta).
This proves projection of r_old=r_j+alpha v(theta) onto K at v(theta),
and gives(A1) with the new nonnegative coefficient.

The coordinate invariants follow by addition of alpha(p,-q): both u,y
increase weakly, and y<=u/epsilon is preserved since q<=p/epsilon.
The induction is therefore closed for every finite ordinary word.
If alpha=0 at an uncapped point, the root is exactly theta_j and the
state and gradient repeat. If alpha=0 at a cap, the capped state repeats.

No physical entry is removed. Empty ordinary blocks need no induction.

The vertical assertion that replaces q<=1 is an identity:

    y_j-q_j=R(theta_j)t_j(sin theta_j+kappa cos theta_j)>=0. (A2)

It holds at the initial endpoint with equality, at every repeated

## 5. Turning bounds, displacements, and thresholds

For two consecutive uncapped angles theta_old<=theta_new set
Delta=theta_new-theta_old. Resolving the exact backward update along
t(theta_old) cancels the added gradient term. It yields

    t_old=B(Delta)+A(Delta)t_new,
    A(Delta)=exp(-kappa Delta)(cos Delta+sin Delta/kappa),
    B(Delta)=exp(-kappa Delta)sin Delta/kappa.

All coefficients are nonnegative because 0<=Delta<=theta_*<pi/2.
Put d=1-kappa^2. Direct differentiation gives

    A'-(d/kappa)A=-(1+kappa^(-2))exp(-kappa Delta)sin Delta<=0.

Since A(0)=1, A<=exp(d Delta/kappa). Always B<=Delta/kappa.
For kappa<1, d>0 and
1+dB<=1+d Delta/kappa<=exp(d Delta/kappa). Therefore

    1+d t_old<=exp(d Delta/kappa)(1+d t_new).

The sum of backward angle decrements is at most theta_*, and the
endpoint coefficient is0. Telescoping proves

    t_j<=[exp(d(theta_*-theta_j)/kappa)-1]/d<=T_sp.

For kappa>=1, the same differential estimate gives A<=1. Using
t_old<=Delta/kappa+t_new instead proves
t_j<=(theta_*-theta_j)/kappa<=theta_*/kappa=T_sp.
No division by d at kappa=1 or multiplication of an inequality by a
negative d occurs in this branch.

T_sp is positive and finite for each allowed pair. For fixed epsilon,
lim_(kappa->1-) [exp(d theta_*/kappa)-1]/d=theta_*;
the right branch theta_*/kappa has the same limit and value at1.
Continuity does not require matching derivatives. Also c>1, so
H_sp=c[1+(1+kappa)T_sp]-1>0 and
A_sp=epsilon+(H_sp+1)/epsilon>0. All subsequent positive scales have
strictly positive finite denominators. No uniform boundedness as
kappa->0 or epsilon->0 is claimed.

At an uncapped point,

    y_j=R(theta_j)[(1+t_j)sin theta_j+kappa t_j cos theta_j]
       <=c[1+(1+kappa)T_sp]=1+H_sp.

The transition into a cap and every subsequent capped step preserve y.
Thus the bound holds everywhere. Summing the actual updates gives
r_0=(epsilon+P,-1-Q), and

    0<=P<=c S, 0<=Q<=H_sp, P>=epsilon Q,

where S=sum_j alpha_j. These are uniform in the finite word length and
individual sizes; there is no small-step assumption.

For the unchanged-threshold rectangle, when kappa<=epsilon we have
N_sp=0. Otherwise the derivative in epsilon computed in Section3 is
negative. Letting epsilon decrease to0 yields

    qmax_sp<=exp(kappa arctan kappa)/sqrt(1+kappa^2).

The logarithmic derivative of this right side in kappa is arctan kappa.
For 0<kappa<=1 it is bounded by exp(pi/4)/sqrt(2)<2. The source's
elementary numerical-constant proof is valid: pi<4,
e=1+1+1/2+sum_(j>=3)1/j!<=5/2+(1/6)/(1-1/4)=49/18,
and (49/18)^2<8 by 2401<2592. These are textual inequalities, not
executed arithmetic. Therefore N_sp<2<=1/epsilon and

    B_sp=1+max(epsilon^(-2),N_sp/epsilon)=1+epsilon^(-2).

This includes kappa1 and epsilon1/2. For arbitrary kappa>0 the same
equality follows whenever epsilon N_sp<=1, including equality in that
condition. Outside it the displayed larger B_sp supplies exactly the
horizontal bound used below. No unproved larger-domain claim is needed.

## 7. One global max-affine envelope and every actual row

For the main realization set

    M_i=P_i+max(epsilon b_i,epsilon+(1+Q_i)/epsilon), sigma_i=1/M_i,
    D_0=1, ell_0=0,
    D_(i+1)=D_i sigma_i(b_i-1), ell_(i+1)=D_i sigma_i(1+Q_i).

All D_i are positive because b_i>=B_sp>1. Define

    Phi_i(z)=D_i^2 phi_i((z_i-ell_i)/D_i,z_(i+1)/D_i), i<k,
    delta_tail=D_k/(1+2s_k),
    Phi_k(z)=delta_tail max(z_k-ell_k,0),
    J_sp=max_(0<=i<=k)Phi_i, f=env_1 J_sp.

Each Phi_i is a finite affine maximum with a zero plane. Thus J_sp
is a single finite affine maximum, fixed from the entire supplied word
before GD starts. Its construction does not change along the path.
It is nonnegative and vanishes at0 by the pre-activation zero regions.

For completeness, the envelope properties follow directly. A proximal
point z exists uniquely since J_sp is continuous, convex, nonnegative,
and its addition to ||x-z||^2/2 is coercive and strictly convex. Write
g=x-z in partial J_sp(z). For another input x', monotonicity of the two
subgradients gives

    ||g-g'||^2<=<g-g',x-x'>.

Hence g is 1-Lipschitz. The trial point z at x+w gives the upper bound
f(x+w)<=f(x)+<g,w>+||w||^2/2. Using the actual proximal point z' at
x+w and the subgradient inequality at z gives the lower bound

    f(x+w)>=f(x)+<g,w>+||w-(z'-z)||^2/2.

The two estimates prove differentiability with gradient g; the lower
supporting inequality proves convexity. Minimum0 is attained at0.

At the start of block i the candidate state has frozen coordinates
x_j=ell_j+D_j U_j for j<i, current coordinate x_i=ell_i+D_i,
and x_j=0 for j>i. Apply the local path in coordinates i,i+1. At its
candidate global proximal point z, subtraction of its proposed gradient
changes only those two coordinates. The following checks establish that
the local path is the global one:

* The current Phi_i has the proposed scaled local subgradient at z.
* For Phi_(i-1), when it exists, its first input remains U_(i-1).
  By(A5), z_i>=ell_i+D_i U_i>=ell_i, so its second input is at least
  ell_i/D_(i-1)=sigma_(i-1)(1+Q_(i-1))>=Y_(i-1). It is zero.
* Any Phi_j with j<=i-2 sees both its coordinates frozen. Its first
  input is U_j and its second is at least ell_(j+1)/D_j>=Y_j.
  Those earlier pieces remain zero.
* For Phi_(i+1), when it exists, (A4) gives
  z_(i+1)<=D_i sigma_i(1+Q_i)=ell_(i+1). Its first input is
  nonpositive and its second is0. It is zero.
* Every later local piece has first input -ell_j/D_j<=0 and second
  input0. The terminal hinge is also zero before activation; if it is
  the immediately next piece, the same(A4) bound applies to it.

The current Phi_i is nonnegative, so it attains J_sp at this point even
if its value is0 and several pieces tie. A subgradient of an active
piece is a subgradient of their maximum: its supporting affine bound
lies below that piece and hence below J_sp everywhere. Therefore the
candidate z satisfies the global proximal condition. Uniqueness proves
the claimed gradient is the actual global gradient. No inactive plane
has been deleted.

At the checkpoint the current coordinate freezes at ell_i+D_i U_i.
The next is D_i(Y_i+sigma_i b_i)=ell_(i+1)+D_(i+1). This proves the
induction across all original physical steps, including consecutive

During the terminal block let u be the sum already executed. It lies in

    D_k-(u+1)delta_tail

## 10. Finite equal-angle witness, paid cost, and uniformity

Fix positive kappa and 0<epsilon<=1/2. For a positive integer M set

    t_0=0, t_l=B_D+A_D t_(l-1),
    r_l=R(theta_l)[(1+t_l)e(theta_l)+kappa t_l t(theta_l)],

normal proves projection at every r_l. The actual chronological word

this is one of the valid cap cases in Section4. Thus this is an actual

approximate one.

    P_M=c(1+t_M)-epsilon, 1+Q_M=c kappa t_M.              (A7)
