## proof
1. Static function, projections, complete rows and true minimum.

Compactness of C_ft gives a finite maximum. Completing the square shows that its unique maximizer is the Euclidean projection P_ft(x) onto C_ft. Indeed minimizing ||x-v||^2 over a convex compact set gives a minimizer, and strict convexity gives uniqueness. The projection criterion is
    <x-P_ft(x),v-P_ft(x)><=0 for every v in C_ft.
Necessity follows by differentiating squared distance at the start of the segment from P_ft(x) to v. Conversely, this inequality and
    ||x-v||^2-||x-P_ft(x)||^2
       =||v-P_ft(x)||^2-2<x-P_ft(x),v-P_ft(x)>
show sufficiency.

Apply that criterion at x and y with the other's projection as test point and add. This yields
    ||P_ft(x)-P_ft(y)||^2<=<x-y,P_ft(x)-P_ft(y)>.
Consequently P_ft is 1-Lipschitz. The maximizing inequalities defining F_ft imply
    0<=F_ft(y)-F_ft(x)-<P_ft(x),y-x>
      <=<P_ft(y)-P_ft(x),y-x><=||y-x||^2.
This proves differentiability with grad F_ft=P_ft. Convexity follows because F_ft is a maximum of affine functions of x. Hence this is one global convex 1-smooth function.
