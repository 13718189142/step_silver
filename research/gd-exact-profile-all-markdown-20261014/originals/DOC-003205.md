## 1. Exact class, recursive family, and conclusion

Set c=13/5, a=c^(-18), b_j=a c^j, and define ordered schedules

    H_0=(),       H_(m+1)=(H_m,b_m,H_m).

Their length is N_m=2^m-1 and their sum is

    S_m=a(c^m-2^m)/(c-2).

For every m>=1 consider ANY finite real Euclidean space, a unit initial
vector x0, a real number lambda, and a real linear operator

    A=lambda I+N,          N^2 x0=0.

The condition is only on the initial Krylov space; arbitrary other action
of A is allowed. At all N_m+1 query indices use the exact polynomial jets

    x_i=P_i(A)x0,       g_i=A x_i,
    P_i(z)=product_(t<i)(1-h_t z),       h=H_m.

Suppose these jets and the optimum sample (0,0,0), with SOME values f_i,
are jointly interpolable by a convex differentiable 1-smooth function.
The values are arbitrary. Let F be ANY such interpolant, including the
maximal one from fact1252b8823bb5a290. Write x_end=x_(N_m), g_end=g_(N_m).

With K=3250 and the finite absolute constant

    C_J=1020+(16/7)c^11(1+8K)(1+9K),

the SAME appended step b=1 satisfies

    2F(x_end-g_end) <= C_J/b_m.                            (1)

No linear-operator constraint is imposed at the new endpoint. Dimension,
lambda, N, conditioning and values may all depend on m. The canonical
sum-based Huber channel is also O(1/b_m). Section7 proves directly from
the accepted benchmark recurrence that B_n<=1+4n^(4/3), so the ratio of
these bounds to U_(2^m)^ZJ tends to zero because c^3>16.

This is a bound on the specified Jordan class, not on unrestricted W.
It does NOT include jointly legal mixtures of different Jordan eigenvalues.

## 2. Exact polynomial identities and a necessary eigenvalue interval

Put u=N x0 and U=||u||. Finite binomial expansion, using N^2 x0=0, gives

    x_i=P_i(lambda)x0+P_i'(lambda)u,
    g_i=lambda P_i(lambda)x0+[P_i(lambda)+lambda P_i'(lambda)]u.  (2)

These identities do not divide by P_i and include repeated polynomial roots.
In particular g0=lambda x0+u and

    A g0=2lambda g0-lambda^2 x0.                           (3)

Let d0=<x0,g0> and e0=||g0||^2. The two optimum interpolation inequalities
imply d0>=e0. The first step of H_m is a>0. Adding its two ordered
interpolation inequalities and dividing by a^2 gives

    <A g0,g0> >= ||A g0||^2 >=0.                          (4)

By (3), the left side is 2lambda e0-lambda^2 d0. If e0=0, then g0=0 and
(3) implies lambda=0; all jets are then stationary. If e0>0, lambda<0
is impossible. For lambda>0 the inequalities d0>=e0 and (4) give

    0<=lambda(2-lambda)e0,

so 0<=lambda<=2. We use this closed interval and do not require endpoint
strictness. Neither an operator-norm bound nor individual eigendirection
legality was assumed.

## 3. A short actual prefix controls the nilpotent component

Consider any initial nonnegative prefix of the same jets, with total sum
T>0 satisfying lambda T<=1/20. For its query indices define

    p_i=product_(t<i)(1-lambda h_t),
    t_i=sum_(t<i)h_t/(1-lambda h_t).

All denominators are positive, and

    19/20<=p_i<=1,       0<=lambda t_i<=1/19.

The lower product bound follows inductively from product(1-r_j)>=1-sum r_j
when r_j>=0 and their sum is at most1. Equation(2) now reads

    g_i=p_i[lambda x0+(1-lambda t_i)u],
    A g_i=p_i[lambda^2 x0+lambda(2-lambda t_i)u].            (5)

Suppose U>=4lambda and U>0. Then

    ||g_i|| >=(9/10)U-(19/20)lambda >=U/2,
    ||g_i|| <=lambda+U <=5U/4,
    ||A g_i||<=lambda^2+2lambda U <=(9/4)lambda U.

At an actual step h_i, reverse ordered interpolation gives

    f_i-f_(i+1)
      >= <g_(i+1),h_i g_i>+||g_i-g_(i+1)||^2/2
       =h_i||g_i||^2-h_i^2<A g_i,g_i>+h_i^2||A g_i||^2/2
      >=h_i U^2/4-3lambda h_i^2 U^2
      >=h_i U^2/10.                                     (6)

The last step uses lambda h_i<=1/20; zero steps cause no division. Sum(6)
over the full chosen prefix. Its terminal value is nonnegative, while
optimum interpolation at the initial query gives

    f0<=<g0,x0>-||g0||^2/2<=lambda+U<=5U/4.

Thus U<=25/(2T). Combining with the alternative U<4lambda proves

    U<=max{4lambda,25/(2T)}<=4lambda+13/T.                 (7)

If lambda=U=0 this conclusion is immediate. The estimate comes from the
actual full-prefix function-value budget, not an imposed normality or
bounded-conditioning hypothesis.

Apply this to H_m with tau=1/100. There are two cases.

LOW FREQUENCY: b_m lambda<tau. Then lambda S_m<tau/(c-2)=1/60, and
S_m>=b_m/c for m>=1. Using(7) on the entire prefix gives

    U<=4lambda+13/S_m,
    ||x_end||<=1+(20/19)S_m U<15,
    ||g_end||<=lambda+U<=5lambda+13/S_m<34/b_m.             (8)

The position bound uses x_end=p_end(x0-t_end u), t_end<=20S_m/19,
and lambda S_m<=1/20. The gradient bound follows directly from(5).

SCALE REACHED: b_m lambda>=tau. Since 2a<tau, there is a first integer
r in[1,m] with b_r lambda>=tau, and b_r lambda<c tau. The actual initial
block of H_m of this depth is H_r. Its sum satisfies

    1/260=tau/c<=lambda S_r
            <c tau/(c-2)=13/300<1/20.

Indeed S_r/b_r=(1-(2/c)^r)/(c-2) lies between1/c and1/(c-2).
Apply(7) to this initial block to obtain the horizon-independent bound

    U<=K lambda,       K=3250.                           (9)

## 4. Complete sector polynomial estimate

Define the full-block polynomial

    p_m(z)=product_(j=0)^(m-1)(1-b_j z)^(2^(m-j-1)).

For EVERY complex z with 0<|z|<=9/4 and Re(z)/|z|>=2/3, we prove

    |z||p_m(z)|^2<=c^11/b_m.                            (10)

This is an algebraic estimate, independent of any interpolation legality.
Choose the unique integer k with b_k|z|<=1<b_(k+1)|z| and set
y=b_k|z| in(5/13,1]. Because a=c^(-18), |z|<=9/4<c, we have k>=17.
Write q=Re(z)/|z| and z/|z|=exp(i theta).

If m<=k, each factor has magnitude at most one: for t=b_j|z|<=1,
1-2qt+t^2<=1 because q>=2/3. Hence |z||p_m(z)|^2<=1/b_m.

Now m>k. A zero factor proves(10) immediately. Otherwise multiplicities give

    log|p_m(z)|/2^(m-k-1)
      =sum_(j=-k)^(m-k-1)2^(-j)log|1-c^j y exp(i theta)|.  (11)

For j<0, log(1+t)<=t applied to the squared magnitude bounds each term
by -q c^j y+(c^j y)^2/2. Put d=2/c=10/13. We have d^17<1/40: d^3<1/2
and d^2<4/5 imply d^17=(d^3)^5 d^2<1/40. Thus the sum of negative-index
terms is at most

    -(13/4)q y+(25/119)y^2.

Here sum_(r=1)^k(2/c)^r>=13/4 and
(1/2)sum_(r>=1)(2/c^2)^r=25/119. For j>=1, c^j y>1 and q>=2/3 give
|1-c^j y exp(i theta)|<=c^j y. The nonnegative upper bounds
j log c+log y can be extended to an infinite sum; their weighted sum is
2log c+log y. The j=0 term is at most (1/2)log(1-(4/3)y+y^2).
Consequently(11) is at most

    E(y)=-(13/6)y+(25/119)y^2
          +(1/2)log(1-(4/3)y+y^2)+log y+2log(13/5).

We give exact details for E(y)<-1/128 on[5/13,1]. For5/13<=y<=1/2,
the quadratic term is at most25/476; D(y)=1-(4/3)y+y^2 is decreasing
and at most322/507; and -(13/6)y+log y<=-1+log(6/13). Hence

    E(y)<=-1+25/476+log A0,
    A0=(78/25)sqrt(322/507),      A0^2=3864/625<25/4.

The first four positive terms of exp(15/16) sum to61791/24576>5/2.
Thus log(5/2)<15/16 and E(y)<-19/1904<-1/128.

For1/2<=y<=1 the derivative has positive denominator and numerator

    E'(y)=B(y)/[2142yD(y)],
    B(y)=900y^4-5841y^3+11372y^2-8925y+2142.

Set t=2y-1 in[0,1]. Direct expansion yields

    16B((1+t)/2)
      =-2422-11870t+15842t^2-8082t^3+900t^4
      <=-2422+t[-11870+15842t-7182t^2]
      <=-2422-3210t<0.

The last inequality uses that 15842t-7182t^2 increases on[0,1] and its
value at1 is8660. Thus E decreases on this interval, proving the claim.
We obtain

    |p_m(z)|<=exp(-2^(m-k-1)/128).

Let r=m-k>=1. Since |z|<=c^r/b_m,

    |z||p_m(z)|^2<=(1/b_m)c^r exp(-2^(r-1)/64).

For1<=r<=11 the extra factor is at mostc^11. For r>=12,
2^(r-7)>=r, by induction from12, and log c<1 since exp(1)>8/3>c.
The extra factor is then at most1. This proves(10), including all roots.

## 5. Polynomial derivatives and the free terminal completion

In the scale-reached case lambda>0. Consider the circle

    z=lambda+(lambda/8)exp(i t),       0<=t<=2pi,

and let M0 be the maximum of |p_m| on this circle. Every point satisfies
7lambda/8<=|z|<=9lambda/8<=9/4 and Re(z)/|z|>=7/9>2/3.
Equation(10) therefore implies

    lambda M0^2 <=8c^11/(7b_m).                          (12)

The finite polynomial expansion around lambda gives the exact coefficient
identities

    p_m(lambda)=(1/(2pi))integral_0^(2pi)
                       p_m(lambda+(lambda/8)exp(i t))dt,
    (lambda/8)p_m'(lambda)=(1/(2pi))integral_0^(2pi)
                       p_m(lambda+(lambda/8)exp(i t))exp(-i t)dt.

To verify them, expand the polynomial in powers of (z-lambda) and integrate
each monomial: every nonconstant Fourier mode has integral zero. No external
complex-analysis theorem and no division by p_m(lambda) is needed. Hence

    |p_m(lambda)|<=M0,        lambda|p_m'(lambda)|<=8M0.

Together with(2),(9), this gives

    ||x_end|| <=(1+8K)M0,
    ||g_end|| <=lambda(1+9K)M0.                           (13)

For every interpolant F, optimum interpolation and unit-step descent give

    2F(x_end-g_end)
       <=2<x_end,g_end>-2||g_end||^2
       <=2||x_end||||g_end||.                            (14)

Unit-step descent follows by integrating the1-Lipschitz gradient along the
unit gradient step; it does not assume that the new gradient equals A times
the new point. Apply(8) to(14) in the low-frequency case, and(12),(13) in
the scale-reached case. Their bounds are respectively1020/b_m and
(16/7)c^11(1+8K)(1+9K)/b_m. Their sum C_J/b_m proves(1).

## 6. An explicit legal genuinely nonnormal calibration

This section is not used in the upper bound. It shows that the class has
nontrivial legal prefixes for every supplied nonnegative schedule, with a
single fixed strongly convex function and a uniform final-step lower witness.

For total sum S choose lambda=1/[10(S+1)], eta=lambda/10,
x0=(0,1), and A=[[lambda,eta],[0,lambda]]. Thus N^2=0 and N!=0.
Write p_i=P_i(lambda), t_i=sum_(t<i)h_t/(1-lambda h_t). The exact positions are

    x_i=(s_i,tcoord_i)=(-eta p_i t_i,p_i).

The use of tcoord_i as a coordinate is distinct from the sum t_i above.
We have p_i>=9/10 and lambda t_i<=1/9. Delete only repeated knots caused
by zero steps. Along every positive step,

    (s_(i+1)-s_i)/(tcoord_(i+1)-tcoord_i)
          =(eta/lambda)(1-lambda t_i) in[0,1/10].

Join these query knots and(0,0) by a continuous piecewise affine graph
s=psi(t). The segment from(0,0) to the last query has slope
-eta t_end with magnitude at most1/90. Extend psi constantly outside the
extreme knots. Then psi(0)=0 and psi is globally1/10-Lipschitz.

Define on R^2 the single function

    F0(s,t)=lambda(s^2+t^2)/2+eta s t-eta integral_0^t psi(v)dv.

Its gradient is (lambda s+eta t,lambda t+eta(s-psi(t))). Thus at every query
it equals A x_i exactly. On an affine piece of psi its Hessian is

    [[lambda,eta],[eta,lambda-eta psi'(t)]].

For any real vector(a0,b0), the absolute value of the perturbation quadratic
2eta a0 b0-eta psi'(t)b0^2 is at most(11/10)eta(a0^2+b0^2). Integrating
the piecewise Hessian along line segments proves that F0 is globally
(89lambda/100)-strongly convex and has gradient Lipschitz constant at most
111lambda/100<1. Its gradient at0 is zero, so F0(0)=min F0=0. All query
values are its actual values; therefore all ordered interpolation inequalities
and the unit initial-distance normalization hold for the SAME data.

For clarity the resulting all-final-step lower bound can also be quantified.
Let x=x_end, g=g_end and z be the orthogonal projection of x onto g-perp.
Here g!=0 and

    |det(x,g)|=eta p_end^2,
    ||g||<=lambda+eta,
    ||z||^2>=p_end^4/121>=1/242.

The determinant identity follows by expanding(2); the terms with t_end
cancel. Strong convexity gives, for every real final b and hence for b>=0,

    2F0(x-bg)>= (89lambda/100)||z||^2
               >=89/[242000(S+1)]>1/[3000(S+1)].           (15)

There is also one test vector satisfying every maximal-interpolant anchor:
put mu=89lambda/100 and w=mu z. Then <w,g>=0. Since F0>=mu||x||^2/2,
its conjugate is at most||w||^2/(2mu). Every quadratic conjugate anchor
H_j from accepted1252b8823bb5a290 lies below F0*, because F0 interpolates
the queried data. Thus, with y_j=x_j-g_j and delta_j=f_j-||g_j||^2/2,

    2delta_j+2<x-y_j,w>-||w||^2
       =2<x,w>-2H_j(w)>=mu||z||^2

for EVERY query and optimum anchor. The maximal completion inherits(15).
This merely calibrates the sum-scale strength of this class; it is weaker
than existing general sum-based lower bounds and is not a new target claim.

## 7. Exact-profile comparison derived from the accepted recursions

Write C_n=S_circle(n)+1 and B_n=2S_bullet(n)+1 as in acceptedb98bb807d514e80d.
Their two increasing join maps become

    Omega(A,D)=(A+D+sqrt((A+D)^2+4AD))/2,
    G(A,D)=2A+D/2+sqrt(D^2+8AD)/2.

For0<=t<=1,

    t^(4/3)+(1-t)^(4/3)+[t(1-t)]^(4/3)<=1.                (16)

Indeed put x=t^(1/3), y=(1-t)^(1/3), so x^3+y^3=1. Direct factorization is

    1-x^4-y^4-x^4y^4
      =x^3 y^3[1/(1+x+x^2)+1/(1+y+y^2)-xy]>=0.

Each reciprocal is at least1/3 and xy<=2^(-2/3)<2/3, the latter checked
by cubing. The endpoint cases hold as well. Since Omega is the positive
root of X^2-(A+D)X-AD, inequality(16) yields

    Omega(A,D)<=(A^(3/4)+D^(3/4))^(4/3).

Induction in the primitive recurrence gives C_n<=(n+1)^(4/3). Also
sqrt(D^2+8AD)<=D+4A implies G(A,D)<=D+4A. The dominant recurrence therefore
gives, for each split k and by induction,

    B_n<=1+4[(n-k-1)^(4/3)+(k+1)^(4/3)]<=1+4n^(4/3).

The last inequality follows from superadditivity of t^(4/3), obtained by
integrating its increasing derivative. Initial values are C_0=B_0=1.
Consequently

    (C_J/b_m)/U_(2^m)^ZJ
       <=(C_J/a)[c^(-m)+4(2^(4/3)/c)^m] ->0,

because c^3=2197/125>16. The canonical Huber value on(H_m,1) is at most
1/(2S_m)<=c/(2b_m), so adding it does not change this conclusion.
