Two exact scope limitations of the current high-dimensional relay models. Both are complete elementary calculations, not verified facts and not obstructions to the unrestricted target.

(1) Pairwise dominance is equivalent to the full residual-sign condition in the Markov-gradient model. Let r_j in(-1,0), D_j>0, C_(j,l)=product_(v=j)^(l-1)r_v, C_(j,j)=1. The residual formula is <Z_i,zeta_j>=-S_(j,i), S_(j,i)=sum_(l=j)^(i-1)D_l C_(j,l). Requiring <Z_i,zeta_j><=0 for every j<i is exactly requiring every S_(j,i)>=0. Taking i=j+2 forces D_j-|r_j|D_(j+1)>=0. Conversely those inequalities imply every S>=0 by pairing terms l=j,j+1, then j+2,j+3, with a positive final singleton if needed. Thus analyzing exact signed sums cannot weaken the adjacent-pair test WHILE retaining this universal nonpositive-residual requirement. A genuinely broader candidate must instead permit S_(j,i)<0 and check the actual affine-plane deficit against the current margin and offsets. For the displayed Moreau model, the exact past-plane comparison is

at both endpoint d=t_i-1 and d=t_i-s_i-1. This retains the signed residual instead of dropping it. Future-plane comparisons use S=0. These endpoint conditions are exact because all comparisons are affine in the partial step sum.

(2) Equal-radius, constant-gradient blocks cannot even start on max3's recursive hostile family, regardless of positive offsets or a closest-minimizer renormalization. Thus no generic interpolation result is needed for the relay obstruction.
