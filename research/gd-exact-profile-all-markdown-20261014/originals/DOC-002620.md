# Exact two-envelope interpolation state across a GD cut

Status: support derivation, unverified; not a target proof or a proposed scalar
comparison with the Zhang--Jiang benchmark. Worker: max3.

Normalize L=1 and the minimizer datum to (x_star,g_star,f_star)=(0,0,0).
For every sampled jet (x_i,g_i,f_i) in a finite history I define

\[
y_i=x_i-g_i,\qquad z_i=f_i-\tfrac12\|g_i\|^2.
\]

The smooth-convex interpolation inequality is exactly

\[
z_i\geq z_j+\langle g_j,y_i-y_j\rangle\quad(i,j\in I).
\]

Define two finite polyhedral envelopes, retaining their full functions:

\[
A_I(u)=\max_{i\in I}\{z_i+\langle g_i,u-y_i\rangle\},
\qquad
B_I(v)=\max_{i\in I}\{\langle v,y_i\rangle-z_i\}.
\]

Both are nonnegative because the minimizer datum is in I. A proposed new jet
at x with gradient g and transformed value z=f-||g||^2/2 is compatible with
every old jet if and only if, writing y=x-g,

\[
A_I(y)\leq z\leq\langle g,y\rangle-B_I(g).
\tag{1}
\]

The left inequality lists all old-to-new constraints. The right inequality
lists all new-to-old constraints. In particular the exact gradient admissibility
test is A_I(x-g)+B_I(g)<=<g,x-g>. There is no maximization or deletion of
cross-correlations before this test.

After accepting (y,g,z), the updates are exactly

\[
A_{I+}(u)=\max\{A_I(u),z+\langle g,u-y\rangle\},\quad
B_{I+}(v)=\max\{B_I(v),\langle v,y\rangle-z\}.
\tag{2}
\]

Thus a full-history state can be represented by two polyhedral functions and
the current point/gradient. A redundant affine piece may be removed only when
it is pointwise dominated in the corresponding entire envelope. The number of
pieces is not bounded independently of the horizon by this derivation.

## Explicit legal witness for any admitted finite transcript

Let phi=A_I for the complete admitted transcript. Its defining inequalities
give phi(y_i)=z_i and
phi(u)>=phi(y_i)+<g_i,u-y_i>. Moreover phi>=0 and phi(0)=0.
Define the globally finite function

\[
f(x)=\min_u\{\phi(u)+\tfrac12\|x-u\|^2\}.
\tag{3}
\]

The minimizer p(x) exists because phi>=0 makes the objective coercive, and is
unique because the quadratic is strictly convex. At x_i, the displayed support
inequality and x_i=y_i+g_i show that the difference between the objective at u
and at y_i is at least ||u-y_i||^2/2. Therefore p(x_i)=y_i,
f(x_i)=z_i+||g_i||^2/2=f_i.

For completeness the smoothness argument is elementary. If p=p(x), minimizing
along the segment from p to an arbitrary u and using convexity of phi gives
phi(u)>=phi(p)+<x-p,u-p>. Thus x-p is a subgradient of phi at p. Applying
this support inequality in both directions at x and x' gives

\[
\langle (x-p(x))-(x'-p(x')),p(x)-p(x')\rangle\geq0.
\]

For r(x)=x-p(x), substitute p(x)-p(x')=x-x'-(r(x)-r(x')) to obtain
||r(x)-r(x')||^2<=<r(x)-r(x'),x-x'>. Cauchy--Schwarz gives that r is
1-Lipschitz. Evaluating (3) at p(x) proves
f(x+d)<=f(x)+<r(x),d>+||d||^2/2. Reversing the points and using the
Lipschitz bound on r gives a matching first-order lower estimate with error
at most 3||d||^2/2. Hence f is differentiable and grad f=r. Convexity follows
by taking convex combinations of candidate minimizing points in the jointly
convex objective of (3). Since phi>=0 and phi(0)=0, f>=0 and f(0)=0.
This proves finite-dimensional realizability, convexity, smoothness, and a valid
minimizer, with no appeal to a merely PSD matrix.

## Relation to the true objective, and its limitation

At step t, query at the point x_t already fixed by the prior GD update; choose
(g_t,z_t) satisfying (1), update the envelopes by (2), and set
x_{t+1}=x_t-h_t g_t. Starting from A=B=0 (the minimizer datum), every complete
admitted transcript is realized by (3), and every legal trajectory satisfies
(1). The final normalized objective is
2 z_n+||g_n||^2 when ||x_0||=1. Consequently taking the supremum of this
quantity over the exact envelope recursion and all finite dimensions is the
actual W_n(h). Zero steps and arbitrary finite positive steps need no exception.

Necessity of the interpolation inequality can also be derived directly: for
fixed x_j put q(x)=f(x)-<g_j,x>. This convex 1-smooth function is minimized
at x_j. The descent estimate, obtained by integrating its Lipschitz gradient,
gives q(x_i-grad q(x_i))<=q(x_i)-||grad q(x_i)||^2/2. Comparing with q(x_j)
is exactly the required inequality.

This is an exact growing-state reformulation, not a reduction in minimax
strength. No invariant here compares its value to U_n^ZJ. A claim that the
envelope game uniformly dominates that benchmark, without a new mechanism,
would merely restate the target lower bound and must be marked BLOCKED.
