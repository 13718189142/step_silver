## 2. Approach 1: build and spend a finite pool of movable coordinates

### 2.1 One fixed function and the records that must be preserved

On a finite weighted probability space, work in the Euclidean space E={X: E X=0}, with squared norm E X^2. For fixed a_i>=0 and E a>0, define

\[
 K_a=\{g\in E:g_i\ge-a_i\},\qquad
 F_a(X)=\max_{g\in K_a}\{\mathbb E[Xg]-\tfrac12\mathbb E g^2\}.
\]

Its gradient is the Euclidean projection onto K_a. It is convex and globally 1-smooth, and 0 is a valid minimizer with value 0. The weighted isometry X_i -> sqrt(p_i)X_i gives an ordinary finite-dimensional Euclidean instance. All radii below are relative to this original 0.

**Radius scope.** When every wall is positive this minimizer is unique. When some walls are zero, the minimum set may contain other points. Every exclusion in Section 3 concerns the selected original minimizer **0** and the denominator ||X_0-0||^2. It does not bound a supremum over other selected minima, nor a denominator measured to the closest point of a polar minimum cone. A different final-body minimizer has not been constructed or analyzed here. Increasing a zero wall can shrink that cone, so an earlier choice of another minimizer cannot simply be retained through a repair.

At a query X, its projection is g_i=max(X_i-t,-a_i), E g=0. An atom is called free in the current backward record when it is unsaturated at **every** recorded later physical query. If a proof also uses auxiliary queries, they must be included in that record. Increasing its wall then preserves those recorded projections and values exactly, as in the parent note.

The following construction uses direct original-variance accounting, and does not invoke any auxiliary primitive-potential equality. Its required auxiliary record is therefore empty. If optional auxiliary queries are retained, only atoms unsaturated at those queries can remain in the movable pool. The all-depth obstruction below applies even before imposing those extra restrictions.

### 2.2 An actual backward step, with a reserve that can be consumed

Suppose a finite mean-zero next law Y and its entire future trajectory have been built. Partition its atoms into a nonempty movable pool F and a frozen set D. Maintain:

1. every atom in F is free in the full later record;
2. every atom in D has Y_i<=0;
3. walls of atoms in D will never be altered.

At a reverse step h=1+d>1, choose any subset J of F to retire, leaving F'=F\J nonempty. Set D'=D union J. For each newly retired atom choose

\[
 a_i' =\max\{a_i,(Y_i)_+/d\},\qquad i\in J.
 \tag{2.1}
\]

These increases preserve the whole later record because J was free. Keep all frozen walls unchanged. With A_D=E[a' 1_(D')] and p_F=P(F'), put

\[
 t=\frac{dA_D-\mathbb E[Y1_{D'}]}{p_F}.
 \tag{2.2}
\]

Every summand d a_i'-Y_i on D' is nonnegative, so t>=0. Now increase the remaining free walls, if necessary, to

\[
 a_i'\leftarrow\max\{a_i',(Y_i-t)/d\},\qquad i\in F'.
 \tag{2.3}
\]

This again preserves every old recorded query. It does not change (2.2), which depends only on D'. Define the predecessor by

\[
 X_i=\begin{cases}
 Y_i-ha_i',&i\in D',\\
 ht/d-Y_i/d,&i\in F'.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{2.4}
\]

This is the parent's genuine inverse with omega=1_(F'). Indeed, with Z=t+da'-Y,

\[
 Z\ge0,\qquad
 \mathbb E[1_{F'}Z]=p_Ft+d\mathbb E[a'1_{F'}]-\mathbb E[Y1_{F'}]
 =d\mathbb E a'.
\]

Thus E X=0; its actual projection at threshold t is -a' on D' and X-t on F'; and X-h P_(K_a')X=Y atom by atom. Atoms in F' are also unsaturated at this newly added query. Atoms in D' satisfy X_i<=0: for old frozen atoms this is immediate, and for newly retired positive Y_i, (2.1) gives X_i<=-Y_i/d<0. Therefore the three invariants hold for the next reverse step.

There is no omitted cap exception: (2.1)--(2.4) hold for every d>0, both d<1 and d>=1. A reflected free atom can become positive or negative; it remains free and is repaired by (2.3) if needed. A frozen atom is never reflected again. No saturation event is erased.

After a finite word, all wall labels, weights and X_0 have been fixed. Forward execution uses this one final function. Later wall modifications have preserved every previously constructed physical iterate and function value; there is no changing objective during that forward run.

## 3. All-depth obstruction for the entire monotone-face class

The recursion above has active sets that only expand in forward time. This is a restriction on the actual physical trajectory, not a guess about its endpoint law. The following derivation covers every K_a trajectory with that property, including arbitrary finite weights, arbitrary nonnegative walls, arbitrary terminal laws and arbitrary numbers of atoms.

### 3.1 Orthogonal geometry of nested simplex faces

For a nonempty coordinate set A, let

\[
 p_A=P(A),\quad b_A=\frac{\mathbb E[a1_{A^c}]}{p_A},\qquad
 (e_A)_i=\begin{cases}b_A,&i\in A,\\-a_i,&i\notin A,\end{cases}
\]
\[
 L_A=\{v:\operatorname{supp}v\subset A,\ \mathbb E v=0\}.
\]

The minimum-norm point on the face with g=-a off A is e_A. Its affine hull is e_A+L_A, and e_A is orthogonal to L_A. If the projection of X lies on this face and is unsaturated on A, then

\[
 g=e_A+P_{L_A}X.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

For A contained in B, e_A-e_B lies in L_B and is orthogonal to L_A and e_B. Refining a nested chain one added atom at a time therefore gives mutually orthogonal nonzero increments

\[
 v=e_A-e_{A\cup\{i\}}.
\]

Each such v is a supporting normal of K_a on the smaller face: its largest coordinate is constant on A, and all other coordinates are smaller. Since g+a>=0 and E(g+a)=E a,

\[
 \langle g,v\rangle\le\|v\|^2\quad(g\in K_a),
 \tag{3.2}
\]

with equality on that smaller face. This uses the actual weighted Euclidean geometry; no non-isometric coordinate transform is inserted.

### 3.2 Simultaneous releases and their chronology

Suppose the actual active sets satisfy

\[
 A_0\subseteq A_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq A_n.
 \tag{3.3}
\]

Boundary atoms may be assigned consistently to either side. At a step from A=A_(r-1) to B=A_r, define the old slack for each entering atom by

\[
 s_i=t_{r-1}-a_i-X_{r-1,i}\ge0.
\]

Refine this simultaneous release by adding the entering atoms in nondecreasing order of s_i. This refinement introduces no extra physical time or query; it only chooses an orthogonal basis.

For an intermediate set A'=A union J and the next entering atom i, direct averaging at the old query gives

\[
 \operatorname{mean}_{A'} X_{r-1}-X_{r-1,i}
 =b_{A'}+a_i+s_i-\frac{\mathbb E[1_Js]}{p_{A'}}.
 \tag{3.4}
\]

The last two terms have nonnegative difference by the chosen order. For the associated nonzero increment v, its normalized X coefficient is

\[
 \xi_{r-1}:=\frac{\langle X_{r-1},v\rangle}{\|v\|^2}
 =\frac{\operatorname{mean}_{A'}X_{r-1}-X_{r-1,i}}{b_{A'}+a_i}
 \ge1.
 \tag{3.5}
\]

At all earlier physical queries the actual face is contained in the smaller refined face, so the gradient coefficient on v is exactly 1. At and after time r it equals the X coefficient, by (3.1). At time r, (3.2) gives xi_r<=1. If S_<r denotes the sum of steps preceding the release step and that step is h=1+d, these facts give the exact chronology

\[
 \xi_{r-1}=\xi_0-S_{<r}\ge1,\qquad
 \xi_r=\xi_0-S_{<r}-h\le1,
\]
\[
 S_{<r}+1\le\xi_0\le S_{<r}+h+1.
 \tag{3.6}
\]

After the release, xi is multiplied by 1-h_t at every subsequent step. Thus the contribution of this entire mode to terminal twice-value, divided by its original squared radius contribution, is at most

\[
 \max\left\{
 \frac{d^2\prod_{\text{after }r}(h_t-1)^2}{(S_{<r}+1)^2},\quad
 \frac{\prod_{\text{after }r}(h_t-1)^2}{(S_{<r}+h+1)^2}
 \right\}.
 \tag{3.7}
\]

The maximum occurs at an endpoint of (3.6), because |1-(S_<r+h)/xi_0| has its maximum there. Importantly, an atom that enters late cannot bring an arbitrary uncharged quadratic deviation: (3.4)--(3.6) tie that deviation to its original radius.

For completeness, zero walls do not create a missing uncharged direction. A zero increment means b_(A')=a_i=0. All walls outside A' are then zero. Use the contrast direction positive and constant on A', negative on i, and zero elsewhere. Its old gradient coefficient is zero. By (3.4) and the same slack ordering its old X coefficient is nonnegative. Feasibility of every g in K_a makes its gradient coefficient nonpositive. After i is active the gradient coefficient equals the X coefficient; that X coefficient did not change in the release step because its old gradient coefficient was zero. It must therefore vanish. Its original coefficient also vanishes. Such directions can be discarded, rather than divided by a zero norm.

### 3.3 The remaining modes and exact original-radius comparison

Directions in L_(A_0) are quadratic from time 0 and have terminal-to-initial squared ratio product_t(h_t-1)^2. The last face has an unreleased normal e=e_(A_n). If e is nonzero and xi_t=<X_t,e>/||e||^2, its gradient coefficient is 1 throughout, so xi_n=xi_0-S_n. Projection optimality against 0 gives xi_n>=1. Its terminal twice-value is

\[
 (2\xi_n-1)\|e\|^2,
\]

and its original radius contribution is xi_0^2||e||^2. Their ratio satisfies

\[
 \frac{2(\xi_0-S_n)-1}{\xi_0^2}\le\frac1{1+2S_n}.
 \tag{3.8}
\]

Any residual original component orthogonal to L_(A_n) and e is orthogonal to every gradient in the chain; it stays constant and contributes only nonnegative additional radius. At the terminal point the envelope formula is exactly

\[
 2F_a(X_n)=\|P_{L_{A_n}}X_n\|^2
            +2\langle X_n,e\rangle-\|e\|^2.
 \tag{3.9}
\]

Equations (3.7)--(3.9) therefore bound the actual full ratio by the largest mode ratio. There is no replacement of the original radius by a radius at a release time.

### 3.4 Evaluate the bound on the complete H_m

Every suffix product of the positive excesses h-1 in H_j is at most 1. This follows by induction: a suffix either lies in the right child, or contains that child and the bridge, whose product is d_(j-1)P_(j-1)<1/c, times a suffix of the left child. Empty suffixes have product 1.

Let E_j be the largest of the two **unsquared** expressions in (3.7), over release steps anywhere in the complete H_j, with the real prefix sums in that word. For a release in its left child, the two quantities are multiplied by d_(j-1)P_(j-1)<1/c. At the bridge and throughout the right child, the denominators are at least 1+S_(j-1), while the required suffix products are at most 1. Therefore

\[
 E_j\le\max\{E_{j-1}/c,\ (1+S_{j-1})^{-1}\},\qquad E_0=0,
\]
\[
 E_j\le\frac1{u c^{j-1}}\quad(j\ge1).
 \tag{3.10}
\]

Consequently every released-mode payoff on H_m is at most

\[
 E_m^2\le(c^2/u^2)c^{-2m},
\]

and every initially quadratic mode is at most P_m^2<c^(-2m). For m>=1536 these are below 1/D_m: it suffices that c^m>2c^2/u^2, whose right side is <50, whereas c^m>2^m. The affine bound (3.8) is 1/D_m. Thus

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \frac{2F_a(X_n)}{\|X_0\|^2}\le\frac1{D_m}
 \quad\text{for every nested-active-set }K_a\text{ trajectory on }H_m.
 \quad}
 \tag{3.11}
\]

The standard constant-gradient Huber trajectory attains 1/D_m, so this upper bound within the stated trajectory class **with selected minimizer 0** is exact. In particular the concrete reserve recursion in Section 2 obeys

\[
 \|X_0\|^2\ge D_m\,[2F_a(X_n)].
\]

At terminal benchmark value B=B_(n_m), it requires original variance at least B D_m; its benchmark-normalized payoff is at most B/D_m<47/50. This rules out the whole monotone-reserve invariant, even with arbitrarily many terminal values and different walls. Adding old auxiliary-query preservation requirements only restricts this class further.

The conclusion does **not** cover a trajectory that leaves and later reenters an active face. It does not prove W_(n_m)(H_m)<=1/D_m for general functions or for all K_a trajectories.

## 4. Approach 2: pay for a real change between opposite saturated faces

The preceding obstruction forces actual recycling, so this approach abandons nested active sets. It uses a different fixed body when multiple reserves are combined: an orthogonal product of interval projection bodies. Each component executes the whole physical word and has a fully charged original radius.

### 4.1 An exact paid-reflection component for an arbitrary word

Take any predetermined nonnegative word and a selected step h_i=1+d>1. Write

\[
 P=\sum_{t<i}h_t,\qquad R=\sum_{t>i}h_t.
\]

For A>0 define the asymmetric Huber function

\[
 f_A(x)=\max_{g\in[-A,1]}\{xg-g^2/2\}.
\]

It is convex, 1-smooth, and minimized at the original 0. Its gradient is clip(x,[-A,1]). It is also a two-atom K_a instance in the weighted mean-zero metric: take X=(x,-x), equal weights, and a=(A,1).

Choose any q>=1, and fix

\[
 A=(q+R)/d,\qquad x_0=-A(1+P).
 \tag{4.1}
\]

Throughout the prefix the gradient is -A and the trajectory ends at x_i=-A. The chosen step gives

\[
 x_{i+1}=dA=q+R.
\]

Throughout the suffix the gradient is 1, and the actual terminal value is x_n=q. The two opposite gradient faces are attained; clipping is strict at the initial query when P>0, and at the post-bridge query when q+R>1. In particular both strict events occur at an internal bridge of H_m with nonempty children. Every step, including zeros elsewhere in the word, is accounted for. The wall A is chosen once before forward execution.

The exact original radius and terminal twice-value are

\[
 x_0^2=A^2(1+P)^2,\qquad 2f_A(x_n)=2q-1,
\]

so the paid payoff is

\[
 \frac{d^2(2q-1)}{(1+P)^2(q+R)^2}.
\]

Its maximum over q>=1 is attained at q=R+1. Thus a real full-word witness has

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 L_i(h)=\frac{(h_i-1)^2}{(1+P)^2(1+2R)},\quad
 A=\frac{1+2R}{h_i-1},\quad x_0=-A(1+P),\quad x_n=1+R.
 \quad}
 \tag{4.2}
\]

The factor A^2(1+P)^2 is exactly the cost of preparing the reflected reserve through the entire prefix. It cannot be replaced by the radius at x_i=-A. This is an actual face-change construction, not an increase of a formerly saturated wall while pretending its old query survives.

### 4.2 A recursive bank with several terminal values

One can build a finite reserve bank on any binary parse of the physical word: at each node whose bridge exceeds 1, add a component (4.2), using the bridge's **global** P and R in the complete word; recurse into the left and right node lists. Nodes with bridge at most 1 add no reflection component. Also retain a baseline Huber component with payoff 1/(1+2 sum h).

For arbitrary positive spatial scales lambda_i, set

\[
 F(x)=\sum_i\lambda_i^2 f_{A_i}(x_i/\lambda_i),
 \qquad (x_0)_i=\lambda_i[-A_i(1+P_i)].
 \tag{4.3}
\]

The gradient is 1-Lipschitz in the ordinary product Euclidean norm. The selected minimizer is 0. Every component runs the same entire physical word without a reset. Its terminal coordinate is lambda_i(1+R_i), so the bank can have many terminal values and many actual face changes at different times. Its original squared radius and terminal objective are exactly the sums of the component quantities.

Consequently its total normalized payoff is the radius-weighted average of its component payoffs. The bank cannot exceed their largest value. This statement is about the explicit product body (4.3); it is not a claim that a coupled simplex K_a decomposes into independent reservoirs.

### 4.3 Quantitative obstruction on every node of the complete H_m

Consider a step at level j in H_m. Its excess is d=a_h c^j. Put s=S_j. The actual global prefix and suffix sums satisfy P>=s and R>=s, because the occurrence has a full H_j child on each side. Also

\[
 \frac d{1+s}\le c-2=:r<3/7.
 \tag{4.4}
\]

Let T=P+R=S_m-(1+d). At fixed T, the denominator in (4.2) is

\[
 (1+P)^2[1+2(T-P)].
\]

Its logarithm is concave on s<=P<=T-s, so its minimum occurs at an endpoint. The smaller endpoint is P=s: the function (1+x)^2/(1+2x) is increasing for x>=0. Therefore

\[
 D_m L_i(H_m)
 \le\frac{d^2}{(1+s)^2}\,
 \frac{D_m}{D_m-2s-2(1+d)}.
\]

The last fraction decreases as D_m increases, and the actual condition R>=s gives D_m>=4s+2d+3. Hence

\[
 D_m L_i(H_m)
 \le\left(\frac d{1+s}\right)^2
       \frac{4s+2d+3}{2s+1}
 \le r^2(3+2r)
 <\frac{243}{343}<1.
 \tag{4.5}
\]

This covers every occurrence and every inherited level with the same enclosing parameters. Even after q and its wall A are optimized, every paid-reflection component in this bank is uniformly worse than the standard Huber component. Any finite positive combination has payoff at most 1/D_m, and hence benchmark-normalized payoff below 47/50.

At the root alone, (4.2) gives the particularly transparent complete-H_m witness

\[
 \frac{d_{m-1}^2}{(1+S_{m-1})^2D_{m-1}}.
\]

It has the correct order c^(-m), but a fixed loss. Formula (4.5), rather than this order statement, is the decisive reason this recursive independent-bank repair cannot close the target.

## 5. What is closed and what is still missing

Approach 1 supplies a genuine reserve recursion that can be executed at every reverse step on H_m, with all eventual wall labels fixed before forward execution and with exact original-variance accounting. Its tempting invariant is quantitatively insufficient: the entire nested-active-set class at the selected original minimum 0 has exact best payoff 1/D_m. Persistent physical unsaturation is therefore not by itself the missing target-level mechanism. Old auxiliary queries cannot repair this loss.

Approach 2 changes the physical mechanism: it crosses opposite saturated faces and pays the preparation radius. Its full-word witnesses and multivalue product bank are realizable, and (4.5) excludes the entire specified bank on H_m. The failure is the cost of the globally prepared reflected coordinates, rather than an unsupported claim that their wall labels were free.

Neither derivation excludes a **coupled** multivalue K_a trajectory with repeated exits and reentries of active faces. Such a trajectory would have to exploit correlations between reserve coordinates that are absent from (4.3), and it would not obey the orthogonal one-release decomposition used in (3.11). No construction or benchmark-scale original-radius upper bound for that coupled mechanism has been obtained in this invocation.

In particular there is no proved uniform all-word lower bound (1-o(1))/B_n, no full-class asymptotic upper certificate, and no complete candidate for formal audit. The unchanged low-mass Huber argument still covers sum h<=(B_n-1)/2. The exact all-integer-split DP has not been replaced by the diagnostic hierarchy.
