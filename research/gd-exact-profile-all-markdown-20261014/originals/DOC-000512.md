# Affine-height polytope Moreau envelopes represent the full finite-word supremum

MAIN-AUTHORED JOINT UNVERIFIED candidate. Original exact-profile minimax
target OPEN. This is a structural equality of suprema, not a quantitative
Zhang--Jiang comparison. No mathematical program or formal call was used.

## 1. Precise proposed theorem

Fix any finite n>=0 and any predetermined finite nonnegative word
h=(h_0,...,h_(n-1)). Normalize advertised smoothness to one. Let W_n(h)
be the original supremum of2(f(x_n)-f_star)/||x_0-x_star||^2 over all
finite-dimensional convex differentiable1-smooth functions with an attained
minimum and x_0!=x_star, under x_(i+1)=x_i-h_i grad f(x_i). Thus W=2R.

Consider the following subclass in arbitrary finite Euclidean dimensions D.
Choose a compact polytope K with at most n+2 vertices, containing0, and a
vector lambda such that

    <lambda,Y> >0 for every nonzero Y in K.

Thus0 is the exposed unique minimizer of the linear functional on K
(K={0} is allowed). Define the ONE globally specified function

    Phi_(K,lambda)(X)
       = min_(Y in K) [<lambda,Y>+||X-Y||^2/2].       (1.1)

Restrict the nonzero initial point further by <lambda,X_0>=0. Run the
same original word h on this function in the usual Euclidean metric.
Let W_n^aff(h) be the supremum of2Phi(X_n)/||X_0||^2 over these choices.

The candidate theorem is

    W_n^aff(h)=W_n(h) for EVERY finite n and h>=0.     (1.2)

Every member of the restricted class is globally convex differentiable
1-smooth with UNIQUE minimizer0. A strictly smooth strongly convex source
trajectory is represented with exact horizontal dynamics in just one extra
coordinate; its terminal objective increases by an explicit nonnegative
O(B^-2) term. General source functions are then covered by regularization
and finite-horizon continuity. No uniform approximation in n,h is claimed
or needed for the fixed-word equality of suprema.

The restriction in(1.1) concerns the Moreau envelope of a linear functional
plus a polytope indicator. Its gradient is X-P_K(X-lambda), NOT P_K(X).
It must not be confused with the previously studied projection-gradient
potential or its polar-cone minimizers.

## 2. Direct properties of the target class

Completing the square gives

    Y(X)=P_K(X-lambda),
    Phi(X)=<lambda,X>-||lambda||^2/2
                                  +dist(X-lambda,K)^2/2. (2.1)

Projection onto the nonempty compact convex K exists and is unique. Its
normal condition is

    <X-lambda-Y(X),Z-Y(X)> <=0 for every Z in K.      (2.2)

For two inputs U,V, adding their normal inequalities gives
<P_K(U)-P_K(V),U-V>>=||P_K(U)-P_K(V)||^2. Consequently I-P_K is
1-Lipschitz, since expanding its squared difference gives at most||U-V||^2.
The usual two trial-point comparisons for half the squared distance,
followed by this continuity, prove its differentiability with gradient I-P_K.
Thus(2.1) is differentiable and

    grad Phi(X)=X-P_K(X-lambda),                     (2.3)

with Lipschitz constant at most one. Convexity also follows directly from
(1.1): for minimizers Y_U,Y_V the convex combination lies in K, the linear
term averages exactly, and convexity of squared norm bounds Phi at the
corresponding convex combination of inputs.

Both terms in(1.1) are nonnegative. Their sum is zero iff Y=0 and X=Y,
using the strict exposing property. Hence Phi>=0 and its complete minimum
set is exactly{0}. These facts prove W_n^aff(h)<=W_n(h), including the
specified normalization and the additional initial orthogonality restriction.

## 3. Strict source and its exact interpolation slack

First let f:R^d->R be mu-strongly convex and ell-smooth, where mu>0 and
0<ell<1. Translate and subtract a constant so its unique minimizer is0
and f(0)=0. Fix any x_0!=0 and its ACTUAL finite original trajectory.
For EVERY i=0,...,n, including the actual unqueried terminal, write

    g_i=grad f(x_i), f_i=f(x_i),
    y_i=x_i-g_i, delta_i=f_i-||g_i||^2/2.

Also use the optimum labels x_*=g_*=y_*=0,f_*=delta_*=0. Set
gamma=(1/ell-1)/2>0. Every ordered original row satisfies

    Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2
        =delta_i-delta_j+<g_i,y_j-y_i>
        <=-gamma||g_i-g_j||^2.                     (3.1)

For completeness, the stronger coefficient1/(2ell) follows by applying
the elementary smooth descent inequality to q(x)=f(x)-<g_i,x>.
This convex ell-smooth function is minimized at x_i, so
q(x_i)<=q(x_j-(1/ell)grad q(x_j))
       <=q(x_j)-||g_j-g_i||^2/(2ell).
Rearranging proves(3.1). The smooth descent inequality follows by
integrating the Lipschitz gradient along that one segment; no external
interpolation theorem is needed beyond this argument.

In particular Q_(*,i)=-delta_i gives

    delta_i>=gamma||g_i||^2>=0.                    (3.2)

Strong convexity makes the gradient injective: its strong monotonicity
implies g_i=g_j only if x_i=x_j, and then y_i=y_j and delta_i=delta_j.
Also g_i=0 iff x_i=0, in which case y_i=delta_i=0. These degenerate
cases are essential when the word has zero steps or revisits a point.

## 4. One extra coordinate with fixed affine heights

Define scalar numbers entirely from the fixed finite source trajectory:

    z_0=0,
    z_(i+1)=(1-h_i)z_i+h_i delta_i, 0<=i<n,
    e_i=z_i-delta_i, 0<=i<=n.                       (4.1)

These are symbolic finite definitions, not an executed numerical
recurrence. They do not depend on the forthcoming lift magnitude B.

For B>0 put in R^(d+1)

    lambda_B=(0,B),
    Y_i=(y_i,delta_i/B),
    X_i=(x_i,z_i/B),
    G_i=X_i-Y_i=(g_i,e_i/B),
    K_B=conv{0,Y_0,...,Y_n}.                       (4.2)

K_B has at most n+2 vertices. Every nonzero Y_i has delta_i>0 by
(3.2) and injectivity; if delta_i=0 then Y_i=0. Therefore the linear
functional lambda_B exposes0 strictly on the WHOLE K_B: a convex
combination with zero height can use only zero vertices. This proves
the required minimum property rather than merely assuming it.

We now choose a finite B that makes every Y_i the actual projection
P_(K_B)(X_i-lambda_B). For each i in{0,...,n} and j in{*,0,...,n},
direct expansion gives

    <G_i-lambda_B,Y_j-Y_i>
       =Q_ij+e_i(delta_j-delta_i)/B^2.              (4.3)

When g_i=g_j, the two points and heights agree, so BOTH sides of the
normal test are exactly zero. Otherwise(3.1) has a strictly negative
margin. Define the finite nonnegative scalar

    M=max { [e_i(delta_j-delta_i)]_+
                       /(gamma||g_i-g_j||^2): g_i!=g_j }, (4.4)

where an empty maximum is0. Choosing B^2>=max(1,2M) makes(4.3)
at most -gamma||g_i-g_j||^2/2 for every distinct-gradient pair.
All other pairs are the exact zero cases already handled. Because K_B
is the convex hull of precisely these points, this checks EVERY normal
inequality, not just adjacent or active rows. At the optimum, projection
P_(K_B)(-lambda_B)=0 also follows directly from its exposing property.

Thus(2.2)--(2.3) prove simultaneously, for the ONE static Phi_B of(1.1),

    P_(K_B)(X_i-lambda_B)=Y_i,
    grad Phi_B(X_i)=G_i,
    Phi_B(X_i)=delta_i+||G_i||^2/2
               =f_i+e_i^2/(2B^2), 0<=i<=n.         (4.5)

Including terminal n in the construction is allowed: an adversarial
function may depend on the whole predetermined word, and is fixed once
constructed. No sample is reselected during this actual lifted run.

## 5. Actual original dynamics and exact initial normalization

For EVERY original update, (4.1)--(4.2) give exactly

    X_i-h_i G_i
       =(x_i-h_i g_i,[(1-h_i)z_i+h_i delta_i]/B)
       =X_(i+1).                                  (5.1)

Thus the lifted points are actual GD iterates of the one function Phi_B,
with the SAME steps. The horizontal coordinates agree with the source
exactly, not approximately; all vertical motion is paid and retained.

The initial point is X_0=(x_0,0), so

    <lambda_B,X_0>=0,
    dist(X_0,argmin Phi_B)^2=||x_0||^2.             (5.2)

This is the TRUE closest-minimum distance because the minimum is unique0.
At the actual terminal,

    2Phi_B(X_n)/||X_0||^2
       =2f(x_n)/||x_0||^2+e_n^2/(B^2||x_0||^2)
       >=2f(x_n)/||x_0||^2.                       (5.3)

Every term is finite. Choosing larger B reduces the additional objective,
but no limit in B is needed for the domination inequality. A large lift
vector is allowed; its norm is not part of the initial radius. The equality
of initial distances has been proved, not inferred by dropping a coordinate.

## 6. General convex smooth source by finite-horizon regularization

Now take ANY original1-smooth convex differentiable f with a selected
minimizer x_star, and any x_0!=x_star. Translate that selected minimizer
to0 and subtract f_star. Other minima are allowed at this stage. For
0<epsilon<1/2 define

    f_epsilon(x)=(1-2epsilon)f(x)+(epsilon/2)||x||^2. (6.1)

It has unique minimizer0, is epsilon-strongly convex, and is
ell_epsilon=1-epsilon smooth. The strict-source construction therefore
applies to its actual GD run from the SAME x_0 for the SAME fixed word.

For fixed n and h, these source runs converge to the original one as
epsilon decreases to0. A direct finite induction proves this: if
x_i^epsilon->x_i, continuity and Lipschitzness of grad f imply
grad f_epsilon(x_i^epsilon)->grad f(x_i), and the update with the fixed
finite number h_i then gives x_(i+1)^epsilon->x_(i+1). Function-value
continuity and the explicit small quadratic in(6.1) yield

    f_epsilon(x_n^epsilon)->f(x_n).                (6.2)

For EACH epsilon, (5.3) constructs a member of the restricted class
with at most n+2 vertices and orthogonal initial point whose payoff is
at least2f_epsilon(x_n^epsilon)/||x_0||^2. Taking the limit in these
scalar inequalities gives

    W_n^aff(h)>=2f(x_n)/||x_0||^2.

The source f, selected minimizer and initial point were arbitrary, so
W_n^aff(h)>=W_n(h). Together with Section2 this proves the proposed
equality(1.2) within this JOINT UNVERIFIED draft.

For arbitrary advertised L>0 first replace f by f/L after translating
the chosen minimizer; the normalized updates are exactly h_i grad(f/L)
and the payoff remains W=2R. Thus the reduction respects the original
normalization. Zero steps, repeated points, hitting the minimum, empty
words, arbitrary finite dimensions and arbitrarily large finite steps
were not excluded. No infinite-dimensional limit or uniform error claim
across words/horizons is used.

## 7. Consequences that are NOT claimed

This candidate would show that a uniform upper inequality for the entire
class(1.1), with its exposed minimum and orthogonal initial point, transfers
to the unrestricted W. It supplies NO such quantitative inequality.
In particular it does not prove W_n(H_m)<=17/(16D_m), any benchmark
separation, or the original every-word lower comparison.

The maximum defining M may depend on every source sample and on the full
word. The construction starts from an already specified source trajectory;
it is NOT a schedule-only adversarial selector. It does not claim attainment
of the original supremum, a uniform bound on B, or that K_B is a simplex.
It does not establish that a single observed anchor attains the complete
prefix functional. Positive affine heights remain quantitatively essential.
