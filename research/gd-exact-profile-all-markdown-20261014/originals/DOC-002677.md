## 1. A finite max-affine construction with a closed history criterion
Partition a predetermined nonnegative schedule chronologically as
(block_0,b_0,...,block_(m-1),b_(m-1),block_m),
where block_i has sum s_i, following the project notation.
Choose positive amplitudes alpha_i and positive virtual budgets t_i with
t_i>=s_i+1,0<=i<=m.
Put
B_i^M=(s_i+b_i-t_i)alpha_i for0<=i<m,
r_i^M=-t_(i+1)alpha_(i+1)/B_i^M,
and require B_i^M>0 and -1<r_i^M<0.
Choose unit vectors recursively in R^(m+1):
zeta_0=e_0,
zeta_(i+1)=r_i^M zeta_i+sqrt(1-(r_i^M)^2)e_(i+1).
Define omega_i=alpha_i zeta_i. Their correlations are explicitly
C_ij=<zeta_i,zeta_j>=product_(l=min(i,j))^(max(i,j)-1)r_l^M
for i!=j, and C_ii=1.
This gives a real PSD Gram matrix and linearly independent gradients.

Let nonnegative offsets eta_i be chosen and define the ONE fixed function
J(z)=max{0,max_(0<=i<=m)(<omega_i,z>-eta_i)},
f(x)=min_z {J(z)+||x-z||^2/2}.                            (1)
Start at X_0=t_0 alpha_0 zeta_0. One may impose t_0 alpha_0=1 for exact initial normalization; otherwise divide the final payoff by(t_0 alpha_0)^2.
The function f is convex differentiable1-smooth, minimized at0 with value0. These facts follow directly from the Moreau minimization of the finite nonnegative convex J. In particular, if plane i is active at z=x-omega_i, then the subgradient optimality condition gives grad f(x)=omega_i and
f(x)=<omega_i,x>-||omega_i||^2/2-eta_i.                   (2)
This directly realizes all values; no interpolation theorem is being invoked as an unverified black box.

Define the intended block-start points by
X_(i+1)=X_i-(s_i+b_i)omega_i.
Set Z_i=X_i-t_i omega_i.
The following three EXPLICIT conditions are sufficient for every intended gradient and hence the exact supplied GD trajectory:
(a) D_i^M:=B_i^M(1-(r_i^M)^2) satisfies
    D_i^M>=|r_i^M|D_(i+1)^M,0<=i<m-1;                  (3)
(b) C_ij alpha_j<=alpha_i for every distinct i,j;      (4)
(c) with delta_i=t_i-s_i-1>=0,
    0<=eta_i<=delta_i alpha_i^2,
    eta_i-eta_j<=delta_i alpha_i(alpha_i-C_ij alpha_j)
    for every distinct i,j.                           (5)

These are conditions on a specified frame and specified affine offsets. Their feasibility at the target scale for arbitrary schedules is NOT assumed.

## 2. Why the entire history is retained
The defining r_i^M gives the exact recurrence
Z_0=0,
Z_(i+1)=Z_i-B_i^M(zeta_i-r_i^M zeta_(i+1)).              (6)
A direct induction using the Markov products gives
<Z_i,zeta_j>=0 for j>=i,
and, for j<i,
<Z_i,zeta_j>=-sum_(l=j)^(i-1)D_l^M C_(j,l).             (7)
To verify(7), note that for j<=i,
<zeta_i-r_i^M zeta_(i+1),zeta_j>
=(1-(r_i^M)^2)C_(j,i),
whereas it is zero for j>=i+1.

The sum on the right of(7) is nonnegative. Pair its terms at l=j,j+1, then j+2,j+3, and so on. Each even-position product C_(j,l) is positive, and the paired contribution equals
C_(j,l)[D_l^M-|r_l^M|D_(l+1)^M]>=0
by(3). A possible final unpaired term is positive. Thus
<Z_i,omega_j><=0 for j<i,
<Z_i,omega_j> = 0 for j>=i.                             (8)
No old plane has been discarded.

At a query in block i let u be the partial ordinary step sum already used, so0<=u<=s_i. The intended point is x=X_i-u omega_i, and
z=x-omega_i=Z_i+d omega_i,
d=t_i-u-1>=delta_i.
The current plane has value d alpha_i^2-eta_i>=0.
By(8), any other plane has value at most
d alpha_i C_ij alpha_j-eta_j.
Its difference from the current plane is therefore at least
d alpha_i(alpha_i-C_ij alpha_j)-eta_i+eta_j.
Condition(4) allows replacing d by its lower bound delta_i; then(5) proves nonnegativity. Consequently plane i dominates all other planes and the zero plane at every query. Formula(2) gives exactly the intended constant gradient throughout block_i, including its checkpoint query. The actual update reaches X_(i+1), completing the induction. Repeated points from zero steps cause no difficulty.

The terminal ordinary block follows omega_m in the same way. Its exact normalized value is
W_n(h)>=
 [2(t_m-s_m-1/2)alpha_m^2-2eta_m]/(t_0 alpha_0)^2.      (9)
Every sampled value is explicitly
f(X_i-u omega_i)=(t_i-u-1/2)alpha_i^2-eta_i.
The construction is a finite-dimensional global function, so every cross-time smooth-convex interpolation inequality and the optimum sample are satisfied.

The amplitude recursion can be written
alpha_(i+1)=|r_i^M| (s_i+b_i-t_i)alpha_i/t_(i+1).          (10)
This displays the multiplicative transfer and its actual losses. The history price is(3)--(5), not an omitted all-schedule comparison.

## 4. Nonseparability and the quadratic comparison
The gradients omega_0,...,omega_m are linearly independent by their nonzero fresh coordinates in the Markov recursion. Adjacent inner products are
<omega_i,omega_(i+1)>=alpha_i alpha_(i+1)r_i^M,
which are nonzero. The global function is not separable in any fixed orthonormal basis: isolation of the face between the zero plane and each plane i yields open smooth regions whose Hessians are the distinct rank-one projectors onto span(omega_i). The normals are independent and adjacent ones are nonorthogonal, so these projectors do not commute. Simultaneous diagonal Hessians would be necessary for orthogonal separability.

For the same schedules, the full quadratic-class normalized error tends uniformly to0. On a scalar eigenvalue lambda in[0,1], each block multiplier is
(1-lambda)(1-b_i lambda).
For0<=lambda<=1/5 it is nonnegative and at most exp(-lambda). On[1/5,1] its absolute value is at most4/5, because b_i belongs to[4,17/4], a subset of[4,5], and the negative minimum magnitude(b_i-1)^2/(4b_i) is at most4/5 while the positive endpoint bound is4/25. Therefore the final normalized quadratic error is at most
max{1/(2 e m),(4/5)^(2m)}.
Spectral diagonalization proves the same statement in every finite dimension. This does not claim that all scalar nonlinear instances are controlled; it isolates a real nonlinear, nonseparable adversary with an explicit full-history certificate.
