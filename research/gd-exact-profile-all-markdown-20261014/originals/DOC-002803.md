## 3. Exact child assumptions and the joint reserve

The following analytical setup is conditional on an **exact** child
certificate. The numerical candidates above are not silently substituted
for that hypothesis.

Let a child word have `t` steps `h_0,...,h_(t-1)>0`, mass `s`, capacity
`d=1+2s`, and clocks

\[
 t_i=1+\sum_{k<i}h_k,\qquad 0\le i\le t.
\]

The terminal node `t` has no physical child step. Let `C_ij>=0`, `i!=j`,
be all child physical-flow coefficients, and let
`pi_i=w_i/2>=0`, `sum_i pi_i=1`, be its normalized absorption weights.
Set `z=sum_i pi_i g_i`. In the original orientation

\[
 Q_{ij}=\langle x_j-g_j,g_i-g_j\rangle\le0,
\]

an exact child certificate has

\[
 K=2\langle x_{\rm in},z\rangle-d\|z\|^2-U_1(x_{\rm out})
       +\sum_{i,j\le t}C_{ij}Q_{ij}\succeq0.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Here `K` is independent of the formal `x_in` and invariant under a common
translation of all gradients. Its physical flow identities are

\[
 \operatorname{div}_i C=2\mathbf1_{i=t}-2\pi_i,
\tag{3.2}
\]
\[
 \sum_jC_{ij}(t_j-t_i)=2h_i-2\pi_i(d-t_i),
 \quad h_t:=0.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Equation (3.3) includes the terminal row: its value is `-2pi_t s`.
These are obtained by removing absorption edges from the full divergence
`2(e_t-e_p)` and clock drift `2h_i` at preterminal nodes, zero at `t,p`.

Subtract the terminal gradient and write

\[
 y_i=g_i-g_t\quad(i<t),\qquad
 A=\sum_{i<t}h_i y_i,\qquad B=\sum_{i<t}\pi_i y_i=z-g_t.
\tag{3.4}
\]

If the exact quotient matrix `K_mat` in these coordinates is positive
definite and `F=[h/s,pi_physical]` has two independent columns, define

\[
 T=(F^T K_{\rm mat}^{-1}F)^{-1}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

For each scalar coordinate, with `v=F^T y=(A/s,B)^T`, completion of a
square gives the exact identity

\[
 y^TK_{\rm mat}y
 =v^TTv+
 (y-K_{\rm mat}^{-1}FTv)^TK_{\rm mat}
 (y-K_{\rm mat}^{-1}FTv).
\tag{3.6}
\]

The cross term cancels because `F^T K_mat^{-1}F=T^{-1}`. Summing over
Euclidean coordinates proves the vector-feature identity. Both the joint
mixed term in `v^TTv` and the **entire remaining nonnegative quadratic**
are retained. If the hypotheses of (3.5) fail, this particular inverse
formula is unavailable; no rank assumption or pseudoinverse is inserted.

The saved extraction used the raw p Schur complement in place of an exact
`K_mat`, so its matrices remain numerical diagnostics only.

\[

\tag{4.1}

\[
 F_{ij}=\mu_{i_L,j_R},\qquad
 E_{ji}=\mu_{j_R,i_L},\qquad 0\le i,j\le t.
\tag{4.2}

\[

 w_j^R=2c\pi_j+2(1-c)\mathbf1_{j=t}

## 5. Law 1: dense absorption-product coupling

For arbitrary scalars `a,c,eta,kappa>=0`, use the complete inherited child
arrays and set, for every cross-child pair,

\[
 F_{ij}=\eta\pi_i\pi_j,\qquad
 E_{ji}=\kappa\pi_j\pi_i.
\tag{5.1}
\]

All pairs are present in the law; no numerical support threshold is used.
Its full absorption array from (4.3) is

\[
 w_i^L=(2a-\eta+\kappa)\pi_i-2a\mathbf1_{i=t},
\quad
 w_j^R=(2c+\eta-\kappa)\pi_j+2(1-c)\mathbf1_{j=t}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

These weights are not canonical. Even allowing all choices in (5.1),
the clock equations impose a rigid condition independent of the PSD
reserve. Define the probability-weighted local clock
`tau=sum_i pi_i t_i`. At any preterminal left node, the inherited,
cross, and absorption drifts respectively are

\[
 a[2h_i-2\pi_i(d-t_i)],\quad
 \eta\pi_i(\ell+\tau-t_i),\quad
 (2a-\eta+\kappa)\pi_i(M-t_i).
\]

Their sum must equal `2h_i`. Hence

\[
 2(a-1)h_i+\pi_i(L-\kappa t_i)=0,
\quad
 L=-2ad+\eta(\ell+\tau)+(2a-\eta+\kappa)M.
\tag{5.3}
\]

For the matched preterminal right node the same calculation, now keeping
its actual global clock `ell+t_i`, gives

\[
 2(c-1)h_i+\pi_i(R-\eta t_i)=0,
\]
\[
 R=-2cd+\kappa(\tau-\ell)
       +(2c+\eta-\kappa)(M-\ell).
\tag{5.4}
\]

Suppose the positive vector `h_i/pi_i`, `i<t`, is not affine in `t_i`.
Equation (5.3) then forces `a=1`: otherwise it explicitly expresses that
vector as an affine function. With `a=1` and distinct preterminal clocks,
(5.3) forces `kappa=0` and `L=0`. Likewise (5.4) forces `c=1`, `eta=0`.
But these values give

\[
 L=2(M-d)=4\ell>0,
\tag{5.5}
\]

a contradiction. Thus this entire scalar-parameter law is impossible for
every such child, before checking root/terminal clocks or PSD.

## 6. Law 2: dense moment-dual coupling of the two joint features

After the first failure, the second law changes the destination profile
rather than dropping cross rows. Its purpose is to place the **linear
cross-memory moment** in the span of the terminal gradient and the actual
joint features `(A/s,B)`. The remaining quadratic terms are retained.

For `0<=theta<=1`, define a probability profile

\[
 q_i=\theta h_i/s+(1-\theta)\pi_i\quad(i<t),\qquad
 q_t=(1-\theta)\pi_t.
\tag{6.1}
\]

It represents exactly

\[
 Z_\theta=\sum_iq_i g_i
     =g_t+\theta A/s+(1-\theta)B.
\tag{6.2}
\]

The physical prefix operator is

\[
 D_j^{\rm local}=g_j+\sum_{k<j}h_kg_k.
\]

Choose its destination weights `v` by the exact moment-matching rule

\[

 \quad\hbox{for all formal child gradients}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

The operator is triangular with diagonal one, so this rule is unique:

\[
 v_t=q_t,\qquad
 v_i=q_i-h_i\sum_{j>i}v_j\quad(i<t).
\tag{6.4}
\]

This is an analytical definition, not a program or an executed recurrence
replay. Define all physical cross-child coefficients by

\[
 F_{ij}=\eta q_i v_j,\qquad
 E_{ji}=\kappa q_j v_i,
\qquad \eta,\kappa\ge0,
\tag{6.5}
\]

retaining arbitrary nonnegative complete-child scales `a,c`. Parent
