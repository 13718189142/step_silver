# P5: a concrete consistent branching policy
Status: complete support construction and two quantitative tests; unverified. No new verification allocation requested. The exact-profile comparison remains open.

## 1. Scalar interpolation state

Normalize L=1, choose minimizer0 and initial point1. A history H is a finite set of gradient knots (y,v), always including(0,0), satisfying
0 <= (v-w)/(y-z) <=1 whenever y>z, and v=w when y=z.
Define
ell_H(x)=max_{(y,v) in H} [v-(y-x)_+],
u_H(x)=min_{(y,v) in H} [v+(x-y)_+].
Every monotone1-Lipschitz derivative interpolating H lies between these envelopes. Conversely both envelopes are global monotone1-Lipschitz functions interpolating H: max/min preserve nondecreasingness and the upper bound1 on increments, and the pairwise history conditions force equality at each stored knot. In particular they agree at0 with value0. Thus ell_H<=u_H. Adding a knot(x,g) preserves the history conditions exactly when ell_H(x)<=g<=u_H(x).

For an endpoint x, the largest normalized objective among all convex1-smooth functions whose derivatives interpolate H and whose value at0 is0 is
Phi(H,x)=2 integral_0^x u_H(z) dz if x>=0,
Phi(H,x)=-2 integral_x^0 ell_H(z) dz if x<=0.
For the first case take derivative u_H globally, and for the second take derivative ell_H globally. These derivatives are continuous, monotone and1-Lipschitz, vanish at0, and integrate to globally convex1-smooth functions minimized at0. The envelopes bound every possible derivative pointwise, which proves maximality with the appropriate integration orientation. The chosen objective therefore reproduces every stored gradient. It is piecewise quadratic with finitely many pieces and lives in dimension1.

This is a derivative-oracle construction: no function values were supplied to GD and none need to be preserved except the final chosen value. The whole h is fixed first. An adversary's random choices construct a function afterward whose exact gradient trajectory is that path; they do not change the GD steps adaptively.

## 2. Actual policy, not optimization over all gradient choices

Fix h=(h_0,...,h_{n-1}) with n>=1 and arbitrary finite nonnegative entries. For each j in{0,...,n-1}, put P_j=sum_{s<j}h_s and c=1/(1+P_j). Policy D_j first forces g_i=c for0<=i<=j. Its points are x_i=1-c sum_{s<i}h_s>=c for i<=j, so these knots are legal with(0,0). After step j,
x_{j+1}=c(1-h_j).
For every t=j+1,...,n-1 let S_t=sum_{s=t}^{n-1}h_s, let H_t be ALL prior knots together with(0,0), and compute
v_t = u_{H_t}(x_t) if x_t>=0, and ell_{H_t}(x_t) if x_t<0,
w_t = projection of x_t/(1+2S_t) onto [ell_{H_t}(x_t),u_{H_t}(x_t)].
With probability1/2 choose g_t=v_t; otherwise choose g_t=w_t. Coins at successive stages are independent. Add(x_t,g_t), then take the fixed GD step x_{t+1}=x_t-h_t g_t. Repeated points, zero steps and x_t=0 are harmless because their feasible intervals force the stored gradient.

After the final step select the global objective attaining Phi(H_n,x_n). This is a finite law on at most2^{n-j-1} legal functions, each with initial squared distance1. The expected normalized error D_j(h) has the exact recurrence
E_n(H,x)=Phi(H,x),
E_t(H,x)=(1/2)E_{t+1}(H union{(x,v)},x-h_t v)
        +(1/2)E_{t+1}(H union{(x,w)},x-h_t w),
with v,w defined by the displayed policy at state(H,x,t).
D_j(h) is E_{j+1} evaluated after its forced prefix. This recurrence is the payoff of two specified actions, not a supremum over all feasible gradients. It is an exact symbolic specification; no exponential enumeration has been run.

Each selected function is legal path by path. Thus W_n(h)>=D_j(h) for every j. Combined with the verified global/single-cut functional K_n from fact b98bb807d514e80d,
W_n(h)>=B_n^branch(h):=max{K_n(h), max_{0<=j<n}D_j(h)}.
One may choose the j with largest expectation after seeing h, or choose a point mass on a sufficiently good leaf. No infimum/expectation interchange is used. This notation B_n^branch is distinct from the benchmark denominator B_n.

## 3. Exact stopping event recovers the selected-cut value

Suppose h_j>1 and j<n-1. Write R=c(h_j-1)>0 and T=S_{j+1}. The first point on the negative half-line is -R, with no negative gradient knots yet. Therefore the feasible interval is[-R,0]. On its w-branch, the chosen gradient is -a, where a=R/(1+2T).
All later points remain <=-a, because total remaining displacement is aT and -R+aT=-a(1+T)<=-a.
The steep envelope at these points is -a. The later tail proposal is at most -a: if s is the tail sum already used, the ratio of its magnitude to a is
(1+2T-s)/(1+2T-2s)>=1.
Thus clamping selects -a. Both actions hence coincide thereafter, so this branch exactly follows an asymmetric Huber tail.
The terminal envelope on the negative half-line is max(z,-a); hence its normalized objective is
R^2/(1+2T)=(h_j-1)^2/[(1+P_j)^2(1+2T)].
The event has probability1/2, giving D_j(h) at least half of that cut term. At j=n-1 there is no coin; the maximal endpoint value is R^2, exactly the cut term with T=0.

This confirms normalization and chronology, but the half-cut bound alone is only a check: K_n already retains the full cut maximum. The benefit sought from D_j is in branches that continue through multiple sign changes and record excursions.

## 4. Quantitative multi-excursion test on the linear schedules

Take n>=4 and h_t=t+1. Choose j=2, so P_j=1+2=3 and c=1/4. The forced prefix has
x_0=1, x_1=3/4, x_2=1/4, x_3=-1/2.
On the event that every later coin takes the steep action, the trajectory is exactly GD on the function with derivative
q(z)=z for z<=1/4,
q(z)=1/4 for1/4<=z<=1,
q(z)=z-3/4 forz>=1.
This is a single global monotone1-Lipschitz derivative. Its derivative values equal the selected extremal envelope at every new query; adding such a knot does not change the pertinent extremal envelope. Positive knots cannot tighten the negative lower envelope beyond z, and negative knots on q(z)=z cannot tighten the positive upper envelope below min(z,1/4+(z-1)_+). Thus induction proves this entire event, not only its initial portion.
In particular x_4=3/2. Thereafter signs alternate and |x_t|>=3/2. For negative x_t the next magnitude is t|x_t|. For positive x_t, t>=4,
x_{t+1}=-t x_t+(3/4)(t+1)<0,
and
|x_{t+1}|>=((t-1)/2)|x_t|>=(t/4)|x_t|,
using |x_t|>=3/2. The last inequality and t>=4 preserve the lower bound3/2.
Therefore
|x_n| >= (3/2) product_{t=4}^{n-1}(t/4).
The chosen final envelope has objective at least that of the displayed global q. Integrating q shows 2f(z)=z^2 for negative z; for positive z>=3/2 it is (z-3/4)^2+3/8>=z^2/4. Hence every terminal point on this event satisfies
2f(x_n) >= (9/16) [product_{t=4}^{n-1}(t/4)]^2.
There are n-3 fair coins after the forced prefix, so
D_2(h) >= 2^{-(n-3)}(9/16)[product_{t=4}^{n-1}(t/4)]^2. (L)
The right side diverges faster than any fixed power of n. For example, from t>=8 each factor in the product is at least2, and the factorial expression gives the full growth:
product_{t=4}^{n-1}(t/4)=(n-1)!/[6*4^{n-4}].
This analytic event calculation uses no numerical sweep or path enumeration.
By contrast, accepted fact b98bb807d514e80d proves K_n(h)<=16/n^2 for the same linear schedules. Thus this actual branching policy detects objective mass missed by every single-cut term on that family. It is a mechanism advance, not an all-schedule lower bound.

## 5. Guarded-long-step test

For h=(1,H,1), H>1, choose j=1. Then c=1/2 and the forced points are1,1/2,-(H-1)/2. On the final interior branch, probability1/2, the gradient is x_2/3 and the envelope is a left Huber tail. Its normalized error is (H-1)^2/12. Consequently
D_1(1,H,1)>=(H-1)^2/24.
This avoids the fixed-one-curvature annihilation that affected the previous two-curvature subclass. It does not imply any bound for arbitrary schedules by itself.

## 6. Remaining obligation and next adversarial tests

No proof presently compares B_n^branch(h) uniformly to U_n^ZJ. The statement
for every epsilon>0 there exists N_epsilon independent of h such that
B_n^branch(h)>=(1-epsilon)U_n^ZJ for all n>=N_epsilon and h>=0
would by itself solve the affirmative direction; it cannot be called a support lemma.
The concrete next test is whether the fair-coin continuation loses nonvanishing mass on recursive schedules near the ZJ sum threshold, even when B_n^branch includes the exact K_n fallback. If it does, change the continuation probabilities or replace the scalar geometry, not merely append another restricted-family no-go. The successful linear/guarded tests do not establish that the comparison is true.
