## 1. Result, normalization, and exact scope

For the precise full-depth geometry defined below, the proposed result is:
for every integer m>=1536 and EVERY initializer realizing the prescribed
original vertex itinerary, its actual terminal closest-minimum score obeys

    2 Phi(z_n) / dist(z_0,argmin Phi)^2
       < 343/(333 D_m) < 17/(16 D_m),
    D_m=1+2 sum_(k<n) h_k.                              (1)

There is no existence assertion for a legal initializer. The estimate
already holds in the relaxation retaining only the ORIGINAL vertices

    i=2^(m-2)-1=N/4-1,   n=N-1,

both ordered normals between them, and their two zero-vertex normals.
Their Gram matrix AND chronological prefix constants are inherited from
the complete original trajectory, not recomputed on a two-step word.
A single positive multiplier, on the original orientation i->n, supplies
the certificate. All other pair and zero constraints remain present but
have multiplier zero. The proof retains the negative cross inner product,
both radial factors, and every tiny Walsh character.

This bounds one fixed body WITH one prescribed itinerary. It is neither
an upper bound for every trajectory on that body nor a full-function upper
bound for H_m. It cannot be combined with a benchmark comparison to claim
minimax separation. The original W=2R normalization is the factor 2 in (1).
For advertised smoothness L>0, use L Phi and steps h_k/L; the same points
and score result.

The all-initializer perspective interface and its weak dual are also derived
below. There is a necessary qualification to the proposed interface: its
zero point is feasible even when the legal-initializer set is empty.
Therefore literal equality to a supremum over legal initializers requires
nonemptiness, or the convention of adjoining the zero score. No conic
strong duality or unproved feasibility is used.

One mechanism suffices. The conditional three-vertex mechanism is not
opened. A relative-potential change cannot escape (1) while satisfying all
frozen original rows; general geometry remains open.

## 2. Frozen objects and complete Gram kernel

Let m>=1536 be an integer and put

    N=2^m, n=N-1, epsilon=1/(4m), beta=1-epsilon,
    c=(1+sqrt(2))(1+epsilon), a=beta/c,
    H_l=1+a c^l, h_k=H_(nu_2(k+1)), 0<=k<n.

Here nu_2 is the exponent of 2 in a positive integer. H_l denotes a
single level-l physical step, whereas H_m in the task denotes the entire
recursively concatenated word. Define its depth-j sum, with the SAME m,

    S_j=sum_(k<2^j-1) H_(nu_2(k+1))
       =a c^j/(c-2)+(1-a/(c-2))2^j-1.                 (2)

At each level l<j there are 2^(j-l-1) steps; summing their 1 and a c^l
parts as finite geometric series proves (2). No recurrence is executed.

Set Kmix=N-1-m and eta=1/(N^2 Kmix). For each nonzero binary character
b in F_2^m, chi_b(u)=(-1)^(b dot u). Let b^(k)=N-2^k, 0<=k<m.
Assign positive character amplitudes omega_b by

    omega_(b^(k))^2=2^k/(N-1),
    omega_b^2=eta for every other nonzero b.

For each binary label u put

    Wframe(u)=((omega_b chi_b(u))_(b!=0),1/sqrt(3)),
    Gray(k)=k xor floor(k/2), w_k=Wframe(Gray(k)),
    T=4/3+1/N^2.

Xor and popcount mean the binary exclusive-or and number of 1 bits.
The character identity chi_(b^(l))(Gray(k))=(-1)^bit_l(k) follows by
cancelling the successive Gray bits from l through m-1. The sum of all
characters is N on equal labels and zero otherwise: pair labels in any
coordinate in which they differ. Subtracting the constant and distinguished
characters gives the EXACT kernel

    B_kl=<w_k,w_l>
      =1/3-eta+sum_(j<m)(2^j/(N-1)-eta)
                       (-1)^(bit_j(k)+bit_j(l))
                     +eta N 1_(k=l)
      =T-2(k xor l)/(N-1)+2eta popcount(k xor l)
                     -eta N+eta N 1_(k=l).           (3)

In particular B_kk=T. The mixed characters are present in every formula.

The exact adjacent level energy and inner product are

    E_l=(2^(l+1)-1)/(N-1)+eta(N/2-l-1),
    B_l^adj=T-2E_l.

Let rho_l be the positive root of

    rho_l^2+(H_l-2)(B_l^adj/T)rho_l+1-H_l=0.

These are the prescribed diagnostic roots. Put

    x=r_0=rho_0(1-4/N), y=r_1=rho_1(1-4/N),
    r_l=1 for l>=2,
    sigma_0=1, sigma_(k+1)=sigma_k r_(nu_2(k+1)),
    v_k=sigma_k w_k, D0=conv{0,v_0,...,v_n}.          (4)

No high-level root replaces r_l=1. Exact low-level counts give

    sigma_k=x^ceil(k/2)y^floor((k+1)/4).             (5)

All character amplitudes and all sigma_k are positive. The full Walsh
matrix, including its constant vertical row, is invertible by the same
orthogonality identity. Thus the N vectors v_k are a basis of R^N. Let V
be the matrix with these vectors as columns and G=V^T V, so G is positive
definite. These independence statements do not omit small characters.

## 3. Legal potentials, actual values, and the complete minimum set

Define

    P_k=sum_(l<k)h_l v_l, A_kl=<P_k,v_l>,
    C_kl=A_kl-A_kk+G_kl-G_kk,
    K_k=A_kk+G_kk, c_n=A_nn+G_nn/2.

For an initializer z_0, write fpot_k=<z_0,v_k>. These are inner-product
potentials, NOT sampled function values. Conversely every real vector
fpot is realized uniquely by

    z_0=V G^(-1) fpot.                              (6)

Consider the fixed function

    Phi(z)=max_(v in D0){<z,v>-||v||^2/2}.

Completing the square gives the unique maximizer projection_D0(z).
A proposed maximizer v satisfies

    <z-v,w-v><=0 for every w in D0.                 (7)

Necessity follows by differentiating squared distance along a segment
from v to w. Conversely expanding ||z-w||^2 shows (7) makes v the unique
nearest point. It suffices to check generating vertices, by linearity.
Adding (7) for two projections proves their firm nonexpansiveness and
hence their 1-Lipschitz property. The two affine maxima bound the remainder
Phi(z')-Phi(z)-<projection_D0(z),z'-z> between 0 and ||z'-z||^2.
Thus Phi is finite, convex, differentiable and 1-smooth, with gradient
projection_D0. These elementary facts also appear in accepted fact
`1e0bf3f41457d8e4`, Part A Section 2; no stronger assertion from that fact
is required here.

At the prescribed points z_k=z_0-P_k, (7) is EXACTLY

    fpot_l-fpot_k<=C_kl for every original ordered pair k,l,
    fpot_k>=K_k for every original k.               (8)

For the second line use w=0. Therefore (8) is both necessary and sufficient
for all v_k, including the terminal v_n, to be the actual projections.
Under (8), z_(k+1)=z_k-h_k grad Phi(z_k) for all k<n, and

    Phi(z_k)=fpot_k-A_kk-G_kk/2,
    Phi(z_n)=fpot_n-c_n>=G_nn/2>0.                 (9)

There is no physical h_n. The complete minimum set is

    Kpolar=cone{v_k}^polar={z:V^T z<=0}, min Phi=0.  (10)

A polar point makes all defining expressions nonpositive, while v=0
supplies zero. If <z,v_k>>0, a sufficiently small positive multiple of
v_k belongs to D0 and has positive defining expression; this proves the
converse. This is the full polar cone, not a selected vertical slice.

## 4. Exact closest-minimum geometry, perspective, and zero boundary

For ANY fpot, let z_0 be (6) and write Rtrue=dist(z_0,Kpolar). Changing
variables d=V^T(z_0-zstar) in the distance minimization gives

    Rtrue^2=r(fpot):=min_(d>=fpot) d^T G^(-1)d.       (11)

Indeed zstar is polar iff V^T zstar=fpot-d<=0, and the vector difference
is V G^(-1)d with precisely that squared norm. The positive definite
quadratic is coercive, so its minimum over the closed nonempty orthant
translate exists and is unique.

For completeness its geometry follows without a duality theorem. At its
minimizer d*, let alpha=G^(-1)d*. Coordinate variations give alpha_k>=0;
if d*_k>fpot_k, both variation signs are allowed and alpha_k=0. Thus

    alpha>=0, d*>=fpot, alpha_k(d*_k-fpot_k)=0.

Put p=V alpha and zstar=z_0-p. Then p belongs to cone{v_k}, zstar belongs
to its polar, and <p,zstar>=0. For any polar y,

    ||z_0-y||^2=||p||^2+||zstar-y||^2-2<p,y>
               >=||p||^2.

This proves the full closest-minimum assertion directly. In particular a
legal fpot has positive radius: fpot_0>=K_0=G_00>0 excludes polarity.

Consider the finite convex program

    maximize 2F_n-2tau c_n
    tau>=0,
    F_l-F_k<=tau C_kl for ALL original ordered pairs,
    F_k>=tau K_k,
    d>=F, d^T G^(-1)d<=tau.                         (12)

The variables F are rescaled potentials. Convexity means the feasible set
is convex and the maximand is linear. There is no solver use here.

Forward direction: for legal fpot set tau=1/r(fpot), F=tau fpot and
choose d=tau d*. Then d^T G^(-1)d=tau and the objective is exactly
2(fpot_n-c_n)/r(fpot).

Reverse direction: given a feasible point with tau>0, fpot=F/tau obeys
ALL of (8), has the unique actual realization (6), and

    r(fpot)<=d^T G^(-1)d/tau^2<=1/tau.

By (9) its terminal numerator is strictly positive. Hence its actual
score is AT LEAST the value of (12). Pointwise equality is not asserted
when the supplied quadratic inequality is slack. At an optimum the two
suprema nonetheless coincide by the two inequalities just proved.

Zero boundary: tau=0 forces d=0 by positive definiteness. The inequalities
d>=F and F>=0 force F=0, so the objective is zero. This point always
exists, independently of itinerary feasibility. Thus (12) equals

    sup({0} union {actual scores of legal initializers}).              (13)

If a legal initializer exists, its score is positive and (13) is exactly
the proposed supremum. If none exists, (12) has only its zero point, while
the conventional supremum of the empty legal set is not zero. Equivalently,
positive-tau feasibility is precisely legal-initializer feasibility.
This qualification matters because full itinerary feasibility is not proved.

The maximum in (12) exists. Since K_0=G_00, feasibility gives
F_0>=tau G_00, d_0>=F_0, and

d^T G^(-1)d>=d_0^2/G_00>=tau^2 G_00.

The first inequality follows by applying Cauchy--Schwarz to
V G^(-1)d and v_0. Consequently tau<=1/G_00 when tau>0. The quadratic
constraint bounds d. The lower bounds F_k>=tau K_k and upper bounds F<=d
then bound F. The feasible set is closed and bounded. If its maximum is
positive, a positive-tau maximizer recovers an actual maximizer via (6).

## 5. Weak-dual signs and complete-square certificate

Let lambda_kl>=0 and nu_k>=0 be any fixed multipliers, and define

    rho=2e_n+out(lambda)-in(lambda)+nu,
    kappa=2c_n-sum lambda_kl C_kl+sum nu_k K_k.

Here e_n is the coordinate vector, out_k=sum_l lambda_kl and
in_k=sum_l lambda_lk. Suppose rho>=0 componentwise and kappa>0.
For every legal potential,

    rho^T fpot-kappa
       =2(fpot_n-c_n)
        +sum lambda_kl [C_kl-(fpot_l-fpot_k)]
        +sum nu_k(fpot_k-K_k)
       >=2(fpot_n-c_n).                             (14)

This explicitly fixes the orientation and both signs. For its closest
polar point zstar, g=V rho belongs to cone{v_k}, so

    rho^T fpot=<z_0,g>
       <=<z_0-zstar,g><=Rtrue ||g||.

Thus, writing b=||g|| and u=1/Rtrue,

    2(fpot_n-c_n)/Rtrue^2
       <=b u-kappa u^2
        =b^2/(4kappa)-kappa(u-b/(2kappa))^2
       <=rho^T G rho/(4kappa).                     (15)

Alternatively, in (12), (14) becomes objective<=rho^T F-kappa tau,
which is <=rho^T d-kappa tau<=b sqrt(tau)-kappa tau; complete the square
in sqrt(tau). This also includes tau=0. No strong-duality assertion is
needed. These multipliers belong to itinerary normals, not the earlier
original smooth-convex GD interpolation dual.
