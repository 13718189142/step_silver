# Exact tests of two terminal-game closures

Status: self-contained UNVERIFIED candidate counterexamples. No verifier request
is allocated. Only fact 1252b8823bb5a290 is used as a mathematical predecessor;
the new minimax candidate 40601f4c6f496ab8 is not a premise.

## 1. Three anchors are necessary even after optimizing the final step

The claim tested here is that, for every legal fixed prefix, its exact optimized
terminal potential can be evaluated with a mixture supported on at most TWO
prefix anchors, counting the optimum anchor. This claim is false already for
one queried GD step in dimension three. The example keeps both orientations of
every queried-prefix interpolation constraint.

Work with L=1 in R^3. Use the optimum jet (0,0,0), the positive first step
h_0=2, and the following prefix:

    x_0=(8,9,3),  g_0=(0,2,1),  f_0=19/2,
    x_1=(8,5,1),  g_1=(1,0,0),  f_1=11/2.

Thus x_1=x_0-2g_0 and ||x_0||^2=154. The proximal points and offsets are

    y_0=x_0-g_0=(8,7,2),    delta_0=f_0-||g_0||^2/2=7,
    y_1=x_1-g_1=(7,5,1),    delta_1=f_1-||g_1||^2/2=5.

Using the signed Q from the predecessor, ALL six prefix residuals are

    Q_(0,star)=-9,   Q_(star,0)=-7,
    Q_(1,star)=-2,   Q_(star,1)=-5,
    Q_(0,1)=-3,      Q_(1,0)=-1.                         (1)

For example the identity Q_ij=delta_i-delta_j+<g_i,y_j-y_i>
gives Q_(0,1)=2-<(0,2,1),(1,2,1)>=-3. Hence the predecessor constructs
the pointwise maximal legal prefix interpolant as the conjugate of

    H(v)=||v||^2/2+
             max{0, <(8,7,2),v>-7, <(7,5,1),v>-5}.       (2)

Call it F. It is one fixed everywhere-finite convex differentiable 1-smooth
function, with minimum F(0)=0, and it interpolates exactly the jets in (1).
For any nonnegative final step b, its terminal point is

    x_2(b)=(8-b,5,1).

Put v_*=(0,1,0). All three affine planes inside the maximum in (2) are
zero at v_*. Thus H(v_*)=1/2, and the conjugate definition gives

    F(x_2(b)) >= <x_2(b),v_*>-H(v_*)=9/2
                         for EVERY b>=0.                   (3)

At b=3, let alpha_star=alpha_0=alpha_1=1/3. The weighted affine bound on
H and completion of a square give

    F(x_2(3))
      <= (7+5)/3 + (1/2)||x_2(3)-(y_0+y_1)/3||^2
       =4+(1/2)||(0,1,0)||^2=9/2.                         (4)

Consequently the exact final-line-search value of this legal fixed prefix is

    min_(b>=0) 2F(x_2(b))/||x_0||^2 = 9/154.               (5)

The minimizing step and mixture are UNIQUE, and all three weights are
strictly positive. Here are two direct ways to check the asserted necessity.

First, equality in (3) makes v_* the unique conjugate maximizer. The
subgradient condition for (2) then requires

    x_2(b)-v_* = alpha_0 y_0+alpha_1 y_1,
    alpha_0,alpha_1>=0,  alpha_0+alpha_1<=1.               (6)

The subgradient condition follows by separating from the convex hull of
the active plane slopes: a point outside that hull gives a direction in
which <x,v>-H(v) increases. Thus it uses only the finite maximum in (2).
The third and second coordinates of (6) read

    2alpha_0+alpha_1=1,
    7alpha_0+5alpha_1=4.

Their unique solution is alpha_0=alpha_1=1/3. The first coordinate then
gives b=3. The remaining weight on the optimum is 1/3 as well.

Second, the full quadratic mixture expression can be checked directly:

    J(b,alpha_0,alpha_1)
      =2(7alpha_0+5alpha_1)
        +(8-b-8alpha_0-7alpha_1)^2
        +(5-7alpha_0-5alpha_1)^2
        +(1-2alpha_0-alpha_1)^2.                          (7)

It is the upper bound on 2F supplied by the weighted conjugate quadratics.
At (b,alpha_0,alpha_1)=(3,1/3,1/3), its value is 9 and all three derivatives
are zero. Its Hessian is positive definite because the three vectors
g_1,y_0,y_1 are independent; their determinant has absolute value three.
Thus the minimizer of (7) on b>=0 and the simplex is unique. Equations
(3)-(4) show that this bound is exact at the minimizing point, independently
of any general hull or minimax theorem.

## 2. Exact loss from using at most two anchors

The failure has an explicit quantitative size. For every feasible mixture,
the best b in (7) is

    b=8-8alpha_0-7alpha_1>=0.

After optimizing it, set

    Y=(7alpha_0+5alpha_1, 2alpha_0+alpha_1).

The remaining expression is precisely

    9+||Y-(4,1)||^2.                                    (8)

The simplex maps bijectively to the triangle with vertices
O=(0,0), A=(7,2), B=(5,1), and the unconstrained target (4,1) has the
three barycentric weights 1/3. A support of size at most two places Y on
the boundary of this triangle. Its distances to the three edges are:

* To [O,A], squared distance 1/53. The projection coefficient is 30/53,
  lying strictly between zero and one; the determinant is 4*2-1*7=1.
* To [O,B], squared distance 1/26. The projection coefficient is 21/26;
  the determinant is 4*1-1*5=-1.
* To [B,A], the projection coefficient from B is -2/5, so the nearest
  point of that segment is B and the squared distance is one.

Therefore optimizing BOTH the last step and all mixtures of support at
most two gives J=9+1/53, strictly larger than its exact value 9. In the
original normalized objective this is

    9/154 + 1/8162,                                     (9)

whereas the true value is 9/154. No count of active anchors in this
example is inferred from a numerical solution.

This refutes a universal two-anchor formula for the fixed-prefix terminal
game even when the final step is optimized. It does NOT assert that this
particular prefix maximizes the full PEP at h=(2,3), and it does NOT refute
the distinct, unproved statement that some globally worst prefix always
admits a smaller terminal support. The full selected certificate of an
entire PEP is also a different object from the fixed-prefix alpha here.

## 3. The whole schedule value is not jointly convex

For EVERY nonnegative schedule all of whose steps satisfy h_t>=2,

    W_n(h)=product_t(h_t-1)^2.                            (10)

Here is a complete elementary proof. Normalize L=1 and the minimizer to
zero. Adding the two interpolation inequalities between x and zero gives
<x,g>>=||g||^2, and smoothness gives ||g||<=||x||. Thus for h>=2,

    ||x-hg||^2
      =||x||^2-2h<x,g>+h^2||g||^2
      <=||x||^2+h(h-2)||g||^2
      <=(h-1)^2||x||^2.

Iterate this inequality and use 2f(x_n)<=||x_n||^2. This proves the upper
bound in (10) in every finite dimension. The scalar function f(x)=x^2/2
starting at x_0=1 has exactly that normalized error, proving equality.

In particular

    W_2(2,4)=W_2(4,2)=9,
    W_2(3,3)=16 > (9+9)/2.                              (11)

Thus the exact unrestricted value is not jointly convex in all step
coordinates, even on strictly positive schedules. This is a value-level
obstruction, stronger than exhibiting a nonconvex response on one fixed
function. It prevents replacing the proven final-step convexity by an
unqualified joint-convexity argument for all earlier steps. It does not
by itself disprove every possible all-step minimax identity.

## 4. Scope for continued proof search

The three-anchor obstruction is inside the full smooth-convex prefix class
and uses a finite, explicit legal function. It is not caused by dropping
incoming queried-prefix inequalities; every such inequality is strictly
satisfied in (1). It shows that any terminal state compression must retain
enough geometry to represent this triangle after projection along the
last gradient. One scalar value and an assumed pair of active anchors
cannot reproduce the fixed-prefix game.
