# Nonlinear failure of the two-copy ruler despite the quadratic tests

Status: source-based candidate, NOT verifier-accepted. No new solver or
fact_submit. The checkpoint application and projection conversion below
are derived afresh; earlier unverified C27 and C29 are not premises.
The external checkpoint construction is the one explicitly permitted in
PROBLEM.md, Section 7, with its source and applicability checked below.

## Statement

Put c=13/5, a=c^(-17), b_j=a c^j, H_0=(),
H_(j+1)=(H_j,b_j,H_j), and

    s_m=sum H_m=a(c^m-2^m)/(c-2),
    G_m=(H_m,H_m), L_m=2(2^m-1), S_m=2s_m.

There is one fixed integer r such that, for every m>=r+4, an actual
finite-dimensional convex differentiable 1-smooth function f with
f(0)=min f=0, started at a unit vector, satisfies on the whole G_m run

    2f(x_(L_m))
      >= (98/81)^(2^(m-r-2)) / [8(1+s_r)].           (1)

In particular the error grows without bound. For the intrinsic energy

    V_h=2sum_(i<n)h_i(f_i-f_n)
        +sum_(i<n)h_i(1-h_i)||g_i||^2
        -S(S+1)||g_n||^2,

the same instance has

    V_(G_m)
      <=1-[s_m/(4(1+s_r))](98/81)^(2^(m-r-2))<0.   (2)

Moreover, a negative-energy instance for this identical ordered schedule
can be realized as F_C(x)=(||x||^2-dist(x,C)^2)/2 on a simplex C with
L_m+2 vertices in dimension L_m+1. The sampled gradients are distinct
vertices, and every normal inequality against a different retained vertex,
including zero, is strict. This last conversion retains the sign of V;
it makes NO assertion preserving (1) after normalizing the new initial
distance.

Together with the independently proved, still unverified quadratic/cut
calculations in C29, this supplies an ACTUAL nonlinear rejection of that
test-safe family. The proof of (1)--(2) does not rely on C29. Neither this
example nor C29 decides primitive capacity for arbitrary schedules or
the original unrestricted Zhang--Jiang exact-profile question.

## 1. External construction, constants, and hypotheses

Source: Yuhan Ye and Kaizhao Liu, Silver Rate Is (Almost) Optimal for
Gradient Descent, arXiv:2609.09152v2, Section 3.1, Lemmas 3.1--3.2 and
the pre-weakening final-value calculation proving Lemma 2.2; proofs in
Appendix A.1--A.3. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09152v2 . The PDF
and extracted text are in downloads/ye-liu-2609.09152.{pdf,txt}. The
arc construction, projection proof, displacement bound, zero regions,
assembly, and terminal calculation were reread for this application.
A fresh Matlas checkpoint-transfer query returned unrelated fixed-step
results, none used. The direct source's identity and version were checked.

The exact input, also given in PROBLEM.md Section 7, is this. Fix positive
epsilon and gamma, and chi=sqrt(1+2gamma+epsilon^2). There are finite
constants A_YL and B_YL, depending only on these fixed parameters, such
that any nonnegative finite schedule with selected checkpoint sizes
z_i>=B_YL admits a finite-dimensional legal function, minimizer zero,
and initial point e_1, with

    2f(x_n)>=1/[2(1+T)]
               product_i [z_i/(chi(A_YL+u_i)+epsilon z_i)]^2. (3)

Here u_i is the sum after the preceding selected checkpoint and before
checkpoint i, and T is the sum after the last selected checkpoint.
The first u_i includes the whole initial ordinary block.

For completeness, the source's local functions are translated/scaled
parameter-one Moreau envelopes of finite support functions. Their
gradients are projections onto finite convex hulls. The backward arc
construction yields the prescribed trajectory for arbitrary intervening
nonnegative steps and a bounded second-coordinate displacement. For a
local checkpoint b>=1+epsilon^(-2), its first coordinate stops at U>=0,
and the function vanishes both when x<=0,y>=0 and when x=U,y>=Y.
Those regions keep every future and past component inactive during the
appropriate part of the concatenated trajectory. The global function is
half the sum of adjacent-coordinate local functions and a terminal
one-sided Huber function. Each coordinate occurs in at most two terms,
so the global smoothness bound is one. Its local steps are h/2, accounting
for B_YL=2(1+epsilon^(-2)). The initial point is e_1 and all activation
offsets are nonnegative, so zero is a common minimizer. There are one
more coordinates than selected checkpoints. If the final transferred
distance is D, the terminal Huber value is D^2/[4(1+T)], yielding (3)
after multiplication by two. No inference from the paper's notation
for its performance measure is used.

Choose epsilon=1/100 and gamma=2099/20000, so chi=11/10 and
B_YL=20002. Choose any permitted fixed finite A_YL. Then choose a
fixed integer r>=18 sufficiently large that

    b_r>=20002,  chi A_YL/b_r<=1/100.

In particular s_r>1. Indeed s_r/b_r>=13/8 for r>=17:
(10/13)^17<1/40 and (c-2)^(-1)=5/3 give this estimate.
All constants are fixed independently of m.

## 2. The one changed ordinary block at the copy boundary

Choose every step of level at least r in EACH copy of H_m. Put M=m-r.
Within each copy, every ordinary block between selected checkpoints is
exactly H_r. The initial and terminal ordinary blocks are also H_r.
At the junction of the two copies, the one intervening ordinary block
is (H_r,H_r), of sum 2s_r. There is exactly one such exceptional block.
The selected level-ell step b_r c^ell occurs 2^(M-ell-1) times in each
copy, for 0<=ell<M. The terminal ordinary sum of the full word is s_r.

For a usual ordinary block,

    chi(A_YL+s_r)/b_r
       <=1/100+(11/10)(5/3)=553/300<37/20.

Hence each transfer factor at level ell is at least

    q_ell=c^ell/[37/20+c^ell/100].

This increases with ell, and direct rational comparisons give

    q_0>=1/2, q_1>=4/3, q_2>=7/2, q_3>=8;
    q_ell>=1 for ell>=4.

When M>=4 the transfer product over one copy, using ordinary block sum
s_r throughout, is therefore at least

    [(1/2)^8(4/3)^4(7/2)^2 8]^(2^(M-4))
       =(98/81)^(2^(M-4)).                         (4)

For the exceptional block, at its actual following checkpoint z, the
ratio of the actual factor to the factor using ordinary sum s_r is

    [chi(A_YL+s_r)+epsilon z]
      /[chi(A_YL+2s_r)+epsilon z]>=1/2.              (5)

All summands are nonnegative, so the denominator is at most twice the
numerator. The full transfer product is thus at least

    (1/2)(98/81)^(2^(M-3)).

Substituting its square and T=s_r in (3) proves (1). All selected
checkpoints exceed B_YL. The special ordinary block is accepted by the
local construction with its actual doubled chronological step list;
no replacement of that list by a single artificial step is made.

## 3. From the objective blowup to a negative primitive energy

For any normalized legal run with minimizer zero and f(0)=0, smooth
convex interpolation (accepted fact 1252b8823bb5a290) gives

    I_i=<g_i,x_i>-f_i-||g_i||^2/2>=0.

Expanding ||x_(i+1)||^2=||x_i-h_i g_i||^2 and summing gives the exact
identity

    V_h=||x_0||^2-||x_n||^2-2sum_(i<n)h_i I_i
                      -2S f_n-S(S+1)||g_n||^2.

For nonnegative steps and S this is at most ||x_0||^2-2S f_n. Using
||x_0||=1, S=2s_m, and (1) proves the first inequality in (2).
Since s_m>=2^M s_r, M>=4, and s_r>1, its subtracted term is greater
than 4s_r/(1+s_r)>2. Thus (2) is strictly negative at every stated
horizon, not just eventually.

## 4. A finite strict simplex witness, with normalization loss visible

Here is the conversion in full, without invoking an unverified reduction.
Write n=L_m; every step of G_m is positive. Let the energy of the actual
nonlinear run above be -v<0. For ell=0,...,n choose
theta_ell=(ell+1)/(2(n+2)), let A_aux=diag(theta_ell), and run
psi(z)=<z,A_aux z>/2 from z_0=(1,...,1) under the same schedule.
The gradients w_i have components theta_ell P_i(theta_ell), where
P_i(t)=product_(j<i)(1-h_j t). Distinct degrees 0,...,n and n+1
distinct theta_ell imply that the w_i are linearly independent. They
also imply that the z_i are pairwise distinct. Since 0<A_aux<I,

    Q_ij^aux=-(1/2)<z_i-z_j,A_aux(I-A_aux)(z_i-z_j)><0, i!=j,

in the convention Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2.

Let E_aux be the finite auxiliary energy and put
delta=v/[2(v+|E_aux|)]. The actual product function
f_mix(x,z)=f(x)+delta psi(z/sqrt(delta)), initialized at
(x_0,sqrt(delta)z_0), has jets
(x_i,sqrt(delta)z_i), (g_i,sqrt(delta)w_i), f_i+delta psi_i.
It remains 1-smooth, its gradient vectors G_i are independent, all its
finite Q_ij are strict, and its energy is at most -v/2. Denote its
points and values by Z_i,F_i for the remainder of this section.

Choose f_* = min_i(F_i-||G_i||^2/2)-1. Independence makes the following
linear equations soluble for z_* in the span of the G_i:

    <G_i,z_*>=f_*-F_i+<G_i,Z_i>-||G_i||^2/2.

Set X_i=Z_i-z_*, V_i=F_i-f_*. Then
V_i=<G_i,X_i>-||G_i||^2/2 and

    <X_i-G_i,G_j-G_i>=Q_ji<0,  i!=j,
    <X_i-G_i,-G_i>=-V_i+||G_i||^2/2<=-1.

Consequently G_i is the projection of X_i onto
C=conv{0,G_0,...,G_n}: the displayed inequalities are the normal
condition against EVERY vertex, hence by convex combinations against
every point of C. Orthogonally project the X_i to the gradient span;
the GD equations, inner products, and all normal inequalities are
unchanged. That span has dimension n+1 and the listed n+2 vertices
are affinely independent.

The function F_C(x)=max_(u in C)(<x,u>-||u||^2/2) is differentiable,
convex, and 1-smooth with gradient P_C: the projection variational
inequalities imply firm nonexpansiveness, and comparison of the two
maxima gives its derivative. It has minimum zero at zero and takes
values V_i at X_i. Translation and common value shifts leave the
intrinsic energy unchanged, so the new energy is at most -v/2.

Finally F_C(X_i)=<G_i,X_i>-||G_i||^2/2 makes every I_i zero, and
expansion of the preceding telescope gives

    V_h=||X_0||^2-||X_n+S G_n||^2<0.

The perturbation and z_* shift need not preserve ||x_0||. Thus the
large normalized objective in (1) belongs to the original source
instance; only the negative sign is asserted for the simplex instance.
