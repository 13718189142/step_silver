# Earlier reset weights: a quantitative bound and an exact obstruction to naive propagation

Status: unverified supplement to the full-certificate descent search. All claims below are proved directly; no earlier unverified finding is used as an assumed theorem.

## 1. A reset criterion for any earlier star-outgoing multiplier

Normalize L=1, f_*=0. Consider a full feasible certificate at level q for a length-n schedule h. Let nu_i=lambda_{*,i}>0 for some i<n. Define

    delta_i=f(x_i)-||g_i||^2/2 >=0,
    A_i=prod_{t=i+1}^{n-1} max{1,(h_t-1)^2}.

The full PSD certificate, with the term nu_i Q_{*,i}=-nu_i delta_i isolated, gives for every legal function

    2f(x_n)+nu_i delta_i <= q||x_0-x_*||^2.                 (1)

Replace h_i by one, leaving every other step in place. The changed trajectory agrees with the old one through x_i and reaches y=x_i-g_i at the next iteration. Unit-step descent gives f(y)<=delta_i.

For any nonnegative step a and any current point z, smoothness gives

    f(z-a grad f(z)) <= f(z)+(a^2/2-a)||grad f(z)||^2.

When 0<=a<=2, this is at most f(z). When a>2, use ||grad f(z)||^2<=2f(z) to obtain an upper bound (a-1)^2 f(z). Thus the unchanged suffix multiplies the objective by at most A_i, and

    W_n(h with h_i replaced by 1) <= (2A_i/nu_i) q.          (2)

In particular nu_i>2A_i gives a genuine fixed-length strict improvement. Every tight certificate at a globally minimizing schedule must therefore obey

    nu_i<=2A_i, 0<=i<n.                                    (3)

At i=n-1, direct endpoint convexity gives the much stronger conclusion nu_{n-1}=0 by the explicit last-step descent already recorded separately. Bound (3) is useful for earlier indices but does NOT assert that their multipliers vanish.

The product A_i is a proved worst-case growth bound, not a guess about conditional history. It can be large and does not itself yield the exact Zhang--Jiang profile.

## 2. Exact failure of carrying final-step convexity through even one later unit step

A tempting extension is to move an earlier step toward one and then use convexity to average the two resulting terminal objective values after the unchanged suffix. This fails for a single unchanged unit step.

Define an everywhere finite convex 1-smooth function on the real line by

    f(x)=x^2/2,                       x<=1;
         x-1/2,                       1<=x<=2;
         (x-1)^2/2+1,                 2<=x<=3;
         2x-3,                        x>=3.

Its derivative is, respectively, x, 1, x-1, and 2. It is continuous, nondecreasing and 1-Lipschitz. The unique minimum is f(0)=0.

Write T(x)=x-f'(x), the unit GD update, and F(x)=f(T(x)). The following exact values hold:

    F(3/2)=1/8,
    F(5/2)=1/2,
    F(2)=1/2 > (F(3/2)+F(5/2))/2 =5/16.                   (4)

Thus f composed with a unit GD step is not convex, even though f itself is convex and 1-smooth.

The points in (4) arise from exactly the proposed schedule interpolation. Start at x_0=9/2, where g_0=2. The first step b=3/2 gives x_1=3/2. Replacing it by the unit step gives x_1=5/2. Taking their midpoint step b=5/4 gives x_1=2. Follow all three by the same final unit step. Their final objective values are respectively 1/8, 1/2, and 1/2. Hence the middle schedule's value exceeds the convex average of the endpoint schedules by 3/16, with a common function and common initial point.

This refutes the exact propagation step needed to extend the penultimate convex-blend proof unchanged to earlier times. It does not refute a different simultaneous schedule/certificate perturbation, and the example is not claimed to be a matching worst-case stationary tuple.

## 3. Consequence for the active program

The final-step descent uses literal convexity of f along the final update line. Earlier reset changes enter a nonlinear suffix, so that proof cannot simply be repeated backward. The quantitative fallback (2) survives, but requires sufficiently large multiplier weight relative to the proved suffix-growth factor.

Likewise the terminal-pair Schur-range descent controls a perturbation at the final point directly; an earlier gradient relation cannot be treated as if the rest of the GD trajectory were affine. Any propagation mechanism must retain or bound the actual suffix response. This is the concrete obstruction to the attempted backward extension, not a conclusion that all full-certificate approaches fail.
