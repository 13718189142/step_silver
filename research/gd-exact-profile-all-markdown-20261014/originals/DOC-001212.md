# Strict simplex witnesses for primitive failure

Status: complete candidate, NOT verifier-accepted. No new fact_submit or
numerical search is allocated. The proof uses the interpolation necessity
in accepted fact 1252b8823bb5a290. The public projection/saturation findings
20c6d21ee60a4cb0 and 4ad60ca9f3ec3e2e were leads, not premises.
All new steps, including the independent-gradient perturbation, are below.

## 1. Exact statement

For a finite nonnegative physical schedule h=(h_0,...,h_(n-1)), put
S=sum h_i and define, on any actual convex differentiable 1-smooth GD run,

    V_h=2 sum_(i<n) h_i(f_i-f_n)
        +sum_(i<n) h_i(1-h_i)||g_i||^2
        -S(S+1)||g_n||^2.                            (1)

Call h primitive when V_h>=0 for every such finite-dimensional run with
a minimizer. Let m be the number of strictly positive steps of h.

The following conditions are equivalent:

* h is primitive;
* for every finite-dimensional compact convex polytope C containing0,
  the function F_C(x)=(||x||^2-dist(x,C)^2)/2 and its GD run satisfy
  ||x_n+S P_C(x_n)||<=||x_0||;
* the same inequality holds for every full-dimensional simplex in
  R^(m+1) with vertices 0,v_0,...,v_m.

More strongly, if h fails primitivity, a negative witness can be chosen
on a simplex of the last form with its m+1 distinct compressed query
gradients exactly v_0,...,v_m. Every projection normal inequality at
one of these query vertices is STRICT against each different vertex,
including zero. Duplicates introduced by zero steps are simply restored.
Thus there are exactly m+2 vertices, dimension m+1, and no limiting
interpolant. The original step values and order are unchanged.

The construction preserves a strictly negative intrinsic energy. It
does NOT preserve the normalized objective error, or the initial distance.
Section7 gives a fixed triangle where the absolute negative energy stays
constant while even the objective normalized by the closest-minimizer
distance tends to zero.

## 2. Projection functions and every normal inequality

Let C be a nonempty compact convex set containing0. The unique maximizer
of <x,v>-||v||^2/2 over v in C is its nearest point p=P_C(x), by completing
the square. Therefore

    F_C(x)=max_(v in C)(<x,v>-||v||^2/2)
          =(||x||^2-dist(x,C)^2)/2.                   (2)

The nearest-point condition is

    <x-p,v-p><=0 for EVERY v in C.                   (3)

Necessity follows by differentiating squared distance along the segment
from p to v; sufficiency follows by expanding ||x-v||^2. Applied twice,
(3) gives ||P_C(x)-P_C(y)||^2<=<P_C(x)-P_C(y),x-y>, so P_C is
1-Lipschitz. Comparing the maxima in (2) gives

    0<=F_C(x+d)-F_C(x)-<P_C(x),d>
      <=<P_C(x+d)-P_C(x),d><=||d||^2.

Consequently F_C is convex, differentiable, and 1-smooth, with gradient
P_C. It is nonnegative and F_C(0)=0. Its minimizer set is precisely the
polar cone C^polar={z:<z,v><=0 for every v in C}: use (3) with p=0.

For proposed query data X_i,G_i, define

    Y_i=X_i-G_i,  F_i=<G_i,X_i>-||G_i||^2/2.

On C=conv{0,G_0,...,G_m}, the COMPLETE projection conditions are

    <Y_i,G_j-G_i><=0 for ALL i,j,
    <Y_i,-G_i><=0 for ALL i.                         (4)

Convex combinations then give (3) for every retained point. No adjacent
or chronological subset replaces (4).

## 3. A finite strict perturbation with independent gradients

Compress zero steps from the supplied negative witness. A zero step repeats
the point, gradient, and function value. Its coefficients in (1) vanish,
and S and the final jet are unchanged, so V_h is unchanged. If m=0 then
V_h=0, and there was no negative witness. Hence assume m>=1 and the
compressed steps are all positive; write their actual energy as -v_def<0.

Construct an auxiliary quadratic on R^(m+1): let

    theta_l=(l+1)/(2(m+2)), 0<=l<=m,
    A_aux=diag(theta_0,...,theta_m),
    psi(z)=<z,A_aux z>/2,  z_0=(1,...,1).

Run the same compressed schedule and denote its jets by z_i,w_i,psi_i.
If P_i(z)=product_(t<i)(1-h_t z), its coordinate gradients are
w_(i,l)=theta_l P_i(theta_l). These m+1 gradients are linearly independent.
Indeed the P_i have distinct degrees 0,...,m and nonzero leading
coefficients. A linear combination vanishing at the m+1 distinct theta_l
is a polynomial of degree at most m with m+1 roots, hence is zero; the
distinct-degree basis then makes every coefficient zero.

Every two sample positions z_i,z_j are distinct, since P_i-P_j is a
nonzero polynomial of degree at most m and cannot vanish at all theta_l.
For the signed residual convention from accepted fact 1252b8823bb5a290,

    Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2,

direct substitution for this auxiliary quadratic gives

    Q_ij^aux=-<z_i-z_j,A_aux(I-A_aux)(z_i-z_j)>/2<0    (i!=j). (5)

All theta_l lie strictly between0 and1. Let E_aux be the finite energy
of this explicitly given quadratic run. Choose

    epsilon=v_def/[2(v_def+|E_aux|)] in(0,1/2].       (6)

Take the fixed product function

    f_mix(x,z)=f_original(x)+epsilon psi(z/sqrt(epsilon)).

Start it at (x_0,sqrt(epsilon)z_0). Its sampled points and gradients are
(x_i,sqrt(epsilon)z_i) and (g_i,sqrt(epsilon)w_i), with values
f_i+epsilon psi_i. It is convex differentiable 1-smooth, and all GD
equations are exact. Energy and every Q are additive across these
coordinates. Hence its energy is

    V_mix=-v_def+epsilon E_aux<=-v_def/2<0,           (7)

its gradients are independent, and all finite distinct-sample Q_ij
are strictly negative by (5). Rename these mixed data x_i,g_i,f_i.

## 4. One origin/value shift makes an actual simplex trajectory

Set

    f_* = min_i(f_i-||g_i||^2/2)-1,
    r_i = f_*-f_i+<g_i,x_i>-||g_i||^2/2.

Let A_gram=(<g_i,g_j>)_(0<=i,j<=m), which is positive definite.
Define z_*=sum_j (A_gram^(-1)r)_j g_j. Then <g_i,z_*>=r_i.
Set X_i=x_i-z_* and F_i=f_i-f_*. The GD equations are unchanged,
and the defining equalities give

    F_i=<g_i,X_i>-||g_i||^2/2.                        (8)

For all finite ordered pairs, the Q residual is unchanged and is
strictly negative. Substituting (8) into it gives

    Q_ij=<X_j-g_j,g_i-g_j><0 for i!=j.               (9)

Moreover <X_i-g_i,g_i>=F_i-||g_i||^2/2>=1. These are ALL the
normal inequalities (4), including strict inequalities against zero.
Consequently the fixed polytope C=conv{0,g_0,...,g_m} satisfies
P_C(X_i)=g_i and F_C(X_i)=F_i at every compressed query.
Thus it is an actual globally legal projection-function realization;
we did not just assign independent values and gradients.

The g_i are linearly independent, so these m+2 points are all vertices
of a simplex of dimension m+1. Orthogonally project all X_i onto
W=span{g_0,...,g_m}. Gradients, values, GD equations, and all inequalities
(9) are unchanged. The removed component is constant along the run.
This puts the entire realization in dimension m+1. All initial points
are nonzero, since <g_0,X_0>=F_0+||g_0||^2/2>0.

Translation of the points and a common shift of the values leave (1)
unchanged; orthogonal compression does too. Therefore its final energy
is exactly V_mix<0. Restoring the original zero steps repeats the
corresponding actual jets and keeps the same polytope, all physical
steps, and the energy. This proves the claimed finite sign reduction.

## 5. The norm identity, including the discarded-slack term

For arbitrary original legal data with minimizer0 and value0 define

    I_i=<g_i,x_i>-f_i-||g_i||^2/2>=0,
    J=sum_(i<n)h_i I_i.

The GD squared-distance telescope gives

    V_h=||x_0||^2-||x_n+Sg_n||^2+2(S I_n-J).         (10)

Indeed ||x_0||^2-||x_n||^2
=2sum h_i<g_i,x_i>-sum h_i^2||g_i||^2; substitute
<g_i,x_i>=f_i+||g_i||^2/2+I_i and expand the final square.

For projection functions, (2) makes every I_i exactly zero. Therefore
the proposed physical-step formula is exactly

    V_h=||x_0||^2-||x_n+S P_C(x_n)||^2.             (11)

It is generally false without the extra slack in (10). Combining (11)
with sections2--4 proves all equivalences in section1. No missing
function values were dropped: they determine r, z_*, and the resulting
positions relative to the simplex.

## 6. A precise full Gram game and its scope

For the compressed length m define the lower-triangular matrix T_h by
(T_h)_ii=1, (T_h)_ij=h_j for j<i, and zero for j>i. Let A_gram be
the positive definite gradient Gram matrix and b_i=<g_i,X_0>.
The complete normal inequalities are precisely

    b_j-b_i <= (T_h A_gram)_ij-(T_h A_gram)_ii   for all i,j,
    b_i >= (T_h A_gram)_ii                      for all i. (12)

The strict simplex construction makes all nontrivial inequalities strict.
Conversely any positive definite A_gram and b satisfying (12) have a
realization: choose independent vectors with this Gram matrix, and set
X_0=sum_j(A_gram^(-1)b)_j g_j. Equations (12) then prove all projection
conditions for the specified simplex and prescribed GD trajectory.

Put d_i=h_i for i<m, d_m=-S. Then sum_i d_i=0 and

    V_h=2 b^T d-d^T A_gram d,
    ||X_0||^2=b^T A_gram^(-1)b.                       (13)

These formulas retain every queried vertex. They are a lossless
finite-dimensional game for primitive validity, not yet a capacity bound.
In particular, no conclusion S+1<=(m+1)^(log_2(1+sqrt(2))) is inferred.

Adding a common constant to every b_i preserves the finite differences
and the primitive energy, and can enforce the origin inequalities. It
changes the initial norm in (13); it therefore cannot be used freely in
an objective-normalized lower bound.

## 7. An exact triangle, and a quantitative normalization failure

Take the one-step schedule h=(3/2), the triangle

    v_0=(1,0),  v_1=(-2/5,1/10), C=conv{0,v_0,v_1},
    X_0=(2,267/20), X_1=(1/2,267/20).

The two query normals are Y_0=(1,267/20) and Y_1=(9/10,53/4).
ALL nontrivial projection comparisons are

    <Y_0,v_1-v_0>=<Y_1,v_0-v_1>=-13/200,
    <Y_0,-v_0>=-1,  <Y_1,-v_1>=-193/200.

Thus these are strict vertex projections, and their complete values are
F_C(X_0)=3/2, F_C(X_1)=21/20. The norm game gives

    V_h=72889/400-72904/400=-3/80<0.                  (14)

For every T>=0 put u=(1,14), X_i(T)=X_i+T u. Since
<u,v_0>=<u,v_1>=1, every finite normal difference above is unchanged,
and the origin comparisons only become more negative. Hence the SAME
fixed triangle realizes the same gradient trajectory for every T,

    F_C(X_i(T))=F_C(X_i)+T,
    V_h(T)=-3/80.

Its minimizer cone is {z:z_1<=0, z_2<=4z_1}. Both coordinates of
X_0(T) are positive, while both coordinates of a cone point are
nonpositive. Expanding squared distances proves that zero is the
closest minimizer to X_0(T). Therefore the genuine normalized terminal
objective is

    [21/10+2T]/[(2+T)^2+(267/20+14T)^2] ->0.          (15)

This is not merely an arbitrary poor choice of minimizer. It proves that
an absolute negative primitive margin alone cannot lower-bound the
normalized objective even on one fixed simplex and one fixed schedule.
It does not upper-bound the worst case for that schedule: other instances
may give a much larger value.

The mechanism holds on every independent-gradient simplex. Let u be
the unique vector in the gradient span with <u,g_i>=1 for all i.
Translation by T u preserves all finite normal inequalities and shifts
all sample values by T, while improving the origin comparisons.
Since sum d_i=0, it leaves V_h unchanged. The closest-minimizer distance
is Theta(T): for each cone point z, <g_i,z><=0, so
||X_0+Tu-z||>=(T+<g_i,X_0>)/||g_i|| for sufficiently large T;
the upper bound O(T) follows by choosing z=0. Thus (15)'s dilution
is a general geometric feature, not a special coordinate artifact.

## 8. A failed capacity-witness simplification

A natural attempt to make the simplex game tractable is to require all
nonzero visited vertices to have one common radius. This is NOT a
universal class of primitive counterexamples, even at length1.

Suppose 0<=h_i<2 and every nonzero sampled projection gradient has norm R.
If both consecutive gradients are nonzero, projection cocoercivity gives

    ||g_i-g_(i+1)||^2
      <=h_i<g_i,g_i-g_(i+1)>
      =(h_i/2)||g_i-g_(i+1)||^2.

They must therefore be equal. If a zero gradient is ever reached, that
point is a minimizer and all later gradients are zero. Before that time
the gradient is constant. If no zero is reached, x_n+Sg_n=x_0 and V_h=0.
If a zero is reached, each h_i<2 step decreases squared distance to zero:
the vertex0 inequality gives <x_i,g_i>>=||g_i||^2, hence

    ||x_(i+1)||^2<=||x_i||^2-h_i(2-h_i)||g_i||^2.

Thus (11) is nonnegative again. In particular equal-radius vertex walks,
including regular-simplex vertex walks, cannot reject h=(3/2), although
the explicit strict triangle (14) does reject it. Allowing projections
onto nonvertex faces is outside this failed simplification.

The exact general simplex reduction succeeds. The equal-radius capacity
ansatz fails, and the unrestricted capacity inequality remains open.
