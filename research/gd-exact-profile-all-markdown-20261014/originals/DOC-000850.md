## 2. Exact same-word Huber price, before choosing any policy

Let Hub(x)=x^2/2 for |x|<=1 and |x|-1/2 otherwise. It is convex,
differentiable and 1-smooth, with unique minimizer0. Start at x_0=D.
Every actual query is x_i=D-p_i>=S+1>1, so

    g_i=1, f_i=D-p_i-1/2, 2f_n=D.

For an empty word S=0 the same calculation uses the boundary point1;
our screened words have S>0. Direct substitution in the ORIGINAL rows gives

    Q_ij=0 for finite i!=j,
    Q_(i,star)=0,
    Q_(star,i)=-(2S-p_i).                                 (3)

Write b_i=lambda_(star,i), a_i=lambda_(i,star), and q=zeta/D with
eta=zeta-1. On this exact Huber transcript,

    M(q)=D eta-sum_(i=0)^n b_i(2S-p_i).                    (4)

Thus a necessary condition for PSD is

    sum_i b_i(2S-p_i)<=D eta,
    eta>=0, sum_i b_i<=D eta/S<=3eta when S>1.             (5)

The last inequality is strict when eta>0; at eta=0 all b_i are zero.

The row price varies from 2S to S, including the terminal price S. No
nonzero optimum-outgoing row is free on this transcript. Function values
and the specified-minimizer radius are unchanged.

The price is exact as an evaluation, but it is not a sufficient PSD test.
There is a second useful restriction that keeps the x_0 mixed terms. Let
K_ij=lambda_ij on finite pairs and let B=sum b_i. The complete finite flow is

    sum_j K_ij-sum_j K_ji+a_i-b_i=2*1_(i=n),
    sum_i a_i=2+B.                                        (6)

On the entire two-dimensional original subspace g_i=v for every finite i,
x_0=x, flow gives

    M(q)=q x^2-(2+B)xv+[D+sum_i b_i(1+p_i)]v^2.            (7)

Indeed finite position terms sum to
sum_i p_i(out_i^K-in_i^K)=2S-sum_i a_i p_i+sum_i b_i p_i;
finite gradient differences vanish, and the optimum terms supply the
remaining a_i p_i and (a_i+b_i)/2. Therefore PSD also requires

    zeta >= D(1+B/2)^2/[D+sum_i b_i(1+p_i)].              (8)

For example, spending the ENTIRE budget (4) on b_n=D eta/S, eta>0,
would give M(D,1,...,1)=0, but the x_0 derivative there equals
2eta-b_n=-eta/S!=0. A PSD quadratic vanishing at a vector annihilates
that vector, as follows by evaluating it on that vector plus t times an
arbitrary direction for both signs of t. Such saturated terminal spending
therefore cannot be PSD without changing the budget allocation. This is a
budget warning, not a fourth screened policy.

## 3. Full value flow and full correction in original coordinates

Put z_i=-sum_(t<i)h_t g_t, so x_i=x_0+z_i. Under (6), (1) is exactly

    q||x_0||^2-sum_i a_i<x_0,g_i>
     +sum_i a_i[-<g_i,z_i>+||g_i||^2/2]
     +sum_i b_i||g_i||^2/2
     +sum_(i!=j finite)K_ij[<g_i,z_j-z_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2].   (9)

In particular its FULL matrix is

    [[q,-a^T/2],[-a/2,C]],                                (10)

on the original n+2 coordinates. Define h_n=0 and out_i=a_i+sum_j K_ij.
Every entry of the gradient block C is

    C_ii=out_i-1_(i=n)-h_i sum_(j>i)K_ij,
    2C_ij=-(K_ij+K_ji)-h_j sum_(v>j)K_iv
                         +h_i[a_j+sum_(v<=i)K_jv], i<j.  (11)

All sums in (11) are over finite labels. Half the total mass incident to i
is out_i-1_(i=n) by (6), including BOTH optimum directions. A forward
position row based at i subtracts h_i K_ij from the diagonal. For i<j,
difference squares contribute -(K_ij+K_ji) to the cross coefficient;
position rows based at i contribute -h_j sum_(v>j)K_iv, and rows based at j
contribute h_i[a_j+sum_(v<=i)K_jv]. This proves (11) without omitting b.

For q>0 the exact full square completion is

    M(q)=q||x_0-sum_i a_i g_i/(2q)||^2
                +sum_(i,j)[C_ij-a_i a_j/(4q)]<g_i,g_j>.   (12)

The terminal, every mixed term, and every original gradient remain.

More generally start with any value-flow base (K^0,a^0,b^0=0) at 1/D.
Permit a signed finite modification deltaK with final K^0+deltaK>=0.
Set r_i=sum_j deltaK_ij-sum_j deltaK_ji. Exact final flow forces

    a_i=a_i^0+b_i-r_i>=0, b_i>=0.                         (13)

The complete value-free correction to the base is

    eta||x_0||^2/D-sum_i(b_i-r_i)<x_0,g_i>
     +sum_i(b_i-r_i)sum_(t<i)h_t<g_i,g_t>
     +sum_i(b_i-r_i/2)||g_i||^2
     +sum_(i!=j)deltaK_ij[<g_i,z_j-z_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2].   (14)

This follows by subtracting (9) for the base. It allows reductions of old
finite multipliers, provided their FINAL signs hold. The three policies
below use particular nonnegative additions and do not exhaust (13)-(14).

A balanced optimum cycle of weight rho_i adds rho_i to both a_i and b_i,
keeps K unchanged, and adds exactly

    rho_i C_i^opt,
    C_i^opt=Q_(i,star)+Q_(star,i)
           =||g_i||^2-<x_0,g_i>+sum_(t<i)h_t<g_i,g_t>.    (15)

For the sum of such cycles, deltaC has diagonal rho_i and entries
2deltaC_ti=h_t rho_i for t<i. Its x_0-to-gradient entries are -rho_i/2.
These are not just positive diagonal corrections. For example a lone
cycle at i>0, with x_0=0, g_i=1 and an earlier g_t=-2/h_t, other gradients0,
has value -rho_i when h_t>0. Adding eta||x_0||^2/D does not change that
negative value. This illustrates why (12), rather than diagonal signs,
is the required test.

For any distinct finite i,j, a directed optimum triangle of weight t_ij
adds t_ij to the three ORIGINAL rows Q_(star,i), Q_(i,j), Q_(j,star).
Its flow is zero and its complete polynomial is

    t_ij T_ij^opt,
    T_ij^opt=||g_i||^2+||g_j||^2-<g_i,g_j>
                  +<g_i,z_j-z_i>-<g_j,x_0+z_j>.         (16)

Only a_j increases. Its new x_0 cross term is -t_ij<x_0,g_j>, and
its Huber price is t_ij(2S-p_i). Both facts matter below.

## 4. The three explicit policies and their actual budgets

Define positive density v_i=h_i for i<n and v_n=S+1, so sum_i v_i=D.
Take eta>=0 and q=(1+eta)/D.

Policy I: on the asymmetric array of Section 5, add balanced optimum cycles

    rho_i=eta v_i/D.                                     (17)

Policy II: use exactly the same cycle law (17), on the source-calibrated
finite array and its original objective completion in Section 7.

Policy III: on the asymmetric array, use

    rho_i=eta v_i/(2D),
    t_(n,L_k)=eta/2, all other added triangles zero.      (18)

Here L_k is the original physical root query of the last join. Thus this
policy changes a finite row too: it adds eta/2 to Q_(n,L_k), as well as
to Q_(star,n) and Q_(L_k,star).

Every addition in (17)-(18) is nonnegative and has zero value flow. For
Policies I and II, (3) gives their EXACT new Huber expenditure

    P_H=eta/D * sum_i v_i(2S-p_i).                        (19)

For Policy III it is

    P_H=eta/(2D)*sum_i v_i(2S-p_i)+eta S/2.               (20)

For eta>0, (19)<=2S eta<D eta. Similarly (20)<=3S eta/2<D eta.
Thus these are feasible budget allocations with genuine paid costs and
positive Huber remainder, not laws rejected merely for spending too much.
At eta=0 the costs vanish. Exact sums (19)-(20), not only their upper
bounds, specify the necessary cost of each policy.

The tests below grant STRICTLY LARGER classes: arbitrary nonnegative
balanced weights, and for Policy III arbitrary nonnegative triangles
star->R_i->L_l->star, with any original left destination. Their failure
therefore includes the explicit policies without fitting a weight profile.

## 6. New full left-block test: Policies I and III fail even with free weights

On the ORIGINAL formal space choose

    g_(L_l)=v for EVERY left query, including L_k,
    g_(R_i)=0 for EVERY right query, including terminal,
    x_0=x.                                                (24)

The original positions are x_(L_l)=x-p_l^c v and x_(R_i)=x-tv.
This is a test subspace of the whole quadratic, not a quotient or a claim
that the formal assignment is interpolation-feasible.

Let B_l be total backward inflow into L_l and F_l total forward outflow.
Left finite flow is -a_(L_l)-F_l+B_l. Multiplying it by p_l^c and summing
shows that all left finite position terms contribute
sum_l p_l^c[-a_(L_l)-F_l+B_l]v^2. The optimum rows contribute
-a_L xv+sum_l a_(L_l)(p_l^c+1/2)v^2, where a_L=sum_l a_(L_l)=2s.
Backward rows contribute (sum_i B_i)v^2/2. Forward rows contribute
sum_l F_l[-t+p_l^c+1/2]v^2. All p_l^c a_(L_l) and p_l^c F_l terms cancel.
Consequently the full base (9), including every arbitrary left coefficient,
restricts to

    M_base(q)=q x^2-2s xv+Gamma_L v^2,
    Gamma_L=sum_i B_i+sum_l p_l^c B_l-t sum_l F_l
           =2s+kappa E.                                 (25)

The final equality uses sum_i B_i=2s+kappa E,
sum_l p_l^c B_l=kappa tE, and sum_l F_l=kappa E, directly from (22).
This exact cancellation is the restrictive cross-block MOMENT of the base.
It is not a general identity for asymmetric certificates.

A balanced cycle on a left label contributes
rho_(L_l)[(1+p_l^c)v^2-xv] on (24); a cycle on a right label is zero.
A backward optimum triangle star->R_i->L_l->star contributes precisely
t_(R_i,L_l)[(1+p_l^c)v^2-xv] by (16). These statements include their
x_0 mixed terms. Let U be the total weight of these left balanced cycles
and backward triangles, and let J be their sum weighted by (1+p_l^c).
Then 0<=J<=(A+1)U, and the complete modified restriction is

    M(q)=q x^2-(2s+U)xv+(Gamma_L+J)v^2.                 (26)

The right balanced weights may be arbitrary; they vanish on this test.
The triangle weights and destinations may be arbitrary as specified;
no Huber budget restriction is needed for the following obstruction.

Suppose A>=1024 and d/A>2/5. Since E<=2A and kappa<=s/(2d),

    Gamma_L<=s(2+A/d)<(9/2)s<(A+1)s.                    (27)

For q<=0, (26) cannot be PSD since its mixed coefficient is nonzero.
For q>0, set v=1 and x=(s+U/2)/q. Full PSD would require

    q >= (s+U/2)^2/(Gamma_L+J)
      > (s+U/2)^2/[(A+1)(s+U)] >= s/(A+1).             (28)

The last inequality follows by subtracting s(s+U) from (s+U/2)^2;
the difference is U^2/4>=0. This explicitly pays the mixed term: merely
raising the positive gradient diagonal would miss its simultaneous change.

Also Ds>t=A+d, so

    qD>Ds/(A+1)>(A+d)/(A+1)
                     >(7/5)A/(A+1)>4/3.               (29)

The final inequality follows from A>20. Thus the exact full-space assignment
just specified is negative at ANY proposed qD<=4/3, regardless of how the
balanced and backward-triangle weights are chosen. This proves the failure
of both explicit Policies I and III and their larger stated classes.

## 7. The full source-calibrated array for Policy II

In this section set S_j=sum H_j, A_j=1+S_j, d_j=a c^j, so the physical
bridge at level j is 1+d_j and A_(j+1)=2A_j+d_j. For any word H_j with
terminal index z define the ORIGINAL primitive history polynomial

    V_j=2sum_(i<z)h_i(f_i-f_z)
              +sum_(i<z)h_i(1-h_i)||g_i||^2-A_j(A_j-1)||g_z||^2.

Start with empty finite array Lambda^0=0. At each join let p be the
physical bridge query and z the parent terminal; i ranges over nonterminal
right-child queries. Use the following exact nonnegative weights:

    u_j=2lambda,
    v_j^w=[u_j d_j+2(A_j+d_j)]/A_j,
    k_j=1+(u_j+v_j^w)/2,
    z_j^w=2A_j,
    p_j^w=4A_j+2d_j-u_j(A_j-1)
                         =2lambda+2(d_j-A_j/lambda).     (31)

Embed the entire left finite array with weight1, the entire right finite
array with weight k_j, and add

    u_j sum_i h_i Q_(i,p)+v_j^w sum_i h_i Q_(i,z)
                             +p_j^w Q_(z,p)+z_j^w Q_(p,z).   (32)

There are no optimum rows in this finite recursion. Define P_j by
V_j+sum Lambda^j_ij Q_ij=P_j. This defines a full quadratic, without
assuming its positivity.

For a check of every coefficient, temporarily write A=A_j,d=d_j,
s=A-1,T=2A+d,K=A(A-1),K_+=T(T-1), u=u_j,v=v_j^w,k=k_j,
p^w=p_j^w,z^w=z_j^w, and r=sum_i h_i g_i over the right
nonterminal queries. For a nonempty right child, function coefficients
cancel precisely when

    u+v=2(k-1), us+p^w-z^w=2(A+d),                       (33)

which (31) satisfies. For the empty right child these equations remain
sufficient and are the chosen policy; no necessity is asserted there.
The four gradient parts of the unweighted terms
in (32) are respectively

    d<r,g_p>+||r||^2/2+sum_i h_i(1-h_i)||g_i||^2/2+s||g_p||^2/2,
    -||r||^2/2+sum_i h_i(1-h_i)||g_i||^2/2+s||g_z||^2/2-<r,g_z>,
    d<g_z,g_p>+<g_z,r>+(||g_p||^2+||g_z||^2)/2,
    -(d+1/2)||g_p||^2+||g_z||^2/2-<g_p,r>-<g_p,g_z>.

After adding the history differences, the correction R_j in
P_(j+1)=P_j^left+k_j P_j^right+R_j has coefficients

    R_j=(u-v)||r||^2/2+(ud-z^w)<r,g_p>+(p^w-v)<r,g_z>
                  +(p^w d-z^w)<g_p,g_z>+C_p||g_p||^2+C_z||g_z||^2,
    C_p=K-d-d^2+(us+p^w)/2-(d+1/2)z^w,
    C_z=-K_++kK+vs/2+(p^w+z^w)/2.

Substitute (31), put t_j=d_j/A_j, t_*=1/lambda,
Y=r-(A_j-1)g_z and Z=g_p-g_z. The SAME complete correction is

    R_j=(t_*-t_j)[(lambda+1)||Y||^2-2lambda A_j<Y,Z>
                           +A_j^2(2+t_*+t_j)||Z||^2].     (34)

For instance the coefficient of ||r||^2 equals
(u-v)/2=(lambda+1)(t_*-t_j), and ud-z^w=-2lambda A_j(t_*-t_j);
substitution of the displayed C_p,C_z supplies the remaining coefficients.
Thus (34) concerns all original gradients and includes every right aggregate,
bridge and terminal cross term.

The bracket in (34) is positive definite in Y,Z. Its first diagonal is
lambda+1, and its determinant divided by A_j^2 is
(lambda+1)(2+t_*+t_j)-lambda^2>=lambda>0. It vanishes on common gradients
because Y=Z=0. Consequently P_j e_j=0 at every depth, where e_j is the
all-ones gradient vector. This is an exact matrix-kernel identity obtained
by induction from (34), without claiming PSD.
