## 1. Notation and the static interpolation construction

Work with smoothness1 in a finite-dimensional Euclidean space, with unit
vector e. Scalar coordinates mean multiples of e; extra coordinates may
be present. Write t=<e,x>. For a finite convex maximum J of affine planes,
its envelope is

    f(x)=min_z [J(z)+||x-z||^2/2].                         (1.1)

If a plane of normal g is active at z and x=z+g, then its supporting
inequality, with the square expanded, shows z uniquely minimizes (1.1).
The resulting value is J(z)+||g||^2/2 and the gradient is g. To justify
the last assertion directly, let z(x) be the unique proximal minimizer
and g(x)=x-z(x). Existence follows from coercivity, and uniqueness from
the strictly convex quadratic. Adding the two subgradient inequalities
for J at z(x),z(y) gives

    ||g(x)-g(y)||^2 <= <g(x)-g(y),x-y>.

The supporting inequality at z(x) gives
f(y)>=f(x)+<g(x),y-x>, while evaluating the proximal objective for y
at z(x) gives the upper remainder bound||y-x||^2/2. Thus f is
differentiable with derivative g; the displayed inequality proves its Lipschitz
constant is at most1. The envelope is convex because its defining
objective is jointly convex. Its minimum and minimizer set coincide
with those of J: equality in J(z)+||x-z||^2/2>=min J requires x=z and
J(z)=min J. These arguments also cover affine ties and flat minimizer
sets. Every construction below specifies one J before the whole run.

For actual smooth jets use the accepted signed residual

    Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2 <=0.      (1.2)

In proximal coordinates z_i=x_i-g_i and H_i=f_i-||g_i||^2/2,
(1.2) is precisely the supporting-plane inequality

    H_j >= H_i+<g_i,z_j-z_i>.

Consequently checking every active affine plane at every proposed
proximal sample checks both directions of every original interpolation
pair, not just adjacent gradient inequalities.

## 7. Solve the offset and reversal scale together

At a feasible beta the numerator of(6.6) is at least beta^2, by the
left inequality in(6.4). Differentiating in mu gives

    partial_mu V=2beta/(3-mu)^2
                   +2[2beta(mu-Y)-d beta^2]/(3-mu)^3>0.

Hence choose the largest permitted mu, not an independent first-cell
optimum. For 0<beta<=1/2 it equals1. For beta>1/2 it equals

    V=[(6-2Y)/beta-(6+2Y)beta
                        -d(1+2beta+beta^2)-4Y]/36.     (7.1)

Its derivative is

    [-(6-2Y)/beta^2-(6+2Y)-2d(1+beta)]/36<0.

If any beta>1/2 is feasible, beta=1/2 is feasible as well: then
Y+C/2<Y+C beta<=mu<1. Thus no omitted larger-beta component can
improve on its boundary.

    beta_opt=min(1/2,delta/d),

## 9. Zero steps, boundaries, and exact scope

Each ordinary list may be arbitrary, nonperiodic, empty, or contain
zero steps. The arguments use its nonnegative partial sums only and
check both endpoint samples; affine comparisons cover every actual
intermediate query. Zero steps duplicate actual jets and cause no
division by a step. Extra zero steps in the complete examples preserve
their payoff and K, while the benchmark comparison follows from
B_n>=3n+1 at their resulting horizon n>=6.

S=0 and S=1/2 are included in rigidity. At S=1/2 the first endpoint
equals the old query2e and gives the same forced gradient; the
strict-distinct-point example uses S=1/5. When T=0, (6.4)-(7.2)
remain exact. If positive second proximal height is desired in that
case, choose 0<=Y<mu<=1 and any beta strictly between0 and
min(1/2,mu-Y). This gives positive second proximal height, with
payoff still exactly(6.6), instead of claiming the height at the
optimizer is strict.

At Y=1, beta>0 is impossible. The case beta=0 is possible with a=1:
J=max(|z|,1), its minimizers form[-1,1], its actual distance from3
is2, and the new terminal gap is0. For Y>1 even beta=0 is impossible.
As Y increases to1 from below, (7.2) tends to0. As epsilon decreases
to0 in(7.3), the score tends toV_opt but H_B decreases to0; no
strict-height optimizer is asserted at that limit. The exact loss
for every positive epsilon is(7.4). All scales are fixed finite
numbers before the run. Multiplying space by a positive factor and
the objective by its square preserves every normalized payoff and
scales(4.2) by that squared factor; multiplying objective by L>0
and using steps h/L gives the corresponding L-smooth statements.

The obstruction does not say that arbitrary words lack legal hard
instances: (8.1) itself has a legal same-sign continuation. Nor does
it invalidate the complete Phi/Psi/Xi state, which correctly retains
the blocking query. It excludes a universal promise to continue
every legal retained history by the displayed two-cell positive
reversal rule, even with freely chosen offsets and fresh geometry.
The exact feasible-range repair is for this concrete retained
history and two supplied blocks, not an all-depth rule. It has no
unmatched recurrence or hidden depth loss: no repeated performance
claim is made. An all-word policy must permit a different chronology,
a same-sign carry, changing gradients within a block, or different
earlier queried data when it encounters this history. Quantitative
arbitrary-word matching by those alternatives remains open.
