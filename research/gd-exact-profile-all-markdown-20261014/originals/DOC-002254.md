## 2. A covariance that represents the cross-gradient terms exactly

Choose parameters

    1>rho_0>rho_1>...>rho_n>0,
    u_i=rho_i h_i for i<n.

The strict ordering is convenient for a nonsingular covariance; weak ordering is possible with degeneracies treated explicitly. Chooseu_n>0 separately. Introduce scalar weightsell_i=h_i fori<n andell_n=u_n/rho_n. The LASTell_n is solely an algebraic weight, NOT a physical step or another update.

Define the matrix

    K_ij=ell_i ell_j [min(rho_i,rho_j)-rho_i rho_j].   (2)

It is positive semidefinite, since

    sum_(i,j)K_ij<g_i,g_j>
      =integral_0^1 ||sum_i ell_i g_i
                   (1_(t<=rho_i)-rho_i)||² dt >=0.   (3)

This is the covariance of a centered Bernoulli-threshold process, often called the Brownian-bridge covariance. Formula(3) proves the PSD statement directly; no external stochastic theorem is needed.

Fori>j, rho_i<rho_j implies

    K_ij=ell_i ell_j rho_i(1-rho_j)=u_i(h_j-u_j).

Its diagonal isK_ii=u_i(ell_i-u_i). Therefore the exact same functional(1) can be written

    D_u=2sum_(i<n)u_i(f_i-f_n)
        +sum_i u_i(1-ell_i)||g_i||²
        +sum_(i,j)K_ij<g_i,g_j>.                     (4)

This does not replace the physical gradients by random ones. The covariance is a deterministic positive matrix acting on the ORIGINAL queried gradient vectors.
