## 1. Objects, realization, and the exact legality condition

Let n=N-1, h=(h_0,...,h_(n-1)), v_i=sigma_i w_i with sigma_i>0,
||w_i||^2=Tframe, and <w_i,e_vertical>=1/R. Put

    D0=conv{0,v_0,...,v_n}, C=cone{v_0,...,v_n},
    P_i=sum_(k<i) h_k v_k,
    Gram_ij=<v_i,v_j>, A_ij=<P_i,v_j>, F_i=<z0,v_i>.

The frozen Gray dictionary at depth j has N=2^j and EVERY nonzero
Walsh character. The j distinguished character weights are
2^k/(N-1), 0<=k<j; each of the remaining Kmix=N-1-j characters has
squared weight eta=1/(N^2 Kmix)>0. Including the constant vertical
character gives an invertible Walsh matrix, and positive row rescalings
by sigma_i preserve independence. Thus every vector (F_i+tau)_i has
an actual realization z0(tau). This fact does not require a numerical
inverse and no inverse is computed. For dependent calibration vertices,
realizability is checked directly instead of using this argument.

Consider the finite convex function

    Phi(z)=max_(g in D0) {<z,g>-||g||^2/2}.

Completion of squares gives the unique maximizer p(z)=projection_D0(z).
Its characterization is <z-p,w-p><=0 for all w in D0. Adding this
inequality for two projections yields
||p(z)-p(y)||^2<=<p(z)-p(y),z-y>, hence p is 1-Lipschitz. The two
maximizing affine expressions bracket the increment of Phi; the
remainder after <p(z),y-z> is bounded by ||y-z||^2. Therefore Phi is
differentiable with gradient p, convex, finite, and 1-smooth.

For z_i=z0(tau)-P_i, the nonzero-vertex normal is exactly

    <z_i-v_i,v_j-v_i>
      = F_j-F_i-[A_ij-A_ii+Gram_ij-Gram_ii].          (1)

It is independent of tau. The zero-vertex normal is

    <z_i-v_i,-v_i>=A_ii+Gram_ii-F_i-tau.            (2)

Consequently, IF every ordered row (1) is nonpositive in exact
arithmetic, all vertices v_i are the actual gradients exactly when

    tau >= tau_min := max_i(A_ii+Gram_ii-F_i).       (3)

The terminal vertex i=n is included and there is no physical h_n.
Under that exact condition the prescribed GD recursion holds and

    f_n(tau)=Phi(z_n)=F_n+tau-A_nn-Gram_nn/2.        (4)

At every tau in (3), (4) is at least Gram_nn/2>0. The saved production
potentials have POSITIVE raw residuals in (1); the common shift leaves
them unchanged. Neither (3) nor a small residual certifies exact legality.

## 2. The full minimum cone and the closest-minimum score

The minimum of Phi is zero, attained precisely on

    C^polar={z:<z,w_i><=0 for all i}.

Indeed a polar point gives value zero, and if <z,v_i>>0, a sufficiently
small positive multiple of v_i in D0 gives a strictly positive value.
This is the full minimum set. It is a nonempty closed convex set (an
intersection of finitely many closed halfspaces), so a closest point
exists in finite-dimensional Euclidean space.

Write q_i(tau)=(F_i+tau)/sigma_i and M(tau)=max_i q_i(tau). Because
A_00=0, (3) gives F_0+tau>=Gram_00=sigma_0^2 Tframe, and therefore
M(tau)>=sigma_0 Tframe>0. The explicit point

    zstar(tau)=z0(tau)-R M(tau) e_vertical           (5)

satisfies <zstar,w_i>=q_i-M<=0 for every original vertex. It is a
selected minimizer whose distance is exactly R M. It is not asserted
to be the closest minimizer.

For any y in C^polar, choose i attaining M. Then

    M=<z0,w_i><=<z0-y,w_i>
      <=||z0-y|| sqrt(Tframe).

Combining this with (5) gives the CONDITIONAL geometry interface

    M/sqrt(Tframe) <= dist(z0,C^polar) <= R M.       (6)

The geometry itself requires realizability and M>0; identification of
f_n as the actual GD terminal objective additionally requires exact (1).
With that hypothesis, define the closest-minimum normalized score
Q(tau)=2 f_n(tau)/dist(z0(tau),C^polar)^2. Since f_n>0, (6) gives

    L(tau)=2 f_n(tau)/(R^2 M(tau)^2)
           <= Q(tau) <=
    U(tau)=2 Tframe f_n(tau)/M(tau)^2.               (7)

Scaling Phi by a Lipschitz constant Lsmooth leaves this score unchanged
when GD uses physical steps h_i/Lsmooth. Thus (7) has the project's
W=2R-normalization (the R in W=2R denotes the alternate performance
functional, whereas the geometric R here is sqrt(3)). The upper bound
in (7) concerns this fixed initializer family; it is not a worst-case
upper bound over functions for a schedule.

Every tiny character contributes to the frame norm. Explicitly,

    sum_distinguished b_t^2=(2^j-1)/(N-1)=1,
    sum_other b_t^2=(N-1-j)/(N^2(N-1-j))=1/N^2,
    Tframe=1+1/N^2+1/3=4/3+1/N^2,
    U(tau)/L(tau)=R^2 Tframe=4+3/N^2.               (8)

No tiny character was deleted in (8). Dictionary size N changes this
norm, the weights and the saved radial law. The four depths 6,8,9,10
are finite analogues with enclosing step parameter 1536, not restrictions
of N=2^1536 geometry and not asymptotic horizons for the original task.

## 3. Exact finite candidate set for the one-dimensional maximization

Set c0=F_n-A_nn-Gram_nn/2. On tau>=tau_min,

    M(tau)=max_i(a_i tau+b_i),
    a_i=1/sigma_i>0, b_i=F_i/sigma_i,
    f_n=tau+c0.

Sort distinct slopes increasingly. For an equal slope keep the largest
intercept, recording all indices when both slope and intercept coincide.
For the upper hull, a new steeper line meets the last retained line at
(b_last-b_new)/(a_new-a_last). Pop the last line while this intersection
is no later than its own activation point. Then append the new line with
that intersection as its start. This works because, after their single
crossing, the steeper line stays above the shallower line. Popping removes
an empty active interval; zero-length ties remain detectable from the full
original line list. No tolerance is used in these structural decisions.

Intersect each retained closed active interval with [tau_min,infinity).
On the interior where original line i is active, a positive constant
multiple of either objective is

    g_i(tau)=sigma_i^2 (tau+c0)/(tau+F_i)^2,
    g_i'(tau)=sigma_i^2 (F_i-tau-2c0)/(tau+F_i)^3.

Because tau+F_i=sigma_i M(tau)>0 there, the only prospective stationary
point is

    tau_i=F_i-2c0.                                 (9)

Retain tau_min, all ACTUAL upper-envelope breakpoints in the domain, and
each (9) lying in its own closed active interval. Include endpoint ties.
As tau tends to infinity, both objectives tend to zero, whereas their
value at tau_min is positive. The continuous objectives therefore attain
a maximum at this finite candidate set. Since U=R^2 Tframe L with a
positive constant, their maximizing shifts coincide exactly.

The numerical child constructs this hull with long-double arithmetic
from the unchanged saved binary64 values, then evaluates EVERY candidate
with M=max_i((tau+F_i)/sigma_i) over the FULL original line list. It never
substitutes a single hull line for this final maximum. Exact floating
ties and near ties are reported separately; the latter use only the
reporting threshold 256*eps64*max(1,|M|). The threshold does not change
the hull, candidates, residuals or objective. It is not a rigorous bound.
An interval-interior full-line audit and signed affine/direct evaluation
differences are saved. Extended precision reduces roundoff but supplies
no interval certificate. All raw defects remain visible.
