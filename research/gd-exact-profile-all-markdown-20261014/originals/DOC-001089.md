# Three-step capacity with arbitrary signed terminal credit

Status: complete candidate proof, NOT verifier-accepted. No SDP/PEP or
fact_submit call. This closes the proposed three-step discovery domain if
the candidate survives verification. It does not settle the general signed
cone question or the original all-horizon normalized objective target.

## 1. Statement and definitions

Let h=(u,v,w) be a nonnegative three-step schedule, let T=1+u+v+w, and put
t=sqrt(2), lambda=1+t, T_0=lambda^2=3+2t. Along gradient descent on a convex
differentiable function f with 1-Lipschitz gradient and a minimizer, write
x_(i+1)=x_i-h_i g_i, g_i=grad f(x_i), f_i=f(x_i), and

 Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2,
 V_h=2sum_(i<3)h_i(f_i-f_3)+sum_(i<3)h_i(1-h_i)||g_i||^2
                                             -(T-1)T||g_3||^2,
 J_h=sum_(i<3)h_i Q_(i,3), E_theta=V_h-theta J_h.

Claim. If T>=T_0 and h differs from (t,2,t), there is a fixed, finite-dimensional
convex differentiable 1-smooth function, with a minimizer, whose full h-run has

              V_h<0 and Q_(i,3)=0 for every i<3.       (1)

Consequently, for EVERY real theta, universal validity of E_theta>=0 with
T>=T_0 forces h=(t,2,t). The same necessary conclusion holds for any intrinsic
certificate V_h+sum lambda_ij Q_ij whose values cancel, whose remainder is PSD
on the complete independent formal gradient space, and whose multipliers are
nonnegative except for arbitrary signs on incoming-terminal finite pairs.
No validity-versus-certifiability equivalence is assumed.

At h=(t,2,t), an explicit ordinary primitive certificate is given in Section 7.
Therefore, for each theta>=0, the exact maximum three-step capacity in the
E_theta-valid class and in its nonnegative-certificate class is T_0, uniquely
attained by Silver. The arbitrary-signed-certificate class has that same
maximum. In particular the conclusion applies throughout 0<=theta<1.

The proof includes the required domain reductions directly. C32 and earlier
max4 findings motivated them, but are not used as unverified premises.

## 2. Legal projection witnesses and the first four constraints

Here is a self-contained way to turn each finite array below into a fixed
legal function. Suppose its GD equations hold, its terminal point satisfies
x_3=g_3, and its assigned values are

 f_i=<g_i,x_i>-||g_i||^2/2.

Set y_i=x_i-g_i. Expansion gives

 Q_ij=<g_i-g_j,y_j>, Q_(i,3)=0,
 V_h=||x_0||^2-T^2||g_3||^2.                         (2)

If every finite Q_ij<=0 and <g_i,y_i>>=0, take K=conv{0,g_0,g_1,g_2,g_3}.
Those inequalities say precisely <y_j,z-g_j><=0 for every vertex z of K and
hence for every z in K. Thus g_j=P_K(x_j). The function

 F_K(x)=max_(z in K)[<x,z>-||z||^2/2]

interpolates all assigned values and gradients. The projection normal
inequalities imply <P_K(x)-P_K(x'),x-x'>>=||P_K(x)-P_K(x')||^2, so P_K is
1-Lipschitz. The maximizing formula gives
0<=F_K(x+d)-F_K(x)-<P_K(x),d><=<P_K(x+d)-P_K(x),d><=||d||^2,
which proves differentiability with gradient P_K. Thus F_K is globally convex
and 1-smooth; it has minimum F_K(0)=0. No unverified interpolation theorem is
needed for these arrays.

At any selected step b>1, with preceding and following sums P,Q, use derivative
clip(x,-(b-1)/(Q+1),1) and start at P+1. The preceding gradients are 1 and the
following gradients are -(b-1)/(Q+1), ending at that negative threshold.
The function obtained by integration has all the properties in (2). Its
energy yields the necessary cut inequality

              T(b-1)_+ <= (P+1)(Q+1).               (3)

For b<=1 this inequality is automatic. This includes schedules with zero
steps. The first and last cuts give

              u,w <= M(T):=2T/(T+1)<2.              (4)

For u,v,w>1 write a=u-1, A=u+1, r=v-1, D=w+1, eta=r/D.
The following additional inequalities are necessary if no witness (1) exists:

 F_Q=1-T^2 a^2 r^2(w-1)^2 >=0,                     (5)
 F_B=A^2-T^2 eta^2 >=0,                            (6)
 F_C=A^2-T^2 r^2(w-1)^2 >=0,                       (7)
 F_N=a u F_B+(1+eta)u^2(2-u^2) >=0.                (8)

For (5), use f(x)=x^2/2 and x_0=1. For (6), use the middle cut in (3),
whose x=(A,1,-r,-eta), g=(1,1,-eta,-eta). For (7), provided r(w-1)<=1,
use derivative min(x,1), start at A, and obtain
x=(A,1,-r,r(w-1)), g=(1,1,-r,r(w-1)). It is a fixed convex 1-smooth
function with minimum at0, and its terminal point is inside its quadratic
part. Each of these functions satisfies (2).

For (8), provided eta a<1, combine the middle-cut base from (6) with the
auxiliary array

 x_aux=(1,-a,ra,eta a), g_aux=(1,-a,eta a,eta a),
 f_aux,i=x_aux,i*g_aux,i-g_aux,i^2/2.

The auxiliary normals are (0,0,w eta a,0). Its only positive finite residual
is Q_aux,(0,2)=(1-eta a)w eta a. Every outgoing optimum inequality holds,
since each g_aux,i*y_aux,i is nonnegative. In the base array,
Q_base,(0,2)=-(1+eta)w eta, and all finite residuals are nonpositive.
Take their orthogonal sum with auxiliary scale sqrt(epsilon), where

                   epsilon=(1+eta)/[a(1-eta a)].

Residuals add, the sole positive auxiliary edge is canceled exactly, every
other finite edge is nonpositive, and every optimum condition holds.
The projection construction above realizes one fixed legal function. Its
energy is F_B+epsilon(1-T^2 eta^2 a^2)=F_N/[a(1-eta a)]; the denominator
is positive. The equality follows by 1-a^2 A^2=u^2(2-u^2).
The terminal point stays core, so every incoming-terminal residual is zero.

## 3. Complete reduction to the asymmetric region

Assume T>=T_0 and assume, for contradiction, that every applicable witness
above has nonnegative energy. First v>1: otherwise T<=2+2M(T) gives
T^2-5T-2<=0, whereas at T_0 this polynomial equals 2t>0 and it increases
thereafter. Thus r>0 and the middle cut applies, initially with A=u+1,D=w+1.
Since T=A+D+r, it gives

 T(T-A-D)<=AD<=(A+D)^2/4,
 T<=lambda(A+D)/2<=lambda(M(T)+1).

Hence T^2+(1-3lambda)T-lambda<=0. The polynomial increases for T>=T_0,
and its value at7 is 56-22lambda>0 because lambda<5/2. Thus T<7, and
u,w<M(7)=7/4. Both endpoints exceed1: if u<=1, then A<=2,D<11/4 and
r>T-19/4, so Tr>T(T-19/4)>=T_0(T_0-19/4)=11/4+5t/2>11/2>AD,
a contradiction. The proof for w is identical.

The extra hypotheses needed in Section 2 now hold everywhere in this box:

 r(w-1)<=A(w^2-1)/T<363/(64T_0)<1,
 eta a<=Aa/T=(u^2-1)/T<33/(16T_0)<1.                (9)

For the first numerical inequality, 363<64T_0 follows from
171<128sqrt(2), whose square is 29241<32768. Also

                   u+w>=2T/lambda-2>=2t.            (10)

If u<=t, then w>=t. The left-minus-right difference in the necessary
inequality Tr(w-1)<=A from (7) is at least
T_0(T_0-t-w-2)(w-1)-(t+1)=T_0(w-t)(2-w)>=0.
It is positive except when u=w=t,T=T_0; equality then gives v=2.
Thus every nonexceptional candidate has u>t.

If w>=t as well, consider q_T(u,w)=T(u-1)(w-1)(T-u-w-2) on
[t,M(T)]^2. It is strictly concave in each endpoint separately. Its minimum
is at a corner; strict concavity excludes equality at an interior endpoint
when corner minima have already been identified. At T_0, since
M(T_0)=1+t/2, the four corner values are 1,lambda/2,lambda/2,lambda/2,
with the value1 only at (t,t). All four corner values strictly increase with
T>=T_0: their factors are positive and increasing, including
T-t-M(T)-2 and T-2M(T)-2, whose derivatives are respectively
1-2/(T+1)^2 and 1-4/(T+1)^2. Thus (5) excludes this case except Silver.
Every other candidate has u>t>w.

For such endpoints, F_N strictly decreases as r>0 increases, with u,w fixed:
the negative squared term in F_B decreases and u^2(2-u^2)<0. At r=1,

 D^2 F_N/u=u^2 D^2+(4-2u^2-u^3)D-(u-1)(u+2)^2=:P_u(D).

This quadratic has one positive and one negative root. At D=lambda,
P_u(lambda)=-(lambda+1)u^3-2u^2+4(lambda+1)<0 for u>t: it vanishes
at t and has negative derivative in u>0. Its positive root is therefore
beyond lambda. Since D<lambda, F_N<0 when r=1, and then also for r>=1.
Necessarily r<1. We have proved, with every witness hypothesis checked,

       t<u<7/4, 1<v<2, 1<w<t, T_0<=T<7,
                       u+w>=2T/lambda-2.             (11)

## 4. The additional two-cut witness forces u<8/5

Here the different letters R=u-1, k=v-1, C=w+1, B=v+w+1=C+k+1 are used
to avoid confusing the first and second bridge. Thus T=B+R+1.
Set eta_1=R/B, zeta=k/C, p=-1/R. Consider the two scalar arrays

 x_base=(1,-R,-C eta_1,-eta_1),
 g_base=(1,-eta_1,-eta_1,-eta_1),
 x_aux=(p,1,-k,-zeta), g_aux=(p,1,-zeta,-zeta).

Both obey the same three GD steps; assign values by f=g*x-g^2/2.
Their normal arrays are respectively
(0,-(B-1)eta_1,-w eta_1,0) and (0,0,-w zeta,0).
The base is an actual clipped-derivative trajectory. Within (11),
R zeta<3/8<1. The auxiliary array's only positive residual is
Q_aux,(0,2)=w zeta(1/R-zeta); all its other finite residuals are zero
except Q_aux,(1,2)<0. Every optimum inequality holds in both arrays.
The base residual Q_base,(0,2)=-(1+eta_1)w eta_1 cancels that positive
edge when the orthogonal auxiliary scale is sqrt(epsilon_1), where

             epsilon_1=eta_1(1+eta_1)/[zeta(1/R-zeta)].

All other finite inequalities remain nonpositive. The projection realization
in Section 2 supplies a fixed globally legal function; its terminal point is
core and every incoming-terminal Q is zero. Its energy is

 V_mix=1-T^2 eta_1^2+epsilon_1(R^(-2)-T^2 zeta^2)
      =(C+kB)(B+R)/[B^2 k(C-Rk)]
                     *{C-Rk[1+R(T+1)]}.             (12)

The prefactor is positive. For an algebra audit, substitute
epsilon_1=R^2 C^2(B+R)/[B^2 k(C-Rk)], put the two energy terms over
that denominator, and use B=C+k+1,T=B+R+1. Thus nonnegative energy forces

                    R k[1+R(T+1)]<=C.              (13)

In (11), w<t and T>=T_0 imply
k=T-2-u-w>1+t-u=t-R, C<lambda. Therefore (13) implies

            R(t-R)[1+(T_0+1)R]<lambda.              (14)

The function H(R)=R(t-R)(1+(T_0+1)R) strictly increases on
t-1<R<3/4. Indeed its derivative is
t-2R+(T_0+1)R(2t-3R); here t-2R>-1/10, T_0+1>6,
R>2/5 and 2t-3R>1/2, so H'(R)>11/10>0. These bounds follow from
7/5<t<3/2. At R=3/5,

           H(3/5)=(201t+27)/125>1+t,

because the difference is (76t-98)/125>0. Thus (14) forces R<3/5,
or u<8/5. This is the extra information absent from the four-test screen.

## 5. The quadratic witness forces a small imbalance

Fix the u,w now under consideration and reduce the formal capacity to T_0,
that is, put v_0=T_0-1-u-w. Write

 q=t-1, delta=u-t, z=t-w, d=delta-z=u+w-2t.

By (10), (11), and Section 4,

       0<delta<8/5-t<1/5, 0<z<=delta,
                   0<=d<delta, v_0=2-d>1.           (15)

For fixed u,w, the product T(u-1)(w-1)(T-u-w-2) increases with T when
T-u-w-2>0. Thus (5) at the actual capacity implies its version at T_0.
Since T_0 q^2=1, that version is

             (q+delta)(q-delta+d)(1-d)<=q^2.

Expanding and rearranging gives

 d[(q+delta)(1-d)-q^2+delta^2]<=delta^2.

Because d<=delta, the bracket is at least (1-q)(q+delta)>0. Hence

 d/delta <= delta/[(1-q)(q+delta)] < 4/7 < 3/5.     (16)

For the strict bound, the fraction increases with delta, so delta<1/5
reduces it to 1/[(1-q)(5q+1)]. Since 2/5<q<1/2,
(1-q)(5q+1)>=7/4: subtracting 7/4 gives
(1/2-q)(5q-3/2)>=0. This proves (16) by rational bounds alone.

## 6. The mixed witness contradicts that small imbalance

For fixed u,w and u>t, write eta=(T-u-w-2)/(w+1). An exact expression is

 F_N/u = u^2+u-1+u(2-u^2)eta-(u-1)T^2 eta^2.       (17)

It strictly decreases with T in the relevant range, because eta increases,
u(2-u^2)<0, u-1>0, and eta>0. Thus F_N>=0 at the actual T would imply
F_N>=0 at T_0. At T_0, hold u=t+delta fixed and vary d; then
w=t-delta+d and eta=(1-d)/(lambda-delta+d). This eta strictly decreases
with d, while the right side of (17) strictly decreases with eta. Thus
F_N/u at T_0 strictly increases with d.

By (16), it suffices to show negativity at the LARGER comparison value
d=3delta/5, that is, w=t-2delta/5 and v_0=2-3delta/5.
Let D=w+1,A=u+1,r=v_0-1 at this comparison point. The numerator

 N(delta)=D^2 F_N/u
         =(u-1)(A^2 D^2-T_0^2 r^2)
                              +(T_0-A)D u(2-u^2)

expands exactly to

 N(delta)=delta[ -4-18t/5
          +(376+294t)delta/25
          -(128+70t)delta^2/25
          +(29-3t)delta^3/25
          -6delta^4/25 ].                           (18)

For 0<delta<1/5, discard the two negative higher-order terms and use
delta<=1/5, delta^3<=1/125, and 29-3t<29. The bracket is at most

 -4-18t/5+(376+294t)/125+29/3125
                   =-(3071+3900t)/3125<0.           (19)

Thus N(delta)<0, contradicting the required nonnegative F_N at T_0.
This eliminates every candidate in (11). Consequently the original assumption
that all the listed legal witnesses have nonnegative energy is impossible
for T>=T_0 outside Silver. A negative witness from this finite explicit list
therefore supplies (1), with its whole trajectory and all terminal slacks.

## 7. Attainment and precise consequences

At h=(t,2,t), use the following nonnegative finite-pair multipliers:

 lambda_01=2, lambda_10=2lambda,
 lambda_21=2t lambda, lambda_31=lambda_13=2lambda,
 lambda_23=2lambda^2+2t lambda, lambda_32=2lambda^3,

with every unlisted entry zero. Expansion of the four value coefficients
and ten independent gradient monomials, using t^2=2, gives

                       V_h+sum lambda_ij Q_ij=0.

Hence V_h>=0 on every legal trajectory. For theta>=0, adding theta h_i
to every incoming-terminal multiplier gives a nonnegative certificate for
E_theta with the same zero remainder. This proves all the attainment claims.

If a putative arbitrary-signed incoming-terminal certificate exists for a
non-Silver h with T>=T_0, evaluate it on (1): the signed terms vanish, all
other multiplier terms are nonpositive, and the PSD remainder would be
at most V_h<0. This is a contradiction on the full formal matrix.

The result is an exact three-step capacity statement, not a claim that
E_theta-validity always forces ordinary primitivity below this capacity or
for longer schedules. It supplies no new upper schedule and no asymptotic
comparison with U_n^ZJ. All mathematics in this note remains unverified.
