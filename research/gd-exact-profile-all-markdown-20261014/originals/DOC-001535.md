## statement
Orthogonal-moment obstruction for randomized gradient-descent adversaries. Define c_0=0 and c_{k+1}=c_k+2+2 sqrt(c_k+1) for all integers k>=0, and define beta_n=4/(1+sqrt(1+2c_n))^2. Let n>=1 be an integer, L>0, and h=(h_0,...,h_{n-1}) in [0,infinity)^n be any predetermined schedule with finite entries. Consider any probability distribution on legal instances in a common finite-dimensional Euclidean space: each realization consists of a convex differentiable f:R^d->R with L-Lipschitz gradient, a minimizer x_star, and an initial point x_0 with ||x_0-x_star||=1. For each realization let x_{i+1}=x_i-(h_i/L) grad f(x_i), 0<=i<n, and set g_i=grad f(x_i)/L, including g_n. Assume all data used in expectations are measurable and E<g_i,g_j>=0 whenever 0<=i<j<=n. Then
E[2(f(x_n)-f(x_star))/(L||x_0-x_star||^2)] <= beta_n <= 2/n^2.
The deterministic conclusion follows by taking a point-mass distribution: every legal trajectory whose n+1 gradients are mutually orthogonal has normalized last-iterate objective error at most beta_n. Moreover n^2 beta_n tends to 2. Consequently, for any positive comparison sequence V_n with n^2 V_n tending to infinity, every family of such randomized adversaries has expected normalized error divided by V_n tending to zero, uniformly over schedules and distributions satisfying the assumptions. This bounds the strength of the orthogonal-moment adversary class; it is not an upper bound on the unrestricted worst-case error W_n(h), and it does not resolve the project's unrestricted minimax target.

## proof
All expectations below refer to the distribution in the statement. No external result is invoked without a derivation.

1. Normalize and prove the required smooth-convex inequality.
In each realization define F(x)=(f(x)-f(x_star))/L. This is convex, has 1-Lipschitz gradient, has minimum value0 at x_star, and its gradient at x_i is g_i. Its gradient at a minimizer is0: if a differentiable function has a minimum, each directional derivative there is0.
For any differentiable function H with 1-Lipschitz gradient and any vectors u,v, the fundamental theorem of calculus along the line segment gives
H(u+v)-H(u)-<grad H(u),v>
=integral_0^1 <grad H(u+tv)-grad H(u),v>dt <= ||v||^2/2.
For fixed y define H(z)=F(z)-<grad F(y),z>. Convexity and grad H(y)=0 imply H(y)<=H(z) for all z. Apply the preceding inequality at u=x and v=-(grad F(x)-grad F(y)). Since grad H(x)=grad F(x)-grad F(y), this gives
H(y)<=H(x)-||grad F(x)-grad F(y)||^2/2.
Rearranging proves, for every x,y,
F(x)>=F(y)+<grad F(y),x-y>+||grad F(x)-grad F(y)||^2/2. (I)
All uses of smooth convex interpolation below are this necessary inequality, now proved directly.

2. Integrability and notation.
Write X=x_0-x_star, p_i=E F(x_i), q_i=E||g_i||^2, and a_i=E<X,g_i> for 0<=i<=n. The notation p_i denotes an expected value, whereas F and g_i can be random. Since ||X||=1 in every realization, E||X||^2=1.
The recursion and the Lipschitz gradient imply
||x_i-x_star||<=product_{t=0}^{i-1}(1+h_t)
and ||g_i||<=||x_i-x_star||. Empty products equal1. The line-segment bound in part1 at x_star gives 0<=F(x_i)<=||x_i-x_star||^2/2. Thus every expectation used here is finite even though the finite steps have no uniform upper bound.
If some q_i=0, nonnegativity implies g_i=0 almost surely. Then x_i is a minimizer almost surely by convexity, and all later iterates equal x_i. Therefore p_n=0, and the required inequality follows since beta_n>0. It remains to handle q_i>0 for every i.

3. Lower bounds forced on the initial displacement.
From (I) against the minimizer, p_n>=q_n/2. Set b=p_n-q_n/2>=0.
Apply (I) with x=x_i, y=x_{i+1}. The GD identity is x_i-x_{i+1}=h_i g_i. Expected orthogonality gives E<g_{i+1},g_i>=0 and E||g_i-g_{i+1}||^2=q_i+q_{i+1}. Hence
p_i-p_{i+1}>=(q_i+q_{i+1})/2
for each 0<=i<n. Summing these inequalities from i through n-1 yields
p_i>=p_n+q_i/2+sum_{k=i+1}^{n-1}q_k+q_n/2
when i<n.
Apply (I) with x=x_star and y=x_i. It gives
E<g_i,x_i-x_star>>=p_i+q_i/2.
The recursion gives x_i-x_star=X-sum_{t<i}h_t g_t. Expected orthogonality therefore gives E<g_i,x_i-x_star>=a_i. For every i, including i=n, we conclude
a_i >= T_i, where T_i=b+sum_{k=i}^n q_k>0. (II)
For i<n this follows by combining the last two displayed inequalities; for i=n it is a_n>=p_n+q_n/2=b+q_n=T_n.

4. A finite-dimensional second-moment inequality.
Expand the nonnegative expectation of a squared norm:
0<=E||X-sum_{i=0}^n(a_i/q_i)g_i||^2
=1-2sum_{i=0}^n a_i^2/q_i+sum_{i=0}^n a_i^2/q_i
=1-sum_{i=0}^n a_i^2/q_i.
The cross terms in the double sum vanish by expected orthogonality. Since a_i>=T_i>0 and q_i>0, (II) gives
1>=sum_{i=0}^n T_i^2/q_i. (III)
This step works directly in the original finite-dimensional space and does not require that any random sample's gradients be orthogonal.

5. Eliminate the intermediate gradient energies exactly.
For 0<=k<=n, claim
sum_{i=0}^{k-1} T_i^2/q_i >= c_k T_k,
where the empty sum for k=0 is0. The claim holds at k=0. Suppose k<n and it holds at k. Since T_k=q_k+T_{k+1},
c_k T_k+T_k^2/q_k
=(c_k+1)q_k+(c_k+2)T_{k+1}+T_{k+1}^2/q_k.
The nonnegativity of
(sqrt(c_k+1)*sqrt(q_k)-T_{k+1}/sqrt(q_k))^2
bounds the sum of the first and third terms below by 2sqrt(c_k+1)T_{k+1}. Therefore the last display is at least c_{k+1}T_{k+1}, proving the induction.
Taking k=n in this claim and adding the final term gives from (III)
1>=c_n T_n+T_n^2/q_n
=q_n z(z+c_n),
where z=T_n/q_n=1+b/q_n>=1.
Since 2p_n=2b+q_n=q_n(2z-1), it follows that
2p_n <= (2z-1)/(z(z+c_n)).
Put r=(1+sqrt(1+2c_n))/2. Then c_n=2r(r-1) and r>=1. The exact identity
z(z+c_n)-r^2(2z-1)=(z-r)^2>=0
implies
(2z-1)/(z(z+c_n))<=1/r^2=beta_n.
Because ||x_0-x_star||^2=1 in each realization, 2p_n is the expected normalized error in the statement.

6. Estimate and asymptotics for beta_n.
An induction gives c_k>=k^2: it holds at0; if it holds at k, then
c_{k+1}=c_k+2+2sqrt(c_k+1)>=k^2+2+2k>=(k+1)^2.
The relation c_n=2r(r-1)<=2r^2 now gives beta_n=1/r^2<=2/c_n<=2/n^2 for n>=1.
For the asymptotic assertion put s_k=sqrt(c_k+1), so s_0=1 and
s_{k+1}=sqrt((s_k+1)^2+1).
Rationalization gives
s_{k+1}-s_k=1+1/(sqrt((s_k+1)^2+1)+s_k+1).
Thus s_k>=k+1 and
s_{k+1}-s_k<=1+1/[2(k+2)].
Summing shows
k+1<=s_k<=k+1+(1/2)sum_{j=2}^{k+1}1/j
<=k+1+(1/2)log(k+1),
where the last bound follows by integrating 1/t on intervals [j-1,j]. Consequently s_k/k tends to1 and c_k/k^2 tends to1. Substitution into beta_n gives n^2 beta_n tending to2.
Finally, 0<=expected normalized error/V_n<=beta_n/V_n, which tends to0 whenever n^2V_n tends to infinity. Every bound depends only on n, giving the asserted uniformity. No interchange of infimum and expectation has occurred.

## intuition
Independent or centered fresh random gradient directions force too much initial-distance energy through global interpolation. This target-specific impossibility result rules out expected-orthogonality adversaries before pursuing correlated nonlinear geometry; it is the worker's one selected initial-wave verification submission.
