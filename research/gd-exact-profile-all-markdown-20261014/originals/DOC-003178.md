## 3. Necessity of the prefix formulas for EVERY positive representation

It is necessary to show that the familiar factor recurrence covers
the entire declared class, rather than only one convenient construction.
For 0<=k<r put

 u_k=(sum_(i<k)h_iU_i)/U_k,
 v_k=(sum_(j=k+2)^r h_(j-1)D_j)/D_(k+1).

The cut flow telescopes, using(1.3), to

 sum_(i<=k)d_i=p_(k+1)(1-t_k+v_k-u_k)=P_(k+1)-1.        (3.1)

Indeed the outgoing cut sum is p_(k+1)(1+v_k), while its incoming
cut sum is p_(k+1)(t_k+u_k). Also u_0=0 and v_(r-1)=0.
All v_k with k<r-1 are strictly positive.

We prove the exact formulas

 u_k=(P_k-R_k)/(1+R_(k+1)),
 v_k=(P_(k+1)-R_(k+1))/(R_(k+1)-1).                     (3.2)

At k=0 the first is u_0=0. Substituting p_1=(R_1^2-1)/2 and
t_0=1/R_1 into(3.1) gives the second. Its positive value when r>=2
already implies P_1>R_1.

For the induction step let 1<=k<r and abbreviate

 P=P_k, R=R_k, Y=R_(k+1), q=U_k/U_(k-1)>0.

The preceding v formula and positivity give P>R>1. The recurrences
and the product p_k=U_(k-1)D_k give

 u_k=(u_(k-1)+h_(k-1))/q,
 v_(k-1)=[p_(k+1)/(p_k q)](h_k+v_k).

The induction hypothesis supplies

 u_(k-1)+h_(k-1)=(P+R)/(1+R),
 v_(k-1)=(P-R)/(R-1).

By(3.1), v_k=u_k+delta_k with

 delta_k=2(P+t_k)/(Y^2-1)-1+t_k,
 p_(k+1)(h_k+delta_k)=P+RY.

Eliminating u_k and v_k gives the following quadratic for q:

 (1+R)(P-R)q^2-2(P+RY)q
                   -(Y^2-1)(P+R)/(1+R)=0.              (3.3)

Its two roots are exactly

 (P+R)(1+Y)/[(1+R)(P-R)],        (1-Y)/(1+R).

This follows by using
(P+RY)^2+(P^2-R^2)(Y^2-1)=(PY+R)^2
in the quadratic formula. The second root is negative, whereas q>0.
Thus the first is the only possible ratio. Inserting it gives
u_k=(P-R)/(1+Y). Adding delta_k gives the v formula, since

 (P-t_kY)(Y-1)+2(P+t_k)+(t_k-1)(Y^2-1)
                        =(Y+1)(P+1+t_k-Y).

This finishes the induction. Consequently every representation obeys

 1<R_k<P_k for 1<=k<r,
 U_k/U_(k-1)=(P_k+R_k)(1+R_(k+1))
                /[(1+R_k)(P_k-R_k)],                  (3.4)
 D_(k+1)/D_k=(P_k-R_k)(R_(k+1)-1)
                /[(R_k-1)(P_k+R_k)].                  (3.5)

The strict prefix inequalities follow from v_(k-1)>0. No uniqueness
of the full unrestricted primitive certificate has been claimed; only
the ratios inside the explicitly declared two-sequence family are forced.

## 5. The new consequence: every contiguous block is product-admissible

For each nonempty contiguous interval I=[l,k) of physical indices,
define its mass, capacity, and product by

 H_I=sum_(i=l)^(k-1)h_i,
 K_I=1+H_I,
 q_I=product_(i=l)^(k-1)t_i.

We prove

                     K_I q_I>=1.                        (5.1)

Equality holds for the whole word. If k<r, the inequality is strict.
This is a consequence of the declared factor class, not a necessary
condition asserted for every primitive word.

For l=0, q_I=1/R_k, and(5.1) is P_k>=R_k, already proved.
Now assume 1<=l<k<=r, and write

 P=P_l>1, R=R_l>1, H=H_I>0, q=q_I,
 P_k=P+H, R_k=R/q,
 b_j=(P_j-R_j)/(R_j-1) for 1<=j<=r.

Formula(3.2) identifies b_j=v_(j-1), including b_r=0. The weighted
tail sum defining v_(l-1) decomposes exactly as

 b_l=sum_(i=l)^(k-1) h_i D_(i+1)/D_l +(D_k/D_l)b_k.

By D monotonicity and b_k>=0 this gives b_l<=H+b_k. Since
b_l+1=(P-1)/(R-1) and b_k+1=q(P+H-1)/(R-q), both denominators
are positive. Multiplication and cancellation yield

                 q>=1-H(R-1)/(P-1).                     (5.2)

The other endpoint condition R_k<=P_k gives R<=q(P+H). Combining
this with(5.2), whose right side decreases in R, yields

 q>=1-H[q(P+H)-1]/(P-1),
 q[P-1+H(P+H)]>=P-1+H.

Here P-1+H(P+H)=(H+1)(P-1+H). Dividing by P-1+H>0 proves
q>=1/(1+H), which is(5.1). If k<r, then R_k<P_k, so the second
inequality is strict and(5.1) is strict as claimed.

For a suffix [l,r), equality in(5.1) holds exactly when
D_l=D_(l+1)=...=D_r. Indeed the terminal prefix equality is exact,
and equality in the tail estimate with b_r=0 requires every positive
summand h_i D_(i+1)/D_l to have D_(i+1)=D_l. This also checks the
possibility of zero multipliers on the boundary of the factor class.

## 6. Exact extremal theorem for the larger hereditary scalar class

This section is a separate elementary proof, included here in full.
For any r>=1, call a positive vector t=(t_0,...,t_(r-1)) admissible
if, with h_i=1+t_i, it satisfies

 K_I q_I>=1 for EVERY nonempty contiguous I,
 A product_(i<r)t_i=1,       A=1+sum_(i<r)(1+t_i).        (6.1)

The empty word has capacity1. Define the benchmark kernel and recurrence
as in accepted04219554e41d9750:

 Omega(a,c)=(a+c+sqrt((a+c)^2+4ac))/2,
 C_0=1,
 C_r=max_(0<=j<r)Omega(C_j,C_(r-j-1)).                   (6.2)

We prove that the maximum capacity over(6.1) is exactly C_r. Moreover
every capacity maximizer admits a recursive maximizing split at a single
bridge index. This describes maximizers of(6.1), not every admissible
vector and not every primitive certificate.

First, maxima exist whenever the admissible set is nonempty. A singleton
condition is t_i(2+t_i)>=1, hence t_i>=c_*=sqrt(2)-1>0. Global equality
implies

 1=A product_i t_i >= A t_j c_*^(r-1) >= t_j c_*^(r-1).

Thus c_*<=t_j<=c_*^(1-r). The conditions are closed on this compact
box. The capacity is continuous, so it attains a maximum.

Fix such a maximizing vector. An interval is called tight when K_I q_I=1.
For notational convenience set K_empty=q_empty=1. For each interval put

 F_I=log(K_I)+sum_(i in I)log(t_i),

so F_I>=0, and tightness is F_I=0. Two tight intervals cannot overlap
without one containing the other. Indeed, if their consecutive left-only,
common, and right-only pieces have physical masses a>0,b>0,c>0, then

 F_I+F_J-F_(I union J)-F_(I intersection J)
 =log[(1+a+b)(1+b+c)/((1+b)(1+a+b+c))]>0,                (6.3)

because the numerator minus denominator inside the logarithm is ac>0.
If I,J were both tight, their left side would be nonpositive by(6.1),
a contradiction. Likewise two adjacent disjoint nonempty tight intervals
of masses a,c cannot occur: their union is an interval, and

 F_I+F_J-F_(I union J)=log[(1+a)(1+c)/(1+a+c)]>0.

Consider now the inclusion-maximal PROPER tight intervals. The first
observation makes them disjoint; the second says any two are separated
by at least one index outside them. Call an index uncovered when it is
in none of these maximal proper intervals, equivalently in no proper
tight interval at all.

There must be at least one uncovered index. Otherwise the proper maximal
intervals would cover the word. A single one cannot cover it because it
is proper, and multiple disjoint ones would then have an adjacent pair,
which was just ruled out.

There cannot be two uncovered indices u,v. To prove this, perturb only
t_u,t_v by t_u -> t_u+epsilon, t_v -> t_v-epsilon. Choose a sufficiently
small nonzero epsilon with epsilon(t_v-t_u)<=0. Positivity persists,
the total sum and A remain unchanged, every proper tight interval is
unchanged, and all other proper interval inequalities remain strict by
continuity and finiteness. But

 (t_u+epsilon)(t_v-epsilon)
     =t_u t_v+epsilon(t_v-t_u)-epsilon^2<t_u t_v.

The global product condition therefore becomes strictly less than1.
Starting at this perturbed vector, increase t_u alone by a positive
amount delta until global equality is restored. Such a finite delta
exists: the global product times A is a strictly increasing continuous
function of t_u and tends to infinity as t_u tends to infinity. Every
proper interval expression is nondecreasing under this increase, so all
its inequalities remain valid. The resulting admissible vector has
capacity A+delta>A, contradicting maximality.

There is therefore exactly ONE uncovered index, say j. All indices to
its left, if any, constitute a single maximal proper tight interval,
because multiple maximal intervals would need another uncovered index
between them. The same holds on its right. Consequently the left word
[0,j) and the right word [j+1,r) are each either empty or saturated
admissible words. Let their capacities be a,c and let d=t_j>0. Their
products are 1/a,1/c. The total capacity and saturation read

                  A=a+c+d,       A d=ac.                (6.4)

Thus A=Omega(a,c). Induction on r gives a<=C_j and c<=C_(r-j-1).
The kernel is increasing in both arguments, so

                  A<=Omega(C_j,C_(r-j-1))<=C_r.         (6.5)

This proves the upper bound for every admissible vector, because A was
a global maximizer. In an equality case strict monotonicity of Omega
for positive arguments forces the two children also to be maximizers,
which proves the asserted recursive structure of maximizers.

For completeness the bound is attained within the scalar class. Join
two saturated admissible child words of capacities a,c by h_bridge=1+d,
where d>0 solves d(a+c+d)=ac. An interval inside a child is admissible.
For an interval crossing the bridge, its left suffix and right prefix
have capacities x<=a,y<=c, where an empty portion has capacity1.
Its product is at least d/(xy), and its capacity is x+y+d. Because

 xy/(x+y+d) <= ac/(a+c+d)=d,

the interval is admissible. The displayed monotonicity follows directly
from the partial derivatives y(y+d)/(x+y+d)^2 and x(x+d)/(x+y+d)^2,
which are positive. Full saturation follows from d/(ac)=1/(a+c+d).
Starting from empty children and using a maximizing split in(6.2) now
constructs a saturated admissible word of capacity C_r. These are also
the actual original primitive compositions supplied by the accepted
certificate join. No auxiliary continuous phase or different recurrence
has replaced the original all-split C recurrence.

## 7. Strict capacity loss for the free positive-factor class at r=5,6

                        A<=Omega(1,C_(r-1)).             (7.1)

We now give exact inequalities showing this is smaller than C_r for
r=5,6. Let lambda=1+sqrt(2) and x=C_2. The balanced primitive scalar

 C_3=lambda^2, C_4=Omega(lambda,x),

 x^2-(1+lambda)x-lambda=0.

                 12/5<lambda<17/7,       4<x<81/20      (7.2)

             A<C_5 when r=5,    A<C_6 when r=6.          (7.3)
