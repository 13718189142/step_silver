## 1. Actual functions, the enlarged dependence class, and the result

Let F be an everywhere finite convex differentiable function on a finite
Euclidean space, with1-Lipschitz gradient and a nonempty minimizer set.
For a supplied word h of length n consider its actual trajectory

    X_(i+1)=X_i-h_i g_i,  g_i=grad F(X_i),
    f_i=F(X_i)-min F,  q_i=||g_i||^2,
    R_init=dist(X_0,argmin F)>0,  V=2f_n/R_init^2.

All directed interpolation inequalities concern this ONE function.
The actual terminal gradient is unrestricted. If any queried gradient
vanishes then all later iterates minimize F and V=0. In the remaining
case define, at the queried indices,

    delta_i=f_i-q_i/2,  Jval_i=f_i+q_i/2,
    kval_i=Jval_i/q_i=1+delta_i/q_i.

For an included minimizer chosen as origin define separately

    eta_i=<X_i,g_i>/q_i,
    z_i=<X_i,g_i>-f_i-q_i/2.

The optimum rows give delta_i>=0 and z_i>=0, and

    kval_i=eta_i-z_i/q_i.                         (1)

Thus a restriction on kval is a restriction on actual values, not on
support about an arbitrarily named minimizer. No restriction on eta_i,
z_i, anchor density, signs of initial coordinates, or dimension is made.

Use the ORIGINAL enclosing-m words, with parameters fixed throughout
every recursive child:

    beta=1-1/m, c=sigma(1+1/m), a=beta/c,

    1+a/2 <= kval_i <= 1+a  for every queried i.  (2)

## 2. Original rows and exact remote slack transport

The accepted original residual is

    Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,X_j-X_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2<=0. (7)

For a general step h_i=1+d_i>1, adding both adjacent rows gives

    q_(i+1)/q_i <=d_i+(d_i-1)t_i.

    (t_i+1)(t_i-d_i)<=0,  0<=t_i<=d_i.             (8)

The nonnegative lower bound uses the assumed adjacent sign. In
particular at a shortest step q_(i+1)<=a q_i.

The reverse adjacent slack, a different quantity from z_i, is

    ell_rev_i=-Q_(i+1,i)
       =delta_i-delta_(i+1)+d_i t_i q_i-q_(i+1)>=0. (9)

Suppose for this paragraph the queried unit directions g_i/sqrt(q_i)
have the FULL Markov product law: their inner product at indices r<s
is the product of the adjacent normalized inner products from r to s.
Set p_(r,s)=product_(k=r)^(s-1)(-t_k), with p_(s,s)=1.
The amplitude ratios telescope to give <g_r,g_s>=q_r p_(r,s).
For i+1<j<n, expanding(7) and the GD recursion
gives the exact transport identity

    Q_(j,i)-Q_(j,i+1)
      =(delta_(i+1)-delta_i)(1-p_(i+1,j))
                         -ell_rev_i p_(i+1,j).      (10)

Indeed the difference before substituting(9) is

    delta_(i+1)-delta_i
         +p_(i+1,j)[q_(i+1)-d_i t_i q_i],

which equals(10). In particular the first remote reverse row is

    Q_(i+2,i)=-ell_rev_(i+1)
       +(1+t_(i+1))(delta_(i+1)-delta_i)
       +t_(i+1)ell_rev_i.                          (11)

Thus adjacent reverse equalities at i and i+1 force
delta_(i+1)<=delta_i. With a constant kval>1, they force
q_(i+1)<=q_i; hence t_i>1 is then impossible by Cauchy--Schwarz.
Attempting to saturate adjacent rows while using a strong projection
omits exactly the reserve increase charged by(11).

Equation(11) alone is not assumed sufficient for legality. The

## 6. The complete norm and the actual closest distance

For the full product-correlated subclass, write A_i=sqrt(q_i) and
u_i=g_i/A_i, for0<=i<n. Its correlations are real numbers rho_i in[-1,0]
with <u_i,u_j>=product_(k=i)^(j-1)rho_k. Actual real coordinates are

    u_0=e_0,
    u_(i+1)=rho_i u_i+sqrt(1-rho_i^2)e_(i+1),       (21)

where each nondegenerate innovation is orthogonal to the past. The
literal finite sign law is

    P(s_0,...,s_(n-1))=2^(-n)
                     product_(i<n-1)(1+rho_i s_i s_(i+1)),
    s_i in{-1,1}.                                 (22)

Summing the last sign successively proves normalization. Equivalently,
start with a fair sign and independent bond signs of means rho_i; this
gives the stated products. Interior rho give positive probability to
every sign word. This law is not a substitute for interpolation.

Conversely the product identities imply that
(u_(i+1)-rho_i u_i)/sqrt(1-rho_i^2) is a unit vector orthogonal to all
earlier u_j, by expanding its inner products. Thus(21) also describes
the canonical coordinates of any such product law.

Let0 be any included minimizer and let X_perp be the component of X_0
perpendicular to every queried gradient. Since previous gradients are
orthogonal to each new innovation, its initial coefficient
w_(i+1)=<X_0,e_(i+1)> satisfies

    eta_(i+1)A_(i+1)=rho_i(eta_i-h_i)A_i
                            +sqrt(1-rho_i^2)w_(i+1).

The initial e_0 coefficient is eta_0 A_0. Orthogonal decomposition
therefore gives the COMPLETE identity

    ||X_0||^2=eta_0^2 q_0+||X_perp||^2
      +sum_(i:|rho_i|<1)
         [eta_(i+1)A_(i+1)-rho_i(eta_i-h_i)A_i]^2
                                             /(1-rho_i^2). (23)

At rho_i=-1 the numerator must vanish and there is no new summand.
Every dense initial coefficient and any perpendicular reserve is paid.
This is a norm about the included minimizer, not automatically the true
closest-minimizer distance. For the whole-class proof we instead use

    q_0<=R_init^2,                                 (24)

which follows from gradient Lipschitzness between X_0 and EVERY minimizer,
followed by taking the infimum. Hence the proof neither drops any initial
cost nor incorrectly declares a conveniently chosen minimizer closest.

their true distances are computed from all their coordinates.
