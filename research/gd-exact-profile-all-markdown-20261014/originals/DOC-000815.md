# A full log-periodic staircase class is controlled by scalar flow time

Status: complete scoped candidate proof, not verifier-accepted. No new verifier
call. Unlike the earlier geometric-grid relay bound, this result puts no
restriction on the crossing pattern, knot visits, or gradients within ordinary
blocks. It covers the entire explicitly specified function class below, with
parameters and finite realizations allowed to depend on the horizon. It does
not cover every scalar convex smooth function or the unrestricted W_n.

## 1. The recursive schedules and a legal multiscale derivative

Set c=13/5, a=c^(-17), b_u=a c^u, H_0=(), and

    H_(m+1)=(H_m,b_m,H_m),
    s_u=sum H_u=a(c^u-2^u)/(c-2).

Fix integers m>=r>=17, and write b=b_r, S=s_r, and J=m-r. Put eta=13/8.
Since (10/13)^3<1/2 and (10/13)^2<4/5,
(10/13)^17<1/40. Therefore S>=eta b.

Choose, independently for each ray sigma in {+,-}, a positive phase theta_sigma
and a parameter ell_sigma satisfying

    b <= ell_sigma <= 1+(S-log c)/(c-1).                     (1)

This interval is nonempty: at ell=b its associated expression
(c-1)(b-1)+log c is smaller than S, since
eta-(c-1)=1/40 and log c<1<c-1. Define

    u_sigma=c ell_sigma-(c-1),
    M_(sigma,z)=theta_sigma c^z,          z any integer.

On ray sigma, prescribe gradient magnitude G_sigma as follows. For each z,
let M=M_(sigma,z) and put

    G_sigma(R)=M,
       ell_sigma M <= R <= u_sigma M;

    G_sigma(R)=R-(u_sigma-1)M,
       u_sigma M <= R <= c ell_sigma M.                     (2)

The second interval is the ramp from gradient M to cM. Its outer endpoint is
the next plateau's inner endpoint. These intervals cover every positive R;
their endpoints agree. Set G_sigma(0)=0.

The profile is continuous, nondecreasing, and 1-Lipschitz, with slopes zero
or one. It satisfies

    G_sigma(cR)=c G_sigma(R),
    0<=G_sigma(R)<=R/ell_sigma<=R/b.                        (3)

For the secant inequality on a ramp, write G=R-(u_sigma-1)M. The ratio G/R
increases to 1/ell_sigma at the ramp's outer endpoint; on a plateau the ratio
is at most its inner-endpoint value 1/ell_sigma. Thus (3) holds everywhere.
Continuity at zero follows from (3).

Define g(x)=G_+(x) for x>=0 and g(x)=-G_-(-x) for x<=0, and define

    f(x)=integral_0^x g(t) dt.

This is one globally fixed convex differentiable 1-smooth function, with
f(0)=0=min f. Across opposite rays, the Lipschitz inequality follows from
G_+(R)+G_-(T)<=R+T. The phases and parameters of the two rays need not agree.
There is no fixed quadratic neighborhood of zero; the knots accumulate there.

## 2. Exact areas and finite versions of the same trajectory

Write ell=ell_sigma and

    K_sigma=1/2+(ell-1)/(c+1).

At the inner endpoint ell M of any plateau,

    f(sigma ell M)=K_sigma M^2.                            (4)

All ramps below that endpoint together traverse gradient magnitudes from
zero to M and contribute M^2/2. The lower plateaus contribute

    (c-1)(ell-1)M^2 sum_(j>=1)c^(-2j)
       =[(ell-1)/(c+1)]M^2,

which proves (4). On the plateau itself,

    f(sigma R)=K_sigma M^2+M(R-ell M).                       (5)

On the following ramp, start with (5) at R=u_sigma M and add
[G_sigma(R)^2-M^2]/2. These are complete areas, including every inner level.
In particular f(sigma cR)=c^2 f(sigma R).

For any one finite nonzero trajectory, a finite-knot function can realize
exactly the same gradients and values. On each ray choose an inner plateau
endpoint ell M below every nonzero sampled radius. Replace the profile below
it by a zero segment up to

    delta=[c/(c+1)](ell-1)M,

then a slope-one ramp of length M, then a plateau at M up to ell M. The
endpoint gradient remains M, and the enclosed area is

    ell M^2-delta M-M^2/2=K_sigma M^2.

Thus all larger-radius gradients and values remain unchanged. Extend the
outermost needed plateau to a cap beyond all sampled radii. This produces
a globally legal finite-knot function and the identical full GD trajectory,
with the same fixed minimizer zero and the same initial-distance normalization.
The number of retained levels may grow with the horizon. This is a trajectory
preserving truncation; arbitrary unrelated core replacements are not claimed
to preserve the bound proved below.

## 3. Every ordinary block contracts by one complete radial period

For either ray, the exact flow time across one multiplicative period is

    integral_(R/c)^R dt/G_sigma(t)
        = tau_sigma :=(c-1)(ell_sigma-1)+log c.              (6)

It is independent of R. Indeed G_sigma(ct)=cG_sigma(t) makes this integral
periodic in log R with a full-period integration window. Evaluating from
ell_sigma M to c ell_sigma M gives plateau time u_sigma-ell_sigma and ramp
time log c, which proves (6). Condition (1) says tau_sigma<=S.

Every step of H_r is at most b/c. By (3), such a step h takes a nonzero
radius R to a strictly positive smaller radius R'=R-hG_sigma(R), with
R'>=(1-1/c)R. Therefore there are no sign crossings within any H_r block,
regardless of its starting radius or how many knots it traverses.

For each of its steps, monotonicity gives

    integral_(R')^R dt/G_sigma(t)
        >= (R-R')/G_sigma(R)=h.                             (7)

Add (7) across the full block. If its input and output radii are A and B,
the flow time integral from B to A is at least S>=tau_sigma. By (6) and
positivity of the integrand, B<=A/c. Hence every H_r, from every point on
either ray, satisfies

    |x_after H_r| <= |x_before H_r|/c.                      (8)

No endpoint is selected separately at successive steps. This is a bound
on every actual trajectory of the fixed function (2), and consequently on
its complete interval image as well.

## 4. Exact coarse-word bookkeeping and the objective bound

The exact decomposition of H_m has 2^J copies of H_r and 2^J-1 checkpoint
steps. A level k checkpoint has h=b c^k and occurs 2^(J-k-1) times,
for 0<=k<=J-1. At such a step, (3) gives

    |x-hg(x)| <= max(1,h/b-1)|x| <= c^k |x|.                (9)

This allows a crossing, a same-sign step, or a zero landing. No prescribed
crossing pattern is used. The total checkpoint exponent is

    sum_(k=0)^(J-1) k 2^(J-k-1)=2^J-J-1.

Combining every copy of (8) with every copy of (9) yields

    |x_(2^m-1)| <= c^(-J-1)|x_0|.                         (10)

When J=0 the formula is just (8); the empty sum convention is consistent.
Finally (3) implies f(x)<=x^2/(2b) by integration from the fixed minimizer.
Therefore every initial point and every function specified by (1)-(2) obey

    2f(x_(2^m-1))/|x_0|^2
        <= b^(-1)c^(-2J-2)
        = a^(-1)c^(-m-J-2)
        <= [1/(a c^2)]c^(-m).                             (11)

The bound is uniform in r, both phases, both admissible ell parameters and
the initial point. These choices can all depend on m. The finite trajectory
preserving versions of Section 2 have the identical objective ratio.

## 5. Comparison with the exact profile and the remaining gap

A self-contained rough exact-DP estimate suffices. For C_n=S_circle(n)+1
and D_n=2S_bullet(n)+1, the shifted joins are

    Omega(A,B)=(A+B+sqrt((A+B)^2+4AB))/2,
    G(A,D)=2A+D/2+sqrt(D^2+8AD)/2.

The inequality Omega(A,B)<=(A^(3/4)+B^(3/4))^(4/3) is equivalent to
t^(4/3)+(1-t)^(4/3)+[t(1-t)]^(4/3)<=1. To prove it put
x=t^(1/3), y=(1-t)^(1/3). The difference from 1 is
x^3y^3[1/(1+x+x^2)+1/(1+y+y^2)-xy]>=0, since each reciprocal is at least
1/3 and xy<=2^(-2/3)<2/3. Induction gives C_n<=(n+1)^(4/3). The estimate
G(A,D)<=D+4A and u^(4/3)+v^(4/3)<=(u+v)^(4/3) give D_n<=1+4n^(4/3).
Since c^3>16, multiplying (11) by D_(2^m-1) proves that the supremum
objective ratio in this class, divided by U_(2^m-1)^ZJ, tends to zero.

The profile class has arbitrary numbers of knots, asymmetric ray phases,
multiple visits, and ordinary blocks that can traverse many knots. Its
limitation is instead its precise discrete homogeneity and the calibrated
flow-time/secant range (1). Functions outside that range, nonperiodic radial
geometry, and the complete scalar class remain open. A scalar class bound
does not upper-bound the unrestricted multidimensional W; the already shared
coupled-coordinate divergent example remains compatible with this result.

## 6. The calibration restriction can be removed for a common staircase parameter

The following extension covers EVERY ell>=1 in the profile (2), with
ell_+=ell_-=ell, while keeping theta_+ and theta_- completely independent.
There is no condition (1), no prescribed crossing pattern, and no restriction
on the initial point. The number of knots, phases, ell, and the finite
trajectory-preserving truncations can all depend on m.

For every m>=17 this larger class satisfies the uniform bound

    2f(x_(2^m-1))/|x_0|^2 <= (c^3/a)c^(-m).                (12)

It consequently also has vanishing ratio to the exact U_(2^m-1)^ZJ, by
the elementary benchmark estimate already proved in Section 5.

### 6.1 Choose the coarse scale and define the two flow clocks

Choose the smallest r>=17 for which b=b_r>=ell. Then b/c<ell<=b; the strict
left inequality also holds when ell=1 and r=17. Set S=s_r. Every step in H_r
is at most b/c, and hence preserves sign by the bound |g(x)|<=|x|/ell.

If m<r, the entire H_m preserves sign. Put w=|g(x_n)| and R=|x_n|. Then
R+s_m w<=|x_0|, while f(x_n)<=Rw-w^2/2. Completing the square gives

    2f(x_n)/|x_0|^2 <=1/(1+2s_m)
       <=[1/(2eta a)]c^(-m),

which is smaller than (12). Hence assume m>=r and put J=m-r.

Reflect the function if needed so that x_0>0. Multiplying a phase theta_sigma
by c does not change its derivative profile. We can therefore choose phase
representatives theta_+=theta and theta_-=q theta with 1<=q<c.

Let G be the template (2) with plateau heights c^z and common parameter ell,
and define its signed flow coordinate on R>0 by

    F(R)=integral_ell^R dt/G(t).

The integral is negative when R<ell. Put

    tau=(c-1)(ell-1)+log c,
    E=(c-1)-log c>0,
    F_+(R)=F(R/theta),       F_-(R)=F(R/(q theta)).

Then F_sigma(cR)=F_sigma(R)+tau. Each positive or negative H_r block reduces
its own flow clock by at least S, by the stepwise integral inequality (7).

### 6.2 Exact worst crossing-clock costs

Define

    D_+=F(b/q),       D_-=F(bq),
    delta=(D_++D_-)/2,
    v=(D_--D_+)/2,
    P_+(R)=F_+(R),    P_-(R)=F_-(R)+v.                    (13)

For a step h=b c^k that crosses from ray sigma to the other ray, its landing
radius is h G_sigma(R)-R<=h G_sigma(R). Its change in the unadjusted clock
is therefore at most

    k tau + F_other(b G_sigma(R))-F_sigma(R).              (14)

The final difference in (14) is maximized at an inner plateau endpoint.
On a plateau its first term is constant and its second increases with R,
so the difference decreases. On a unit-slope ramp, use the current gradient
u as coordinate; then dR/du=1 and its derivative is

    b/G_other(bu)-1/u >=(ell-1)/u>=0,

by the secant bound G_other(y)<=y/ell. Thus it increases on a ramp. At all
inner plateau endpoints on one ray the difference has the same value, by
the common radial period and common tau. That value is exactly D_+ or D_-
from (13). The additive constant v therefore makes BOTH crossing directions
have adjusted-clock change at most k tau+delta.

This same bound also holds for a checkpoint that does not cross. Its clock
decreases by at least h>=b, by (7). Since b/q>=ell/c and bq>=ell,

    delta>=-tau/2>-b,

where tau<(c-1)ell<2b. Consequently -b<=k tau+delta for every k>=0.
A zero landing has zero terminal objective and needs no logarithmic clock.
We have proved, for every nonzero trajectory and every checkpoint,

    change of P <= k tau+delta.                            (15)

### 6.3 Uniform bound on the phase-dependent cost

We now prove

    tau+delta <=(c-1)b<S.                                 (16)

This quantitative inequality is essential; it is not an assumed profile
closure. Put p=b/ell, so 1<=p<c. For 1<=z<=c, the template gradient is at
least 1 on [ell,ell z], whence

    F(ell z)<=ell(z-1),       tau=(c-1)ell-E.               (17)

Reduce p q and p/q by powers of c into z_1,z_2 in [1,c]. Let K be the sum
of those two powers. Then

    z_1 z_2=p^2/c^K,
    2delta<=K tau+ell(z_1+z_2-2).                          (18)

For z_1,z_2 in [1,c], their sum is at most 1+z_1z_2 when the product is
at most c, and at most c+(z_1z_2)/c when the product is at least c.
These follow respectively from (z_1-1)(z_2-1)>=0 and
(c-z_1)(c-z_2)>=0.

If 1<=p<=sqrt(c), the possible K are -1 and 0. Applying these two sum bounds
in (18) gives

    delta <=[ell(p^2-1)+E]/2.

Therefore

    tau+delta <=ell[p^2/2+c-3/2]-E/2 <=(c-1)b.

The last inequality is equivalent, after dropping the favorable E/2, to
(p-1)(p-(2c-3))<=0. It holds because sqrt(c)<2c-3 for c=13/5.

If sqrt(c)<=p<c, the possible K are 0 and 1. The same calculation gives

    delta <=ell[c-2+p^2/c]/2,
    tau+delta <=ell[3c/2-2+p^2/(2c)]-E <=(c-1)b.

For the last inequality, the difference divided by ell, before the favorable
-E term, is the convex quadratic
p^2/(2c)-(c-1)p+3c/2-2. At p=sqrt(c) it equals
(c-1)(3/2-sqrt(c))<0; at p=c it equals -(c-1)(c-2)<0.
Convexity therefore makes it nonpositive on the full interval. This proves
the first inequality in (16). The second follows from S>=eta b and
eta=13/8>c-1=8/5.

### 6.4 Complete clock recurrence and normalization

There are 2^J ordinary H_r blocks, the checkpoint level sum is
2^J-J-1, and the number of checkpoints is 2^J-1. Adding all actual clock
changes using (15) gives

    P_final-P_initial
       <=-2^J[S-tau-delta]-(J+1)tau-delta
       <=-(J+1)tau-delta.                                 (19)

All crossing histories and all ray changes are included. The two clocks
differ from a common reference by a controlled phase amount: since
q in [1,c), one has F_+(R)-tau<=F_-(R)<=F_+(R), and 0<=v<=tau.
The latter follows because D_--D_+=F(bq)-F(b/q)<=2tau.
Thus, for either final ray, P_final>=F_+(|x_n|)-tau, whereas
P_initial=F_+(|x_0|). Together with delta>=-tau/2, (19) yields

    F_+(|x_n|)-F_+(|x_0|) <=-(J-1/2)tau
                                  <=-(J-1)tau.

The clock is increasing and has period increment tau, so

    |x_n|<=c^(1-J)|x_0|.

This bound is deliberately loose when J=0, but remains valid. The global
secant bound gives f(x)<=x^2/(2ell), and b/c<ell implies

    2f(x_n)/|x_0|^2
        <=ell^(-1)c^(2-2J)
        <=b^(-1)c^(3-2J)
        =a^(-1)c^(-m)c^(3-J)
        <=(c^3/a)c^(-m),

which proves (12).

The remaining restriction is genuine discrete radial homogeneity with factor
c and a COMMON ell on both rays. Their phases are arbitrary. Section 1 also
allows different ell values in its stated calibrated range. These results
do not cover arbitrary independently scaled rays outside that range,
different radial periods, nonperiodic knot geometry, or the entire scalar
class. The full multidimensional exact-profile target remains unresolved.
