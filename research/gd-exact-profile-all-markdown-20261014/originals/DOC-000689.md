# Positive off-diagonal entries and exact chronological decimation

Status: **unverified proof draft**, sixth launch, second continuation round.
No kernel evaluation, optimizer, installation, or fact submission was used.
The uniform positive-semidefinite conclusion and the original minimax Goal
remain open. This document proves some of the actual coefficient claims;
it does not assume the remaining thirteen-band estimates.

## 1. Definitions and the coefficient that is being estimated

Fix an integer m>=128 and keep epsilon=1/m fixed throughout. Set

 lambda=1+sqrt(2), c=lambda(1+epsilon), a=(1-epsilon)/c,
 d_k=1+a*c^k  (k>=0), H_0=(), H_(j+1)=(H_j,d_j,H_j).

The extension of d_k past m-1 is only a coefficient device. The actual
queried word is H_m with its last d_0 removed. Write h_i for its steps,
s_i=sum_(r<i)h_r, and s'=s_i+h_i when discussing a single step.

On the infinite original ruler word define B_00=1 and

 B_(i+1),k=(1-h_i/d_k)B_ik+(h_i/d_(k-1))B_i,(k-1),

with a zero second term at k=0. For each occupied state k, h_i<=d_k:
the first occurrence of d_j kills state j, and all smaller states have
already died. This also proves B_ik>=0, sum_k B_ik=1, and
B_ik=0 for i>=2^k or k>i.

For arbitrary formal vectors y_k put g_i=sum_k v_ik*y_k, where
v_ik=B_ik/d_k. The row-finite symmetric matrix E is defined by

 sum_(k,l)E_kl<y_k,y_l>
 =sum_i [2s_(i+1)h_i<g_(i+1),g_i>
       +s_(i+1)||g_(i+1)-g_i||^2+h_i(1-h_i)||g_i||^2].   (1)

Each coefficient is a finite sum: a term containing state k occurs by
the step immediately before query2^k. In particular E_kl=0 for k<l
and l>2^k. The m-principal block is the actual finite residual kernel.
The coefficient convention in (1) means that an off-diagonal entry
appears twice in the quadratic form.

## 2. Two elementary bounds for the actual chronological word

For the elementary parameter bounds used below, lambda>12/5 and
lambda<29/12 give 12/5<c<39/16. The upper comparison follows from
(29/12)(129/128)=3741/1536<39/16. Consequently
a>(127/128)/(39/16)=127/312>2/5. Moreover

 d_0=sqrt(2)-2epsilon/[lambda(1+epsilon)],
 d_2=2+sqrt(2)-lambda*epsilon^2,

so 7/5<d_0<3/2 and d_2<7/2; for the first lower bound use
sqrt(2)>141/100 and 2epsilon/[lambda(1+epsilon)]<5/768.
In particular 2<c<3 and 1<d_0<2. Also

                         d_(k+1)>2d_k  (k>=2).            (2)

Indeed d_(k+1)-2d_k=(c-2)a*c^k-1. At k=2 the quantity
before subtracting1 is (1-epsilon)c(c-2), and

 c(c-2)=1+2lambda(lambda-1)epsilon+lambda^2 epsilon^2
        >1+6epsilon.

Consequently (1-epsilon)c(c-2)>1+5epsilon-6epsilon^2>1.
The expression increases with k, proving (2).

Second, every original physical step satisfies

                             h_i<s_i+2.                  (3)

Let S_j=sum H_j and C_j=S_j+2-d_j. Then C_0=2-d_0>0.
The recurrences S_(j+1)=2S_j+d_j and
d_(j+1)=c*d_j-(c-1) give the exact identity

 C_(j+1)=2C_j+(3-c)(d_j-1)>0.

The prefix before the FIRST d_j is H_j, so (3) holds there. Every later
occurrence has a larger elapsed clock and the same step size. This proves
(3) at every position, without changing the enclosing epsilon.

There is a sharper envelope that does exclude the recorded swap foil.
Put rho=c-2 and gamma=rho-a=c-1-d_0>0. The exact word-sum formula gives

 rho(S_j+1)+1-d_j=gamma*2^j.

Thus every original occurrence h_i=d_j obeys

 rho(s_i+1)+1-h_i=gamma*2^j+rho(s_i-S_j)>=0.              (3a)

If the third and fourth steps are exchanged, the first d_2 has
s_i=d_0+d_1 instead of S_2=2d_0+d_1. Its slack in (3a) becomes
4gamma-rho*d_0<13/128-14/25<0. Here rho>2/5, d_0>7/5, and
gamma<(13/4)epsilon, as proved explicitly in Section7 below. This
distinguishes the original chronology from that particular foil.
Equation (3a) alone is not asserted to prove b_i or u_i.

## 3. Nonnegative off-diagonal entries, term by term

For a fixed step h=h_i, write v_k=v_ik and v_(-1)=0. The scaled birth
recurrence is

 v'_k=v_k+(h/d_k)(v_(k-1)-v_k).

For k<l, expansion of that step's contribution to E_kl gives exactly

 C_kl*v_k*v_l
 +[s'h^2(d_l-1)/(d_k*d_l)]v_(k-1)*v_l
 +[s'h^2(d_k-1)/(d_k*d_l)]v_k*v_(l-1)
 +[s'h^2/(d_k*d_l)]v_(k-1)*v_(l-1),                     (4)

where

 C_kl=h[2s'+1-h-s'h(1/d_k+1/d_l-1/(d_k*d_l))].           (5)

To check (4), the first term of (1) contributes
s'h(v'_k*v_l+v_k*v'_l), the second contributes
s'(v'_k-v_k)(v'_l-v_l), and the third contributes
h(1-h)v_k*v_l. Expanding these three expressions gives (4)-(5).

The last three coefficients in (4) are nonnegative. Suppose k>=2.
If v_k*v_l=0, the possibly signed coefficient C_kl costs nothing.
Otherwise state k is occupied, so h<=d_k. Equation (2) gives
d_l>=d_(k+1)>2d_k and hence

 h(1/d_k+1/d_l-1/(d_k*d_l))<3/2.

It follows from (3) that

 C_kl>h(s'/2+1-h)=(h/2)(s_i+2-h)>0.                     (6)

Thus every physical step contributes a nonnegative amount to E_kl
when 2<=k<l. This proof keeps the signed terms of the original step
until they combine into C_kl; it does not take their absolute values.

For k=0,1, support leaves only E_01 and E_12. The first two steps,
whose unscaled state vectors are successively V_0,V_1,V_2, give

 E_01=(d_0-1)/d_1>0,
 E_12=(d_0+d_1)(d_1-1)/(d_1*d_2)>0.

These calculations and (4)-(6) prove

                    E_kl>=0 for every k!=l.              (7)

In particular the actual second-neighbor weights obey e_0=e_1=0 and

                    e_k=E_(k,k+2)>0 for k>=2.             (8)

For strictness, just before the first d_k both B_ik and B_i,(k+1)
are positive when k>=2. For B_ik, use the path that takes a birth on
each of its first k steps and then stays. All those birth probabilities
are positive, and every subsequent step before the first d_k is smaller
than d_k. For B_i,(k+1), take the first k+1 births and then stay; this
is possible because k+1<=2^k-1, and all later pre-d_k steps are smaller
than d_(k+1). At the d_k step, the third term in (4) with l=k+2 is
strictly positive: s'>0, d_k>1, v_k>0, v_(k+1)>0. This proves (8).

No uniform lower bound such as e_k>=1/15 is proved by this strictness
argument: the displayed positive path probabilities have not been
estimated uniformly.

## 4. Exact consequences for the unresolved row margins

For all nonnegative indices define

 A_i=E_(i,i+1), e_i=E_(i,i+2),
 b_i=A_i-2(e_(i-1)+e_i),
 u_i=E_ii-A_(i-1)-A_i+e_(i-2)+e_i
                         -sum_(|l-i|>=3)|E_il|,

with negative subscripts equal to zero. Let F_i=sum_l E_il be the
finite unweighted row sum, and ellhat_i=sum_l E_il*d_l the constant
load obtained by polarizing (1) against the constant gradient.

Equation (7) removes all absolute values from the far off-diagonal
sum. Therefore the exact full-row identity is

                     u_i=2E_ii+2(e_(i-2)+e_i)-F_i.         (9)

In particular a proof of u_i does not need to identify alternating
signs of individual far entries. It still needs signed cancellation
between the diagonal, the neighboring entries, and the row sum.
Nonnegative entries do not imply either b_i>=2/15 or u_i>=0.

This does not discard a terminal coefficient. In the inherited finite
decomposition, the triple starting at m-2 remains the square
e_(m-2)||y_(m-2)+2y_(m-1)||^2. Its coefficient now has the sign in (8).
The last diagonal remains

 u_(m-1)+sum_(l>=m+2)E_(m-1,l)+b_(m-1)+e_(m-1).

Neither its required quantitative lower bound nor the completed
constant-column remainder follows from (7) alone.

## 5. A scalar constant-load formula with the signed difference retained

For this section all sums run over the infinite coefficient word, but
every state-specific sum is finite. Set

 mu_ik=h_i B_ik/d_k,
 L_k=sum_(r=0)^k d_r,
 M_k=sum_i h_i*mu_ik,
 N_k=sum_i s_(i+1)*h_i*mu_ik,

and put M_(-1)=N_(-1)=0. The flow equation is
B_(i+1),k-B_ik=mu_i,(k-1)-mu_ik. Summing it proves
sum_i mu_ik=1 for each transient state. Weighting it by s_(i+1)
and summing by parts gives

 sum_i s_(i+1)(mu_ik-mu_i,(k-1))=sum_i h_i B_ik=d_k.

Starting with a zero predecessor moment shows
sum_i s_(i+1)mu_ik=L_k. Thus M_k and N_k are respectively the mean
exit step and the mean product of exit clock and exit step for state k.

Polarizing (1) against the constant gradient gives

 ellhat_k=sum_i h_i[(s_i+1)v_ik+s_(i+1)v_(i+1),k].         (10)

For example, the first term in (1) contributes
s'h(v_ik+v_(i+1),k), its increment-square term contributes zero,
and its last term contributes h(1-h)v_ik. Their sum is (10).
Substitute the birth recurrence into (10), then use the two occupation
identities just proved. The result is

             ellhat_k=2L_k+1-M_k-(N_k-N_(k-1))/d_k.        (11)

In detail, before the recurrence contribution the sum is
2L_k+1-M_k. The remaining term is
sum_i s'h_i^2(v_i,(k-1)-v_ik)/d_k
=(N_(k-1)-N_k)/d_k. This checks its sign and its denominator.

Formula (11) is an identity, not a new condition replacing the load
inequalities. It identifies the chronological mixed moment that must
be estimated. Bounding N_k and N_(k-1) separately loses the signed
quantity needed for the desired 2d_k<=ellhat_k<=11d_k/4.

### 5.1 The first required constant-load row is settled uniformly

The row k=2 can be evaluated and bounded without estimating the higher
birth states. Put t=d_0, u=d_1, d=d_2, and theta=1-t/d. The nonzero
values of B_n2 before its extinction are B_22=1 and B_32=theta.
Also B_12=0 and B_42=0. Substitution in (10), using the original
first four steps (t,u,t,d), gives

 ellhat_2=[u(t+u)+t(t+u+1)
                 +(t(2t+u)+d(2t+u+1))*theta]/d.

Collecting its terms, with no inequality, gives

 ellhat_2/d=((t+u)/d)^2+(2t+u+1)/d-t^2(2t+u)/d^3.         (11a)

The actual parameters obey
7/5<t<3/2, 199/100<u<2, and 17/5<d<7/2.
The only new lower bound, d>17/5, follows from
d=2+sqrt(2)-lambda*epsilon^2,
sqrt(2)>141/100, and lambda*epsilon^2<(5/2)/128^2<1/100.

For a lower bound in (11a), its three displayed positive magnitudes
obey, in order,

 ((t+u)/d)^2>(339/350)^2>9/10,
 (2t+u+1)/d>579/350>33/20,
 t^2(2t+u)/d^3<(45/4)/(75/2)=3/10.

Here d^3>(17/5)^3>75/2. Hence ellhat_2/d>9/4.
For the upper bound, the corresponding comparisons are

 ((t+u)/d)^2<(35/34)^2<17/16,
 (2t+u+1)/d<30/17<177/100,
 t^2(2t+u)/d^3>(49/25)(19/4)/43=931/4300>21/100.

The last line uses 2t+u>19/4 and d^3<(7/2)^3<43. Therefore

                   9/4<ellhat_2/d_2<1049/400<21/8.        (11b)

In particular the required interval [2,11/4] holds at k=2 for every
actual m>=128. The unresolved constant-load rows start at k=3.

### 5.2 A clock-slack identity, with the nonmonotone term exposed

Put rho=c-2, alpha_clock=rho/(1+rho), tau_i=s_(i+1), and

 omega_i=alpha_clock*tau_i+1-h_i
        =[rho(s_i+1)+1-h_i]/(c-1)>=0,
 Z_k=sum_i mu_ik*omega_i,
 W_k=sum_i mu_ik*tau_i*omega_i,
 H2_k=sum_i mu_ik*tau_i^2.

Take all predecessor quantities with index-1 to be zero. The sharp
original clock inequality (3a) supplies omega_i>=0. Weighting the
birth flow by tau_i^2 and summing by parts gives

 H2_k-H2_(k-1)=sum_i(2tau_i*h_i-h_i^2)B_ik
              =d_k(2L_k-M_k).

The definitions also give
M_k=alpha_clock*L_k+1-Z_k and
N_k=alpha_clock*H2_k+L_k-W_k. Substitution in (11) yields the exact
signed formula

 ellhat_k=c*L_k/(c-1)^2-1/(c-1)
                  +Z_k/(c-1)+(W_k-W_(k-1))/d_k.           (11c)

The explicit level sum is
L_k=c*d_k/(c-1)+k-1+gamma/(c-1). Thus the first two terms on the
right of (11c) equal

 [c^2/(c-1)^3]d_k+c(k-1)/(c-1)^2
                         +c*gamma/(c-1)^3-1/(c-1).       (11d)

For k>=2 this exceeds2d_k: c^2/(c-1)^3 decreases for c>1,
and its value at39/16 is24336/12167=2+2/12167. The remaining
non-gamma terms are at least1/(c-1)^2, and gamma>0.

This calculation does NOT prove the lower load bound. Although
Z_k>=0 and W_k>=0, the difference W_k-W_(k-1) has not been signed.
Its summand cannot be treated as increasing just because birth-state
exit times increase. In fact it has a large decrease in the ORIGINAL
actual word: at the third step, i=2 and h_2=t=d_0,

 tau_2*omega_2=(2t+u)[gamma+rho(t+u)]/(c-1)>4,

whereas at the fourth step, the first d_2,

 tau_3*omega_3=(2t+u+d_2)*4gamma/(c-1)<1.

For the first comparison use 2t+u>47/10 and
rho(t+u)/(c-1)>(2/5)(339/100)/(3/2)>9/10.
For the second use 2t+u+d_2<17/2 and
4gamma/(c-1)<13/[128(7/5)]=65/896. These hold for every
actual m>=128. The expected difference in (11c) may still be
nonnegative, but a proof must use the original exit distribution;
pathwise monotonicity of this clock weight is false.

## 6. An exact reduction that distinguishes the original lower-level order

Put t=d_0, ell=c-1, gamma=ell-t, and

 Z(z)=c*z/(1+ell*z),
 A(z)=(1-t*z)(1+ell*z)=1+gamma*z-t*ell*z^2.

These are new scalar functions, unrelated to the adjacent entries A_i.
They are well-defined at every nonnegative reciprocal physical node.
The affine-geometric node relation gives

 (1-t*z)(1-d_(j+1)*z)=A(z)(1-d_j*Z(z)),
 Z(1/d_(j+1))=1/d_j.                                    (12)

Write the original infinite step word in consecutive pairs. The exact
pair at position r is (t,d_(nu_2(r+1)+1)), where nu_2 is the exponent
of2 in a positive integer. After removing every t and lowering the
remaining level by1, the compressed word is the same original ruler
word, with the SAME epsilon. For its first r steps write
P_r(z)=product_(i<r)(1-h_i*z) and s_r=sum_(i<r)h_i. Then

 P_(2r)(z)=A(z)^r P_r(Z(z)),
 P_(2r+1)(z)=(1-t*z)A(z)^r P_r(Z(z)),
 s_(2r)=c*s_r-gamma*r,
 s_(2r+1)=c*s_r-gamma*r+t.                               (13)

All four identities follow by multiplying or summing the individual
pairs in (12). This is a chronological identity, not an identity of
the full multiset alone. The foil that exchanges the third and fourth
steps destroys the displayed consecutive-pair description.

The Newton factors also transform exactly:

 phi_(k+1)(z)=(1-t*z)(1+ell*z)^k phi_k(Z(z)),              (14)

where phi_k(z)=product_(j<k)(1-d_j*z). At epsilon=0, interpreted only
as a coefficient limit, t=ell=sqrt(2), gamma=0, and A(z)=1-2z^2.
At every actual epsilon>0 the linear term gamma*z and the clock defect
-gamma*r in (13) must both be retained. In fact

 gamma=lambda*epsilon+2epsilon/[lambda(1+epsilon)]>0.

Equations (12)-(14) do not yet establish a bound for E or the mixed
moments in (11). They give an exact original-order transformation in
which those estimates can be pursued without replacing an embedded
block's parameters by epsilon=1/j.

## 7. The decimation multiplier changes sign around one

It is false that the multiplier A at all required physical nodes can
be discarded by a uniform inequality A<=1, or by a uniform inequality
A>=1. Both failures occur at every actual m>=128, without using a
standalone-depth parameter in an embedded block.

For z=1/d_j,

             A(1/d_j)-1=(gamma*d_j-t*ell)/d_j^2.           (15)

We have 7/5<t<3/2, t<ell<3/2, and

 2epsilon<gamma
   <(lambda+2/lambda)epsilon
   =(3sqrt(2)-1)epsilon<(13/4)epsilon.                    (16)

At j=2, d_2<7/2, so
gamma*d_2<91/1024<49/25<t*ell. Therefore A(1/d_2)<1.
At the actual highest level j=m-1,

 gamma*d_(m-1)>(4/5)2^(m-1)/m>9/4>t*ell.

Here a>2/5 and c>2 give the first bound. For the second, 2^(m-1)/m
is increasing for m>=2, and even 2^10/128=8 already exceeds45/16.
This proves A(1/d_(m-1))>1. Both indices are in the original residual
coordinate set. These are counterexamples only to discarding the
decimation factor in one direction, not to the desired band bounds.

There is nevertheless a useful uniform estimate. For k>=8 define

 X_k=(65/16)epsilon*(2/c)^k+(225/32)*(2/c^2)^k.

Then, for every 0<=z<=1/d_k and every integer 0<=r<=2^(k-1),

 0<A(z),
 r|A(z)-1|<=X_k<1/80,
 |A(z)^r-1|<=(80/79)X_k<1/79,
 0<=gamma*r/d_k<=(65/16)epsilon*(2/c)^k.                 (17)

For r=0 all expressions with r vanish. For positive r, (16),
t*ell<9/4, a>2/5, and d_k>a*c^k give

 r|A(z)-1|
 <=2^(k-1)[gamma/d_k+t*ell/d_k^2]
 <(65/16)epsilon*(2/c)^k+(225/32)*(2/c^2)^k.

Also c>12/5, so 2/c<5/6 and 2/c^2<3/8. For k>=8, the exact rational
comparisons (5/6)^8<1/4 and (3/8)^8<1/2500 show

 X_k<65/8192+9/3200<1/80.

Positivity follows from z<=1/d_k<1/t. With delta=|A(z)-1|, if A(z)>=1
the binomial expansion gives

 A(z)^r-1<=sum_(j>=1)(r*delta)^j<=X_k/(1-X_k).

If A(z)<1, 1-A(z)^r<=r*delta<=X_k. These prove the third estimate in
(17); the last follows from the linear part of the same calculation.

Thus the multiplier and clock defects are uniformly small far up the
actual spectrum, despite the change of sign in (15). Their precise
rates include BOTH epsilon*(2/c)^k and (2/c^2)^k. This does not yet
control E: converting pointwise spectral estimates to a birth coefficient
requires retaining the signed divided differences, not taking a large
absolute bound on the entire inverse Newton matrix.

## 8. An early-time bound for the entire nearby band

This localization is in PHYSICAL TIME, in addition to the earlier
localization in matrix-column distance. It controls the part of a near
entry accumulated before the low reciprocal columns die.

Fix j>=2 and let E^early_kl be the contribution to (1) of the first
2^j steps, namely the prefix (H_j,d_j). If k,l>=j+6, then

                         |E^early_kl|<10^(-10).           (18)

Here is a uniform proof. Write T=sum(H_j,d_j). The exact word sum is

 sum H_j=a*c^j/(c-2)+(1-a/(c-2))*2^j-1.

Because 0<a/(c-2)<1 and 2<c, this sum is less than c^j-1.
Consequently T<(1+1/a)d_j<(7/2)d_j. All steps in the prefix are at
most d_j, and d_j>3.

For an integer r>=0 define

 p_r=min{1,(7/2)^r/[r!*2^(r(r-1)/2)]}.

At each query of this prefix, the probability of state j+r is at most
p_r. To prove this, condition on a birth path's first arrival at j.
Reaching j+r requires r subsequent births on increasing distinct
steps. Each specified set has probability at most the product of its
steps divided by d_j...d_(j+r-1); all stay factors are at most one.
The sum of those step products is at most T^r/r!, and (2) bounds the
denominator below by d_j^r*2^(r(r-1)/2). This proof applies at either
end of every step and for every arrival time.

The sequence p_r decreases for r>=3. Thus, for k>=j+6,

 0<=v_ik,v_(i+1),k<=p_6/d_k,
 |v_(i+1),k-v_ik|<=h_i[(5/2)p_5+p_6]/d_k^2.

For the second line, apply the birth recurrence and use
d_k/d_(k-1)<c<5/2. Define Q_6=(5/2)p_5+p_6. The exact fractions give

 p_5=16807/3932160<1/230,
 p_6=117649/1509949440<1/12500,
 Q_6<1/92+1/12500<11/1000.

The first and last terms of (1), polarized for a general k,l, are at
most [T^2+2d_j*T]p_6^2/(d_k*d_l) in absolute value. This uses
2sum_i s_(i+1)h_i=T^2+sum_i h_i^2 and
sum_i h_i^2<=d_j*T. The increment-square term is at most
T^2*d_j*Q_6^2/(d_k^2*d_l^2), since
sum_i s_(i+1)h_i^2<=T^2*d_j. These statements also apply when k=l.

Now d_k,d_l>2^6*d_j. The two contributions are respectively less than

 77/[4*2^12*12500^2] < 1/30000000000,
 49*121/[12*2^24*1000^2] < 1/30000000000.

Their sum is less than10^(-10), proving (18). Every estimate is
uniform in both the row indices and the enclosing m.

For a thirteen-entry row centered at i>=14, take j=i-12. Then both
indices of each entry E_(i,i+r), -6<=r<=6, are at least j+6, so (18)
controls its entire initial prefix through the first d_(i-12). This
does not remove any of the later repeated low steps; their physical
order and elapsed clock remain part of the late contribution.

## 9. Only eighteen reciprocal nodes remain in the late contribution

For this section fix an ACTUAL required row 24<=i<=m-1, put j=i-12,
and consider l=i+r with -6<=r<=6. Extended indices l>=m are only the
same-epsilon coefficient device, as in Section1. All contributing
physical steps are still through the first d_i and are in the original
queried word.

Define R_kb=(d_k/d_b)phi_k(1/d_b) for b>=k, and zero for b<k.
Its triangular inverse D has entries

 D_bk=product_(0<=r<=k,r!=b)(1-d_r/d_b)^(-1),  0<=b<=k,
 D_bk=0 for b>k.                                         (19)

One direct check uses Lagrange interpolation at the distinct nodes
1/d_0,...,1/d_k. Comparing highest-degree coefficients shows that the
weights reciprocal to the products of node differences annihilate
polynomials of degree below k and recover the leading coefficient
at degree k. Apply this identity to phi_r, whose leading coefficient
is (-1)^r product_(s<r)d_s, and rescale by d_b/d_k. The resulting
inverse of R is exactly (19).

Writing P_n(z)=product_(t<n)(1-h_t*z), the corresponding scalar
coefficient identity is

                  v_nk=sum_(b=0)^k D_bk*P_n(1/d_b)/d_b.  (20)

Indeed P_n(z)=sum_k B_nk phi_k(z), by the birth recurrence. Evaluation
at the first k+1 reciprocal nodes kills every phi_r with r>k, and
inversion by (19) gives (20).

For every query n>=2^j and every b<=j, P_n(1/d_b)=0 exactly; the same
is true at the following query. Thus all such nodes disappear from
the late part of (1). Define M^late_ab to be the quadratic coefficient

## 10. A uniform lower bound for the surviving mass at a killing step

Let pi_k=B_(2^k-1),k be the probability still in state k immediately

## 12. Why the moment proof does not automatically prove the signed margins

The new argument settles linear constant loads, but a termwise positive
quadratic-storage proof is impossible even in the original word. For every
k>=3, consider only its actual first physical step h=d_k. State k-1 is
already zero, state k has probability pi_k>17/100, and it is killed by
this step. The coefficient of ||y_k||^2 in this SINGLE summand of (1) is
therefore exactly

 [T_k+d_k(1-d_k)]pi_k^2/d_k^2
     =pi_k^2[-1+(T_k/d_k+1)/d_k].                         (50)

By (41), T_k/d_k+1<883/200. Also d_k>=d_3>34/5. Since
883/1360<2/3, (50) is strictly smaller than

                   -pi_k^2/3<-289/30000.                 (51)

This is an exact original-word counterexample to requiring nonnegative
diagonal cost at EACH physical step. It does NOT contradict any proposed
inequality for the accumulated E, b, e, or u. It identifies the signed
cancellation still needed: positive contributions from preceding lower
steps must pay these negative killing-step diagonals. The successful
single-exit moments do not bound those pair products by themselves.

The two outstanding direct routes are consequently still distinct:
(a) record-state products and actual last-block evolution, now effective
for loads, need joint product estimates for b and u; (b) paired K/T
decimation with the retained clock needs a signed coefficient estimate
after the stable inverse. Neither route has supplied those estimates.
No counterexample to a REQUIRED accumulated band inequality was found.

## 13. Scope and remaining work

The new proved-in-draft part of the stated interface is e_i>=0 for
every required index. The stronger entrywise result (7), the identities
