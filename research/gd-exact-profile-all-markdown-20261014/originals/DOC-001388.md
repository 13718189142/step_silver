# C34. Exact coordinate-path projection relay and its near-critical obstruction

Status: UNVERIFIED joint candidate. No verifier or numerical solver was used.
This is an exact obstruction for the defined coordinate-flow family, not an
upper bound on unrestricted W and not a primitive-capacity theorem.

## 1. Actual correlated real vectors and the whole trajectory

Partition a nonnegative schedule into N ordinary blocks of masses S_i,
separated by checkpoints b_i>1, 0<=i<N-1. Each ordinary block can be an
arbitrary ordered list with that mass. Set M_i=S_i+1, L_i=b_i-1 and
T_i=S_i+b_i=M_i+L_i for i<N-1. Choose an artificial terminal parameter
T_(N-1)>=M_(N-1). Let e_0,...,e_(N-1) be the standard orthonormal basis.

Choose alpha_i>0, beta_i>=0 for i<N-1, with beta_(N-1)=0, and impose

    T_(i+1) alpha_(i+1)=T_i beta_i.

For a nonzero terminal payoff all beta_i must be positive. Define

    p_i=alpha_i e_i-beta_i e_(i+1)  (i<N-1),
    p_(N-1)=alpha_(N-1)e_(N-1),
    C=conv(0,p_0,...,p_(N-1)),
    F(x)=max_{p in C} (<x,p>-||p||^2/2).

The strict concavity of the maximization gives its unique maximizer P_C(x).
The projection variational inequality proves
||P_C(x)-P_C(z)||^2<=<P_C(x)-P_C(z),x-z>, hence F is convex and
differentiable with 1-Lipschitz gradient P_C. Differentiability follows by
bounding the two maximization differences between the corresponding linear
forms and then using the displayed Lipschitz estimate. F>=0 and F(0)=0.

Start at x_init=T_0 alpha_0 e_0. At phase i, after ordinary partial mass
u in [0,S_i], prescribe

    x_i(u)=(T_i-u)alpha_i e_i+u beta_i e_(i+1).

At the checkpoint, subtracting b_i p_i gives T_i beta_i e_(i+1), which is
the next prescribed start. Thus the SAME whole GD trajectory is realized
if P_C(x_i(u))=p_i at every actual partial mass.

Write y_i(u)=x_i(u)-p_i. Its two coordinates are
(T_i-u-1)alpha_i and (u+1)beta_i, both nonnegative. Put

    delta_i(u)=<y_i(u),p_i>
              =(T_i-u-1)alpha_i^2-(u+1)beta_i^2.

For j<=i-2 and j>=i+2, <y_i(u),p_j>=0. For j=i-1 this inner
product is -beta_(i-1)(T_i-u-1)alpha_i<=0. For j=i+1 it is
(u+1)beta_i alpha_(i+1). The comparison with zero is 0<=delta_i(u).

Consequently ALL normals against ALL vertices and zero hold if and only if

    L_i alpha_i^2-M_i beta_i^2
                    >= M_i beta_i alpha_(i+1),     i<N-1,       (1)

together with T_(N-1)>=M_(N-1). Necessity uses the actual last query in
each ordinary block, including an empty block's only query. Sufficiency
uses the fact that delta_i(u)-(u+1)beta_i alpha_(i+1) decreases with u.
The terminal phase has only its nonnegative own normal and nonpositive
comparison with its preceding vertex. Thus no remote condition is omitted.

Using the transfer relation, (1) is precisely

    (beta_i/alpha_i)^2
          <= L_i T_(i+1)/[M_i(T_i+T_(i+1))].                    (2)

The term M_i beta_i^2 in (1) records the mass already accumulated in the
next coordinate during the ordinary block. Replacing it by beta_i^2
would give a false, much stronger relay.

The correlations are explicit:
<p_i,p_(i+1)>=-beta_i alpha_(i+1)<0, all nonadjacent correlations zero.
The p_i are linearly independent because their coordinate matrix is
triangular with positive diagonal. Thus C is an actual N-simplex,
not a formal Gram matrix.

## 2. Actual normalization and exact optimal payoff

The minimizer set is C^polar={z:<p_i,z><=0 for all i}: these inequalities make every maximand nonpositive, while if <p_i,z>>0, a sufficiently small positive multiple of p_i in C gives a positive maximand. The terminal
inequality forces z_(N-1)<=0. Backward substitution then forces every
coordinate z_i<=0. Therefore 0 is a closest minimizer to x_init, whose
true squared distance to the minimizer set is T_0^2 alpha_0^2.

At the final point, with D=2S_(N-1)+1,

    2F_end / dist(x_init,argmin F)^2
      = (2T_(N-1)-D)/T_(N-1)^2
                         product_(i=0)^(N-2)(beta_i/alpha_i)^2. (3)

For fixed T_(N-1), equality in every bound (2) maximizes this expression.
It gives legal positive parameters recursively from any alpha_0>0. Thus
the exact optimum for this family is

    E(T)=(2T-D)/T^2
        product_(i=0)^(N-2) [L_i T_(i+1)/(M_i(T_i+T_(i+1)))].   (4)

For N>=2 let C_last=T_(N-2). All T dependence in (4), up to a positive
constant, is

    phi(T)=(2T-D)/[T(T+C_last)],  T>=M_(N-1).

Its derivative has numerator -2T^2+2DT+D C_last. Therefore its unique
maximum occurs at

    T_opt=(D+sqrt(D^2+2D C_last))/2 >= D >= M_(N-1),

and

    phi(T_opt)=2/[D+C_last+sqrt(D^2+2D C_last)].

For N=1, (3) is maximized at T=D and gives 1/D, the global Huber value.

The primitive defect is tracked on the same normalized trajectory. Let s be
the mass of the ENTIRE schedule and let
V=2 sum_t h_t(F_t-F_end)+sum_t h_t(1-h_t)||g_t||^2
  -s(s+1)||g_end||^2.
At every projection query F_t=<x_t,g_t>-||g_t||^2/2, so
2h_t F_t+h_t(1-h_t)||g_t||^2
=||x_t||^2-||x_(t+1)||^2. Telescoping gives

    V=||x_init||^2-||x_end+s g_end||^2,

and therefore, with T=T_(N-1),

    V/dist(x_init,argmin F)^2
      =1-[(T-S_(N-1)+s)/T]^2
                   product_(i<N-1)(beta_i/alpha_i)^2.           (5a)

Equations (3) and (5a) report BOTH the normalized objective and the normalized
primitive defect; neither is inferred from the sign of the other.

## 3. Every dyadic-depth version fails on the near-critical word

Fix m>=128, lambda=1+sqrt(2),
c=lambda(1+1/m), a=(1-1/m)/c, and
H_0=(), H_(j+1)=(H_j,1+a c^j,H_j).
Let S_j be its mass and M_j=S_j+1. Direct geometric summation gives

    M_j=A c^j+(1-A)2^j,  A=a/(c-2).

Here 0<A<1: c(c-2)>lambda(lambda-2)=1 while ac=1-1/m<1.
Also 2<c<49/20 and c-2<9/20. For example sqrt(2)<99/70 gives
lambda<169/70, and (169/70)(129/128)<49/20.

Choose any r in [0,m-1]. Partition H_m into N=2^k copies of H_r, where
k=m-r, separated by its actual higher-level checkpoints. Then all M_i=M=M_r
and

    L_i=a c^(r+nu_2(i+1)),  0<=i<N-1,
    q=a c^r/M <= c-2 < 9/20,
    T_i=M(1+q c^(nu_2(i+1))).

Here nu_2(j) is the exponent of2 dividing the positive integer j.
In particular the first and last nonterminal T_i equal T_0=M(1+q).
Put D=2M-1. Exact counting of binary valuations gives

    product_(i<N-1)(L_i/M)=q^(N-1)c^(N-k-1)
                         =(qc)^(N-1)c^(-k).                   (5)

For internal transitions i<N-2, every checkpoint of level ell>=1
is flanked by two level0 checkpoints. Therefore

    product_(i<N-2)(1+T_(i+1)/T_i)
      = product_(ell=1)^(k-1)
          [(T_0+T_ell)^2/(T_0 T_ell)]^(2^(k-ell-1))
      >= 2^(N-2),                                           (6)

where T_ell=M(1+q c^ell). Each bracket is at least4 by
(T_0-T_ell)^2>=0. There are (N-2)/2 brackets, with multiplicity.

Telescoping the T_(i+1)/T_i factors in (4) and retaining the terminal
factor gives the exact formula

    E_opt = phi(T_opt) (qc)^(N-1)c^(-k) /
            product_(ell=1)^(k-1)
             [(T_0+T_ell)^2/(T_0 T_ell)]^(2^(k-ell-1)).        (7)

In particular phi(T)<= (2T-D)/T^2 <=1/D, so

    E_opt <= [2/(D c^k)] (qc/2)^(N-1).                       (8)

Let K=1/(2M_m-1). From the formula for M_j and q<=c-2,

    M_m=M[(1-q/(c-2))2^k+(q/(c-2))c^k] <=M c^k.

Consequently for k>=2,

    E_opt/K <= 4 (qc/2)^(N-1)
             <4 (441/800)^(N-1)
             <=4 (441/800)^3 <1.                            (9)

Every genuinely multitransition dyadic-depth relay in this family is thus
strictly below K, uniformly in its free scalar parameters. If k tends to
infinity, its ratio to K tends to0 at least geometrically in 2^k.

For completeness k=1 is also below K. Set epsilon=1/M in (0,1]. The exact
two-phase optimum divided by K is

    2q[2(2+q)-epsilon] /
     [3+q-epsilon+sqrt((2-epsilon)(4+2q-epsilon))].

It is bounded above by

    R(q)=4q(2+q)/[2+q+sqrt(3+2q)].

This function increases for q>=0: write it as
4q/[1+sqrt(3+2q)/(2+q)]; the ratio in the denominator decreases,
since its derivative has numerator -(1+q). Therefore

    R(q)<R(9/20)=441/[245+10sqrt(390)]<1;

the last inequality follows from 196^2<39000.

Allowing r=m gives the single-phase Huber construction and attains K.
Thus the optimal payoff of the UNION of all these dyadic-depth incidence
relays is exactly K. This does not claim that all simplex walks, all
correlated walks, or all smooth-convex functions have payoff at most K.

## 4. Why the obstruction is useful and what it does not close

The family is legal in dimension N with every old/future normal retained,
uses N distinct mutually overlapping gradients, and has an exact
closest-minimizer calculation. Its failure is quantitative; no translated
negative-energy sign is used. The penalty in (6) comes from adjacent
coordinate transfer and survives the near-critical perturbation qc=1+O(1/m).

A tree transfer can avoid following N consecutive fresh coordinates, but it
must retain waiting coordinates. Every waiting future vertex then contributes
a positive normal comparison throughout the intervening phases. That is a
different construction class, not covered by (9).
