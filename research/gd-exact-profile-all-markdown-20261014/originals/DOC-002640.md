# A genuine finite objective residual and its exact block transport

Status: unverified exact identities and a scoped limitation. No solver or verifier calls. The eleven-step rule with bridge factors(7/10,1,1/5) is closed by max3's explicit counterexample, which was read and matches the present primitive energy definition. None of the constructions below attempts to repair that rule.

Use smoothness1 and Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2<=0. For nonnegative proof weights u on a GD block write

    D=sum u_i, d=x_start-x_end, U=sum u_i g_i, e=U-d,
    E=2sum u_i(f_i-f_end)+sum u_i||g_i||^2
       +2sum u_i<g_i,x_start-x_i>-||U||^2.

For any minimizer, direct expansion gives

    ||x_start-x_star||^2+2sum u_i Q_(i,star)-2D(f_end-f_star)
       =||x_end-x_star-e||^2+E.                      (1)

Thus E>=0 yields the original last-iterate bound W<=1/D.

## 1. A nonprimitive proof-weight state with no loss of Huber mass

The physical schedule is the pair(3/2,3/2). Write its three gradients as p,m,q, its displacement as d=(3/2)(p+m), and define

    t=d-3q, r=p-(d+q)/4.

For 0<epsilon<=1/64, choose actual proof weights

    u=(3/2-epsilon, 3/2, 4+epsilon).

Then D=7 and

    e=4q-epsilon(p-q), v=e-4q=-epsilon(r+t/4).       (2)

These are not the primitive weights(h,4), although the physical block is primitive. All three weights are positive. The exact interpolation identity is

    E=(21epsilon/2)(-Q_01)+(1-5epsilon/4)(-Q_02)
        +(4+29epsilon/4)(-Q_10)
        +(9+29epsilon/4)(-Q_12)+(16+4epsilon)(-Q_21)
        +(r,t)^T M_epsilon (r,t),                    (3)

where the full symmetric matrix is

    M_epsilon =
      [ 2epsilon-epsilon^2, -31epsilon/12-epsilon^2/4 ]
      [ -31epsilon/12-epsilon^2/4,
                         1/9+7epsilon/36-epsilon^2/16 ].

Every actual interpolation coefficient in (3) is nonnegative. Equation (3) follows either by expanding E and the five Q terms directly, or by the following retained-slack derivation, which also explains the cross term.

Let V be the pair's physical primitive energy and H=2(f_0-f_2)-6||q||^2-||r||^2. Define the adjacent cycle quantities C_01=-Q_01-Q_10 and C_12=-Q_12-Q_21. They satisfy

    C_01=3<q,r>-<q,t>/4-||r||^2
                         +(7/6)<r,t>-13||t||^2/144,
    C_12=-(3/2)<q,r>+(5/8)<q,t>+||r||^2/2
                         -(5/12)<r,t>+25||t||^2/288.

The correlated identity in max4_joint_boundary_storage.md gives, more generally,

    V-epsilon H
      =(epsilon/2)(-Q_01)+(1-5epsilon/4)(-Q_02)
        +(4-11epsilon/4)(-Q_10)
        +(9-19epsilon/4)(-Q_12)+(16-8epsilon)(-Q_21)
        +(16-15epsilon)||t||^2/144.                  (4)

This can be checked using the one-parameter primitive identity there with
kappa=(16-15epsilon)/(64-28epsilon), so that
(1-7epsilon/16)kappa=(16-15epsilon)/64.

The exact change from V to E has q-cross terms 12epsilon<q,r>+5epsilon<q,t>. The nonnegative combination 10epsilon C_01+12epsilon C_12 has exactly those same q-cross terms. Subtracting its quadratic expression, and adding it to the actual interpolation coefficients in (4), yields (3). This is why keeping only the two scalar slack bounds would have missed the finite objective state: the adjacent cycles supply the required correlations with q.

Set nu=1/(2epsilon). The matrix after subtracting nu||v||^2 from (3) is

    M_epsilon-(epsilon/2)(1,1/4)(1,1/4)^T.           (5)

Its first diagonal entry is (3/2)epsilon-epsilon^2>0, and its determinant is

    epsilon[1/6-(1037/144)epsilon-(29/18)epsilon^2]. (6)

For epsilon<=1/64 the bracket is at least1321/24576>0. Thus (5) is positive definite, proving the concrete finite residual certificate

    E >= nu ||e-4q||^2, D=7, nu=1/(2epsilon).        (7)

The mass7 is exactly the physical Huber mass2sum(h)+1. No limiting argument is used to make (7) valid: every indicated positive epsilon gives an actual finite certificate. The family has nu tending to infinity as the weights tend to the primitive weights.

## 2. A transport with a nonprimitive input and an inherited quadratic term

Let a prefix have nonnegative weights, D_a=2K-1 with K>=1, and the actual finite certificate

    E_a >= nu||e_a-Kg||^2, nu>0,

where g is the gradient at its endpoint y. The pair construction above provides K=4 and any nu=1/(2epsilon), epsilon<=1/64.

Take an arbitrary tail block, starting at y'=y-tau g and ending at z. Its own nonnegative weights v have mass D_b>0, gradient aggregate U_b, energy E_b>=0, start gradient p_b and final gradient q_b. The four vectors g,p_b,q_b,U_b remain distinct. Nothing below identifies the tail start gradient with the bridge gradient, or replaces its aggregate by an endpoint gradient.

Put

    sigma=1/(nu+1), k=1+1/nu, eta=1-sigma=1/k,
    c=(2K-sigma)/D_b,
    beta=(1+sqrt(1+4k c))/(2k),
    tau=1+K/(k beta).

Thus k beta^2=beta+c. Add eta to the prefix terminal proof weight and scale every tail weight by beta. All new proof weights are nonnegative and

    D_new=2K-sigma+beta D_b=k beta^2 D_b.             (8)

Write v_a=e_a-Kg. The actual tail-to-anchor interpolation sum is

    A_y=-2sum_(i in tail) v_i Q_(i,y)
       =2D_b(f_y-f_z)-E_b-||U_b||^2
          -2(tau-1)<U_b,g>-D_b||g||^2.              (9)

Expansion of the new intrinsic energy and substitution of (9) gives exactly

    E_new=(E_a-nu||v_a||^2)+(beta+c)E_b+c A_y
          +(1/nu)||nu v_a-eta g-beta U_b||^2
          +sigma(2K-1)||g||^2.                      (10)

This is a finite-horizon certificate with actual multipliers c v_i on -2Q_(i,y). It preserves the squared boundary quantity nu v_a-eta g-beta U_b; resetting it at a join is not part of the identity. The tail's p_b and q_b have zero explicit rows in the quadratic remainder because (9) has eliminated the full tail value sum exactly, not because those gradients were equated.

For completeness, the full nonzero boundary matrix in(v_a,g,U_b) is

    [ nu,       -eta,                         -beta          ]
    [ -eta, eta^2/nu+sigma(2K-1),          eta beta/nu       ]
    [ -beta,   eta beta/nu,                  beta^2/nu      ].

It is the sum of the outer product (nu,-eta,-beta)(nu,-eta,-beta)^T/nu and diag(0,sigma(2K-1),0), hence PSD in every Euclidean dimension. If the independent gradients p_b,q_b are added to the boundary vector, their rows and columns are zero. Equation (1) now gives W<=1/D_new for the actual concatenated physical schedule.

To verify (10) without trusting a completion of squares, the raw quadratic coefficients before subtracting the square are

    ||v_a||^2: nu,
    <v_a,g>: -2eta,       <v_a,U_b>: -2beta,
    ||g||^2: sigma(2K-sigma),
    ||U_b||^2: c+beta-beta^2=beta^2/nu,
    <g,U_b>: 2[beta(tau-eta-K)+c(tau-1)]
               =2eta beta/nu.

The g-diagonal remainder equals sigma(2K-1), using eta=nu/(nu+1).

## 3. Calibration succeeds, but this closed subrule cannot improve ConPD

For fixed K and D_b, as nu tends to infinity the parameters in part2 tend to the ordinary ConPD parameters and

    D_new -> G(K,D_b).

There is no fixed calibration loss forced by a finite nonprimitive input: the actual pair family in part1 supplies arbitrarily large finite nu while retaining D_a=7. If desired, nu may be selected separately for each prescribed horizon.

Nevertheless, the specific transport in part2 always gives strictly LESS mass than the ordinary ConPD join. Let b_0>0 solve b_0^2-b_0=2K/D_b, so G(K,D_b)=2K+b_0 D_b. Since k>1 and c=(2K-sigma)/D_b<2K/D_b, its positive root beta satisfies beta<b_0. Hence

    D_new=2K-sigma+beta D_b < G(K,D_b).               (11)

Thus (10) is an actual finite-residual propagation and calibration test, not an improving upper-bound rule. Repeating this inequality with no further correlations cannot certify a strict gain over the corresponding primitive-prefix G operations.

There is also a legal hard-instance explanation for the lack of a free local gain. For a prefix with physical sum K-1, one bridge tau=1+r, and a tail with physical sum T, the accepted one-cut fact b98bb807d514e80d gives

    W >= r^2/[K^2(2T+1)].

If a proposed certificate were Huber-tight with mass2(K+T+r)+1, it would have to satisfy

    r^2[2(K+T+r)+1] <= K^2(2T+1).                  (12)

When the tail's own mass is2T+1, ordinary ConPD meets this condition at equality. The affine prefix and tail portions of this witness have t=H=0 for every embedded pair. Consequently the retained pair terms cannot by themselves permit a larger Huber-tight single bridge. This statement concerns this binary setting and does not exclude coordinated multi-bridge certificates or nonsaturated objective weights.
