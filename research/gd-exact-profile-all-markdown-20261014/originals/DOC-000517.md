The proof is self-contained and uses no predecessor or external theorem.

1. Properties of the target class.

For a nonempty compact convex K_ah in a Euclidean space, let P_ah(U) be the unique minimizer of ||U-Y||^2 over Y in K_ah. Existence follows from compactness; uniqueness follows from strict convexity of the squared norm on the convex set. For Y in K_ah, the characterization of Y=P_ah(U) is
    <U-Y,Z-Y> <= 0 for every Z in K_ah.                         (A)
Necessity follows by differentiating the squared distance at the endpoint Y of each segment from Y to Z. Conversely, expanding
    ||U-Z||^2=||U-Y||^2-2<U-Y,Z-Y>+||Z-Y||^2
shows that (A) implies Y minimizes squared distance.

Applying (A) at U and V, using the other projection as comparison point, and adding gives
    <P_ah(U)-P_ah(V),U-V> >= ||P_ah(U)-P_ah(V)||^2.
Let r_ah(U)=U-P_ah(U). Expanding ||r_ah(U)-r_ah(V)||^2 gives
    ||r_ah(U)-r_ah(V)||^2
      =||U-V||^2-2<U-V,P_ah(U)-P_ah(V)>
                   +||P_ah(U)-P_ah(V)||^2
      <=||U-V||^2.
In particular r_ah is 1-Lipschitz.

Put d_ah(U)=dist(U,K_ah)^2/2. For any displacement v, using P_ah(U) as a trial point at U+v, and P_ah(U+v) as a trial point at U, gives respectively
    d_ah(U+v)-d_ah(U) <= <r_ah(U),v>+||v||^2/2,
    d_ah(U+v)-d_ah(U) >= <r_ah(U+v),v>-||v||^2/2.
The difference of the two linear terms has absolute value at most ||v||^2 by the Lipschitz bound. Subtracting <r_ah(U),v> therefore leaves an error between -3||v||^2/2 and ||v||^2/2. This proves differentiability and grad d_ah(U)=r_ah(U).

Completing the square in the definition of Phi_ah yields
    Phi_ah(X)=<lambda_ah,X>-||lambda_ah||^2/2+d_ah(X-lambda_ah).
Its unique minimizing Y is P_ah(X-lambda_ah), and
    grad Phi_ah(X)=lambda_ah+r_ah(X-lambda_ah)
                 =X-P_ah(X-lambda_ah).                         (B)
The Lipschitz bound for r_ah proves that this gradient has Lipschitz constant at most 1.

To check convexity directly, fix U,V and t in [0,1]. Let Y_U=P_ah(U-lambda_ah) and Y_V=P_ah(V-lambda_ah). The point tY_U+(1-t)Y_V lies in K_ah. Use it as a trial point at tU+(1-t)V. Its linear height is t<lambda_ah,Y_U>+(1-t)<lambda_ah,Y_V>, and convexity of the squared norm bounds its squared-distance term by the same convex combination of the two squared-distance terms. It follows that
    Phi_ah(tU+(1-t)V)<=t*Phi_ah(U)+(1-t)Phi_ah(V).
All values are finite because the minimized expression is continuous on the nonempty compact K_ah.

The strict exposing condition implies <lambda_ah,Y>>=0 on K_ah, with equality only when Y=0. Both terms in the defining minimum are nonnegative. At X=0 the choice Y=0 gives value zero. If Phi_ah(X)=0, its attained minimizing Y must have zero height and X=Y; hence Y=X=0. Thus argmin Phi_ah={0}. This includes K_ah={0}, for which Phi_ah(X)=||X||^2/2. With advertised smoothness 1, every restricted instance is consequently an original instance with selected minimizer zero. Therefore W_aff_n(h)<=W_n(h) at L=1.

2. Strict source and every ordered original row.

Fix a mu-strongly convex, ell-smooth source with mu>0 and 0<ell<1, minimum f(0)=0, and x_0!=0, as in the statement. All samples indexed by 0,...,n are its actual iterates, including n; add the genuine optimum sample star. Use the definitions g_i, v_ah(i), y_ah(i), delta_ah(i) and gamma_ah in the statement.

For any differentiable function with ell-Lipschitz gradient, integration along a segment from u to u+w gives the descent inequality
    f(u+w)<=f(u)+<grad f(u),w>+(ell/2)||w||^2.
Indeed, after subtracting the constant and linear terms, the integral remainder is
    integral_(t=0)^1 <grad f(u+tw)-grad f(u),w> dt,
which is at most integral_(t=0)^1 ell*t*||w||^2 dt.

For any ordered sample pair i,j in {star,0,...,n}, consider q_ah(x)=f(x)-<g_i,x>. It is convex and ell-smooth, and grad q_ah(x_i)=0, so x_i is a global minimizer. Applying the descent inequality at x_j with displacement -(1/ell)grad q_ah(x_j) yields
    q_ah(x_i)<=q_ah(x_j-(1/ell)grad q_ah(x_j))
             <=q_ah(x_j)-||g_j-g_i||^2/(2ell).
Define the ordered residual, in the project's usual orientation,
    Q_ij=v_ah(i)-v_ah(j)+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2.
Rearrangement gives
    Q_ij <= -gamma_ah||g_i-g_j||^2.                            (C)
Substituting x_i=y_ah(i)+g_i and v_ah(i)=delta_ah(i)+||g_i||^2/2 also gives exactly
    Q_ij=delta_ah(i)-delta_ah(j)+<g_i,y_ah(j)-y_ah(i)>.          (D)
With i=star, Q_(star,j)=-delta_ah(j); thus
    delta_ah(j)>=gamma_ah||g_j||^2>=0.                         (E)

Adding the strong-convexity inequalities in the two directions gives
    <g_i-g_j,x_i-x_j> >= mu||x_i-x_j||^2.
Therefore equal gradients imply equal points, equal source values, equal y_ah, and equal delta_ah. A zero gradient forces x_i=0, and hence y_ah(i)=delta_ah(i)=0. Conversely delta_ah(i)=0 forces g_i=0 by (E), and then Y_ah(i)=0 in the forthcoming construction. The argument covers coincident labels, repetitions, and zero steps without deleting any index.

3. Simultaneous normal tests for one fixed lift.

Define the finite scalars z_ah(i), e_ah(i) by the statement. They depend only on the fixed finite source trajectory and word. They are finite and independent of B_ah, since their definitions use only finitely many affine operations on finite source data and finite steps. Form lambda_ah, Y_ah, X_ah, G_ah and K_ah exactly as stated.

The convex hull K_ah is compact and has at most n+2 vertices. Its listed generators have
    <lambda_ah,Y_ah(i)>=delta_ah(i)>=0.
Each nonzero listed generator has strictly positive height by (E) and the zero-gradient implication. A convex combination of these generators and zero can have zero height only if every positive weight is on a zero generator. Such a combination equals zero. Consequently lambda_ah strictly exposes zero on all of K_ah.

For every 0<=i<=n and every j in {star,0,...,n}, expand:
    <G_ah(i)-lambda_ah,Y_ah(j)-Y_ah(i)>
      =<g_i,y_ah(j)-y_ah(i)>
          +(e_ah(i)/B_ah-B_ah)(delta_ah(j)-delta_ah(i))/B_ah
      =Q_ij+e_ah(i)(delta_ah(j)-delta_ah(i))/B_ah^2.            (F)
When g_i=g_j, the source points and heights agree, so Y_ah(i)=Y_ah(j), Q_ij=0, and the correction term is zero. The lifted points X_ah(i) need not agree on a horizontal revisit, and no agreement of their vertical coordinates is assumed or needed.

For g_i!=g_j, the denominator gamma_ah||g_i-g_j||^2 in M_ah is strictly positive. There are finitely many such pairs, so M_ah is finite. If B_ah^2>=max(1,2M_ah), the positive part of the correction in (F) is at most gamma_ah||g_i-g_j||^2/2. This follows directly from the defining maximum when M_ah>0. If M_ah=0 every such positive part is zero. Combining with (C), the normal in (F) is at most
    -gamma_ah||g_i-g_j||^2/2
on every distinct-gradient pair, and zero on every equal-gradient pair.

Since these comparison points generate K_ah, linearity in the comparison point proves
    <X_ah(i)-lambda_ah-Y_ah(i),Z-Y_ah(i)> <=0
for every Z in K_ah and every i=0,...,n. This includes the terminal as the projecting sample and as a comparison generator, all ordered remote pairs, and every sample-to-optimum normal. At the actual lifted optimum X=0, the projection normal at Y=0 is <-lambda_ah,Z><=0 for every Z in K_ah, so P_ah(-lambda_ah)=0 also holds.

The normal test (A) and gradient formula (B) now give
    P_ah(X_ah(i)-lambda_ah)=Y_ah(i),
    grad Phi_ah(X_ah(i))=X_ah(i)-Y_ah(i)=G_ah(i)
for every i=0,...,n. Evaluation at this actual minimizing Y gives
    Phi_ah(X_ah(i))
      =delta_ah(i)+||G_ah(i)||^2/2
      =v_ah(i)+e_ah(i)^2/(2B_ah^2).                           (G)
If a source gradient is zero while z_ah(i) is nonzero, Y_ah(i)=0 but the lifted vertical residual is retained. Its normals against nonzero generators are distinct-gradient pairs in (F), so the same simultaneous bound controls them.

4. Actual dynamics, radius, and terminal domination.

For each original update 0<=i<n,
    X_ah(i)-h_i G_ah(i)
      =(x_i-h_i g_i,(z_ah(i)-h_i e_ah(i))/B_ah)
      =(x_(i+1),((1-h_i)z_ah(i)+h_i delta_ah(i))/B_ah)
      =X_ah(i+1).
The equality of G_ah(i) with the gradient of the single static function Phi_ah proves that all these points are its actual GD trajectory. The entire source trajectory and finite scalars were fixed before B_ah and the polytope were chosen. The function is fixed before the lifted run, including its terminal, and is never reselected during that run.

Since z_ah(0)=0, the initial point is X_ah(0)=(x_0,0), is nonzero, and has inner product zero with lambda_ah. The complete minimizer set was proved to be {0}, so
    dist(X_ah(0),argmin Phi_ah)^2=||X_ah(0)||^2=||x_0||^2.
Using (G) at the actual terminal gives
    2Phi_ah(X_ah(n))/||X_ah(0)||^2
      =2f(x_n)/||x_0||^2+e_ah(n)^2/(B_ah^2||x_0||^2).
This proves every assertion of the constructive statement. The extra term is nonnegative. For fixed source and word its coefficient is fixed and finite, so it is of order B_ah^(-2). Terminal domination already holds at each allowed finite B_ah.

5. Arbitrary sources, selected minima, and equality of suprema.

First work at advertised smoothness 1. Choose an arbitrary original source, any one of its minimizers x_star, and x_0!=x_star. Translate coordinates by x_star and subtract f(x_star). In these coordinates f(0)=0=min f, grad f(0)=0, and the denominator is the positive number ||x_0||^2. No uniqueness or closest-point assumption has been made for the source's selected minimizer.

For each 0<epsilon<1/2, put
    f_eps(x)=(1-2epsilon)f(x)+(epsilon/2)||x||^2.
Convexity of f and positivity of 1-2epsilon give epsilon-strong convexity. Its gradient has Lipschitz constant at most
    (1-2epsilon)+epsilon=1-epsilon<1.
Both terms are nonnegative; equality at zero and the positive quadratic show that its unique minimum is zero at zero.

Let x_i^eps be the actual trajectory of f_eps from this same x_0 with the same h. For each fixed finite word these trajectories converge to the original trajectory as epsilon decreases to zero. The initial points agree. If x_i^eps tends to x_i, then these points are bounded near epsilon=0 and continuity of grad f gives
    grad f_eps(x_i^eps)
      =(1-2epsilon)grad f(x_i^eps)+epsilon x_i^eps
      ->grad f(x_i).
The update with the fixed finite h_i then gives x_(i+1)^eps->x_(i+1). Finite induction proves convergence at n. Continuity of f and boundedness of x_n^eps imply
    f_eps(x_n^eps)
      =(1-2epsilon)f(x_n^eps)+(epsilon/2)||x_n^eps||^2
      ->f(x_n).

For EACH epsilon the constructive assertion applies to this strict source with mu=epsilon and ell=1-epsilon. It gives some restricted instance, in one extra dimension and with at most n+2 vertices, whose payoff is at least 2f_eps(x_n^eps)/||x_0||^2. Consequently
    W_aff_n(h)>=2f_eps(x_n^eps)/||x_0||^2
for every epsilon. The left side is the same fixed-word supremum throughout. Taking the limit in these scalar inequalities yields
    W_aff_n(h)>=2f(x_n)/||x_0||^2.
Since the original source, selected minimizer, dimension, and initial point were arbitrary, W_aff_n(h)>=W_n(h). Together with the reverse inclusion inequality proved in paragraph 1, this establishes equality at advertised smoothness 1.

For any L>0, translate the selected minimizer and replace the original objective by (f-f(x_star))/L. The resulting source is 1-smooth. Its updates with h are exactly the original updates with h/L on f, and its normalized payoff is exactly 2(f(x_n)-f(x_star))/(L||x_0-x_star||^2). Conversely, multiplication of any restricted Phi_ah by L supplies an admissible L-smooth original objective with the same trajectory and payoff. Thus the same equality holds for every advertised L and respects W_n=2R_n.

The order of choices is fixed n,h; arbitrary original source and selected minimizer; each epsilon; its actual finite run; a finite B_ah for that run; then one restricted instance. No exchange of a horizon limit with a supremum, no uniform choice of B_ah, and no convergence of the lifted polytopes is used. If n=0 the sample set is {star,0}, the recurrence has no updates, and every displayed construction and inequality still applies. Zero steps, repeated samples, equal gradients, source arrivals at zero, arbitrary finite dimensions, and large finite nonnegative steps were covered without deleting any sample. This proves only the stated representation equality and constructive identities.
