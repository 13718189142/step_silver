# Geometric gradient ladders cannot sustain the full recursive crossing pattern

Status: complete scoped candidate proof, not verifier-accepted. No new verifier
call. This is a growing-complexity scalar obstruction with an explicit gradient
recurrence. It does not assume nested inward returns. Its hypotheses are the
specified crossing pattern and a geometric grid for gradients on one ray.

## 1. The schedule and the class being tested

Put c=13/5, a=c^(-17), b_u=a c^u, H_0=(), and

    H_(m+1)=(H_m,b_m,H_m),
    s_u=sum H_u=a(c^u-2^u)/(c-2).

Fix m>r>=18, set J=m-r, b=b_r, and S=s_r. The exact word H_m consists
of 2^J copies of H_r separated by 2^J-1 checkpoint steps. Their levels form

    T_0=(),       T_(j+1)=(T_j,j,T_j),

where a level ell stands for the actual step b c^ell. In zero-based order,
every even-indexed checkpoint has level zero. The odd-indexed checkpoints
have levels ell>=1; level ell occurs 2^(J-ell-1) times for 1<=ell<=J-1.
The last checkpoint has level zero.

Let f be ANY fixed differentiable convex scalar function with 1-Lipschitz
derivative, f(0)=0=min f. Assume its GD trajectory starts at x_0>0 and has
positive terminal objective. Impose the following two trajectory hypotheses:

1. Every displayed checkpoint crosses zero strictly; no step within a copy
   of H_r crosses zero. Gradients inside those copies may vary arbitrarily.
2. The positive gradient magnitudes just before the level-zero checkpoints
   all belong to one grid {theta gamma^z : z is an integer}, with theta>0
   and gamma>=c. Both parameters may depend on the complete horizon.

There is no grid assumption on negative gradients, no symmetry assumption on
f, no fixed core curvature, and no restriction on whether a return is inward,
overlapping, or outward. The number of crossings is 2^J-1 and can grow with m.

Then the normalized terminal objective satisfies

    2f(x_(2^m-1))/x_0^2
      <= [(b-1)^2/((S+1)^2(2S+1))] gamma^(2-2J)
      <= [1/(2 eta^3 b)] gamma^(2-2J),   eta=13/8.            (1)

In particular, uniformly over r, all gradient-grid parameters, every legal
choice of knots and all such trajectories,

    2f(x_(2^m-1))/x_0^2 <= [c/(2 eta^3 a)] c^(-m).           (2)

The ratio of the supremum in (2) to the exact U_(2^m-1)^ZJ tends to zero.

## 2. Proof of the exact gradient recurrence

For a same-sign block of total step sum S ending at gradient magnitude v,
all earlier magnitudes in that block are at least v and its final radius is
at least v. Consequently its starting radius A satisfies

    A >= (S+1)v.                                           (3)

A crossing step beta whose input gradient magnitude is u has landing radius
at most (beta-1)u, since the input radius is at least u. Thus two consecutive
checkpoints of levels (0,ell), and the two intervening same-sign H_r blocks,
give the following bound between their successive positive checkpoint
gradient magnitudes u and u_next:

    u_next/u <= [(b-1)(b c^ell-1)]/(S+1)^2.                 (4)

This follows by applying (3) first on the negative block and then on the
positive block. It does not make any assumption about their derivative
values between endpoints beyond monotonicity on each ray.

Since (2/c)^17<1/40, we have S>=eta b for r>=18. Also

    eta^2=169/64 > 13/5=c.

Therefore (4) is strictly smaller than

    c^ell/eta^2 < c^(ell-1) <= gamma^(ell-1).                (5)

Hypothesis 2 makes u_next/u an integer power of gamma. It follows that

    u_next/u <= gamma^(ell-2).                             (6)

This strict-integer step is the essential use of the geometric grid. Without
the grid, (5) alone does not imply (6).

Pair the checkpoints as (0,ell), leaving the final level-zero checkpoint
unpaired. The total exponent in (6) is

    sum_(ell=1)^(J-1) (ell-2)2^(J-ell-1)=1-J.               (7)

For example this sum is empty and zero for J=1. In general it follows by
subtracting twice sum 2^(J-ell-1)=2^(J-1)-1 from
sum ell 2^(J-ell-1)=2^J-J-1. Thus if u_first and u_last denote the positive
gradient magnitudes at the first and last checkpoints,

    u_last <= gamma^(1-J) u_first.                         (8)

The first H_r gives x_0>=(S+1)u_first. After the last checkpoint the negative
landing radius A is at most (b-1)u_last. The remaining H_r stays on that ray.
Let w be its terminal gradient magnitude and R its terminal radius. Then
R+S w<=A. Integrating the globally fixed derivative on this ray gives

    f(x_n)<=Rw-w^2/2,
    2f(x_n)<=2A w-(2S+1)w^2<=A^2/(2S+1).                  (9)

Indeed the derivative magnitude at radius t<=R is at most min(t,w). This
includes all terminal area and keeps the minimizer fixed at zero. Combining
(8)-(9) proves the first bound in (1). The second follows from S>=eta b.

Since J>=1 and gamma>=c, gamma^(2-2J)<=c^(2-2J). Substituting b=a c^r
and m=r+J bounds (1) by

    [1/(2 eta^3 a)] c^(-m) c^(2-J)
       <=[c/(2 eta^3 a)]c^(-m),

which proves (2). The proof applies even when r and all profile parameters
change with m. No finite-parameter compactness claim is being substituted.

## 3. Why a homothetic staircase relay necessarily enters this obstruction

The proposed self-similar positive-ray profile uses plateau heights

    M_z=theta gamma^z

at radial intervals [ell_0 M_z,u_0 M_z], where 1<=ell_0<=u_0. Adjacent
plateaus can be connected by a nondecreasing 1-Lipschitz derivative only if

    gamma ell_0-u_0 >= gamma-1.                            (10)

Equality corresponds to a slope-one connecting ramp; strict inequality
allows gentler or nonconstant connecting slopes. Together with an arbitrary
legal negative-ray derivative this defines a globally fixed convex smooth
function. The infinite staircase extends continuously to zero; alternatively
one can retain the finitely many relevant levels and attach a legal inner
core and outer cap before the run. All endpoints and slopes must be fixed.

Consider the proposed relay in which each positive-ray H_r block remains on
one of these plateaus, and every displayed checkpoint crosses. Its motion
during such a block has radial length S M_z. Therefore the plateau width
must satisfy

    u_0-ell_0 >= S.

Together with (10), this forces

    (gamma-1)(ell_0-1)>=S.                                 (11)

At the first, level-zero checkpoint, the departure radius is at least
ell_0 M_z. A strict crossing with step b requires ell_0<b. Hence

    gamma > 1+S/(b-1) > 1+eta =21/8 > c.                   (12)

The same positive profile therefore satisfies the grid hypothesis of
Section 1 automatically. Its negative ray and every negative H_r block may
be arbitrary subject to the stated sign pattern. The construction may use
an increasing number of plateaus, may repeatedly revisit them, may have
different curvatures between them, and may change all its parameters with
the horizon. It is nevertheless bounded by (1)-(2).

This is a concrete failure of the intended multiscale plateau relay: the
geometric spacing needed to accommodate an entire ordinary block forces a
full gradient-level loss at the frequent low checkpoints. The rarer high
checkpoints cannot compensate; their exact total exponent is 1-J.

## 4. Exact-profile comparison and boundary of the result

Here is a self-contained sufficient benchmark estimate. For the exact DP set
C_n=S_circle(n)+1 and D_n=2S_bullet(n)+1. Their joins are

    Omega(A,B)=(A+B+sqrt((A+B)^2+4AB))/2,
    G(A,D)=2A+D/2+sqrt(D^2+8AD)/2.

The inequality Omega(A,B)<=(A^(3/4)+B^(3/4))^(4/3) reduces to
t^(4/3)+(1-t)^(4/3)+[t(1-t)]^(4/3)<=1. With x=t^(1/3) and
y=(1-t)^(1/3), its difference from 1 is
x^3 y^3[1/(1+x+x^2)+1/(1+y+y^2)-xy]>=0: each reciprocal is at least 1/3
and xy<=2^(-2/3)<2/3. Induction gives C_n<=(n+1)^(4/3). Since
G(A,D)<=D+4A and u^(4/3)+v^(4/3)<=(u+v)^(4/3), induction in the dominant
DP gives D_n<=1+4n^(4/3). Now c^3>16, so multiplying (2) by D_(2^m-1)
proves that the normalized exact-profile ratio tends to zero.

The theorem does NOT cover arbitrary geometric-gradient profiles when their
trajectory skips one of the prescribed crossings, crosses inside H_r, or
has positive checkpoint gradients outside the stated grid. It does NOT
exclude general scalar functions, and it does NOT bound multidimensional W.
In particular the already shared coupled-coordinate separation is compatible
with it.

A next scalar construction must evade the explicit lost-grid-level step:
for example by traversing several knots inside the positive H_r blocks with
dense gradient levels, or by altering the coarse crossing pattern. Merely
adding more homothetic plateau levels does not evade the proof.
