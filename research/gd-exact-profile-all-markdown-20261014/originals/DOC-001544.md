## statement
A tighter exact all-split benchmark envelope and a conditional GD certificate threshold.

Fix L>0. For every integer n>=0 and predetermined nonnegative finite word h=(h_0,...,h_(n-1)), run gradient descent
x_(t+1)=x_t-(h_t/L)*grad f(x_t), 0<=t<n.
Let W_n(h) be the supremum of 2(f(x_n)-f(x_star))/(L||x_0-x_star||^2) over every finite Euclidean dimension, every convex differentiable function f with L-Lipschitz gradient and an attained minimizer x_star, and every x_0!=x_star. Let R_n(h) be the same supremum without the numerator factor 2, so W_n(h)=2R_n(h). Put W_n_star=inf_(h in [0,infinity)^n) W_n(h), including the empty word at n=0.

Define lambda=1+sqrt(2), rho=log_2(lambda). For nonnegative real x,y put
Omega(x,y)=(x+y+sqrt(x^2+6xy+y^2))/2,
G(x,y)=2x+y/2+sqrt(y^2+8xy)/2.
Define C_0=B_0=1 and, for EVERY integer n>=1,
C_n=max_(integer 0<=k<n) Omega(C_k,C_(n-k-1)),
B_n=max_(integer 0<=k<n) G(C_k,B_(n-k-1)).
These are the original primitive and dominant benchmark capacities, with EVERY integer split retained. Put U_n^ZJ=1/B_n.

For every integer n>=0,
C_n<=(n+1)^rho and B_n<=(12/5)(n+1)^rho.

For each integer m>=1536, set epsilon=1/(4m), beta=1-epsilon, c=lambda(1+epsilon), and a=beta/c. Define the finite words
H_0=(), H_(j+1)=(H_j,1+a*c^j,H_j), 0<=j<m.
The same enclosing m, epsilon, beta, c, and a are used at every child. Here the hierarchy symbols H_j,a,c are local to this statement. Put S_j=sum H_j, n_m=2^m-1, and D_m=1+2S_m. Then H_m is a predetermined strictly positive word of length n_m and
B_(n_m)/D_m<47/50
for every integer m>=1536.

Finally, the following is a CONDITIONAL implication, with its entire hypothesis left unproved: IF
W_(n_m)(H_m)<=17/(16D_m) for EVERY integer m>=1536,
as a bound on the full function-class supremum W defined here, THEN for every such m,
B_(n_m) W_(n_m)(H_m)<799/800,
W_(n_m)_star/U_(n_m)^ZJ<799/800,
and
liminf_(n->infinity) W_n_star/U_n^ZJ<=799/800<1.

No unconditional full-function upper bound on W_(n_m)(H_m) is asserted. The unconditional conclusions are the two all-n benchmark envelopes and the hierarchy benchmark ratio. The original unrestricted minimax problem remains unresolved by this result alone.

## proof
The accepted predecessor b98bb807d514e80d is used only to identify the original benchmark coordinates and normalization: C_n=S_circle(n)+1, B_n=2S_bullet(n)+1, the displayed Omega and G recurrences, and the factor-2 definition of W_n. All inequalities needed here are proved in this proof. No saved numerical enclosure, evaluated recurrence, other accepted estimate, or unverified finding is used.

1. Exact exponent and positivity.

The identity lambda^2=2lambda+1 gives lambda(lambda-2)=1 and lambda^3=7+5sqrt(2). Since 2>49/25, sqrt(2)>7/5, so lambda^3>14. Therefore
lambda^15>14^5=537824>524288=2^19.
Taking the increasing base-2 logarithm gives rho>19/15>1. In particular rho/2>19/30. Also sqrt(2)<3/2, whence lambda<5/2 and lambda^2=2lambda+1<6.
The finite recurrences define positive finite capacities: C_0=B_0=1, and Omega and G map positive finite pairs to positive finite numbers. Each maximum is over a nonempty finite set when n>=1.

2. Primitive power inequality, including zero arguments.

For t>0 let E_be(t)=(1+t)^rho-1. It is positive. For z in the real line set Q_be(z)=log E_be(exp z), where log is natural. With t=exp z,
Q_be'(z)=rho*t*(1+t)^(rho-1)/E_be(t).
Since dt/dz=t, differentiation and collection of factors gives
Q_be''(z)
=rho*t*(1+t)^(rho-2)*[(1+rho*t)E_be(t)-rho*t*(1+t)^rho]/E_be(t)^2
=rho*t*(1+t)^(rho-2)*[(1+t)^rho-1-rho*t]/E_be(t)^2.
The function of t in the last square bracket is zero at t=0 and has derivative rho*((1+t)^(rho-1)-1)>0 for t>0. Consequently Q_be''(z)>0 for every real z. Convexity applied at z and -z yields
Q_be(z)+Q_be(-z)>=2Q_be(0).
Exponentiating and using 2^rho=lambda gives
E_be(t)*E_be(1/t)>=E_be(1)^2=(lambda-1)^2=2 for every t>0.

Take x,y>0, let u=x^(1/rho), w=y^(1/rho), and T_be=(u+w)^rho. Then
T_be-x=x E_be(w/u), T_be-y=y E_be(u/w).
Multiplying proves (T_be-x)(T_be-y)>=2xy, or
T_be^2-(x+y)T_be-xy>=0.
The roots of the latter polynomial are
[(x+y) +/- sqrt((x+y)^2+4xy)]/2.
For x,y>0 the smaller root is negative, the larger is Omega(x,y), and T_be>0. Thus T_be>=Omega(x,y). If one of x,y is zero, Omega(x,0)=x and Omega(0,y)=y, so the same inequality is equality; at (0,0) both sides are zero. This proves
Omega(x,y)<=(x^(1/rho)+y^(1/rho))^rho for all x,y>=0.

The polynomial x^2+6xy+y^2 is nondecreasing in each nonnegative variable, as is x+y. Monotonicity of the square root shows that Omega is nondecreasing in each argument on the whole nonnegative quadrant. For the primitive induction, C_0=1=(0+1)^rho. Suppose the bound is proved at all indices below n>=1. For every integer k with 0<=k<n, both k and n-k-1 are below n, so
Omega(C_k,C_(n-k-1))
<=Omega((k+1)^rho,(n-k)^rho)
<=(k+1+n-k)^rho=(n+1)^rho.
Taking the maximum over every allowed integer k proves C_n<=(n+1)^rho.

3. A scalar inequality that will control every dominant split.

For every real p>=19/30 and x in [0,1], we claim
(5/6)x^(2p)+(1-x)^p<=1.
The exponents are positive, so zero bases present no ambiguity. Both summands are nonincreasing functions of p for each fixed x in [0,1]. It suffices to establish the claim at p=19/30. Define the continuous function
Psi_be(x)=(5/6)x^(19/15)+(1-x)^(19/30), 0<=x<=1.
It has endpoint values Psi_be(0)=1 and Psi_be(1)=5/6. On the open interval,
Psi_be'(x)=(19/18)x^(4/15)-(19/30)(1-x)^(-11/30).
Thus each interior critical point satisfies
x^(4/15)*(1-x)^(11/30)=3/5,
equivalently x^8*(1-x)^11=(3/5)^30.
For x in (0,1), logarithmic differentiation of x^8*(1-x)^11 gives
8/x-11/(1-x)=(8-19x)/(x(1-x)).
It is strictly negative for x>8/19. Since 2/3>8/19, every x in [2/3,1) satisfies
x^8*(1-x)^11<=2^8/3^19<(3/5)^30.
For the strict inequality, multiply positive denominators and use the exact integer comparison
2^8*5^30=238418579101562500000000
<239299329230617529590083=3^49.
It follows that every interior critical point of Psi_be is in (0,2/3).

At an interior critical point, the derivative equation gives
(5/6)x^(19/15)=(x/2)*(1-x)^(-11/30).
Hence its value is
Psi_be(x)=(1-x/2)*(1-x)^(-11/30)=g_be(x).
The right side defines a positive continuously differentiable function on [0,2/3]. Its logarithmic derivative is
g_be'(x)/g_be(x)=-1/(2-x)+11/[30(1-x)]
=(19x-8)/[30(1-x)(2-x)].
The denominator is positive on [0,2/3]. Therefore g_be decreases on [0,8/19] and increases on [8/19,2/3]. Its maximum on [0,2/3] is at an endpoint. At the endpoints,
g_be(0)=1,
g_be(2/3)^30=2^30/3^19<1,
where 2^30=1073741824<1162261467=3^19.
Because g_be(2/3)>0, this proves g_be(2/3)<1. In particular every interior critical value of Psi_be is at most 1.
The continuous function Psi_be attains a maximum on [0,1]. If a maximizing point is an endpoint the value is at most 1 by the endpoint calculation; if it is interior its derivative vanishes and the critical-value argument applies. This proves Psi_be<=1 and therefore the claimed scalar inequality for all p>=19/30 and x in [0,1]. No differentiability at x=1 is required.

4. Dominant power inequality and induction over all splits.

Put kappa_be=12/5. For x,y>=0 define
r_be=(sqrt(y)+sqrt(y+8x))/2.
Direct squaring yields
r_be^2=y/2+2x+sqrt(y^2+8xy)/2=G(x,y).
Thus sqrt(G(x,y))=r_be, the larger nonnegative root of
z^2-sqrt(y)z-2x=0.
The other root is nonpositive. For a positive trial z, if this polynomial is nonnegative, then z is at least its larger root. This includes x=0 or y=0; when both vanish the larger root is zero.
The formula for G is coordinatewise nondecreasing on x,y>=0, because its linear terms and its square-root radicand have that property.

We prove B_n<=kappa_be(n+1)^rho by strong induction. At n=0 this is 1<=12/5. Suppose it holds below n>=1. For each original integer split k with 0<=k<n set u=k+1, w=n-k, and N_be=u+w=n+1. Then u,w>=1 and the primitive bound already proved gives
G(C_k,B_(n-k-1))<=G(u^rho,kappa_be*w^rho).
Take the positive trial z=sqrt(kappa_be)*N_be^(rho/2). The root polynomial for this last G value is nonnegative at z if and only if
kappa_be*N_be^rho-kappa_be*w^(rho/2)*N_be^(rho/2)-2u^rho>=0,
or, after division by the positive quantity kappa_be*N_be^rho,
(2/kappa_be)*(u/N_be)^rho+(w/N_be)^(rho/2)<=1.
Apply part 3 with x=u/N_be in (0,1) and p=rho/2>19/30. Because 2/kappa_be=5/6 and w/N_be=1-x, that part gives exactly this inequality. Consequently
G(u^rho,kappa_be*w^rho)<=z^2=kappa_be(n+1)^rho.
The same bound holds for EVERY split k, so its maximum B_n satisfies it. This completes the dominant induction, including n=0.

5. Exact mass of the same-enclosing-m hierarchy.

Fix an arbitrary integer m>=1536. The parameters epsilon,beta,c,a are those in the statement and do not change with j. They satisfy
0<epsilon=1/(4m)<=1/6144<1/8,
0<beta<1, c>lambda>2, a>0.
Every bridge 1+a*c^j is strictly positive. Length starts at zero and satisfies length(H_(j+1))=2length(H_j)+1, so induction gives length(H_j)=2^j-1 for all 0<=j<=m.
Summing the literal concatenation gives S_0=0 and
S_(j+1)=2S_j+1+a*c^j.
Define A_be,j=1+S_j for 0<=j<=m and v_be=a/(c-2)=beta/[c(c-2)].
Then A_be,0=1 and A_be,j+1=2A_be,j+a*c^j.
For all these indices the exact solution is
A_be,j=(1-v_be)2^j+v_be*c^j.
Indeed the initial value is 1, and substituting the right side into the recurrence gives
(1-v_be)2^(j+1)+(2v_be+a)c^j
=(1-v_be)2^(j+1)+v_be*c^(j+1),
using v_be(c-2)=a.

For t>2, t(t-2) is strictly increasing since its derivative is 2t-2>0. Thus c>lambda>2 and lambda(lambda-2)=1 imply c(c-2)>1. Since 0<beta<1, one has 0<v_be<1, so A_be,m>=v_be*c^m.

Expanding c=lambda(1+epsilon) gives
c(c-2)=1+2(lambda+1)epsilon+lambda^2 epsilon^2
<1+7epsilon+6epsilon^2,
where the strict upper bound uses lambda<5/2 and lambda^2<6.
The numerator beta=1-epsilon is positive, so
v_be>(1-epsilon)/(1+7epsilon+6epsilon^2).
Furthermore
(1-8epsilon)(1+7epsilon+6epsilon^2)
=1-epsilon-50epsilon^2-48epsilon^3<1-epsilon.
Division by the positive denominator shows v_be>1-8epsilon=1-2/m>0.

The binomial expansion at integer m>=2 has nonnegative terms. Its first three terms give
(1+1/(4m))^m
>=1+1/4+[m(m-1)/2]/(16m^2)
=41/32-1/(32m)>0.
Also 2^m>m for integers m>=1: at m=1 this is 2>1; if 2^m>m then 2^(m+1)>2m>=m+1. Since lambda>2, lambda^m>m and lambda^(-m)<1/m.

Now D_m=1+2S_m=2A_be,m-1 is positive. Using the exact A_be,m formula, then the positive lower bounds just proved, yields
D_m/lambda^m
>=2v_be*(1+1/(4m))^m-lambda^(-m)
>2(1-2/m)*(41/32-1/(32m))-1/m
=41/16-99/(16m)+1/(8m^2)
>41/16-99/(16m)
>=41/16-99/(16*1536)
=20959/8192
>120/47.
The last strict comparison follows from
20959*47=985073>983040=8192*120.
Every division and multiplication in this chain is by a positive quantity, and m=1536 is included.

At the actual horizon n_m=2^m-1, part 4 gives
B_(n_m)<=(12/5)(2^m)^rho=(12/5)lambda^m.
Combining the upper bound on B with the strict lower bound on D gives
B_(n_m)/D_m<(12/5)/(120/47)=47/50.
The strictness is retained because D_m/lambda^m>120/47 and B_(n_m)>0. This proves the unconditional hierarchy comparison with the SAME enclosing parameters at every child.

6. Conditional conclusion and normalization.

The definitions of W_n and R_n have identical suprema and differ only by multiplication of each nonnegative payoff by 2. Multiplication by this positive constant commutes with the supremum, so W_n=2R_n. We use W throughout, with no rescaling of the supplied hypothetical bound.

For this part ONLY, assume the full-class upper bound
W_(n_m)(H_m)<=17/(16D_m)
for every integer m>=1536. H_m is an admissible predetermined word of length n_m, so the infimum defining W_(n_m)_star satisfies
W_(n_m)_star<=W_(n_m)(H_m).
Because B_(n_m)>0 and U_(n_m)^ZJ=1/B_(n_m), part 5 gives
W_(n_m)_star/U_(n_m)^ZJ
=B_(n_m)*W_(n_m)_star
<=B_(n_m)*W_(n_m)(H_m)
<=(17/16)*B_(n_m)/D_m
<(17/16)*(47/50)=799/800.
The same displayed chain also proves B_(n_m)*W_(n_m)(H_m)<799/800.

For every integer cutoff N there is an m>=1536 with n_m>=N, since n_m=2^m-1 tends to infinity. Thus the infimum of W_n_star/U_n^ZJ over all n>=N is at most 799/800. Taking the increasing limit of these tail infima proves
liminf_(n->infinity) W_n_star/U_n^ZJ<=799/800<1.
This conclusion is conditional on the full-class upper hypothesis, which has not been established in this proof. Only the benchmark envelopes and the hierarchy benchmark comparison are unconditional.

## intuition
A logarithmic-coordinate convexity identity bounds the primitive join at the silver exponent. An exact critical-point argument gives the 12/5 dominant envelope. The inflated same-m hierarchy has enough scalar mass for a 47/50 benchmark ratio; converting this into a minimax improvement still requires the explicitly unproved full-function upper hypothesis.
