## 2. The exact matrix necessity being tested

Use the actual smooth-convex rows

    Q_ij = 2f_i-2f_j-2<g_j,x_i-x_j>-||g_i-g_j||^2,
    Q_star,i = -2(f_i-f_star)+2<g_i,x_i-x_star>-||g_i||^2.

All these rows are nonnegative on normalized convex 1-smooth data. Let

    Z_p = sum_(i=0)^n u_i^p g_i.

The prescribed doubled residual is exactly

    D_p = ||x_0-x_star||^2 - sum_i u_i^p Q_star,i
          -2Bcert(f_n-f_star)-||x_0-x_star-Z_p||^2.       (2.1)

For any zero-row-sum C with nonnegative off-diagonal entries and column sums b_i=u_i (i<n), b_n=-sum_(i<n)u_i, the source identity gives

    D_p = sum_(i,j) C_ji Q_ij + g^T R_p(C) g,            (2.2)

where Bmat has its actual physical lower entries h_j and diagonal 1, and

    R_p(C) = K_p+diag(u_i(1-ell_i)+b_i)
             -(C Bmat+Bmat^T C^T).

Therefore one legal trajectory with D_p<0 gives, for EVERY such C,

    g^T R_p(C) g = D_p-sum_(i,j)C_ji Q_ij <= D_p < 0.  (2.3)

This tests the entire non-diagonal matrix. It does not impose individual diagonal signs, use a free-gradient relaxation, or infer anything from positivity of K alone.

The coefficient constraints on C themselves are nonempty: for example, C_ni=u_i for i<n, C_nn=-sum_(i<n)u_i, and all other entries zero satisfy them. Equation (2.3) will exclude PSD for this explicit C and every other admissible C, including signed interval expansions whose final off-diagonal entries are nonnegative.

## 3. One fixed function and an exact complete-hierarchy trajectory

Choose the symmetric Huber function

    f(x) = x^2/2              if |x|<=1,
           |x|-1/2           if |x|>=1.

The function is convex and differentiable, and its derivative g(x)=clip(x,[-1,1]) is 1-Lipschitz. Its unique global minimizer is the ORIGINAL point x_star=0. At every real query x,

    -2f(x)+2x g(x)-g(x)^2 = 0.                         (3.1)

Thus every Q_star,i vanishes on this fixed function. No function, wall, or minimizer is changed during the trajectory.

Write s_j=sum H_j and k_j=a c^j. Define

    X_1=beta,
    X_j=s_(j-1)+1+k_(j-1)-X_(j-1),    j>=2.           (3.2)

We next prove that these starts are legal for every level of the literal inherited word and that

    T_(H_j)(X_j)=(-1)^j t,       t=a beta=beta^2/c.     (3.3)

### 3.1 Exact room for the saturated left child

Put

    alpha = beta/[c(c-2)] = a/(c-2).

Then 0<alpha<1, and direct summation of the physical word gives

    s_j+1 = alpha c^j + (1-alpha)2^j.                  (3.4)

For j>=1 define

    d_j = k_(j+1)-(s_j+1)
        = (beta-alpha)c^j-(1-alpha)2^j.

The first term is strictly positive:

    d_1=beta c-2-a
       =epsilon[2/(c0(1+epsilon))-c0 epsilon] > 0.     (3.5)

Indeed epsilon<=1/6144, c0<5/2 and 1+epsilon<2 make the first term in the bracket greater than 2/5 and the second less than 5/(2*6144). Moreover,

    d_(j+1)=c d_j+(c-2)(1-alpha)2^j > d_j >0.         (3.6)

Let e_j=k_j-X_j. Equation (3.2) gives e_1=0 and

    X_(j+1)=s_j+1+e_j,
    e_(j+1)=d_j-e_j.                                  (3.7)

Inductively 0<=e_j<=d_(j-1) for j>=2: if this holds, then 0<=d_j-e_j<=d_j by (3.6). In particular k_j>=X_j>0 for every j.

### 3.2 Physical forward execution

The base word H_1 contains the one actual step 1+a. Since X_1=beta<1,

    T_(H_1)(X_1)=beta-(1+a)beta=-a beta=-t.

For j>=2, the start is X_j=s_(j-1)+1+e_(j-1). During the entire first H_(j-1), the iterate remains at least 1 and its gradient is exactly 1. The left child ends at 1+e_(j-1). The following bridge has length 1+k_(j-1), also uses gradient 1, and lands exactly at

    1+e_(j-1)-(1+k_(j-1))=-X_(j-1).

The map x -> x-h clip(x,[-1,1]) is odd for every physical step h. Hence the last child is the negative of the already constructed complete H_(j-1) trajectory. This proves (3.3) by induction.

For odd enclosing m, take x_0=X_m. The ORIGINAL squared radius is X_m^2>0, and the ACTUAL terminal point and gradient are

    x_n=g_n=-t,        f_n=t^2/2.                      (3.8)

All physical gradients except the last physical query have absolute value 1. The last physical gradient is (+beta) for odd m, and its step length is h_(n-1)=1+a>1. This description follows by descending the rightmost recursive child until the base H_1; it includes all 2^m-1 physical updates.

## 4. Uniform enclosing-m asymptotics of the actual trajectory

All limits below let the enclosing m tend to infinity while retaining its parameters throughout all children. They are not limits with fixed internal depth followed by an unproved interchange.

Set

    r = sqrt(2)-1 = 1/c0,
    v = r^2/(1+2sqrt(2)) >0.

We prove

    X_m/S -> r,
    V_m/S^2 -> v,        V_j=sum_(h in H_j)h^2,
    M_m/S^2 -> v,        M_j=sum_(i in H_j) h_i x_(i+1), (4.1)

where the M_j path starts at X_j and uses the same enclosing parameters.

### 4.1 The starting point and the physical second moment

Let

    d(c)=(c-1)/(c+1),
    P=alpha c d(c), Q=2(1-alpha)/3, R0=beta-P-Q.

Solving (3.2), with (3.4), gives the exact finite-depth expression

    X_j=P c^(j-1)+Q 2^(j-1)+R0(-1)^(j-1).            (4.2)

For example, the coefficient of c^(j-1) follows from
P(1+1/c)=alpha+beta/c, using beta=alpha c(c-2). This also checks the base X_1=beta.

The physical second moment is exactly

    V_j=(2^j-1)
        +2alpha(c^j-2^j)
        +a^2(c^(2j)-2^j)/(c^2-2).                    (4.3)

The identity can also be checked from V_j=2V_(j-1)+(1+k_(j-1))^2 and V_0=0.

Write

    v(c)=(c-2)^2/(c^2-2).

Since c belongs to the fixed compact interval [c0,c0(1+1/6144)] and alpha is uniformly positive, (3.4), (4.2) and (4.3) imply, with constants uniform in the enclosing m and all 1<=j<=m,

    s_j=alpha c^j+O(2^j),
    X_j=d(c)alpha c^j+O(2^j),
    V_j=v(c)alpha^2 c^(2j)+O(c^j+2^j).              (4.4)

At j=m, the relative errors tend to zero because 2/c<=2/c0<1. As c->c0 and beta->1, d(c)->r and v(c)->v. This proves the first two limits in (4.1), and t->r.

### 4.2 Exact first moment of the physical iterates

For j>=2 abbreviate s=s_(j-1), k=k_(j-1). The left child has x_(i+1)=X_j-T_i-h_i, so its contribution to M_j is

    s X_j-(s^2+V_(j-1))/2.

The bridge contributes -(1+k)X_(j-1). The final child is the negative of the previous critical trajectory. Therefore

    M_j+M_(j-1)
      =s X_j-(s^2+V_(j-1))/2-(1+k)X_(j-1).           (4.5)

The exact base is M_1=-(1+a)a beta. Negative small-depth values are allowed; no positivity of the moment has been assumed.

Substitute (4.4) into (4.5) and use the exact equality
k_(j-1)=(c-2)alpha c^(j-1). The leading term is

    F(c)alpha^2 c^(2j-2),
    F(c)=2d(c)-(1+v(c))/2,

and the error is O((2c)^j), uniformly over the same compact parameter set. Define

    w(c)=F(c)/(c^2+1).

Then E_j=M_j-w(c)alpha^2 c^(2j) satisfies

    E_j+E_(j-1)=O((2c)^j).

Summing this scalar recurrence with absolute values gives E_j=O((2c)^j), with a uniform constant: the geometric ratio 2c is uniformly greater than 1, and the base is uniformly bounded. Thus

    M_m/S^2=w(c)+O((2/c)^m) -> w(c0).                (4.6)

Finally w(c0)=v. One exact check is

    v=(8sqrt(2)-11)/7,
    4r-1=v(2c0^2+3),

which is equivalent to 2r-(1+v)/2=v(c0^2+1). This proves the last limit in (4.1). Every error estimate above is uniform in the actual enclosing-m parameters.

## 5. Approach 1: Profile A fails on that original trajectory

Denote its physical weight and weighted physical gradient by

    U_A=sum_(i<n) rho_i^A h_i,
    G_A=sum_(i<n) rho_i^A h_i g_i.

The fixed-profile identity gives

    U_A=3S/4+V_m/(4S),
    u_n^A=1+kappa S-U_A.                             (5.1)

The actual GD recursion h_i g_i=x_i-x_(i+1) and a finite summation by parts give

    G_A=X_m-x_n/2-M_m/(2S).                          (5.2)

To check the endpoint coefficient, rho_i-rho_(i-1)=-h_(i-1)/(2S) and rho_n=1/2. Using M_m including its final term h_(n-1)x_n yields precisely (5.2), without adding a terminal step.

On the odd-m trajectory x_n=g_n=-t, put

    Z_A=G_A-t u_n^A.

Equations (4.1), (5.1) and (5.2) give the exact limiting coefficient

    Z_A/S -> r-v/2-r(kappa-3/4-v/4)
             = -9r/68-(2-r)v/4 <0.                  (5.3)

No decimal sign test is needed: r>0, v>0 and r<1. Consequently Z_A<0 for every sufficiently large odd enclosing m.

By (3.1), (3.8) and (2.1),

    D_A=X_m^2-Bcert t^2-(X_m-Z_A)^2
       =2X_m Z_A-Z_A^2-Bcert t^2 <0.                (5.4)

All terms use the original radius X_m^2 and the actual terminal iterate. The strict failure is at the COMPLETE depth m, not at an internal fixed level. In fact its leading negative term is of order S^2, since X_m/S->r and Z_A/S has a strictly negative limit.

By (2.3), there is no admissible C with R_A(C) PSD for every sufficiently large enclosing m. For each of the infinitely many odd m in (5.4), the actual original gradient vector is a negative direction of R_A(C) for every admissible C.

## 6. Approach 2: the fixed Profile B also fails

Use the SAME physical function, word, original start/minimizer and odd-m trajectory. For physical indices i<n set

    delta_i=rho_i^A-rho_i^B
           =T_i(S-1-T_i)/[2S(S+1+T_i)].              (6.1)

Since the last physical step is 1+a>1, every such T_i satisfies
0<=T_i<=S-(1+a)<S-1. Hence delta_i>=0. This statement is restricted to physical indices: rho_n^B>rho_n^A, and no false terminal inequality is used.

For z=T_i/S in [0,1],

    delta_i <= 1-z/2-1/(1+z)
              =z(1-z)/[2(1+z)] <= r^2/2.           (6.2)

The last maximum occurs at z=r, since the derivative has the sign of 1-2z-z^2. This is an exact uniform bound.

Let Delta=sum_(i<n)delta_i h_i. Then U_B=U_A-Delta and u_n^B=u_n^A+Delta. Therefore

    Z_B-Z_A=-sum_(i<n)delta_i h_i(g_i+t)
            <= (r^2/2) N_minus,                     (6.3)

where N_minus=sum_(g_i<0)h_i(-g_i). For (6.3), terms with g_i>=0 are nonpositive, and a term with g_i<0 is at most delta_i h_i(-g_i). No equality between the profiles' terminal rhos is presumed.

On the odd-m trajectory all physical gradients have magnitude 1 except the last, which is +beta. Thus

    sum_(i<n)h_i |g_i|=S-(1+a)(1-beta),
    sum_(i<n)h_i g_i=X_m+t,

and consequently

    N_minus=[S-(1+a)(1-beta)-X_m-t]/2,
    N_minus/S -> (1-r)/2.                            (6.4)

Combining (5.3), (6.3) and (6.4),

    limsup_(odd m) Z_B/S
       <= -9r/68-(2-r)v/4+r^2(1-r)/4
       < -9r/68+r/16
        = -19r/272 <0.                              (6.5)

Here r(1-r)<1/4 because r is in (0,1) and is not 1/2; the additional negative v-term is simply retained or discarded in the valid direction.

It follows that Z_B<0 for every sufficiently large odd enclosing m. The same original-radius identity gives

    D_B=2X_m Z_B-Z_B^2-Bcert t^2<0.                  (6.6)

Thus (2.3) also rules out every admissible full-PSD C for Profile B at those complete depths.

## 7. What this settles and what remains open
