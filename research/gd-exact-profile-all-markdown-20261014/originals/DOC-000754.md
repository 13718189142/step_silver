# Exact interval reachability for odd scalar gradients

Status: unverified construction and supporting calculations. No further verifier
submission is allocated. This is not the arbitrary-schedule profile theorem.

## 1. A legal scalar construction with an exact one-state recursion

Let g:[0,infinity)->[0,infinity) be nondecreasing, 1-Lipschitz, and g(0)=0.
Extend it oddly to the real line and define f(x)=integral_0^|x| g(r) dr.
The odd extension is nondecreasing and 1-Lipschitz: for opposite signs use
g(r)+g(s)<=r+s. Thus f is convex, differentiable, 1-smooth, even, and minimized
at zero. It is fixed before the run.

For each nonnegative step h define

    M_h(R)=max_{0<=r<=R} |r-h g(r)|,       R>=0.

For a prescribed schedule h_0,...,h_(n-1), set

    R_(t+1)=M_(h_t)(R_t),                R_0>0.                 (1)

The set of all points reachable after t steps from the entire initial interval
[-R_0,R_0] is exactly [-R_t,R_t]. Indeed x->x-h g(x) is continuous and odd.
The image of a compact interval under a continuous real function is an interval;
oddness makes this interval symmetric, and its radius is the displayed maximum.
Apply this observation inductively. In particular some single initial point
x_0 in [-R_0,R_0] produces terminal point R_n. The point can be chosen after
the complete prescribed schedule is known, by successively choosing preimages.
It is not changed during the trajectory. If R_n>0, that initial point is nonzero.

Consequently

    W_n(h) >= 2 integral_0^R_n g(r) dr / R_0^2.                (2)

The possibly smaller actual initial distance only strengthens (2). No change of
normalization and no random/adaptive step choice is involved.

Taking the supremum in (2) over R_0>0 gives exactly the worst-case normalized
gap for this fixed f over all nonzero scalar initial points. One direction was
just proved. Conversely take R_0=|x_0| for any prescribed initial point; its
terminal absolute value is <=R_n and f is nondecreasing on the positive ray.
Taking also the supremum over g therefore gives exactly the full even-function
scalar adversary class, rather than merely a subfamily of its trajectories.
This equality does not identify its value or compare it to the project target.

The same idea is exact for every scalar convex 1-smooth function, without
symmetry, using two endpoints. Choose two nondecreasing 1-Lipschitz profiles
g_+,g_- on [0,infinity), both zero at zero; define f(x)=integral_0^x g_+(r)dr
for x>=0 and f(x)=integral_0^(-x)g_-(r)dr for x<=0. Its derivative on the
negative ray is -g_-(-x), and the cross-ray Lipschitz check is unchanged.
For each sign define

    D_h^sign(R)=max_(0<=r<=R)(r-h g_sign(r)),
    O_h^sign(R)=max_(0<=r<=R)(h g_sign(r)-r).

Starting from A_0=B_0=R_0, the exact reachable interval [-A_t,B_t] obeys

    B_(t+1)=max(D_h^+(B_t),O_h^-(A_t)),
    A_(t+1)=max(D_h^-(A_t),O_h^+(B_t)).                     (1a)

Both maxima are nonnegative because r=0 is allowed. Connectedness gives the
same exact reachability proof, and the terminal normalized witness is

    2 max(integral_0^B_n g_+, integral_0^A_n g_-)/R_0^2.     (2a)

Taking the supremum over R_0 and over the two fixed profiles gives exactly the
entire one-dimensional smooth-convex adversary class. This is still not an
optimization over the higher-dimensional class in the project target. The
state is Markov only after the whole function has been fixed: choosing new
profiles inconsistently after each radius update would invalidate the proof.

For a finite piecewise-linear g, every maximum in M_h is attained at R, zero,
or one of the gradient knots in [0,R], because r-hg(r) is affine between knots.
The retained terminal area is a sum of trapezoids plus the final partial piece.
This keeps a globally fixed legal derivative and all its areas. It permits
arbitrary repeated inner returns. The scalar state is exact here because it
represents a whole reachable interval, not a single point with forgotten data.

## 2. Symmetric Huber interval maps

For g(r)=min(r,1), direct maximization gives

    M_h(R)=|1-h|R                         if 0<=R<=1,
    M_h(R)=R-h                            if R>=1 and 0<=h<=1,
    M_h(R)=max(R-h,h-1)                   if R>=1 and h>=1.    (3)

All formulas agree at their common endpoints. M_h is continuous and
nondecreasing as a function of R. For h>=1, the equivalent formula is

    M_h(R)=max(R-h,(h-1)min(R,1)).

Write E(R)=R^2 for R<=1 and E(R)=2R-1 for R>=1. For a Huber derivative with
core curvature lambda in (0,1] and threshold radius r>0, the substitution x=ry
gives the unit-core maps (3) with steps lambda h. Its objective normalization
from initial interval radius rR is lambda E(R_n)/R^2. Thus optimizing the
threshold and the initial point can be done through the exact interval maps;
testing only one initial point of a fixed Huber function does not do this.

## 3. An exact full-class bound for the unit-curvature Huber subfamily

Put c=13/5, a=c^(-17), b_j=a c^j, and H_0=(), H_(m+1)=(H_m,b_m,H_m).
Let s_m=sum H_m=a(c^m-2^m)/(c-2). Write F_m for composition of the maps (3)
in the order H_m. Every threshold radius is allowed in this section, but the
core curvature is exactly one.

All steps in H_17 are less than one. Starting from R>=0, its interval radius
is nonincreasing. If its final radius is greater than one, every radius during
this block was greater than one, so F_17(R)=R-s_17. Conversely if R>s_17+1,
each partial sum is <=s_17, so all radii are >1 and the same formula holds.
Therefore, since M_1(R)=(R-1)_+,

    M_1(F_17(R))=(R-s_17-1)_+.

The following H_17 is radius-nonincreasing. Hence

    F_18(R) <= (R-v_18)_+,     v_18=1+s_17.                   (4)

For d=2/c=10/13, d^3<1/2 and d^2<4/5 imply d^17<1/40. Therefore
s_17/b_17=(1-d^17)/(c-2)>=13/8, and b_17=1. Thus v_18>=21/8>c=b_18.

Suppose F_m(R)<=(R-v_m)_+ and v_m>=b_m. For b>=1, equation (3) gives
M_b(y)<=max(y-b,b-1). Monotonicity and the inductive bound yield

    F_(m+1)(R)
      <= (M_(b_m)((R-v_m)_+)-v_m)_+
      <= (R-2v_m-b_m)_+,

because b_m-1-v_m<=0. Set v_(m+1)=2v_m+b_m. Since c<3,
v_(m+1)>=3b_m>=c b_m=b_(m+1), so the induction holds for all m>=18.
Explicitly v_m=s_m-2^(m-18)s_17.

For a unit-curvature symmetric Huber function with arbitrary threshold and
initial point, E(F_m(R))<=2(R-v_m)_+. Consequently

    sup_(R>0) E(F_m(R))/R^2 <= 1/(2v_m) <= 1/(2a c^m).       (5)

The scalar maximization uses max_(R>=v) 2(R-v)/R^2=1/(2v), attained at R=2v.
Thus all repeated-return trajectories of every unit-curvature symmetric Huber
function are controlled on H_m. This is not a bound for all core curvatures,
all scalar convex functions, or the unrestricted worst case.

## 4. A sharper quadratic-prefix calculation

The same H_m has every scalar quadratic prefix product bounded in absolute
value by one, uniformly in the curvature lambda in [0,1]. This strengthens an
earlier coarse bound of 27 in the recursive-family package, but is not used as
an accepted fact.

For lambda>0 choose k>=17 and y in (1/c,1] so that y=a lambda c^k. Let
P_m=product_(j=0)^(m-1)(1-a lambda c^j)^(2^(m-j-1)) and
R_u=P_u(1-a lambda c^u). Each ordered prefix is a product of R_u indexed by
the nonzero binary digits of its length. It suffices to bound every |R_u| by1.
For u<=k all relevant steps times lambda are <=1, so the assertion follows.

For u=k+r, r>=1, first bound whole blocks. Unless a factor is zero, put
d=2/c. Summing log(1-z)<=-z for negative scales and
log|1-c^j y|<=j log c+log y for positive scales gives

    log|P_u|/2^(u-k-1)
       <= -2y(1-d^k)/(c-2)+log[y(1-y)]+2log c
       <= -5/4+log(169/100)
       <= -14/25 < -1/2.

Thus |R_(k+r)|<=c^r exp(-2^(r-2)). For r>=4 this is <=1, since c<e and
2^(r-2)>=r. (The latter inequality holds at r=4 and is preserved by doubling.)

For r=1,2,3 put A=P_(k+1). Then |A|<=1-y, and all c^j y-1 for j>=1 are
nonnegative. Define L_j=(c^j-1)^2/(4c^j), the maximum of
(1-y)(c^j y-1) over real y. We have

    |R_(k+1)| <= L_1,
    |R_(k+2)| <= L_1 L_2,
    |R_(k+3)| <= L_1^2 L_2 L_3.

Here L_1=16/65<1/4, L_2=5184/4225<5/4, and
L_3=1073296/274625<4. The displayed three bounds are respectively <1/4,
<5/16, and <5/16. This proves the claim. Cases containing a zero factor are
immediate. Lambda=0 also gives prefix products equal to1.

In particular, for the unit-core maps with scaled steps lambda H_m, the entire
initial interval [-1,1] remains in the core during H_m, and its final radius is
|P_m(lambda)|. This justifies exact whole-block skipping in tiny interval-map
checks; it is not a computational proof of the all-curvature Huber bound.

## 5. Current boundary and failed simplifications

For arbitrary lambda, choose k so all steps of lambda H_k are <=1. Its radius
map obeys F_k(R)<=max(1,R-lambda s_k). If lambda s_j>=lambda b_j-2 at subsequent
levels, that inequality propagates with the exact sums: apply (3) between the
two copies and absorb the overshoot peak lambda b_j-1 into lambda s_j+1.
This bounds large input radii but leaves the small-radius objective ratio open.

A proposed two-line majorant max(qR,R-v) was not closed at full sum capacity.
The internal peak term q(b-1)min(F(R),1) survives composition; dropping it
incorrectly suggests faster contraction. In particular, at the input where
the first block reaches radius1 it produces q(b-1), and any new linear bound
must dominate that peak. This is the precise gap in the attempted simple
radius-potential induction, not an all-scalar stability theorem.

Tiny exploratory checks used three curvatures (.5,.75,.9), three initial radii
(10,100,10^6), and four depths (20,26,34,44). Whole-core and whole-outside blocks
were evaluated by their formulas; the largest call count was155, and times
were below .001 seconds per case. Logarithmic radii avoided underflow. Every
fixed tested interval eventually contracted very sharply. These 36 values
give neither a uniform-in-curvature nor a uniform-in-radius theorem.

The next construction problem is to choose a fixed multi-knot monotone gradient
for (1) with a quantitatively large terminal area. The interval formulation
removes chronology from the reachable-state description, but it does not remove
the global monotonicity/Lipschitz restrictions on the derivative. No comparison
of its optimized value with the exact ZJ benchmark has been proved.
