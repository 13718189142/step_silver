## Claim and interfaces

Use the project's exact S_circle, S_bullet, B_n=2S_bullet(n)+1 and U_n=1/B_n. Put lambda=1+sqrt(2) and rho=log_2(lambda). Suppose a nonnegative primitive schedule a of length r>=1 has shifted capacity A=1+sum(a) satisfying

    A>(r+1)^rho.                                      (1)

Then there are predetermined nonnegative schedules H_n of length (r+1)n, constructed solely with the usual ConPP/ConPD operations and repeated copies of a, such that

    W_((r+1)n)(H_n)
      <=[sqrt(A B_n)-(sqrt(A)-1)]^(-2).                (2)

Moreover,

    liminf_(n->infinity) W_((r+1)n)(H_n)/U_((r+1)n)
      <=(r+1)^rho/A<1.                                (3)

Thus this stronger finite seed hypothesis is sufficient for the negative answer required by the project, along an infinite subsequence, without assuming convergence of B_n/n^rho or replacing the exact DP by a decimal profile.

The analytic schedule interfaces invoked here are the following standard Zhang–Jiang closure statements, stated to remove ambiguity about the hypotheses. At L=1 write Q_(i,star)=f_i-f_star-<g_i,x_i-x_star>+||g_i||^2/2. A schedule h with s=sum(h) is primitive if

  ||x_0-x_star||^2+2sum_(i<n) h_i Q_(i,star)-2s(f_n-f_star)
  >=||x_n-x_star||^2+s(s+1)||g_n||^2

for every legal smooth convex GD trajectory. A schedule h is dominant if there are nonnegative weights u_0,...,u_n summing to 2s+1 such that

  ||x_0-x_star||^2+2sum_i u_i Q_(i,star)-2(2s+1)(f_n-f_star)
  >=||x_0-x_star-sum_i u_i g_i||^2.

The empty schedule has both properties. If a,b are primitive, ConPP(a,b)=[a,phi(sum(a),sum(b)),b] is primitive. If a is primitive and b is dominant, ConPD(a,b)=[a,psi(sum(a),sum(b)),b] is dominant. Dominance implies W(h)<=1/(2sum(h)+1), since all Q_(i,star)<=0. ## 1. A finite positive benchmark limsup

For shifted primitive masses x,y>=0 set

  F(x,y)=(x+y+sqrt(x^2+6xy+y^2))/2.

Let q=1/rho. The inequality

  F(x,y)^q<=x^q+y^q                                  (4)

holds. Here is an elementary proof. Zero arguments give equality. For positive arguments write x=exp(m-t), y=exp(m+t), t>=0, using symmetry. Then

  F(x,y)=exp(m+arsinh(cosh(t))).

Inequality(4) is equivalent to J(t)>=0, where

  J(t)=q^(-1)log(2cosh(qt))-arsinh(cosh(t)).

Because 2^(1/q)=lambda, J(0)=0; direct exponential asymptotics give J(t)->0 as t->infinity. Its derivative is

  J'(t)=tanh(qt)-sinh(t)/sqrt(1+cosh(t)^2).

Define alpha(t)=artanh(sinh(t)/sqrt(1+cosh(t)^2)). Its derivative is cosh(t)/sqrt(1+cosh(t)^2), strictly increasing from1/sqrt(2) to1. Also1/sqrt(2)<q<1: lambda>2 gives the upper inequality; lambda^3=7+5sqrt(2)<16 gives q>3/4>1/sqrt(2). Hence alpha(t)-qt is strictly convex, vanishes at0, initially decreases, and tends to infinity. Its derivative increases to1-q>0, so it has exactly one positive zero t_0. Monotonicity of tanh implies J'>0 on(0,t_0) and J'<0 on(t_0,infinity). The two zero endpoint limits imply J(t)>0 for t>0, proving(4).

Write P_k=1+S_circle(k). Every primitive DP tree with k+1 leaves has root capacity at most(k+1)^rho by(4) and induction from unit leaves. Thus

  P_k<=(k+1)^rho.                                    (5)

The shifted dominant join is

  G(x,d)=2x+d/2+sqrt(d^2+8xd)/2.

Since sqrt(d^2+8xd)<=d+4x, one has G(x,d)<=d+4x. Unroll an optimizing dominant tree along its unique dominant continuation. If the attached primitive subtrees have leaf counts l_1,...,l_t, then each l_i>=1 and sum_i l_i=n. The final dominant leaf has mass1. By(5),

  B_n<=1+4sum_i l_i^rho<=1+4n^rho.                    (6)

The final inequality follows by repeated(a+b)^rho>=a^rho+b^rho for a,b>=0 and rho>1; for fixed a its difference has nonnegative derivative in b and vanishes at b=0.

Conversely, balanced primitive trees with2^k leaves attain shifted mass lambda^k, since F(x,x)=lambda x. For n=2^k the legal dominant split consisting of this(n-1)-step primitive prefix, one ConPD bridge, and an empty continuation gives

  B_n>=G(lambda^k,1)>=2lambda^k=2n^rho.              (7)

Consequently the real number

  K=limsup_(n->infinity) B_n/n^rho

exists in the extended-real sense and satisfies2<=K<=4. In particular it is finite and strictly positive. No single normalized limit or periodic-phase theorem is used.

## 2. Replace every primitive leaf; retain the terminal dominant leaf

For each n>=1 choose one fully expanded optimizing dominant DP tree T_n. It has n internal nodes and n+1 leaves. Exactly one leaf, at the end of its dominant continuation, has dominant type. All other n leaves have primitive type. This count follows inductively: at each dominant node the left subtree is a pure primitive tree and the right subtree contains exactly one dominant leaf; all-primitive nodes have two primitive children.

Replace each of the n primitive empty leaves by the certified schedule a. Keep the single dominant terminal leaf empty. Re-evaluate every bridge with phi or psi from its new child sums. This specifies a concrete nonnegative schedule H_n independent of the objective. Closure gives a primitive schedule at every primitive node and a dominant schedule at every dominant node. The original n bridge steps remain, and the inserted copies contribute rn steps. Therefore

  length(H_n)=(r+1)n.                                (8)

Positive homogeneity of F implies that every primitive subtree's mass is now exactly A times its old mass. Let D_n=2sum(H_n)+1. Along the dominant continuation compare the true construction with an auxiliary scalar evaluation having the same scaled primitive children but terminal scalar mass A instead of1. Homogeneity of G says that the auxiliary root mass is exactly A B_n.

For fixed x>=0 let

  T_x(z)=(z+sqrt(z^2+8x))/2.

Accepted fact0be8b54e0a33381c proves sqrt(G(x,d))=T_x(sqrt(d)) and that T_x is increasing and1-Lipschitz on nonnegative arguments. The true and auxiliary terminal square-root masses differ by sqrt(A)-1. Propagate both through the identical sequence of T_x maps. The difference remains between0 and sqrt(A)-1. Hence

  sqrt(A B_n)-(sqrt(A)-1)<=sqrt(D_n)<=sqrt(A B_n).    (9)

The lower bound is at least1 because B_n>=1. Dominance gives W(H_n)<=1/D_n, which proves(2). This handles the unmatched dominant leaf explicitly rather than scaling a nonexistent terminal schedule.

## 3. The input/output subsequences and exact comparison

Put m=r+1. Choose integers n_j->infinity with B_(n_j)/n_j^rho->K. From(9) and B_n=O(n^rho),

  D_(n_j)/n_j^rho->A K.

Indeed the difference A B_n-D_n is bounded above by2(sqrt(A)-1)sqrt(A B_n), hence is O(n^(rho/2)) and becomes zero after division by n^rho. On the output horizons mn_j, the definition of the same global limsup gives

  limsup_j B_(mn_j)/(mn_j)^rho<=K.

Both statements concern the same input subsequence and its multiplied output subsequence. Their inequality directions therefore yield

  limsup_j W_(mn_j)(H_(n_j))/U_(mn_j)
  <=limsup_j B_(mn_j)/D_(n_j)
  <=m^rho/A.

This proves(3). Under(1), choosing delta=(1-m^rho/A)/2>0 ensures W_(mn_j)(H_(n_j))<=(1-delta)U_(mn_j) for all sufficiently large j. The lengths mn_j are unbounded, as required.
