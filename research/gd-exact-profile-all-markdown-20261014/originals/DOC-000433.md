## 1. Complete formal setting

Let r>=1 be an integer, h_0,...,h_(r-1)>=0, s=sum h_i, and A=1+s.
Set h_r=0 only as a notational convention. Let P_i=sum_(t<i)h_t for
0<=i<=r; thus P_0=0, P_r=s, and this is the prefix-sum P_i already used
in the project. Let e be the all-ones column of length r+1. All r+1
formal gradients are retained, including at zero steps.

Let Lambda_ij>=0 for every distinct finite pair i,j in{0,...,r}, with
zero diagonal, and let L_flow=diag(Lambda e)-Lambda. Put

    omega=(-2h_0,...,-2h_(r-1),2s)^T,
    T_ii=1, T_it=h_t for t<i, T_it=0 for t>i,
    D_prim=diag(h_0(2-h_0),...,h_(r-1)(2-h_(r-1)),-s(s+2)),
    M_prim=D_prim+(L_flow T+T^T L_flow^T)/2.

Assume the finite value-flow equation L_flow^T e=omega. Feasibility of
the actual primitive interface additionally requires M_prim positive
semidefinite on the whole space R^(r+1).

For completeness, this is the full certificate for the primitive energy

    V_h=2sum_(i<r)h_i(f_i-f_r)+sum_(i<r)h_i(1-h_i)||g_i||^2
                                             -s(s+1)||g_r||^2.

Use x_i=x_0-sum_(t<i)h_t g_t and
Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2. The value coefficients
in V_h+sum Lambda_ij Q_ij cancel by the stated flow equation. The
position terms in the sum are sym(L_flow(T-I)); the norm terms are
sym(L_flow)-diag(omega)/2. Adding the diagonal of V_h gives exactly
M_prim. Therefore V_h+sum Lambda_ij Q_ij=<M_prim,Gram(g_0,...,g_r)>.
Here sym sends a square matrix to half its sum with its transpose, and the last pairing is the trace pairing.
Accepted fact1252b8823bb5a290 proves Q_ij<=0 on every actual smooth
convex trajectory, including repeated sample positions. Thus a feasible
full matrix implies V_h>=0 on all such trajectories.

## 2. Forced constant-gradient kernel and all row equations

The scalar identity sum_(i<r)h_i P_i=(s^2-sum_i h_i^2)/2 follows by
expanding s^2 and collecting every unordered pair of distinct steps.
Since T e=e+(P_0,...,P_r)^T and sum_i omega_i=0,

    e^T D_prim e=-s^2-sum_i h_i^2,
    e^T L_flow T e=omega^T T e=s^2+sum_i h_i^2.

Consequently e^T M_prim e=0 for every array satisfying the value flow.
For a symmetric PSD matrix this forces M_prim e=0: for any real vector y,
nonnegativity of (e+t y)^T M_prim(e+t y) for both signs of sufficiently
small real t forces y^T M_prim e=0.

For i<r,

    (T^T omega)_i=-2h_i+2h_i(P_i+h_i),
    (M_prim e)_i=h_i(1+P_i)+(1/2)sum_j Lambda_ij(P_i-P_j).

At the terminal index,

    (M_prim e)_r=-s(s+1)+(1/2)sum_(j<r)Lambda_rj(s-P_j).

Hence every feasible seed has the exact row-moment equations

    sum_j Lambda_ij(P_j-P_i)=2h_i(1+P_i),  0<=i<r,       (S1)
    sum_(j<r)Lambda_rj(s-P_j)=2s(s+1).                    (S2)

For h_i>0, (S1) requires at least one actual outgoing coefficient to an
index j>i with P_j>P_i. This is because all earlier/equal-prefix terms
have nonpositive displacement, whereas the right side is positive.

## 3. Exact elimination of terminal multipliers

Choose all r(r-1) off-diagonal queried-to-queried entries
Rseed_ij=Lambda_ij>=0, 0<=i,j<r, i!=j, with Rseed_ii=0. Write

    out_seed_i=sum_(j<r)Rseed_ij, in_seed_i=sum_(j<r)Rseed_ji,
    u_seed_i=Lambda_(i,r), v_seed_i=Lambda_(r,i), tail_i=s-P_i.

If tail_i>0, (S1) determines the outgoing terminal coefficient:

    u_seed_i=[2h_i(1+P_i)-sum_(j<r)Rseed_ij(P_j-P_i)]/tail_i.     (S3)

The value flow at node i determines the incoming terminal coefficient:

    v_seed_i=2h_i+out_seed_i+u_seed_i-in_seed_i.                           (S4)

Both u_seed_i and v_seed_i must be nonnegative. There is no upper-triangular,
lower-triangular, symmetric, rank, or support restriction on Rseed.

If tail_i=0, no division is made. Instead retain u_seed_i>=0 as a free
variable and require

    sum_(j<r)Rseed_ij(P_j-P_i)=0.                            (S5)

Here h_i=0 and P_i=s, since the steps are nonnegative. Thus (S5)
is equivalent to Rseed_ij=0 whenever P_j<s. Equation(S4) still defines
v_seed_i and still requires v_seed_i>=0. No formal gradient is removed.

Equations(S3) or(S5) give every nonterminal row of M_prim e=0. Summing(S4)
gives sum_i v_seed_i-sum_i u_seed_i=2s, which is precisely terminal value flow.
With all value-flow equations in place, e^T M_prim e=0 was proved in
Section2; therefore the sum of the entries of M_prim e is zero. Its first
r entries are already zero, so its terminal entry is also zero.
Equivalently, (S2) follows automatically. Thus this reconstruction
preserves every row condition, including the terminal one.

Let K_seed be the leading r-by-r principal block of the resulting full M_prim.
As M_prim e=0, the complete matrix is exactly

    M_prim = [ K_seed,           -K_seed*1 ;
          -1^T*K_seed,       1^T*K_seed*1 ],                       (S6)

where 1 is the all-ones vector of length r. For every scalar coordinate
of the original gradients its quadratic form is

    (g_0-g_r,...,g_(r-1)-g_r)^T K_seed(g_0-g_r,...,g_(r-1)-g_r).

Therefore full M_prim PSD is EQUIVALENT to K_seed PSD: necessity follows by
restriction to g_r=0, sufficiency by this identity. The identity retains
all original formal coordinates. It does not assume that any repeated
gradients agree.

Every entry of K_seed is explicit. For i<r,

    K_seed_ii=h_i(2-h_i)+out_seed_i+u_seed_i
                         -h_i*(sum_(i<j<r)Rseed_ij+u_seed_i),    (S7)

and for 0<=i<j<r,

    2K_seed_ij=-(Rseed_ij+Rseed_ji)-h_j*(sum_(j<k<r)Rseed_ik+u_seed_i)
                                      +h_i*sum_(k<=i)Rseed_jk.  (S8)

To derive (S7)--(S8), use (L_flow T)_ij=L_ij+h_j sum_(k>j)L_ik
and distinguish whether that sum includes the diagonal of L_flow.
The diagonal D_prim affects only(S7). This is a coefficient identity
on all independent formal gradients.

For r=4 and h_3>0 all four tail_i are positive. The original twenty
finite multiplier variables are thus parameterized exactly by the
twelve entries Rseed_ij, the eight sign constraints u_seed_i,v_seed_i>=0, and the
full K_seed PSD condition. If h_3=0 use(S5) for zero tails; the statement
does not exclude such seeds. Conversely every set of parameters
meeting these displayed conditions reconstructs a feasible full seed
certificate. This is a lossless reformulation, not a restricted ansatz.

## 4. Concrete endpoint and last-edge necessary conditions

Assume s>0. Both endpoint steps obey

    0<=h_0,h_(r-1)<=2(s+1)/(s+2)<2.                     (S9)

For the first endpoint, if h_0<=1 this follows because the upper bound
in(S9) is greater than1. If h_0>1, put out_0=sum_(j>0)Lambda_0j.
Equation(S1) gives2h_0=sum_(j>0)Lambda_0j P_j<=s*out_0.
The full first diagonal is

    M_prim_00=h_0(2-h_0)+(1-h_0)out_0>=0.

Since h_0-1>0, the last two inequalities imply
s*h_0(2-h_0)>=2h_0(h_0-1). Divide by h_0>0 and rearrange to get(S9).
This derivation also rules out h_0>=2 without a separate assumption.

For the final endpoint, the case h_(r-1)=0 is immediate. If it is
positive, then s-P_j>=h_(r-1) for every j<r. The terminal diagonal
and(S2) give

    sum_(j<r)Lambda_rj >=s(s+2),
    2s(s+1)>=h_(r-1)*sum_(j<r)Lambda_rj.

Division by s>0 proves the second endpoint bound. In terms of the
sum t of all other steps, either endpoint b must therefore satisfy

    b^2+t b<=2(t+1),
    b<=(-t+sqrt(t^2+8t+8))/2.                           (S10)

The right side is the original empty-leaf primitive bridge phi(0,t).
This does not imply that the remainder of the word is primitive.
It therefore supplies no induction proving an all-length capacity cap.

If h_(r-1)>0, its row in(S1) gives the sharper exact last-edge identity

    Lambda_(r-1,r)=2(1+P_(r-1))
      +sum_(j<r-1)Lambda_(r-1,j)(P_(r-1)-P_j)/h_(r-1), (S11)

so in particular Lambda_(r-1,r)>=2(1+P_(r-1)). If that last step
is zero, do not divide; its row equation instead forces every outgoing
edge to a strictly smaller prefix sum to vanish, as in(S5).

## 5. All-curvature necessary polynomial and saturation

For 0<=tau<=1, take the actual scalar convex1-smooth function
f_tau(x)=tau*x^2/2 with x_0=1. Define

    Poly_i(tau)=product_(0<=k<i)(1-tau*h_k), Poly_0=1.

These are exactly its actual iterates, with gradients tau*Poly_i(tau).
The update telescopes as

    1-Poly_r(tau)^2
       =sum_(i<r)tau*h_i*(2-tau*h_i)*Poly_i(tau)^2.

Inserting its values and gradients into the displayed V_h yields

    V_quad(tau)=1-tau*(1-tau)*sum_(i<r)h_i Poly_i(tau)^2
        -[1+s*tau+s(s+1)*tau^2]*Poly_r(tau)^2 >=0         (S12)

for EVERY tau in[0,1]. To verify the equality, the coefficient of
Poly_i^2 from the first two terms in V_h is
tau*h_i+tau^2*h_i*(1-h_i). This equals
tau*h_i*(2-tau*h_i)-tau*(1-tau)*h_i, so the telescope gives(S12).
Feasible full M_prim implies V_h>=0, as proved in Section1. No quadratic
test is asserted sufficient for a certificate or for primitivity.

At tau=1 all finite interpolation residuals Q_ij vanish exactly and

    A^2*product_(i<r)(1-h_i)^2<=1.                       (S13)

If equality holds in(S13), the vector p=(Poly_0(1),...,Poly_r(1))^T
has p^T M_prim p=V_quad(1)=0. PSD therefore forces M_prim p=0 by the same
two-sign argument used for e. For s>0 this vector is not proportional
to e: proportionality and Poly_0=1 would make every Poly_i=1, forcing
every h_i=0 from the update. Thus rank(M_prim)<=r-1; for the four-step
seed it is at most3. In the lossless coordinates(S6), the nonzero
vector(Poly_0(1)-Poly_r(1),...,Poly_(r-1)(1)-Poly_r(1)) lies in ker K_seed.
This extra null constraint is conditional on exact saturation; it
must not be imposed on an unsaturated candidate.

## 6. Symbolic boundary calibration

For one step h_0=b>0, there are no interior Rseed entries. Equations(S3)--(S4)
give u_seed_0=2 and v_seed_0=2b+2, and(S7) gives K_seed=[2-b^2]. Therefore these exact
coordinates recover feasibility precisely for0<b<=sqrt(2). The empty-value
case b=0 is recovered with every multiplier zero. These are symbolic
identities, not sampled numerical checks.

For h=(0,b), set both queried Rseed entries zero. The formulas give u_seed=(0,2),
v_seed=(0,2b+2), and K_seed=diag(0,2-b^2). For h=(b,0), use Rseed=0, u_seed=(2,0),
v_seed=(2b+2,0), and K_seed=diag(2-b^2,0); the second outgoing terminal coefficient
is selected using the zero-tail branch(S5). Both full matrices retain all
three independent formal gradients, and both recover exactly the same
one-step nonzero spectrum without equating the two gradients at the
repeated actual query. This checks the intended interpretation of the
zero-step boundary.
