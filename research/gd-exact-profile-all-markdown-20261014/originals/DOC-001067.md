# A two-dimensional obstruction at a saturated primitive cut

Status: complete candidate proof, NOT verifier-accepted. No solver or verifier
call. The only existing fact used is 1252b8823bb5a290, for its explicit finite
smooth-convex interpolation construction. Every other identity is proved here.

## 1. Definitions and an energy identity

For a nonnegative physical schedule h of length n let T_tot=1+sum_i h_i.
For normalized smoothness 1 and an optimum jet (0,0,0), put

    Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2,
    V_h=2 sum_(i<n) h_i(f_i-f_n)
          +sum_(i<n) h_i(1-h_i)||g_i||^2
          -T_tot(T_tot-1)||g_n||^2.

Here primitive validity means V_h>=0 on every legal convex 1-smooth GD
trajectory. Suppose the sampled data additionally satisfy

    f_i=<g_i,x_i>-||g_i||^2/2 for every i,
    x_n=g_n.

The first condition is exactly Q_(i,star)=0; the second gives
f_n=||g_n||^2/2. Expanding

    ||x_0||^2-||x_n||^2
       =2 sum h_i<g_i,x_i>-sum h_i^2||g_i||^2

therefore gives the exact identity

    V_h=||x_0||^2-T_tot^2||g_n||^2.                 (1)

For any such data, whether or not yet jointly interpolable, write y_i=x_i-g_i.
Direct substitution also gives

    Q_ij=<g_i-g_j,y_j>.                             (2)

## 2. General two-bridge construction

Let a,b,c be arbitrary finite nonnegative blocks, with capacities

    A=1+sum a_i, B=1+sum b_i, C=1+sum c_i.

Take r_L,r_R>0 and h=(a,1+r_L,b,1+r_R,c). Define

    D_tail=B+C+r_R, T_tot=A+D_tail+r_L,
    eta_cut=r_L/D_tail, beta_inner=r_R/C,
    p_aux=-B/r_L.

The first scalar array is a legal Huber trajectory: start at A and use
derivative clip(x,-eta_cut,1). During a and at its endpoint the gradient is
1. Its endpoint is 1. The first bridge lands at -r_L=-eta_cut D_tail.
Every remaining query has gradient -eta_cut, and the final point is
-eta_cut. All the data satisfy the hypotheses of (1), so

    V_base=A^2-T_tot^2 eta_cut^2.                   (3)

The second scalar array is defined as follows. During a use constant
gradient p_aux, start at A p_aux, and end at p_aux. The first bridge lands
at -r_L p_aux=B. On the remaining schedule (b,1+r_R,c) use the ACTUAL
Huber trajectory with derivative clip(x,-beta_inner,1), starting at B.
The b block ends at 1, the second bridge lands at -r_R=-C beta_inner,
and c ends at -beta_inner. Assign every value by
f_i=g_i x_i-g_i^2/2. Thus (1) yields

    V_aux=A^2 B^2/r_L^2-T_tot^2 beta_inner^2.        (4)

The second array is generally NOT interpolable across the first bridge.
It is only auxiliary data; its possible violations are accounted for below.

For epsilon_mix>0, combine both arrays into two-dimensional data

    X_i=(x_i^base,sqrt(epsilon_mix) x_i^aux),
    G_i=(g_i^base,sqrt(epsilon_mix) g_i^aux),
    F_i=f_i^base+epsilon_mix f_i^aux.

The GD equations hold coordinate by coordinate, all Q_(i,star) remain zero,
and X_n=G_n. Consequently

    V_mix=V_base+epsilon_mix V_aux.                 (5)

Here is a complete interpolation audit. Partition the samples into L (a
and its endpoint), M (b and its endpoint after the first bridge), and R (c
and its final endpoint after the second bridge). Overlaps are assigned to
the group whose gradient is used at the relevant bridge. The groups are
disjoint in original time: L includes the first bridge query, M includes
the second bridge query, and R includes the terminal sample.

* Within L all gradients in each scalar array agree, so Q=0.
* Within M union R the auxiliary data are one legal Huber trajectory;
  the base gradients agree, so all Q<=0 there.
* From L to M, the auxiliary target has y_j>=0 and the source gradient
  p_aux<1, so Q_aux<=0. From M to L, the auxiliary target y_i is a
  nonnegative multiple of p_aux<0 and 1-p_aux>0, so Q_aux<=0.
* From L to R, if R_j is the remaining sum of physical c steps after its
  target sample j, the two residuals are

      Q_base=-(1+eta_cut)eta_cut R_j,
      Q_aux=beta_inner(-p_aux-beta_inner)R_j.

* From R to L, if H_i is the remaining sum of physical a steps after its
  target sample i, then

      Q_base=-(1+eta_cut)H_i,
      Q_aux=(-beta_inner-p_aux)p_aux H_i.

Zero remaining sums give zero in both arrays. Thus duplicate positions
caused by zero steps do not require a separate limiting argument.

The optimum-outgoing inequalities hold too: Q_(star,i)=-<G_i,Y_i>,
and the inner product is nonnegative separately in each scalar array.
For the auxiliary L data this is H_i p_aux^2>=0, and in the other groups
it follows directly from their plateau coordinates. Q_(i,star)=0 already.

When r_L r_R<BC, one has -p_aux>beta_inner. The ONLY potentially positive
auxiliary residuals are L-to-R, and all interpolation inequalities hold for

    0<epsilon_mix<=epsilon_max,
    epsilon_max=eta_cut(1+eta_cut)
                    /[beta_inner(-p_aux-beta_inner)].             (6)

When r_L r_R>BC the ONLY potentially positive auxiliary residuals are
R-to-L, and all inequalities hold for

    0<epsilon_mix<=epsilon_max,
    epsilon_max=(1+eta_cut)
                    /[(-p_aux)(beta_inner+p_aux)].                 (7)

When r_L r_R=BC both cross families are nonpositive for any epsilon_mix.

All these are complete original-index interpolation checks, including both
cross directions and both optimum directions.

## 3. One fixed globally legal function

Include the zero optimum sample and define, on R^2,

    H_mix(v)=max_i { c_i+<X_i,v-G_i>+||v-G_i||^2/2 },
    c_i=<G_i,X_i>-F_i=||G_i||^2/2,
    f_mix(x)=sup_v { <x,v>-H_mix(v) }.

The maximum includes i=star. This is the explicit finite interpolation
construction of accepted fact 1252b8823bb5a290, proof section 2, applied to
the entire finite data set. The checked Q inequalities imply that f_mix
interpolates every (X_i,G_i,F_i), is convex differentiable and 1-smooth,
and has f_mix(0)=0=min f_mix. Thus the mixed data are one actual GD
trajectory of one fixed function; they are not an adaptive oracle or a
reset of the function at a bridge.

## 4. A universal quantitative two-cut necessary condition

Any primitive h as above necessarily satisfies

    r_L r_R [A^2+r_L(T_tot+A)] <= A^2 BC.           (8)

First assume r_L r_R<BC and choose epsilon_mix=epsilon_max in (6).
Using D_tail=B+C+r_R, substitution into (3)-(5) gives

    V_mix = (BC+r_R D_tail)(D_tail+r_L)
              /[D_tail^2 r_R(BC-r_L r_R)]
             * {A^2 BC-r_L r_R[A^2+r_L(T_tot+A)]}.

The prefactor is positive, proving necessity of (8) in this case. For an
algebra check, before the final factorization the numerator is

    (BC+r_R D_tail)
       {A^2[BC(D_tail+r_L)-r_L r_R D_tail]
                                     -T_tot^2 r_L^2 r_R}.

Use A^2 D_tail+T_tot^2 r_L
=(D_tail+r_L)[A^2+r_L(T_tot+A)] to obtain the displayed factorization.

If r_L r_R>BC, set z_aux=-p_aux=B/r_L and beta_inner>z_aux, and use (7).
Equation (5) becomes

    V_mix=(beta_inner+eta_cut z_aux)/(beta_inner-z_aux)
             * {A^2-T_tot^2[(1+eta_cut)beta_inner/z_aux-eta_cut]}.

The bracket is strictly smaller than A^2-T_tot^2<0. Hence no such schedule
is primitive. If r_L r_R=BC, V_aux=z_aux^2(A^2-T_tot^2)<0, so sufficiently
large epsilon_mix in the fully legal mixed data again gives V_mix<0.
This excludes the two remaining cases and completes (8).

No quadratic-balance assumption about a,b,c appears in this argument.

## 5. Saturated first cut forces its inner tail cut

Suppose in addition r_L T_tot=A D_tail. Then eta_cut=A/T_tot, V_base=0,
and A^2+r_L(T_tot+A)=A T_tot. Condition (8) reduces to

    T_tot r_L r_R<=ABC,
    equivalently r_R D_tail<=BC.                   (9)

Therefore a positive defect r_R D_tail>BC in the inner tail cannot be
repaired by any signed storage or full collection of interpolation edges
while keeping this outer cut exactly saturated. This statement concerns
primitive validity of this actual schedule, not all objective certificates.

The geometry also explains the conclusion without (8): at outer saturation
(4) becomes (T_tot/D_tail)^2[B^2-D_tail^2 beta_inner^2], which is strictly
negative exactly when the tail cut fails. Any sufficiently small positive
mixture allowed above then has V_mix<0.

## 6. Explicit refutation candidate for the outstanding eight-step seed

Set u=10/7, C=27/7, A=B=4, and

    D_tail=(165+sqrt(65641))/42,
    r_R=D_tail-55/7,
    T_tot=(4+D_tail+sqrt((4+D_tail)^2+16D_tail))/2,
    r_L=T_tot-4-D_tail.

The schedule is

    (3/2,3/2,1+r_L,3/2,3/2,1+r_R,10/7,10/7).

Directly from these root equations,

    r_R D_tail=196/9, r_L T_tot=4D_tail,
    BC=108/7<196/9.

Thus (9) fails. Here is a concrete mixing scale without an unspecified
smallness parameter. Define eta_cut=r_L/D_tail, beta_inner=r_R/C,
p_aux=-4/r_L, and

    epsilon_mix=eta_cut(1+eta_cut)
                      /[2 beta_inner(-p_aux-beta_inner)].          (10)

It is positive: the first defining polynomial has its positive root in
(10,101/10), and substituting 5/2 into
r_L^2+(4+D_tail)r_L-4D_tail shows r_L<5/2. Hence
r_L r_R<(5/2)(157/70)=157/28<108/7=BC. Formula (10) is half of (6).

For reference the two position arrays are

    base: (4,5/2,1,-r_L,-r_L+(3/2)eta_cut,
           -r_L+3eta_cut,-C eta_cut,-(1+u)eta_cut,-eta_cut),
    aux:  (4p_aux,(5/2)p_aux,p_aux,4,5/2,1,
           -r_R,-(1+u)beta_inner,-beta_inner).

The gradient arrays are

    base: (1,1,1,-eta_cut,-eta_cut,-eta_cut,
                           -eta_cut,-eta_cut,-eta_cut),
    aux:  (p_aux,p_aux,p_aux,1,1,1,
                           -beta_inner,-beta_inner,-beta_inner).

Values are g_i x_i-g_i^2/2 in each coordinate. Sections 2-3 show that
their mixed two-dimensional jets come from the explicit fixed f_mix.
Finally beta_inner D_tail=1372/243=4(343/243), so

    V_mix = epsilon_mix(T_tot/D_tail)^2
                  [16-(1372/243)^2]
          = -epsilon_mix(T_tot/D_tail)^2 * 937600/59049 < 0.        (11)

This gives an exact concrete primitive counterexample for the proposed
eight-step seed. It closes that seed's proposed primitive amplification.
It makes no claim that a different direct objective certificate is
impossible, and does not settle the unrestricted asymptotic target.

## 7. Retuning both bridges does not repair the fixed shortened-tail family

The obstruction is wider than the exact radical seed. For ANY nonnegative
blocks of capacities A=B=4 and C=27/7, every primitive schedule
(a,1+r_L,b,1+r_R,c) with r_L,r_R>0 has

    T_tot<81/5<9^rho, rho=log_2(1+sqrt(2)).         (12)

This rules out the required primitive amplification seed throughout the
two-parameter bridge family with these three physical blocks, without a
quadratic-product assumption and without requiring either cut to saturate.

Here is the entire scalar deduction. The first-cut Huber array from section2
gives T_tot r_L<=A(B+C+r_R). The same construction at the second bridge
(start on its positive plateau at A+B+r_L, negative slope r_R/C) gives
T_tot r_R<=C(A+B+r_L). For a fixed T=T_tot set

    R=T-A-B-C,
    U(T)=A(T-A)/(T+A), S(T)=C(T-C)/(T+C),
    L(T)=R-S(T).

Both cuts force L(T)<=r_L<=U(T), with r_R=R-r_L. At
T0=81/5 these endpoints and their complementary r_R values are

    r_L=L(T0)=128/65,  r_R=S(T0)=216/91,
    r_L=U(T0)=244/101, r_R=R-U(T0)=6812/3535.

Let Phi_T(z)=z(R-z)[A^2+z(T+A)]. Its derivative is

    Phi_T'(z)=A^2 R+2[(T+A)R-A^2]z-3(T+A)z^2.

Since R>0, this quadratic has one negative and one positive root. Thus
Phi_T increases and then decreases on (0,R); its minimum on any closed
subinterval is attained at an endpoint. The two exact endpoint excesses
over the necessary bound A^2 BC in (8), at T0, are

    Phi_T0(L(T0))-A^2 BC=26650944/1922375>0,
    Phi_T0(U(T0))-A^2 BC=97846272/1785175>0.

For T>=T0, U and S increase, while their derivatives
2A^2/(T+A)^2 and 2C^2/(T+C)^2 lie in (0,1). Hence L=R-S and R-U
also increase. Each endpoint product z(R-z)[A^2+z(T+A)] strictly increases,
because all its positive factors increase. If the cut interval is empty,
the cuts already fail; otherwise both endpoint excesses stay positive and
Phi_T(z)>A^2 BC throughout the interval. This contradicts (8) and proves
T<T0.

For the last inequality in (12), let lambda=1+sqrt(2), so 2^rho=lambda.
One has 9^6-2^19=7153>0 and

    lambda^19=9369319+6625109 sqrt(2)
       >9369319+(7/5)6625109
       =(81/5)^6+8890332269/15625.

Therefore 9^(6rho)>lambda^19>(81/5)^6 and (12) follows.

All endpoint arithmetic here is exact rational arithmetic for two displayed
values. It is not a reopened schedule search or numerical PEP screen.
