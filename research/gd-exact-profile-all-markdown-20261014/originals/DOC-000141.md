# Finite q-product identities: source pointers for the actual birth kernel

Context only; no Danus fact or matched theorem. Main read
[NIST DLMF17.2](https://dlmf.nist.gov/17.2) and17.5 on2026-09-27.
DLMF17.2.1 defines the finite q-Pochhammer product. Equations17.2.7--17.2.20
give finite product transformations, and17.2.35 gives the finite q-binomial
theorem. The series identity17.2.37 carries a convergence condition on z;
infinite-product conventions must also be checked. Finite polynomial
identities should be preferred when they suffice.

The published worker draft cefde310a2e5268e already has an explicit inverse
birth-coordinate formula using q_c=1/c and products
P_r=product_(s=1)^r(1-q_c^s), derived there by interpolation. This note
does NOT claim that NIST supplies the six missing coefficient inequalities.
A possible next mechanism is to use exact finite product/cancellation
identities to expose the actual row sums and signed tail terms before
estimating them. This is a research suggestion, not a derived result.

Required boundaries: retain the physical additive1 in d_j, the same
enclosing-m parameters, exact finite truncation (especially the penultimate
row), all alternating signs and the full constant load. Replacing every
signed term by an independent absolute bound may lose the needed margin.
Positivity of Gaussian binomial coefficients alone does not imply positivity
of the target quadratic form. Any infinite comparison needs uniform,
quantitative truncation bounds in BOTH row and horizon indices.

The six hypotheses in the terminal-charge package are still unproved.
Neither a source name nor a formal q-product rewriting is their proof.
No kernel replay, solver, installation, verifier or allocation change was
made by this source check.
