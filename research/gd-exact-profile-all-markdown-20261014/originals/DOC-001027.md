## 1. Canonical coordinates and prerequisites

Use exactly the canonical schedule coordinates

    h=(1+a,1+b,1+c,1+d), C=1+sum h=5+a+b+c+d.

The compact-domain derivation appended to the joint proof establishes, for
any full four-step seed of the requested capacity,

    193/25<=C<87/10,
    0<a,d<77/97<1,
    0<b,c<1552559/1081453<sqrt(3).

It also derives the actual adjacent-triangle necessary inequalities. The
first and middle instances, in these same coordinates, are

    a*b*[1+a*(C+1)] <= C-2-a-b,                       (1)
    b*c*[1+b*(C+2+a)/(2+a)^2] <= 2+d.                (2)

Every denominator is positive. In particular (2) gives b*c<2+d.
No reversal, prefix primitivity, fixed capacity, or multiplier support is
assumed. These are consequences of full actual witnesses in the appendix.

Inequality (1) also forces a*b<1. Indeed it rearranges to

    b <= (C-2-a)/(1+a+a^2*(C+1)).

The denominator minus a times the numerator is

    1+3a+2a^2-C*a*(1-a)
      > 1-6a+11a^2
      =11*(a-3/11)^2+2/11 >0,

where C<9 and 0<a<1 justify the strict first inequality. Thus a*b<1
throughout the remaining full domain, rather than just on a trial word.

## 2. Complete minimization of the C81 family on that domain

Define

    D84=2+d,
    ell84=c*(1+d)*(D84-b*c)/D84^2 >0,
    H84(u)=(u+1)*(a*u-1),
    R84=(1-a+sqrt((1+a)^2+4*a*ell84))/(2*a).

The actual triangle construction in the appendix permits precisely

    u>=b, z>0, ell84<=z^2<=H84(u).

Since H84(1/a)=0 and H84 is strictly increasing for u>=1/a, these
constraints are equivalent to u>=R84 and ell84<=z^2<=H84(u). Here

    R84>1/a>b,

so the competing endpoint u=b and the vanishing-height boundary never
occur in this compact domain. The exact primitive energy is

    E84(u,z)=((1+a)*u-1)^2+(1-b^2)*z^2
                 -C^2*(b*c/D84)^2.                  (3)

For b<=1, minimization first sets z^2=ell84. The remaining square is
strictly increasing for u>=R84>1/a, hence is minimized at R84.

For b>1, minimization first sets z^2=H84(u). Its derivative as a
quadratic in u has positive leading coefficient

    q2=(1+a)^2+a*(1-b^2)
        >(1+a)^2-2a=1+a^2>0.

At u=1/a the derivative equals

    (1+a)*(2/a+1-b^2)>0,

because a<1 and b^2<3. It is increasing afterwards, so again the minimum
is at R84, where H84(R84)=ell84. The case b=1 agrees with both formulas.

Consequently the infimum is attained by the SINGLE actual boundary

    u=R84, z=sqrt(ell84),

and every full seed must satisfy

    E84:=((1+a)*R84-1)^2+(1-b^2)*ell84
                         -C^2*(b*c/D84)^2 >=0.       (4)

Whenever (4) fails, substituting these explicit u,z into the C81 triangle
gives a negative actual witness. Its gradients are

    (u,0), (-1,z), (b,0), (-b*c/D84,0), (-b*c/D84,0),

and its iterates are

    ((1+a)*u-1,z), (-1,z), (b,-b*z),
    (-b*c,-b*z), (-b*c/D84,-b*z).

All original pairs and the optimum comparisons are checked in the
appended C81 construction; no limiting interpretation is needed since
ell84>0. This is an explicit rule throughout the inequality-failure
region, not a finite curvature or schedule test.

## 3. Exact polynomial branch form, including the sqrt(2) boundary

The remaining square root can itself be removed with exact sign branches.
Put

    L84=c*(1+d)*(D84-b*c),
    K84=1-2*a-a^2=2-(1+a)^2,
    Y84=(1+a)^2*D84^2+4*a*L84 >0,
    U84=2*(1+a)*K84*D84,
    T84=D84^2*((1+a)^4+K84^2)
          +4*a*((1+a)^2+a*(1-b^2))*L84
          -4*a^2*C^2*b^2*c^2.

Direct substitution of R84 into (4) gives the identity

    4*a^2*D84^2*E84=T84+U84*sqrt(Y84).               (5)

Thus E84>=0 is exactly the following finite polynomial condition:

* If K84>0: either T84>=0, or T84<0 and T84^2<=U84^2*Y84.
* If K84=0: T84>=0.
* If K84<0: T84>=0 and T84^2>=U84^2*Y84.

For the first branch, a nonnegative T84 makes the sum in (5) positive;
when it is negative both sides of U84*sqrt(Y84)>=-T84 are nonnegative,
so squaring preserves the inequality. For the third branch, (5) requires
T84>=-U84*sqrt(Y84)>0, and squaring is again legitimate. The middle
branch has no radical term. In particular the boundary h0=sqrt(2) is
retained exactly, not allocated to a neighboring sign case by continuity.

## 4. Scope

trajectory there. It does not show E84<0 for every remaining schedule.
The simultaneous nonnegative region with the other tests is not proved
empty or nonempty here; none of these conditions is a seed certificate.

## 1. The exact curvature inequality with smaller degrees

Use a fixed nonnegative word, a cut j with h_j>1, and the C83 definitions

    P=sum_(i<j)h_i, R=sum_(i>j)h_i, H=P+h_j,
    s=H+R, C=1+s, eta=(h_j-1)/(1+R),
    V0=(1+P)^2-C^2*eta^2, C0=eta*(1+eta),
    p_i(k)=product_(t<i)(1-k*h_t), E(k)=1+k*R.

Here k is curvature in (0,1]. Put p=p_(j+1) and define

    B(k)=sum_(i<=j)h_i*[1+k*(1-h_i)]*p_i(k)^2,
    Pcut(k)=E(k)^2*B(k)-H*[1+k*(s+R+1)]*p(k)^2.

The polynomial Pcut has a double zero at k=0. To check this, let
P_i=sum_(t<i)h_t. Then

    B(0)=H,
    B'(0)=sum_(i<=j)h_i*(1-h_i-2P_i)=H-H^2,
    p(0)=1, p'(0)=-H.

The derivative of E^2*B at zero is 2R*H+H-H^2. The derivative of
the second term is H*(s+R+1-2H)=H*(2R+1-H), the same number.
Both constant terms are H. Consequently

    Qcut(k):=Pcut(k)/k^2                            (1)

is a polynomial of degree at most 2j+1, including at zero. This is
exact division in the polynomial ring, not a limiting numerical test.

For R>0 define the polynomials

    d_i(k)=[E(k)*p_i(k)-p(k)]/k,
    T_i(k)=2R*p(k)*d_i(k)-(1-k)*d_i(k)^2,
    Tplus(k)=max(0,T_0(k),...,T_j(k)).

Each numerator in d_i vanishes at zero. The complete all-pair C83
calculation gives

    beta=k^3*Tplus/(2R*E^2),
    V_k=k^3*Qcut/E^2.

Since the canceled factor k^3/E^2 is strictly positive for the
original curvature domain, the exact necessary inequality becomes

    V0*Tplus(k)+2R*C0*Qcut(k)>=0, 0<k<=1.            (2)

The two middle cuts of a four-step word therefore have degree at most
THREE and FIVE on every branch, rather than five and seven. Branch
selection and beta=0 are unchanged. If Tplus=0, (2) is exactly
Qcut>=0 because R,C0>0; no division by beta has taken place.

At R=0 there is no repair credit or Tplus definition: the auxiliary
data are legal by themselves and their exact energy is

    V_k=k^3*Qcut(k),

so Qcut>=0 remains the necessary test. This retains the last cut.

## 2. The entire interval before any scaled prefix step exceeds one

Suppose 0<k<=1 and k*h_i<=1 for every i<=j. Then

    V_k>=0.                                         (3)

This statement does not require the base cut inequality or a bound on R.
Here is a direct proof. For i<=j write r=k*h_i and
t=k*sum_(i<l<=j)h_l. All factors 1-k*h_l are nonnegative. The product
of the later factors is at most 1/(1+t): indeed
1-u<=1/(1+u) for 0<=u<=1, and product(1+u_l)>=1+sum u_l.
It follows, without dividing by a possibly zero prefix factor, that

    p(k)^2<=p_i(k)^2*(1-r)^2/(1+t)^2.                (4)

For 0<=r<=1, t>=0 and 0<k<=1 the exact difference

    (1+k-r)*(1+t)^2-(1+k+r+2t)*(1-r)^2
      =r*[2k+(1-k)r-r^2]
         +2*(k+r-r^2)*t+(1+k-r)*t^2                 (5)

is nonnegative. The bracket in the first term is a concave quadratic
in r; its endpoint values are 2k and k, so it is positive throughout
[0,1]. The other coefficients are also nonnegative. Combining (4)--(5)
therefore gives

    [1+k*(1-h_i)]*p_i^2
      >=[1+k+k*h_i+2k*sum_(i<l<=j)h_l]*p^2.

Multiplying by h_i and summing, the identity
sum h_i*(h_i+2sum_(i<l<=j)h_l)=H^2 gives

    B(k)>=H*[1+k*(H+1)]*p^2.                        (6)

Finally

    [1+k*(H+1)]*E^2-[1+k*(H+2R+1)]
      =2k^2*R*(H+1)+k^2*R^2*[1+k*(H+1)]>=0.

This and (6) prove Pcut>=0, hence (3). Every zero product is already
covered by (4); no continuity assumption hides a denominator zero.

For any schedule that also passes the actual base cut, V0>=0. Thus
beta*V0+C0*V_k>=0 automatically throughout

    0<k<=1/max_(i<=j)h_i.                            (7)

This removes a whole interval with a sign proof; checking a finite grid
or one endpoint was not used as a proxy for that interval.

## 3. The first cut adds no curvature constraint beyond its base cut

Take j=0 and write x=h0, R=sum_(i>0)h_i, s=x+R, C=1+s. Direct expansion
of (1) gives the affine polynomial

    Qcut(k)=x*s*(s+2)
        +x*[R^2*(1-x)-x^2*(x+2R+1)]*k.              (8)

Its value at zero is positive. At k=1 it is

    Qcut(1)=(1+R)^2-C^2*(x-1)^2.

When x>1, the first base-cut inequality is C*(x-1)<=1+R, so this
second endpoint is nonnegative as well. An affine polynomial whose
two endpoint values are nonnegative is nonnegative on [0,1]. Therefore
V_k>=0 for every curvature, and the full j=0 C83 family is redundant
once that cut has passed. In the compact positive domain h0>1, so
this argument covers its whole first-cut branch.

## 4. Exact branch identities at the second cut

Take j=1, x=h0, y=h1, H=x+y, R=h2+h3, s=H+R, Z=x*y. Then

    p_1=1-x*k, p_2=(1-x*k)*(1-y*k), E=1+R*k,
    d_0=s-Z*k, d_1=(R+y)*(1-x*k),
    T_1=(R+y)*(1-x*k)^2
                    *[(R-y)-((2y-1)*R-y)*k],        (9)
    T_0=(s-Z*k)*[2R*p_2-(1-k)*(s-Z*k)],             (10)
    T_0-T_1=x*E*J(k),                               (11)
    J(k)=2R*p_2-(1-k)*(d_0+d_1).

On the compact domain, x<2, y>1 and R>2. The constant coefficient
of J is -x-2y<0, its quadratic coefficient is

    x*[R*(2y-1)-2y]>2*x*(y-1)>0,

and J(1)=2R*(x-1)*(y-1)>0. Thus J has exactly one positive root
theta in (0,1), because the product of its two roots is negative.
It is negative below theta and positive above theta. Moreover
theta>max(1/x,1/y). At k=1/x, p_2=d_1=0 and d_0>0, so J<0.
At k=1/y, p_2=0 and

    d_0+d_1=E*y*(2-x/y)>0,

since x<2 and y>1, so again J<0.

For k>=max(1/x,1/y), p_1<=0 and p_2>=0. In this interval d_1<=0,
so T_1<=0 directly from its defining formula. Also d_0>0: the compact
bounds imply x<9/5, y<49/20, s>=168/25, hence
s-x*y>168/25-441/100=231/100>0. Therefore

    Tplus=max(0,T_0)                                (12)

throughout this high-curvature interval. The bracket

    J0(k)=2R*p_2-(1-k)*(s-Z*k)

in (10) has positive leading coefficient Z*(2R-1). It is negative
at both reciprocal roots and positive at 1. There is exactly one
root rho in (max(1/x,1/y),1), its larger real root. Consequently the
zero-credit branch is selected up to rho, and T_0 is selected from
rho to 1. The equality k=rho is preserved and satisfies T_0=0.

The interval between the two reciprocal roots is NOT discarded.
There p_2<=0 and d_0>0, so T_0<=0, but T_1 may still be positive
when x>y. Formula (9), including its zero at k=1/x and its linear
factor, is the exact branch criterion on that interval. When y>=x,
p_1>=0 there and d_1>=0, so T_1<=0 and beta=0 on the whole interval.
Every order case and every credit-zero boundary is retained.

## 2. A bound used in the mixed-sign intervals

For a prefix of mass H and a suffix of mass R put

    F_HR(k)=[1+k*(H+2R+1)]/(1+k*R)^2.

It decreases as R increases, since its derivative with respect to R is
-2k^2*(H+R+1)/(1+k*R)^3. When R>=2 and H<=17/4,

    F_HR(k)<3/2,

because after using R=2 the numerator of the positive difference is
(24k^2-13k+2)/4; its discriminant is 169-192<0 and leading
coefficient is positive. When R>=1 and H<=67/10,

    F_HR(k)<3,

because after using R=1 the corresponding numerator is bounded below
by 3k^2-(37/10)k+2, whose discriminant is 1369/100-24<0.

In all cases the auxiliary energy has the exact form

    V_k/k=B(k)-H*F_HR(k)*p_(j+1)(k)^2.               (3)

The C85 no-overshoot proof already handles k<=1/max_(i<=j)h_i.
Only the remaining mixed-sign intervals need estimates.

## 3. The second cut: both possible step orders

## 4. The third cut: all mixed-sign patterns

    |p_3| <= max((1-k)^2*(m*k-1),(1-k)*(m*k-1)^2)

    p_1^2<(9/29)^2<1/10, |p_2|<1/4.

## 5. Unique auxiliary-energy sign change at high curvature

Now every prefix factor is negative. Let

    f_x(k)=x*[1-k*(x-1)]/(x*k-1)^2.

Its derivative is

    f_x'(k)=x*[x*(x-1)*k-x-1]/(x*k-1)^3.             (6)

For x=h0<m0 this is strictly negative, and f_x>0.

For j=1, divide V_k/k by (x*k-1)^2. The result is

    L1(k)=f_x(k)+y*[1-k*(y-1)]
                   -H*F_HR(k)*(y*k-1)^2.           (7)

All three terms together have strictly negative derivative. The first
two decrease. For the final term, the logarithmic derivative of the
positive product F_HR(k)*(y*k-1)^2 is

    (H+2R+1)/(1+k*(H+2R+1))
       +2y/(y*k-1)-2R/(1+k*R)>0,

because y/(y*k-1)>1/k>R/(1+k*R). Thus L1 strictly decreases.

For j=2, dividing V_k/k by (x*k-1)^2*(y*k-1)^2 gives

    L2(k)=f_x(k)/(y*k-1)^2+f_y(k)
                 +z*[1-k*(z-1)]
                 -H*F_HR(k)*(z*k-1)^2.             (8)

The last two terms strictly decrease. To control the first two, (6)
and x<m0 imply

    f_x'(k)<-(34/25)/(64/125)=-85/32,
    d/dk[f_x/(y*k-1)^2]
          <-(85/32)*(400/841)<-1.                  (9)

Indeed 1+2x-x^2>34/25, x>1, and 0<x*k-1<4/5. The extra term in
the derivative in (9) is negative because f_x>0. If f_y'<=0 there
is nothing further to bound. If f_y'>0, (6) gives
