## 11. Exact elimination of the entire lower-clipping parameter

The new inequality is not an isolated parameter choice. The whole
one-parameter scalar family can be eliminated exactly. Let
h=(1+a,1+b,1+c,1+d), with all shifts positive, P=2+a,
H=3+c+d, C=P+b+H. Assume cd<1; the earlier legal last-triangle
condition forces this on any candidate. For every alpha>0, take the
same function with derivative clamp(x,[-alpha,1]), start at x0=P,
and perform all four original steps. Then its energy is nonnegative
for EVERY alpha>0 if and only if the following conditions all hold:

 C b/H <=P,
 C b d/(2+c)<=P,
 C |bc-(1+d)min(bc,1)|<=P,
 and, when bc>=1, C d<=P.                              (10)

This equivalence concerns only this particular one-dimensional function
family; it is not a characterization of full primitive feasibility.
Every strict violation gives a finite positive alpha and one actual
negative-energy function. Equality and zero limits are retained.

Proof. Every sample of every function in this family has saturated
value f_i=x_i g_i-g_i^2/2. Thus V=P^2-Y(alpha)^2, where
Y(alpha)=x4+s g4, s=C-1. The first two steps reach x1=1 and x2=-b.
For alpha>=b the remainder is independent of alpha: x3=bc and
x4=bc-(1+d)min(bc,1). The assumption cd<1 guarantees x4>=-b:
if bc<=1 use x4=-bcd; if bc>=1, then b>=1/c>d and x4>=-d>-b.
Consequently the final lower clipping is inactive. If x4>1 then
Y=x4+s>C>P and the third condition in (10) fails too. Otherwise
Y=C x4, exactly the third condition.

For 0<alpha<=b, g2=-alpha and x3=-b+(1+c)alpha. Split at

 alpha_A=b/H, alpha_B=b/(2+c),
 alpha_C=(b+1)/(1+c) when bc>=1.

Their relevant order is 0<alpha_A<alpha_B<=b, and, when defined,
alpha_B<alpha_C<=b. For alpha<=alpha_B the third query remains
on the lower flat part, so g3=-alpha and x4=-b+(2+c+d)alpha.
For alpha<=alpha_A one has g4=-alpha and
Y=-b-(P+b)alpha, a decreasing function, reaching -C b/H.
From alpha_A to alpha_B, x4 increases and g4 follows x4 until
possibly reaching the upper cap1. Thus Y increases throughout that
interval. Its endpoint at alpha_B has x4=d b/(2+c), with
Y=C d b/(2+c) if x4<=1, and Y=x4+s otherwise. If x4>1 the
second condition in (10) and nonnegative energy both fail. The
limit |Y(0)|=b is smaller than C b/H since C>H.

For alpha between alpha_B and min(b,alpha_C), with alpha_C omitted
when bc<1, the third query is on the quadratic part: g3=x3 and
x4=-d x3 decreases with alpha. On each final clipping branch,
Y is one of x4+s, C x4, or x4-s alpha. Each is decreasing, and
they agree at their boundaries. Thus |Y| attains its maximum at
an endpoint of this interval. At its lower endpoint we already
have the second condition. If its upper endpoint is b, we already
have the third condition. Otherwise x3=1 at alpha_C, and cd<1
implies alpha_C>=1/c>d; hence x4=g4=-d and Y=-C d.

For alpha_C<=alpha<=b when bc>=1, g3=1, and x4=x3-(1+d)
increases with alpha. Also x4+alpha>0 at alpha_C and increases,
so the final lower cap stays inactive. Y increases, first as C x4
and then, if x4>1, as x4+s. Again its absolute maximum is at an
endpoint, already covered by the last and third conditions.
These regimes exhaust every alpha, and prove (10).

For the compact candidate box b<1552559/1081453<2<2+c, the second
condition is implied by Cd<=P when bc>=1. On bc<=1, the third
condition is Cbcd<=P, so the two new/old return constraints are

The max over c>0 of min(2+c,1/c) is1+sqrt(2), attained only at
