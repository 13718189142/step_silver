## 2. One legal objective, its exact areas, and basic ray estimates

Integration gives the exact energy, including all knot and tail areas:

    2F(r)=kappa*r^2,                         0<=r<=1,
    2F(r)=(r-v)^2+kappa*v,                   1<=r<=u,
    2F(r)=2B*r-C_tail,                       r>=u,
    C_tail=B^2+v*(2B-kappa).                         (2.1)

At r=1 both adjacent values equal kappa; at r=u both equal B^2+kappa*v.
The first derivatives agree as well. The middle interval collapses when
B=kappa. When kappa=1, v=0 and this is the unit-curvature Huber law.

The derivative g is continuous and nondecreasing, with piecewise slopes
kappa,1,0. Partitioning any interval at the knots proves that it is
1-Lipschitz. Thus f is everywhere finite, convex, differentiable, and
1-smooth. Its unique minimizer is0, because G(r)>0 for every r>0. Its value
there is0, and its true initial distance is |s|.

For completeness the complete actual transcript is smooth-convex legal.
For x<y put Delta=g(y)-g(x), so 0<=Delta<=y-x. On [x,y],

    g(t)-g(x)>=max(0,Delta-(y-t)),
    g(y)-g(t)>=max(0,Delta-(t-x)).

Integrating each lower triangle yields Delta^2/2. These two inequalities
give every ordered interpolation row

    f_i-f_j+g_i*(x_j-x_i)+(g_i-g_j)^2/2<=0

for actual physical, terminal, and optimum samples. Repeated positions
have identical values and gradients. The scalar Gram matrix is positive
semidefinite: each quadratic form is the square of the corresponding real
linear combination of actual vectors. No terminal sample or ordered pair
is omitted.

We shall use

    0<=G(r)<=min(r,B),
    G(r)>=kappa*min(r,1).                              (2.2)

For the first inequality, kappa*r<=r and r-v<=r. For the second, inside
the core equality holds; outside the core both the cap B and the uncapped
gradient are at least kappa.

For p=G(r), monotonicity and smoothness give G(t)<=min(t,p) on [0,r].
Since p<=r, integration yields

    2F(r)<=2r*p-p^2.                                  (2.3)

For r<=u a second conversion is valid:

    2F(r)<=G(r)^2/kappa.                              (2.4)

It is equality in the core. In the middle p=r-v>=kappa, and the difference

This covers r=1,u and B=kappa. It is NOT asserted beyond the cap knot:
the energy there keeps growing although the gradient is constant.

A strict sign crossing means two successive nonzero iterates have opposite
signs. If a trajectory never strictly crosses and never hits zero, all
radii decrease. For a segment of total step mass T, terminal radius r,
terminal gradient p, and original segment radius R, all its queried

    R>=r+T*p,
    2F(r)<=2R*p-(1+2T)*p^2<=R^2/(1+2T).              (2.5)

## 4. One-step branches and the first-crossing price

For h=1+d>=1 the positive-source signed update is

    (1-h*kappa)*r,          0<=r<=1,
    h*v-d*r,               1<=r<=u,
    r-h*B,                 r>=u.                    (4.1)

The expressions agree at r=1,u. The exact image radius of [-R,R] is

    E_h(R)=max(
       |1-h*kappa|*min(R,1),
       [d*min(R,u)-h*v]_+,
       [R-h*B]_+ ),                                  (4.2)

where [w]_+=max(w,0). Each available affine piece has its extrema at
an endpoint. The middle positive endpoint is bounded by the core
endpoint; its negative endpoint gives the second term. The tail has
its negative endpoint at the cap knot and its positive endpoint at R.
If R<1, the unavailable middle expression satisfies
dR-hv<=(h*kappa-1)R, since the difference is h*v*(R-1)<=0.
If R<u, a positive unavailable tail expression is at most the actual
positive endpoint R-h*G(R). These observations prove (4.2) also when
a formal piece is absent.

Each signed update is continuous, odd, and fixes0, so its interval image
is the entire symmetric interval specified by (4.2). Thus M_j is a
nondecreasing interval-image radius, and exactly

    M_(j+1)=M_j composed with E_(1+d_j) composed with M_j. (4.3)

These are images of actual compatible starts. No recurrence was executed.

For a strict crossing with source r and gradient p=G(r), its landing z obeys

    z=(1+d)*p-r<=d*p.                                (4.4)

For a pre-cap source r<u with a positive same-side output, necessarily
h*kappa<1, and that output lies in the core. For r<=1 this is direct
from (4.1). For 1<=r<u, the output equals

(1-h*kappa)*min(r,1). A source exactly at u may instead be classified
as saturated; its two formulas agree.

If all steps of H_j satisfy h*kappa<=1, the core [0,1] is absorbing
and its radius contracts with nonnegative factors. The full physical

    P_j(kappa)=product_(h in H_j)(1-kappa*h)
       <=exp(-kappa*S_j).                            (4.5)

The inequality follows by multiplying 1-z<=exp(-z). No suffix is dropped.

    R>=r+P*p>=v+(1+P)*p>=v+A_k*p.                    (4.6)

increasing its gradient, and kappa*(1+d_l)<1. Every following H_l step
is then core preserving, and its gradient cannot increase. Zero is
absorbing. This accounts for the entire literal suffix in (3.6).
For H_j every child depth invoked here is at most j-1.

For the base j=1, set w=c^(-q)*B and R=d_1*w=beta*w. Since R<B,

Assume both conclusions through j>=1. Start the parent H_(j+1) at

    R<=d_(j+1)*c^(-q)*B=c*d_j*w, w=c^(-q)*B.

    p_out<=kappa*(v-t)*exp(-L*t).
