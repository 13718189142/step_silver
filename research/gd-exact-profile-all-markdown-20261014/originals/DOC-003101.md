## 8. Exact full-history state and its actual cost

This section records an algebraic state for a fixed arbitrary finite
dictionary. It is included to make the missing closure precise, not
to claim that defining a proximal optimization solves the problem.

Let there be k complete finite arc stages with their centers and
scales fixed before the run. There are N=3k+1 affine planes including
the plane0, in ambient dimension k+1. Write each plane as

    L_mu(y)=<a_mu,y>+b_mu, mu=0,...,N-1,
    a_0=0, b_0=0,
    K_munu=<a_mu,a_nu>, z_mu=<a_mu,x>+b_mu.              (42)

K is the ACTUAL slope Gram. If a slope of stage i is
A_i*u_i-B_i*u_(i+1) and one of stage j>i is
A_j*u_j-B_j*u_(j+1), its inner product is exactly

    -(-r)^(j-i-1)*(r*A_i+B_i)*(A_j+r*B_j).             (43)

Within a stage the orthonormal(u_i,e_(i+1)) formula applies.
Equivalently the vector synthesized from a coefficient column t has
squared norm sum t_i^2+2 sum_(i<j)(-r)^(j-i)t_i*t_j. It is not ||t||_2^2.
The direction Gram has eigenvalues between(1-r)/(1+r) and
(1+r)/(1-r): absolute row sums bound it above, while its inverse
quadratic form on measurements m_i=<u_i,x> is

    ||x||^2=m_0^2+sum_(i=1)^k (m_i+r*m_(i-1))^2/s^2.  (44)

Expanding(44) bounds the inverse by(1+r)/(1-r) and proves the
lower bound. Formula(44) is also an exact radius charge when such
measurements, rather than x itself, are prescribed.

At a current input x, let w be any vector of active convex weights,
and H the common proximal height. Its complete equations are

    w_mu>=0, sum_mu w_mu=1,
    z-K*w<=H*1,
    w_mu*(z_mu-(K*w)_mu-H)=0 for every mu,
    g=sum_mu w_mu*a_mu, y=x-g,
    F(x)=H+(1/2)*w^T*K*w.                              (45)

These conditions are equivalent to the proximal condition:
the inequalities state that H=J(y), the complementarity says every
weighted plane is active, and g is their convex combination.
The vector g is unique even if w is not. With a physical step h>=0,
the exact next evaluation column is

    z^+=z-h*K*w.                                      (46)

For completeness, the subgradients of a finite maximum at y are
exactly the convex hull of its active slopes. A convex combination
gives a subgradient by summing the active affine lower bounds. For
the other inclusion, if g is outside that finite compact convex hull,
its closest point a separates it strictly in direction g-a. The
directional derivative of the finite maximum is the maximum of the
active slope evaluations in that direction, smaller than the
evaluation of g. This contradicts the subgradient inequality.
This justifies the necessity of the weights in(45), as well as their
sufficiency.

There are N dynamic plane evaluations, N candidate weights and one
height. Storing an unrestricted explicit Gram requires N^2 entries;
the frame and stage coefficients compress its representation but do
not delete any inequality in(45). An affinely independent active
set has at most k+2 planes. To see that some such representation
exists, an affine dependence among positively weighted slopes permits
moving the weights along a zero-sum dependence until one weight
vanishes; repeat without changing g. This is a finite reduction in
an argument, not an executed active-set search.

For a fixed affinely independent active set A, the equations for its
weights and height are the explicit linear system

    [ K_AA  1 ][ w_A ] = [ z_A ],
    [ 1^T   0 ][  H  ]   [  1  ].                     (47)

It is invertible: in its homogeneous nullspace, multiplying the first
row by w_A^T and using 1^T*w_A=0 gives
||sum w_mu*a_mu||^2=0, so affine independence gives w_A=0 and H=0.
No matrix in(47) was formed or solved by a program in this round.

There is also a useful coordinate-free form. Let P_A be the orthogonal
projector onto the span of active slope differences, and g_perp=(I-P_A)g.
As long as the proposed next point has this same active face, equality
of its plane values and the affine combination of slopes give

    g^+=g-h*P_A*g,
    y^+=y-h*g_perp,
    H^+=H-h*||g_perp||^2.                              (48)

For the first identity, the change in g lies in the difference span,
and the active-plane equality makes the change in x-g orthogonal to
that span. For the last identity every active slope has the same
projection g_perp onto its orthogonal complement.

Every retained or future plane has slack

    sigma_mu=H-L_mu(y)>=0.

Its EXACT prospective update inside this face is

    sigma_mu^+=sigma_mu
                +h*(<a_mu,g_perp>-||g_perp||^2).        (49)

The barycentric weights from(47) must remain nonnegative, AND every
one of(49) must remain nonnegative. These are the tests; dropping
old slacks gives a different, insufficient state.

For the actual face{p,q}, P_A is projection on v, g_perp=g_bar,
and(48) is exactly(20). It passes both ordinary steps by Section4.
The physical step2 fails the weight test(28). Replacing that test
by clamping fails the old endpoint's slack as well, by Section5.
Thus(46) is an exact state description with an explicitly witnessed
change of face, not an invariant two-plane transfer theorem.

The state retains the full history. Formula(43) has alternating signs
and magnitude r^(j-i-1) times the displayed coefficients. In a regime
k*s^2->0, these weights are 1+o(1) uniformly for all separations up

This is a count of nondecaying constraints, not a proof that their
