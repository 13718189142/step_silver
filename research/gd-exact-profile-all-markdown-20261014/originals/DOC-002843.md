b_j=1+a*c^j; H_0=(), H_(j+1)=(H_j,b_j,H_j). The length is n=2^m-1.

levels are J,J,J+1,...,m-2. Their masses obey S_0=0,
S_(j+1)=2S_j+b_j. The final suffix starts at query 2^(m-1).

Write C=beta+sqrt(1+2beta), R_circle=1+beta, kap=1+beta^2.
In coordinates (p,-q), the FULL convex body is
K={0<=q<=1, beta*q<=p, (p-beta)^2+(q+beta)^2<=R_circle^2}.
Its boundary consists of the segment from 0 to (C,0), the segment
from 0 to (beta,-1), and the circular arc connecting their far endpoints.
The displayed disk intersects those two half-planes in exactly the
horizontal sections beta*q<=p<=beta+sqrt(R_circle^2-(q+beta)^2).

For any centered normalized input w=(r,s), its projection is w if
w belongs to K. Otherwise a nearest boundary point is among:
(clip(r,0,C),0); (beta*t,-t), t=clip((beta*r-s)/kap,0,1);
and the radial point (beta,beta)+R_circle*(w-(beta,beta))/||w-(beta,beta)||
when that point lies on the stated arc. These are the exact nearest
points on the two segments and on the arc whenever the latter has an
interior stationary minimum; remaining arc minima are endpoints already
included. Choose the candidate maximizing <g,w>-||g||^2/2.
The production implementation can return the radial disk projection
directly when it belongs to the arc, since K is a subset of that disk.
For s>=0 the horizontal projection is exact. For r<=0 the diagonal
projection is exact, by maximizing first over U=p-beta*q>=0.
These are projection calculations, never projections of the GD state.

The full support function at d=(d_x,d_y) is the maximum of
0, C*d_x, beta*d_x-d_y, and
beta*(d_x+d_y)+R_circle*sqrt(d_x^2+d_y^2) if d_x>0, d_y<0 and
(-d_y)*sqrt(1+2beta)>=beta*d_x. This is obtained by maximizing the
linear functional along the entire arc; the derivative has a unique
interior maximum in precisely that angular sector. The endpoints pay
all other directions, including d_x=0. Thus projection KKT is checked
against the whole convex hull by h_K(w-g)-<w-g,g>, not sampled arc points.

from u=beta,y=1. Reading the actual reversed physical step h, alpha=h/2,
