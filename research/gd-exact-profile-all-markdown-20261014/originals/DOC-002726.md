## Common actual-function construction

Let a finite probability space have positive probabilities \(p_i\), and put

\[
E=\{X:\mathbb E X=0\},\qquad
K=\{g\in E:g_i\ge-1\},\qquad
F_K(X)=\max_{g\in K}\left(\mathbb E[Xg]-\tfrac12\mathbb E g^2\right).
\]

This is an ordinary Euclidean space under \(X_i\mapsto\sqrt{p_i}X_i\). The set \(K\) is compact: its defining conditions give \(-1\le g_i\le(1-p_i)/p_i\). It is convex, and zero is in its relative interior. The maximizer is the Euclidean projection \(P_KX\). Projection optimality gives

\[
\langle X-P_KX,k-P_KX\rangle\le0\quad(k\in K).
\]

Adding the inequalities for two inputs proves that the projection is 1-Lipschitz. Strong concavity in the maximization variable gives the gradient \(\nabla F_K=P_K\); hence \(F_K\) is globally convex and 1-smooth. It has unique minimum zero at zero: for nonzero \(X\), a sufficiently small positive multiple \(sX\) lies in \(K\), and \(F_K(X)\ge(s-s^2/2)\|X\|^2>0\).

The unique scalar \(t\) satisfying \(\mathbb E(X-t)_+=1\) gives

\[
g=(X-t)_+-1.
\]

Indeed this is the projection's clipped Lagrange-multiplier formula, and its mean is zero. The stop-loss function is continuous and strictly decreasing below the maximum atom, with range from infinity to zero. Moreover \(\mathbb E(X+1)_+\ge1\), so \(t\ge-1\). Writing \(U=(X-t)_+\), one has \(\mathbb EU=1\), \(\mathbb E[Xg]=t+\mathbb EU^2\), and therefore

\[
F_K(X)=t+\tfrac12+\tfrac12\mathbb EU^2
       =t+1+\tfrac12\mathbb Eg^2.                 \tag{1}
\]

Every constructed step below is exactly \(X'=X-hg\). A probability atom can be split into several atoms with identical current and future values: aggregate probabilities, thresholds, moments and future dynamics remain unchanged. Thus backward splitting specifies one finite probability space before the forward run. It does not change the function during that run. All old, cross and terminal projection rows follow from the one actual set \(K\); no selected subset of interpolation rows replaces them.

### Optimize the allocation, including the threshold

For fixed \(a_*\), (11) is a fractional-knapsack problem with resource \(\omega Z\). Its cost per resource unit is \(Z\). If \(0<d<1\), minimize \(J\) by filling the smallest positive \(Z\), equivalently the largest \(Y\), first. If \(d>1\), the coefficient in (11) is negative, so maximize \(J\) by filling the largest \(Z\), equivalently the smallest \(Y\), first. At most one atom needs a fractional split. Ties may be combined or resolved in the original atom order.

The threshold can also be optimized exactly. On an interval of \(a_*\) where the cutoff atom has value \(z\), let \(A\) be the set of fully selected atoms, with mass \(p_A\), first moment \(\mu_A\), and second moment \(\nu_A\). Directly eliminating the fractional mass gives

\[
J(a_*)=a_*\bigl(d-\mu_A+zp_A\bigr)+\nu_A-z\mu_A-zd,
\]
\[
J'(a_*)=d+\mathbb E[1_A(z-Y)].                                 \tag{15}
\]

For \(d>1\), \(A\) is a lower tail. Since its allocated deficit is at most \(d\),

\[
0\le\mathbb E[1_A(z-Y)]\le\mathbb E[1_A(a_*-Y)]\le d.
\]

The optimized value in (11) is consequently strictly increasing in \(a_*\), with piecewise derivative at least

\[
2h+2d(d^{-2}-1)=2+2/d>0.
\]

Its global minimum is at \(a_* =\max(d,q)\). The same endpoint is optimal for \(d=1\), when the coefficient of \(J\) vanishes.

For \(0<d<1\), \(A\) is an upper tail and (15) gives the variance derivative

\[
\frac{h^2}{d}-(d^{-2}-1)\mathbb E(Y-z)_+.                       \tag{16}
\]

As \(a_*\) increases, the greedy cutoff \(z\) can only move upward. Thus (16) is nondecreasing: the optimized predecessor variance is convex and piecewise affine in \(a_*\). Its slope is eventually \(h^2/d>0\). A minimum is attained at the endpoint \(\max(d,q)\) or at a feasible upper-tail breakpoint

\[
a_*=(d+\mathbb E[Y1_A])/\mathbb P(A),\qquad\mathbb P(A)>0.      \tag{17}
\]

This is a finite exact selection rule, not an instruction to run a solver. If several thresholds minimize the radius, choosing one requires retaining its resulting distribution for subsequent inverse steps. The same current radius does not make those distributions interchangeable.
