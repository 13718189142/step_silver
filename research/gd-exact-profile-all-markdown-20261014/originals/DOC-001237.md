# Exact sublevel state for joins that raise the minimum

Status: UNVERIFIED joint proof and counterexample, worker max, 2026-09-26.
No verification call is allocated. This does not assert the unrestricted
Zhang--Jiang comparison. It extends the zero-preserving state test rather
than contradicting its explicitly restricted sufficiency statement.

## 1. What the zero-level profile loses

For a finite convex polyhedral function J on a Euclidean space, write
env J(x)=min_z[J(z)+||x-z||^2/2]. All functions below have an attained
minimum. The unique minimizing z is the proximal point. If x-z is the
gradient of an affine plane active in J at z, the subgradient inequality
and the strict quadratic term show that z is the unique proximal point.
Moreover env J is convex, differentiable, and has 1-Lipschitz gradient
x-z. One direct proof of the Lipschitz assertion adds the two proximal
subgradient inequalities, obtaining
||g(x)-g(x')||^2 <= <g(x)-g(x'),x-x'>.
The minimizing inequalities give differentiability with this gradient.
Finally min env J=min J and their minimizer sets coincide: equality in
J(z)+||x-z||^2/2 >= min J requires x=z and J(z)=min J.

Consider the two functions on coordinates (t,y)

    J_A(t,y)=max(0,1-t,-y),
    J_B(t,y)=max(0,1-t,-y,3(1-t-y)/4).

Both have precisely the zero set {t>=1,y>=0}. Start at x_init=(-1,-7)
and take the one-step prefix h=(7). Both functions have the same legal
proximal samples:

    query x_0=(-1,-7), proximal z_0=(-1,-6), height V_0=6,
    gradient g_0=(0,-1);
    query x_1=(-1,0), proximal p=(0,0), height V_1=1,
    gradient g_1=(-1,0).

At z_0 the values of (0,1-t,-y,3(1-t-y)/4) are (0,2,6,6).
At p they are (0,1,0,3/4). Thus the selected gradients really are
active-plane subgradients for both functions. The GD equality
x_0-7g_0=x_1 holds. Set u=(1,0), so the same boundary gradient is
-m_boundary u with m_boundary=1.

The entire proposed state, including the added gradient magnitude, agrees:

    Phi(t)=max(0,1-t),
    Psi(s)=min(1,6+s),
    Dist(t)=(t+1)^2+49 for t>=1, and +infinity otherwise.       (1)

Here Phi(t)=J(p+tu), Psi(s)=min_i[V_i-s<u,z_i-p>], and Dist is
the squared distance from the same x_init to the zero slice at t.
In fact all prefix queries, gradients, heights, initial points, boundary
points, and zero sets agree, a stronger equality than the state requires.

Append the identical fresh-coordinate cell in a new coordinate w. Choose
bridge b=3/2, scale d=1/2, cap c=1, endpoint epsilon=1/2, local initial
query (epsilon,-1), and no ordinary steps. Its center, common plane
height, and two planes are

    p_new=(1/4,0,1/2),   F_cell=13/16,
    C(t,y,w)=11/16+t/2,
    E(t,y,w)=1+t/4-w/2.

These are exactly F_cell+d<t-axis,z-p_new> and
F_cell+d<(epsilon,0,-1),z-p_new>. For T=A,B define

    Jbar_T=max(J_T,C,E).

The old query x_1 is sent by the prescribed bridge to x_2=(1/2,0,0).
At p_new the two new planes both equal 13/16, while both old functions
equal 3/4. Therefore g_2=(1/4,0,-1/2) is an active normal and
x_2=p_new+g_2. The new planes at the two old proximal points have values

    at z_0=(-1,-6,0): C=3/16, E=3/4, both <=6;
    at p=(0,0,0):     C=11/16, E=1, both <=1.

This checks both directions of every cross-block comparison. All gradients
and all GD iterates are actual gradients and iterates of env Jbar_T.

Nevertheless neither Jbar_T has a zero. Indeed its minimum is

    mu=19/24,

because its planes 1-t and 11/16+t/2 have minimum possible maximum
19/24, attained only when t=5/24. At that t the endpoint constraint is
w>=25/48. The remaining minimum-set constraints are exactly

    argmin Jbar_A={t=5/24, y>=-19/24, w>=25/48},
    argmin Jbar_B={t=5/24, y>=-19/72, w>=25/48}.              (2)

For B, the extra inequality is
(3/4)(19/24-y)<=19/24, equivalent to y>=-19/72; it dominates
y>=-19/24. Every displayed point satisfies all the other planes, proving
both attainment and the complete descriptions in (2).

The actual closest minimizers to (-1,-7,0) are thus the respective corners,
and their squared distances are

    R_A^2=(29/24)^2+(149/24)^2+(25/48)^2=30931/768,
    R_B^2=(29/24)^2+(485/72)^2+(25/48)^2=976801/20736.       (3)

Both have F_cell-mu=1/48>0 and ||g_2||^2=5/16. Their normalized last
errors for the identical schedule (7,3/2) are therefore different:

    2(env Jbar_A(x_2)-mu)/R_A^2 =272/30931,
    2(env Jbar_B(x_2)-mu)/R_B^2 =7344/976801.                (4)

Subtracting the same mu from the WHOLE respective functions normalizes
their minima to zero and changes none of their GD iterates. Every retained
past function value is reduced by that same constant. This is not an
independent reset of a checkpoint value.

Conclusion: Phi,Psi,Dist and m_boundary cannot determine the true distance
normalization of a minimum-raising join for general allowed polyhedral
histories. The previous zero-preserving rule correctly rejects this join;
its restricted sufficiency is unaffected. The stronger rule that accepts
all legal joins needs more information than the zero slice.

## 2. The missing information can be written explicitly

Define the two-parameter sublevel distance profile

    Xi(t,r)=min{||x_init-z||^2:J(z)<=r, <u,z-p>=t},          (5)

with value +infinity when the set is empty. Extend it by +infinity for
r<0 when min J=0. The minimum exists whenever feasible: the feasible
set is closed and the squared norm is coercive.

For the preceding two histories, for r>=0 and t>=1-r the exact profiles
are

    Xi_A(t,r)=(t+1)^2+max(0,7-r)^2,
    Xi_B(t,r)=(t+1)^2+max(0,7-r,8-t-4r/3)^2.              (6)

They are +infinity outside that domain. These formulas follow by projecting
the initial y-coordinate -7 onto y>=-r for A and onto
y>=max(-r,1-t-4r/3) for B. At r=0 both reduce to (1), while at the
actual raised minimum r=19/24 and t=5/24 they give the two different
old-coordinate costs in (3).

## 3. Exact finite update with global recentering allowed

Take any old J_old=max(0,L_1,...,L_M) with min J_old=0. Let its entire
old ambient space contain x_init, p, u, every old normal and every old
proximal point z_i. The retained samples have heights V_i=J_old(z_i),
and the boundary query is p-m_boundary u, m_boundary>0. Retain Phi,
Psi as in (1) and Xi as in (5). No assumption about a small state size
is made.

Specify finite new-cell normals (a,-q), a>0, 0<=q<=1, including a single
q=0 cap (c,0) and endpoint (epsilon,-1). The local queries (u_j,-y_j)
and selected normals (a_j,-q_j) obey their prescribed GD differences and
all local support inequalities, with terminal query and selected normal
(epsilon,-1). Write

    U_j=u_j-a_j,
    h_j=< (a_j,-q_j),(U_j,-(y_j-q_j)) > >=0,
    r_0=(R_x,-R_y),   lambda=sqrt(1+epsilon^2).

A supplied bridge b>1 and scale d>0 determine

    theta=(b-1)m_boundary-d R_x.

As in the current joining geometry require theta>=0. Choose F_cell>0,
and a new unit coordinate e orthogonal to the entire old ambient. Put

    p_new=p+theta u+d R_y e,
    L_(a,q)=F_cell+d<a u-qe,z-p_new>,
    Jbar=max(J_old,all L_(a,q)),
    u_new=(-epsilon u+e)/lambda,
    m_new=d lambda.

The new query/proximal data are

    x_j=p_new+d(u_j u-y_j e),
    g_j=d(a_j u-q_j e),
    z_j=p_new+d(U_j u-(y_j-q_j)e),
    V_j=F_cell+d^2 h_j.

The complete two-direction interpolation test is exactly

    max_j[Phi(theta+d U_j)-d^2 h_j] <= F_cell
       <= min_(a,q)[Psi(d a)+d a theta-d^2 R_y q].         (7)

An old plane at a new z_j depends only on its old projection
p+(theta+d U_j)u, giving the left side. A new plane at an old z_i is
F_cell+d a(<u,z_i-p>-theta)+d^2 R_y q, giving the right side.
Together with the supplied local inequalities this checks every pair.
It gives the proximal data for the same single Jbar, without requiring
that Jbar still have a zero.

The unrecentered outgoing and acceptance profiles are

    Phibar(tau)=max{Phi(theta-epsilon tau/lambda),
                     F_cell+max_(a,q)[-d(epsilon a+q)tau/lambda]},

    Psibar(s)=min{Psi(-epsilon s/lambda)
                            +s(d R_y-epsilon theta)/lambda,
                 min_j[F_cell+d^2h_j
                            +s d(epsilon U_j+y_j-q_j)/lambda]}. (8)

For each real r set

    Z_r(t)=max_(a,q),q>0[d R_y+(F_cell-r)/(d q)
                                      +(a/q)(t-theta)],
    w(t,tau)=lambda tau+d R_y+epsilon(t-theta).

The exact sublevel-distance update is

    Xibar(tau,r)=inf_t {Xi(t,r)+w(t,tau)^2:
        t<=theta+(r-F_cell)/(d c), w(t,tau)>=Z_r(t)}.       (9)

This follows by writing an enlarged point as z=z_old+w e. Its old
coordinate t=<u,z_old-p> must obey J_old(z_old)<=r. The cap gives the
upper bound on t, each positive-q plane gives one lower bound on w,
and fixing the new outgoing slice determines w=w(t,tau). Orthogonality
to x_init as well as the entire old ambient gives the squared-distance
sum. Conversely an attaining old point for Xi(t,r), with that appended
w, realizes exactly the displayed cost. Thus (9) is an equality, including
empty cases; finite minima on fixed new slices are attained by closedness
and coercivity.

Since Jbar is the maximum of finitely many affine functions, includes zero,
and is finite, its minimum mu>=0 is attained. One elementary way to see
attainment is to project its nonempty epigraph polyhedron onto the value
coordinate; Fourier--Motzkin elimination gives a polyhedron in R, and
the projection is closed and bounded below. Equivalently, using (9),

    mu=min{r>=0: Xibar(tau,r)<infinity for some tau}.       (10)

Now recenter the WHOLE function by J_new=Jbar-mu. Its exact new state is

    Phi_new=Phibar-mu,  Psi_new=Psibar-mu,
    Xi_new(tau,r)=Xibar(tau,r+mu),  r>=0.                 (11)

All old and new proximal heights are reduced by the same mu; gradients
and iterates do not change. As Jbar>=mu everywhere, inserting the zero
plane into the maximum of all shifted planes leaves J_new unchanged.
The boundary gradient magnitude remains m_new. In particular the exact
normalized endpoint error is

    [2(F_cell-mu)+d^2(1+epsilon^2)] / min_tau Xibar(tau,mu). (12)

The denominator is the distance to an actual closest minimizer of the
same function whose gradients realize the entire supplied history.

This is a finite rule. Xi is a convex extended-real piecewise quadratic
function of (t,r): fix any active face of the linearly parameterized
polyhedron defining (5); its equality-constrained Euclidean projection
is affine in (t,r), and its squared distance is quadratic. Primal and
normal-cone conditions select polyhedral parameter regions. There are
finitely many active subsets; if a subset is dependent, retain a basis
for the affine hull and describe its normal cone with the full active
set. This does not require nondegeneracy. On every piece, (9) minimizes
a quadratic in one scalar t over linear inequalities in (t,tau,r).
The positive coefficient from the fixed t-slice squared distance permits
the unconstrained affine vertex to be clipped to the feasible interval.
Checking the finitely many pieces gives another finite piecewise quadratic
profile. No solver or numerical sweep is part of the construction.

## 4. In the arc-chain regime the amount of recentering is scalar

There is a useful exact consequence when the old zero-slice domain is
[F_old/m_boundary,infinity), where F_old=J_old(p)>0. Assume also that
-m_boundary u is an active old boundary normal. For every
0<=r<=F_old,

    {t:Xi(t,r)<infinity}=[(F_old-r)/m_boundary,infinity).   (13)

The supporting plane gives the lower bound. If r<F_old and t is at
least that bound, take a zero point with outgoing coordinate
t_zero=t F_old/(F_old-r), and form the convex combination with p whose
weight on that zero point is (F_old-r)/F_old. Its coordinate is t and
its value is at most r. If r=F_old, use p when t=0, and for t>0 mix p
with any zero point at a coordinate at least max(t,F_old/m_boundary).
This proves every claimed feasible slice without using a distance bound.

Assume the cross-tests (7) hold. Let R_cap=d c. Then the actual minimum
in (10) is the explicit value

    mu=max(0,[R_cap F_old+m_boundary F_cell
                  -m_boundary R_cap theta]/[m_boundary+R_cap]). (14)

To prove it, for any r<=min(F_old,F_cell) the old sublevel is feasible
at exactly the t in (13). The new cap requires
t<=theta-(F_cell-r)/R_cap. Every positive-q plane can be satisfied
by making w sufficiently large. Thus feasibility is equivalent to

    (F_old-r)/m_boundary+(F_cell-r)/R_cap <= theta.

The displayed root is no greater than F_old because the new cap at p
is at most F_old. It is no greater than F_cell because the old boundary
plane at p_new is at most F_cell. These are included in (7), so the
calculation applies at the claimed nonnegative minimum itself. This
proves both the lower bound and attainment in (14).

For a concave-arc cell satisfying

    a-epsilon q>=0,
    (a-epsilon q)/c+q>=1,

the endpoint lower bound on w dominates every other positive-q bound
on the cap-feasible range whenever r<=F_cell. Subtract the endpoint
bound from a general bound and use
t-theta<=-(F_cell-r)/(d c); the result is at most
(F_cell-r)/(d q)[1-q-(a-epsilon q)/c]<=0.
Consequently the new zero-slice domain after (11) is exactly
[(F_cell-mu)/(d lambda),infinity). The domain hypothesis used in
(13) is therefore preserved by these joins, even when mu>0.

In particular a positive minimum does not make the geometric join
illegal. It consumes function-value height, with the exact identity

    (F_old-mu)/m_boundary+(F_cell-mu)/R_cap=theta          (15)

whenever the positive part in (14) is active. The earlier zero-preserving
budget was the condition mu=0. Allowing mu>0 requires the sublevel
distance state, as the counterexample proves; it does not erase the
loss or determine its normalization from Dist alone.

## 5. Remaining quantitative question

This package gives an actual larger class of legal iterative joins and
an exact, growing finite state. Cross-history legality (7), the loss (14),
and the nearest-minimizer cost (9) all remain in the same construction.
A policy selecting cells, scales and levels for every prescribed schedule
with normalized endpoint error at least (1-o(1))U_n^ZJ is still absent.
None of the displayed example ratios is claimed to optimize W_n.
