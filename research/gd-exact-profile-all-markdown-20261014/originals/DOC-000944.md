### 4.1 Strict fixed-depth coercivity at the limiting Silver word

For this subsection only, let `epsilon=0`, so

\[
c_0=1+\sqrt2,\quad a_0=1/c_0,\quad
C_j^0=c_0^j,\quad k_j^0=a_0 C_j^0.

\[
E_j^{\rm std}\ge0,\qquad
\Gamma_j^{\rm std}=(C_j^0)^2-C_j^0,
\tag{4.1}
\]

for every physical interpolation-feasible dataset. The proof uses only nonnegative original rows. It proves (4.1) at the exact limiting word and makes no assertion of higher standard primitive positivity at positive epsilon.

We need the equality statement, not merely (4.1):

> If `E_j^std = 0` and the terminal gradient is zero, then every gradient in that word is zero.

For the leaf, equality in (2.3) gives `Q_01=Q_10=0`. With `g_1=0`, their sum is

\[
Q_{01}+Q_{10}=2(h-1)\|g_0\|^2,
\]

so `g_0=0`. For a parent, equality in (3.7) gives zero child residuals and zero endpoint rows, since all their coefficients are positive. The right child, whose terminal gradient is zero, has every gradient zero by induction. Thus `G=0` and `w=y`. The bridge/terminal row sum then is

\[
Q_{vy}+Q_{yv}=2k\|p\|^2.
\]

It must vanish, hence `p=0`. The left induction finishes the proof. This argument uses just the original interpolation inequalities and physical displacement identities, so it also applies to a feasible finite Gram representation without first constructing a realizing function.

The weakened residual at the limiting word satisfies

\[
E_j^{\rm weak}=E_j^{\rm std}+\frac3{10}(C_j^0)^2\|g_{N_j}\|^2.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Consequently it can vanish on interpolation-feasible data only when *all* gradients vanish.

Here is a precise dimension-uniform coercivity consequence. Fix `j`, put `n=N_j`, normalize

\[
\sum_{i=0}^n\|g_i\|^2=1,
\]

and represent the gradients by their `(n+1)`-square Gram matrix `M`, with `M PSD` and `trace M=1`. Translate the terminal position and value to zero in the interpolation system. Physical positions are then determined by

\[
x_i-x_n=\sum_{t=i}^{n-1}h_t g_t.
\]

The two endpoint rows imply

\[
\langle g_n,x_i-x_n\rangle+\tfrac12\|g_i-g_n\|^2
\le f_i-f_n
\le\langle g_i,x_i-x_n\rangle-\tfrac12\|g_i-g_n\|^2.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Since each gradient norm is at most one, these function differences have absolute value at most the fixed word mass `S_j^0`. Hence the set of such normalized feasible Gram/value data is compact: PSD trace-one matrices form a compact set, the values are bounded, and all interpolation constraints are closed. It is nonempty, for example from a nonconstant quadratic history followed by normalization.

The continuous functional (4.2) has strictly positive value everywhere on this compact set, by the equality argument and `trace M=1`. It therefore has a positive minimum `eta_j`. Homogeneity gives

\[
E_j^{\rm weak}\ge\eta_j\sum_{i=0}^{N_j}\|g_i\|^2
\qquad(\epsilon=0),\qquad \eta_j>0.
\tag{4.4}
\]

No numerical optimization, solver, or finite-array calculation is hidden in this compactness proof. The finite Gram size removes dependence on the original ambient dimension.

### 4.2 Transfer to actual enclosing parameters; no fixed-depth obstruction

For each fixed `j`, the actual coefficients `H_j(m)`, `S_j(m)`, and `Gamma_j(m)` converge to their limiting values as `m -> infinity`. Suppose there were a sequence of enclosing parameters tending to infinity and actual histories at this fixed depth with

\[

\]

The limit is an interpolation-feasible normalized dataset for the exact Silver word with residual at most `eta_j/2`, contradicting (4.4).

Thus the following statement is established in this manual draft:

\[
\boxed{\text{For every fixed }j\ge1\text{, there is }M_j<\infty
\text{ such that for all }m\ge M_j,
\quad E_j\ge\tfrac12\eta_j\sum_i\|g_i\|^2.}
\tag{4.5}
\]

It holds for all finite dimensions and all original convex 1-smooth histories, with the actual same-enclosing-m coefficients. Therefore, if the requested all-depth input fails along a sequence `m -> infinity`, its violating depth must tend to infinity along a subsequence; bounded-depth failures are impossible eventually.

### 4.3 A separate all-prefix exclusion for cone projections

There is also an exact exclusion of farther-minimizer absorption for the whole cone-projection subclass, at arbitrary inherited depth. Let `K` be a closed convex cone and

\[
f(x)=\tfrac12\|P_Kx\|^2,
\qquad \arg\min f=K^\circ.
\]

Write `g=P_Kx`, `n=x-g`. The projection optimality conditions for a cone give `n in K^polar`, `<n,g>=0`, and, for any vector `z`, `<z,P_Kz>=||P_Kz||^2`. These follow by testing the projection condition against zero and positive multiples of the projected vector.

For `h>1` and `r=h-1`, the next gradient `g^+=P_K(n-rg)` obeys

\[
\|g^+\|^2=\langle g^+,n-rg\rangle
\le-r\langle g^+,g\rangle\le r\|g^+\|\|g\|.
\]

Hence `||g^+|| <= r||g||`. For any originally selected minimizer `z_star in K^polar`,

\[
\|x-z_\star\|^2-\|x-hg-z_\star\|^2
\ge h(2-h)\|g\|^2.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Define physical prefix products `P_0=1`, `P_t=prod_(i<t)(h_i-1)`. Every prefix of every actual inherited `H_j` satisfies `P_t <= 1`. To prove this, the total product is

\[
P(H_j)=\beta^{2^j-1}c^{-j},
\tag{4.7}
\]

by induction on the concatenation. Prefixes in the left child obey the claim inductively. After that child and its bridge, the product is

\[
P(H_j)\,a c^j=a\beta^{2^j-1}<1,
\]

and multiplication by any right-child prefix again preserves the bound.

Set `v_i=||g_i||^2/P_i^2`. These numbers are nonnegative and nonincreasing. For every physical prefix length `T >= 1`, summation by parts gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i<T}h_i(2-h_i)\|g_i\|^2
={}&(1-P_T^2)v_{T-1}\\
&+\sum_{i=0}^{T-2}(1-P_{i+1}^2)(v_i-v_{i+1})\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.8}
\]

Combining (4.6)--(4.8) yields

\[
\boxed{\|x_T-z_\star\|\le\|x_0-z_\star\|
\quad\text{for every physical prefix and every original }z_\star\in K^\circ.}
\tag{4.9}
\]

This is a distance bound from the original start at each prefix; it does not claim the distance decreases at each individual long step. It rules out reaching a more distant selected-minimizer point in this entire subclass, even if the terminal objective is zero. The same conclusion holds for a compact cone truncation whenever its cap is nonbinding on the complete history; its polar minimizer set is unchanged.

The prior R11 cone result concerned objective decay and the closest polar-minimizer radius. Equation (4.9) addresses every originally selected polar minimizer and the proposed stall defect. It does not exclude a bounded origin-vertex body whose cap becomes active, a nonhomogeneous body, or other smooth-convex functions. No general-history inequality follows from it.
