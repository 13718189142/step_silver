## 2. Algebraic isolation of zero steps

This section supplies the required zero-boundary bookkeeping; it is not
advertised as a new result beyond the earlier round38 necessary conditions.

Put t_i=h_i-1 for i<r and define

 d_i=sum_(j!=i)(mu_ij-mu_ji),
 I_i=sum_(j<i)mu_ji, J_i=sum_(j>i)mu_ji.

Value matching gives d_i=h_i for i<r and d_r=-s. At a nonterminal i,
the coefficient of ||g_i||^2 on the right of (2) is

             -d_i-2I_i-2J_i+2h_i J_i.

Indeed each squared difference contributes its two incident weights;
only Qbar_(j,i) with j>i contributes a trajectory diagonal, with
coefficient 2h_i. Matching V_h therefore gives

                 t_i J_i-I_i=(1-t_i^2)/2.             (3)

If 0<h_i<=1, its left side is nonpositive and its right side is positive.
Thus every positive h_i exceeds1. If h_i=0, equation (3) gives
I_i+J_i=0. Nonnegative entries make the entire incoming mu column zero;
d_i=0 then makes its entire outgoing row zero as well. Hence the entire
original Lambda row AND column vanish at every zero-step index.

List the positive-step indices as iota(0)<...<iota(m-1) and retain the
ORIGINAL terminal index by setting iota(m)=r. Restrict Lambda to these
indices and relabel h_(iota(j)) as the jth positive step. Every removed
step is zero and every incident multiplier was already zero. The
retained principal T is precisely the prefix matrix for this relabeling.
Moreover, if h_i=0, the ith column of original T is the coordinate column
e_i. Since the corresponding column and row of L_flow are zero, both
the ith column and row of the full remainder vanish. Thus (1) restricts
to the retained array and extends back to every independent original
zero-step gradient without imposing a relation among any gradients.

All-zero words have the zero array and no nonempty positive interval.
Otherwise Sections3--7 use this purely algebraic positive relabeling,

For completeness, evaluating (2) on the scalar unit quadratic, with
g_0=1 and g_(i+1)=-t_i g_i, makes every Qbar_ij zero and gives

## 3. Exact mixed matching with every crossing entry

For a<b, one half of the coefficient of <g_a,g_b> on the right of (2) is

 mu_ab+mu_ba-h_a sum_(j<=a)mu_jb+h_b sum_(j>b)mu_ja.

Here h_r is assigned the auxiliary value0 only when b=r. The first two
terms are from the squared differences; the next two are respectively
from trajectory segments ending at b and starting at a. Consequently

 t_a mu_ab=mu_ba-h_a sum_(j<a)mu_jb
                           +h_b sum_(j>b)mu_ja.        (5)

The sum preceding a has a MINUS sign. In particular it cannot be
discarded as an additional nonnegative supply.

One useful cumulative form is obtained by setting

 F_ab=sum_(j<=a)mu_jb, F_(-1)b=0,
 R_ab=sum_(j>=b)mu_ja, R_a,(r+1)=0,   a<b.

Equation (5) becomes

 t_a F_ab+F_(a-1)b=R_ab+t_b R_a,(b+1),                 (6)

where t_r=0 is only an auxiliary terminal placeholder. The original
signs imply F_ab>=F_(a-1)b>=0 and R_ab>=R_a,(b+1)>=0.
They do not give a separable expression for either cumulative array.
No factorization has been imposed in (5)--(6).

## 4. First attempted mechanism: an isolated interval quadratic

Fix ANY nonempty interval I=[l,k), with 0<=l<k<=r. Let

 H=sum_(i=l)^(k-1)h_i,
 v_l=1, v_(i+1)=-t_i v_i for l<=i<k,
 p=v_k, q=|p|=product_(i=l)^(k-1)t_i,
 A_i^out=sum_(j<l)mu_ji,
 B_i^out=sum_(j>=k)mu_ji,       l<=i<k.

These last two quantities are the actual incoming auxiliary weights
from the two exterior sides. Set formal g_i=v_i on I and g_i=0 outside
I, including the terminal, and set every f_i=0. Up to an arbitrary
translation, positions inside are v_i, positions on the left are1,
and positions on the right are p.

For an internal ordered pair, Qbar_ij=-(v_i^2-v_j^2). An incoming edge
from the left to i has residual v_i^2-2v_i; an incoming edge from the
right has residual v_i^2-2p v_i; every outgoing edge from i to the
exterior has residual -v_i^2. All-exterior residuals vanish. Using
d_i=h_i, the right side of (2) is therefore

 -sum_(i in I)h_i v_i^2
           -2sum_(i in I)v_i(A_i^out+p B_i^out).

The left side is sum_(i in I)h_i(1-h_i)v_i^2. Since
h_i(2-h_i)v_i^2=v_i^2-v_(i+1)^2, this gives the exact identity

 sum_(i in I)v_i(A_i^out+p B_i^out)=(p^2-1)/2.         (7)

This is a factor-free interval cut identity. Its exterior terms remain
signed through v_i and p. Nonnegative A_i^out,B_i^out alone give no
upper bound on the alternating weighted sum that would compare q with
1/(1+H). Deleting A_i^out for an interior interval is unjustified.
Thus this first direct attempt stops at the signed boundary moment (7).

## 5. Second mechanism: an exact interval-defect identity

The next test retains the exterior times, rather than replacing the
exterior gradients by zero. Define global prefix sums S_i=sum_(t<i)h_t

Values and positions agree at the two boundaries. This is a FORMAL
polynomial test. It is not claimed to be interpolable by one static
convex smooth function, and is not used as a primal witness. Indeed

All within-region residuals vanish. The complete list of possibly
nonzero residuals is

 Qbar_ji=2(S_j-S_k)p(psi_i-p),   j>=k, i<k.            (9)

The reverse orientation between the left and middle regions, and the
reverse orientation between the middle and right regions, is zero.
For a left/right pair, both expressions in (9) apply to its respective
orientations, so no crossing pair is lost or counted twice.

For example, for i<l and l<=j<k, put d=S_l-S_i. Direct substitution is

For l<=i<k and j>=k, put b=S_j-S_k. Direct substitution gives
Qbar_ij=0 and Qbar_ji=2b p(v_i-p). For a left/right pair the same
substitution gives 2d(1-p) in the first orientation and 2b p(1-p)
in the second. This proves the full list (9).

Define the two ACTUAL weighted boundary sums

            (P+1)^2-(P+H+1)^2 p^2=2(C_L+C_R).         (11)

-s(s+2)p^2+2sR p^2. Their sum is (P+1)^2-(P+H+1)^2p^2.
