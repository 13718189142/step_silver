# Mixed gradient-moment storage: exact transport and local loss

Status: candidate derivation, not verifier-accepted. No solver or new verifier
call is used. This completes the unfinished calculation in the previous round's
local notes. It does not establish the project's unrestricted target.

## Definitions

Normalize smoothness to 1. For a nonnegative GD block h with samples x_i,
g_i=grad f(x_i), f_i=f(x_i), let

    S=sum h_i, A=S+1, q=g_n,
    V_h=2 sum_(i<n) h_i(f_i-f_n)
        +sum_(i<n) h_i(1-h_i)||g_i||^2-S A||q||^2,
    b_h=sum_(i<n) h_i(h_i-2),
    B_h=sum_(i<n) h_i(h_i-2)||g_i||^2,
    P_theta(h)=V_h-theta(B_h-b_h||q||^2).

Here B_h is a trajectory quantity, whereas b_h is a schedule scalar; neither is
the project's benchmark denominator B_n. For actual samples of the same convex
1-smooth function set

    I_ij=f_j-f_i-<g_i,x_j-x_i>-||g_i-g_j||^2/2 >=0.

The proof of this inequality can be given without an external theorem: subtract
the affine function with gradient g_i from f, so x_i is a minimizer of the
resulting convex 1-smooth function. Its one-step descent bound from x_j with
step 1 gives f_j-f_i-<g_i,x_j-x_i> >= ||g_j-g_i||^2/2.

## 1. A complete objective certificate, including the price

Translate a chosen minimizer to zero and its value to zero. Write X=x_n.
Telescoping squared distances and expanding the optimum slacks gives

    V_h=||x_0||^2-||X||^2-2S f_n-S A||q||^2
        -2 sum h_i I_(i,*),
    B_h=||X||^2-||x_0||^2
        +2 sum h_i[I_(i,*)+I_(*,i)].

Thus the following is an EXACT equality:

    (1+theta)||x_0||^2-2(2S+1)f_n
      =P_theta(h)
       +2(1+theta) sum h_i I_(i,*)
       +2theta sum h_i I_(*,i)+2A I_(n,*)
       +(X,q)^T M_theta (X,q),                         (1)

    M_theta = [[1+theta, -A],[-A, A^2-theta b_h]].

For theta>=0 all displayed optimum-edge coefficients are nonnegative. The
matrix is PSD exactly when theta[A^2-(1+theta)b_h]>=0. Therefore, if

    P_theta(h)>=0 on every legal block trajectory,
    theta>=0,  b_h<=A^2/(1+theta),

then W_n(h)<=(1+theta)/(2A-1). In particular the extra factor 1+theta cannot be
omitted. Equation (1) gives the actual full boundary matrix and all new
interpolation multipliers, not only a distance estimate.

Negative theta is not certified by this conversion: its incoming optimum
coefficients are negative. Even the boundary matrix fails for -1<theta<0,
since b_h<=S^2-2S=A^2-4A+3<A^2 and hence
theta[A^2-(1+theta)b_h]<0. This is a restriction on (1), not on every conceivable
conversion of P_theta.

## 2. Generalized physical primitive join with all actual multipliers

Take physical head a of capacity K=1+sum a_i, ending at y with gradient g,
then a bridge of length 1+r, then physical tail b of capacity A=1+sum b_i,
ending at z with gradient q. Tail internal indices i mean all its gradient
query samples before z. Let

    0<=r<A,
    c=(K+r)/(A-r),  k=1+2c,
    Delta=r(K+A+r)-KA,
    J=2c[sum_(i internal to b) b_i(I_(i,y)+I_(i,z))
          +I_(z,y)+r I_(y,z)].

Every multiplier in J is nonnegative. Repeated or coincident samples are
interpreted as the actual sample data; no inherited point or value is reset.
Direct expansion yields

    V_(a,1+r,b)=V_a+k V_b+c Delta(||q||^2-||g||^2)+J.   (2)

For checking (2), subtract V_a+k V_b from the left. Its function part is
2(K+r)(f_y-f_z)-4c sum b_i(f_i-f_z). Its gradient part is

    (K^2-K-r-r^2)||g||^2
    -2c sum b_i(1-b_i)||g_i||^2
    +[-(K+A+r-1)(K+A+r)+k(A-1)A]||q||^2.

Expand J, use x_i=y-(1+r)g-sum_(j<i in b)b_j g_j and
z=y-(1+r)g-sum_(j in b)b_j g_j. The terms containing internal gradient products
cancel because c(A-r)=K+r. The remaining coefficients are precisely the
displayed ones after c Delta(||q||^2-||g||^2) is added.

Moreover

    b_(a,1+r,b)=b_a+b_b+r^2-1,
    B_(a,1+r,b)=B_a+B_b+(r^2-1)||g||^2.

Consequently (2) gives the exact new storage identity

    P_theta(a,1+r,b)=P_theta(a)+k P_(theta/k)(b)+J
       +[c Delta+theta(b_a+r^2-1)](||q||^2-||g||^2).    (3)

The scalar closure equation is therefore

    c Delta+theta(b_a+r^2-1)=0.                       (4)

Inputs require DIFFERENT labels theta and theta/k. Same-label copying is not a
valid consequence of (3). At theta=0, (4) is the usual primitive join, including
its exact Silver scaling. No loss is inserted into that calibration.

## 3. Nontrivial finite calibration and an exact equality test

For two empty inputs K=A=1, equation (4) reduces to

    (1-theta)r^2+2(1+theta)r-(1+theta)=0.

At theta=1 it gives r=1/2, i.e. the step h=3/2, and (3) reads

    P_1(3/2)=6 I_10+3 I_01 >=0.

At theta=0 it gives h=sqrt(2). This exhibits a real capacity gain when theta
is added, but the objective certificate at theta=1 is only 2/4=1/2.

For any block and the legal full-curvature function f(x)=||x||^2/2, define
Pi_h=product_(i<n)(1-h_i). Then

    P_theta(h)=[1+theta-(A^2+theta(1-b_h))Pi_h^2]||x_0||^2. (5)

This follows directly from the two identities in section 1: every optimum
slack vanishes and q=x_n=Pi_h x_0. If a recursively constructed block is built
from empty blocks using only (3)-(4), all interpolation slacks also vanish on
this quadratic, so the bracket in (5) must be exactly zero. It is a useful
hostile equality check for any proposed parameters; no numerical search is
needed.

## 4. The balanced deformation has a well-defined finite first variation

Let lambda=1+sqrt(2), t=sqrt(2)-1=1/lambda. For each fixed m, define a local
family h_m(theta), of length 2^m-1, recursively near theta=0:

    h_0(theta)=(),
    h_(m+1)(theta)=(h_m(theta),1+r,h_m(theta/k)),

where r,k solve (4) and k=1+2(K+r)/(A-r), with

    K=A_m(theta), A=A_m(theta/k),
    A_m(theta)=1+sum h_m(theta).

This is an implicit finite family, not an assertion that a common fixed
positive theta works for every m. Its smooth local existence follows
inductively from the implicit-function theorem. At theta=0, one has

    K=A=lambda^m, r=t lambda^m, c=lambda, k=lambda^2.

The partial derivative of the first equation with respect to r is
2lambda sqrt(2)lambda^m>0. Its derivative with respect to k is zero: the tail
argument theta/k has zero k-derivative at theta=0. The derivative of the second
equation with respect to k is 1 for the same reason. Thus the two-by-two
Jacobian is invertible. All physical steps, k and A-r remain positive in a
small neighborhood for each fixed m. Inductively (3) then supplies a complete
nonnegative interpolation certificate for P_theta. The input labels retain
the sign of theta. For sufficiently small theta>=0, the objective matrix in
(1) is also PSD because b_h<A^2 at theta=0.

Define a_m=A_m'(0) and write b_m=b_(h_m(0)). The undeformed recurrence gives

    b_0=0, b_(m+1)=2b_m+t^2 lambda^(2m)-1,
    b_m=beta lambda^(2m)-gamma 2^m+1,
    beta=(8sqrt(2)-11)/7,
    gamma=(8sqrt(2)-4)/7.

Differentiating the capacity join at theta=0 yields

    a_0=0,
    a_(m+1)=sqrt(2)a_m
       -[b_m+t^2 lambda^(2m)-1]/[2lambda sqrt(2)lambda^m]. (6)

To see every derivative in (6), the standard capacity map
Omega(K,A)=(K+A+sqrt((K+A)^2+4KA))/2 has both first derivatives lambda/2
at K=A. The derivatives of the two child capacities are a_m and
a_m/lambda^2, so their combined contribution is sqrt(2)a_m. The direct
theta-derivative of (4) is the second term of (6); derivatives of c disappear
there because Delta=0.

Put eta=sqrt(2)/lambda=2-sqrt(2), d=beta+t^2=(10-6sqrt(2))/7, and
v_m=a_m/lambda^m. Then exactly

    v_(m+1)=eta v_m+[-d+gamma eta^(2m)]/[2lambda^2 sqrt(2)],
    v_m={-d(1-eta^m)/(1-eta)
           +gamma(eta^m-eta^(2m))/(eta-eta^2)}
          /[2lambda^2 sqrt(2)].                       (7)

In particular

    lim_(m->infty) v_m=-(10-6sqrt(2))/[14lambda sqrt(2)]<0. (8)

The operations here are: differentiate at theta=0 for each finite m, then
take the m-limit of the exact formula (7). No interchange with an unproved
fixed-theta asymptotic limit is being used.

## 5. Every finite balanced first variation loses after objective conversion

Compare the certified bound to its own undeformed primitive bound:

    R_m(theta)=(1+theta)(2lambda^m-1)/(2A_m(theta)-1).

For m>=1,

    R_m'(0)=1-2a_m/(2lambda^m-1).                    (9)

This is strictly positive for every m. An explicit uniform derivative bound
is available. Formula (7) gives v_1=1/(lambda^3 sqrt(2)). For m>=1 its forcing
term is at most the m=1 forcing term, and

    gamma eta^2-d < (1-eta)(gamma-d),

because the difference is gamma(eta^2+eta-1)-d eta<0, with
eta^2+eta-1=7-5sqrt(2)<0. Induction therefore gives v_m<=v_1 for all m>=1.
If v_m is negative, (9) is greater than 1. Otherwise the factor
2lambda^m/(2lambda^m-1) is at most its m=1 value. Hence

    R_m'(0)>=1-2/[lambda^2 sqrt(2)(2lambda-1)]>0.    (10)

Thus for EACH fixed finite balanced horizon, this concrete deformation is
worse for every sufficiently small positive theta. Its large-horizon
derivative has the limit

    lim R_m'(0)=1+(10-6sqrt(2))/[14lambda sqrt(2)]>1. (11)

This closes the proposed infinitesimal balanced improvement in the specific
P_theta storage and conversion (1). It does not rule out finite deformations,
unbalanced trees, another conversion, or more general moment storage. In
particular (10) alone supplies no common neighborhood in theta for all m.

## Literature and scope

The active mechanism was checked against the already downloaded exact text of
Fazlyab et al., arXiv:1705.03615, Theorem 3.1 and its full proof. Its method
requires a proved supply inequality, nonnegative coefficients, and an actual
terminal PSD bound. Equations (1)-(4) give those objects for this narrower
candidate; that paper supplies no completeness theorem or asymptotic
improvement. Matlas returned no relevant long-step composition result on the
new targeted query, and web search provided no additional useful mechanism.
No external theorem is a proof premise of the algebra above.

The practical next question is whether a function-value-coupled moment can be
