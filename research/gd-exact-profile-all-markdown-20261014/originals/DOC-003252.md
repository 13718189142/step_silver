# Unequal quadratic-balanced blocks cannot improve a two-bridge primitive join

Status: self-contained candidate obstruction, not verifier-accepted. This
extends finding2599c72fdf8542a0 from three identical blocks to three independent
blocks. No solver or verifier call is used.

Let a,b,c be finite nonnegative physical GD blocks, with capacities

    A=1+sum a_i, B=1+sum b_i, C=1+sum c_i.

Assume each block is quadratic-balanced in the explicit sense

    |product_i(1-a_i)|=1/A,
    |product_i(1-b_i)|=1/B,
    |product_i(1-c_i)|=1/C.                           (1)

Empty blocks are allowed. For r,s>=0, form

    h=(a,1+r,b,1+s,c), T=1+sum h_i=A+B+C+r+s.

If h is primitive, in the exact sense that the energy

    V_h=2 sum_i h_i(f_i-f_n)+sum_i h_i(1-h_i)||g_i||^2
        -(T-1)T||g_n||^2

is nonnegative for every convex 1-smooth GD trajectory, then

    T <= max{Omega(Omega(A,B),C),Omega(A,Omega(B,C))}, (2)

where Omega(x,y)=(x+y+sqrt((x+y)^2+4xy))/2. This Omega is the exact shifted
primitive recurrence in verified fact b98bb807d514e80d. The obstruction below
does not require the individual blocks to be primitive; only (1) and their
nonnegative physical steps are needed.

## 1. The three exact necessary tests

For a general nonnegative primitive schedule with capacity T, choose a step
1+u, u>0, having prefix sum P and tail sum R. Define

    a_minus=u/(R+1), g(x)=min(1,max(-a_minus,x)),
    f(x)=integral_0^x g(t)dt, x_0=P+1.

The derivative is continuous, monotone, and 1-Lipschitz. The prefix stays on
the positive affine ray and ends at1. The selected step lands at-u. Since
(R+1)a_minus=u and all tail partial sums lie in[0,R], the tail stays on the
negative affine ray and ends at-a_minus. Every sample satisfies

    f_i-g_i x_i+g_i^2/2=0,

and the terminal point is in the quadratic core. Expanding the GD squared
distance telescope gives exactly

    V_h=(P+1)^2-T^2 a_minus^2.

Consequently

    T u <= (P+1)(R+1).                              (3)

At u=0 the same inequality is automatic. Applied to the two bridges, (3)
gives

    T r <= A(B+C+s),  T s <= C(A+B+r).               (4)

On the full-curvature quadratic f(x)=x^2/2, x_0=1, the same expansion gives
V_h=1-T^2 product_i(1-h_i)^2. By (1), this implies

    T r s <= ABC.                                   (5)

All three tests use actual fixed one-dimensional functions.

## 2. Solve the scalar feasible region exactly

Put H=A+B+C and R=T-H=r+s. For fixed T the cut constraints (4) are precisely

    r<=u_A(T)=A(T-A)/(T+A),
    s<=u_C(T)=C(T-C)/(T+C).                          (6)

Let M be the right side of (2), and suppose for contradiction T>M.
Because Omega(B,C)>B+C and Omega(A,B)>A+B, monotonicity of Omega gives

    T>Omega(A,B+C), T>Omega(C,A+B).

These inequalities are equivalent to

    T(T-H)>A(B+C), T(T-H)>C(A+B),

respectively. They imply R>u_A(T) and R>u_C(T). Thus any feasible r must lie
in the nonempty interval

    L=R-u_C(T) <= r <= U=u_A(T),   0<L<=U<R.         (7)

The product r(R-r) is concave in r, so its minimum on (7) is attained at L
or U. At U the first cut is an equality. Writing s_U=R-U, this equality and
the total-capacity equation give

    B+C+s_U=F_A(T),  F_A(T)=T(T-A)/(T+A).

For T>=A this function is strictly increasing: its derivative is
1-2A^2/(T+A)^2>=1/2. If D=Omega(B,C), the defining quadratic equation for
Omega gives F_A(Omega(A,D))=D. Since T>Omega(A,D),

    s_U>D-B-C.

The positive number rho=D-B-C solves rho(B+C+rho)=BC. Therefore

    T U s_U=A(B+C+s_U)s_U>ABC.                     (8)

At L the second cut is an equality. The identical argument with A and C
interchanged gives r_L>Omega(A,B)-A-B and

    T L(R-L)=C(A+B+r_L)r_L>ABC.                    (9)

Concavity now implies T r(R-r)>ABC throughout (7), contradicting (5).
This proves (2), including r=0 or s=0 since under T>M the interval is
strictly inside(0,R).

## 3. Sharpness of the necessary-test bound

For the parenthesization a,(b,c), put

    D=Omega(B,C), s=D-B-C,
    T=Omega(A,D), r=T-A-D.

Both bridges are positive, Tr=A(B+C+s), and Trs=ABC. The other cut also
holds. One direct check is as follows: the binary equations give rT=AD and
sD=BC, so Ts=ABC/r. The required Ts<=C(A+B+r) is equivalent to
AB<=r(A+B+r). Since r(T)=AD and T=A+B+C+r+s, subtracting gives

    r(A+B+r)-AB = AD-r(C+s)-AB
                           = (A-r)(C+s)>0,

since r=AD/T<A. Interchanging A and C proves feasibility for the other
parenthesization as well. Hence M is the exact maximum of the three scalar
tests. If the input blocks themselves are primitive, the usual binary
composition constructs primitive schedules attaining these capacities; the
upper bound (2) needs no such assumption or attainment claim.

The scalar quantity in (2) is exactly the larger capacity of the two binary
parenthesizations. In particular unequal block sizes cannot evade the earlier
equal-copy obstruction while each block retains exact quadratic balance.

## Scope and next opening

This is a primitive-energy obstruction, not a bound on every direct objective
certificate. It also does not apply when any input has
|product(1-h_i)|<1/(1+sum h_i). Then (5) weakens. The next candidate is a
slightly shortened two-step block (u,u), u<3/2, whose exact additional
gradient-variation storage is derived separately. Its loss of quadratic
balance is real and quantitative; it cannot be ignored when testing bridges.
