# Exact hostile test for the reset-doubling family

Status: self-contained unverified counterexample package. No further fact submission under the current allocation. It excludes every seed depth k >= 6 in the stated family, uniformly over subsequent doublings. It does not exclude the small remaining seed depths or all endpoint-potential constructions.

Use the notation of research/max4_reset_seed_boundary_certificate.md. Put lambda=1+sqrt(2), sigma=1/lambda, A=lambda^k, B=beta_k-1, R=A+B+2. Then A=2B^2+B, and the actual seed is [pi^(k),B+1,1]. Let H_j=q^(k,j), with shifted sum R_j=lambda^j R, and H_(j+1)=[H_j,1+sigma R_j,H_j]. Every H_j ends with the same two steps (B+1,1). Its length satisfies len(H_j)+1=2^j(2^k+2).

For k>=6 we have A>=lambda^6=99+70sqrt(2)>197>171, hence B>9. In particular a=B/2>lambda. Define a one-dimensional smooth convex function by
f(x)=x^2/2 for -a<=x<=1;
f(x)=x-1/2 for x>=1;
f(x)=-a x-a^2/2 for x<=-a.
Its derivative clip(x,-a,1) is continuous, monotone, and 1-Lipschitz, and f(0)=0=min f.

Fix j>=0 and start H_(j+1) at x_0=R_j-B-2>0. The prefix of its first H_j before the last two steps has total sum R_j-B-3. All steps are nonnegative, so every sampled point of that prefix stays in the right affine region, and the prefix ends exactly at x=1. The (B+1)-step then reaches -B=-2a. The unit step reaches -a, with terminal gradient -a.

The following central step 1+sigma R_j sends -a to y=sigma R_j a. Since sigma a>1, we have y>R_j. The second H_j has total sum R_j-1. Every one of its iterates therefore stays in the right affine region with gradient1, and its final point is
x_N=R_j(sigma a-1)+1>1.
Consequently the exact normalized objective of this legal instance is

W_N(H_(j+1)) >= [R_j(B/lambda-2)+1]/(R_j-B-2)^2,
N+1=2^(j+1)(2^k+2).

This is a genuine lower bound for the proposed schedules; it is not an upper bound or a negative answer to (P).

Comparison with the exact benchmark, without decimal replacements:
Let P_m=S_circle(m)+1. The original recursion gives P_(2m+1)>=lambda P_m and P_(m+1)>=P_m. Thus P_(2^r-1)>=lambda^r. Since the dominant recurrence permits the split (n-1,0), B_n=2S_bullet(n)+1>=2P_(n-1)-1. Hence B_n>=2lambda^r-1 whenever n>=2^r. Here N>=2^(k+j+1), so B_N>=2lambda^(k+j+1)-1.

The displayed lower bound is strictly larger than (B/lambda-2)/R_j. Multiplication by B_N yields
W_N(H_(j+1))/U_N^ZJ > (A/R)(2B-4lambda) - (B/lambda-2)/(lambda^j R).
For B>9, A=2B^2+B>9(B+2), so A/R>9/10. Also lambda<5/2, hence 2B-4lambda>8. Finally,
(B/lambda-2)/(lambda^j R)<B/A=1/(2B+1)<1/19.
Therefore the ratio is greater than 36/5-1/19=679/95>7 for every k>=6 and j>=0.

The sharper asymptotic lower constant for each fixed seed depth is
liminf_(j->infinity) W_(len H_(j+1))(H_(j+1))/U_(len H_(j+1))^ZJ >= (A/R)(2B-4lambda).
It tends to infinity as k tends to infinity. Large reset seeds cannot be rescued by increasing the number of balanced doublings in this family.

Scope and next obligation:
This exact test does not close k<=5. In particular the previously interesting k=4 seed has B/2<lambda, so the central jump in this witness lands inside the region the second block can traverse. No upper certificate or successful recursion is known for that remaining case. A later candidate must either establish a concrete boundary-variable inequality for it or change the block rule; claiming that a unit step controls terminal gradients is invalid.
