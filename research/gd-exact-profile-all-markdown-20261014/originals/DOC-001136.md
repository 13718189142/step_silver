    v*n<=s*(2C-(C+1)*v), so v<=2C/(C+1)<2.                       (9)

    eps:=k*R-D
       =(1+Hbar)*||X||_M^2
         -2*(k*mu*u*a+qcorr/beta)*<E,X>_M+k*mu*u^2*m

         +c03*Lambda03+c04*Lambda04+c14*Lambda14+c23*Lambda23,      (10)

where

 beta^2*||J||_M^2+2*beta*b*w*<J,B>_M+k*ell*mu^2*Tcut

STRICTLY positive. Here is the full sign and PSD check.

    qmax=beta*u*(sqrt(k*mu*(1+Hbar))-a*k*mu).

Then

    0<=qcorr<qmax<beta*u/(2a).                                  (11)

If qcorr=0 this follows from Hbar>0. Otherwise put

    qcorr=(beta/a)*Vprod*(u*tau^2-a*beta)
                              /(Sden-Vprod+Vprod*tau^2),
    qmax=(beta*u/a)*tau/(1+tau).

The numerator of qcorr is positive in this case. The inequality
qcorr<qmax is equivalent to

    Vprod*(1+tau)*(u*tau-a*beta)<u*tau*Sden.

Its left side is smaller than 2*Vprod*u*tau. Moreover
2*Vprod<=2v<2+b+c+v=Sden, using bc<=1 and v<2. This proves the
strict inequality. The upper bound on qmax follows from
tau/(1+tau)<1/2.

The definition of qcorr gives c04>=0, and c14,c23>0.

    eps=sigma*m+(1+Hbar)*||Y||_M^2+the nonnegative costs in (10).  (12)

## 4. The actual quadratic column and joint first-row budget

Define

    pquad=(1,-a,ab,-abc,abc*(v-1))^T,
    Q=1-C^2*a^2*b^2*c^2*(v-1)^2,
    delta=u^2-2, W0=1+bc*(v-1).

Tp=e and Le=0. Telescoping h_i(2-h_i)p_i^2=p_i^2-p_(i+1)^2 gives

    ||pquad||_M^2=Q>=0,
    2<E,pquad>_M=u*(m-delta)
             +a^2*(beta*Lambda20+(1-bc)*Lambda30+W0*Lambda40).    (13)

For the row formula, let O0=sum_(j>0)Lambda0j and
I0=Lambda20+Lambda30+Lambda40. Then m=1-a^2-aO0 and node0 flow
gives Lambda10=O0+2u-I0. With Te0=u e-a e0 and
Mp=Dmat pquad+T^T L^T pquad/2 one obtains

 pquad^T L e0=uO0+2au-a*(beta*Lambda20+(1-bc)*Lambda30+W0*Lambda40),
 2<E,pquad>_M=2(1-a^2)-a*pquad^T L e0,

which gives (13). Both incoming weights 1-bc and W0 are nonnegative,
and 1-bc<=W0, including v<1.

By (12), eps>=sigma*m+c20*Lambda20+cB*B0. Define

    xi_cap=min(c20/(a^2*beta), cB/(a^2*W0)),

omitting the second term if W0=0. For every real xi with
xi<=xi_cap and xi<sigma/u,

    eps>=xi*u*delta-xi^2*Q/(sigma-xi*u).                         (14)

Subtract xi*u*delta using (13). All remaining multiplier coefficients
are nonnegative: for xi>=0 this is the definition of xi_cap and the
order of the incoming weights; for xi<0 the coefficients increase.
The remaining Gram expression after adding xi^2Q/(sigma-xi*u) is

    ||sqrt(sigma-xi*u)*E+xi*pquad/sqrt(sigma-xi*u)||_M^2>=0.

Thus this is the same ORIGINAL matrix and column, not independent
energy selections. Let Qplus=Q+u^2*delta. Sending xi to -infinity
shows Qplus>=0, and

    Qplus=a^2*(A^2-C^2*b^2*c^2*(v-1)^2).

That prerequisite is the old scalar-return condition and is not
counted as an independent improvement.

For completeness, when Q,Qplus>0 the free maximizing xi is
xi_free=(sigma/u)*(1-sqrt(Q/Qplus)). If xi_free<=xi_cap, (14)
has supremum

    Bcal=(sigma/u^2)*(sqrt(Qplus)-sqrt(Q))^2.

Otherwise its supremum is

    Bcal=xi_cap*u*delta-xi_cap^2*Q/(sigma-xi_cap*u).

The latter case automatically has xi_cap<sigma/u. For Q>0,Qplus=0,
the supremum is sigma*Q/u^2. For Q=0,Qplus>=0 it is
u*delta*min(xi_cap,sigma/u), and it is zero when Q=delta=0.
These statements follow on putting y=sigma-xi*u>0: the objective is

 sigma*delta+2*sigma*Q/u^2-(delta+Q/u^2)*y-Q*sigma^2/(u^2*y).

The earlier first-row budget is also retained, on its stated domain
0<a<1 and a*eta<1, where

    eta=b/(1+w+v), nu=(1+eta)*(1-a*eta)/a,
    Erow=a+3+1/a-(u*delta/a)*eta-C^2*eta^2.

Its exact identity is

 Erow=nu*(m+a*(Lambda20+B0))+(1+eta)^2*M11
                 +(1+eta)*eta*((w+v)*Lambda12+v*Lambda13).        (15)

Here is a direct derivation. For orthonormal physical vectors U,V set
gfirst=-eta*U+sqrt(nu)*V and use saturated values on

 Xphys=(U+u*gfirst,U,-b*U,-(1+v)*eta*U,-eta*U),
 Gphys=(gfirst,U,-eta*U,-eta*U,-eta*U).

The nonzero residuals are Q20=Q30=Q40=-a*nu,
Q12=-(1+eta)*(b-eta), and Q13=-(1+eta)*v*eta. Every other finite
residual is zero. Use ||gfirst||^2=[1+(1-a)*eta]/a and
<U,gfirst>+a||gfirst||^2=1-a*eta to check the target0 residuals.
The energy telescope gives Erow and the two gradient columns modulo
e are sqrt(nu)*E and (1+eta)*e1, proving (15).

In particular, for EVERY lambda>=0, (14) holds with its left side
eps+lambda*Erow, sigma replaced by sigma+lambda*nu, and cap replaced
by

 min((c20+lambda*nu*a)/(a^2*beta),
                         (cB+lambda*nu*a)/(a^2*W0)).              (16)

This follows by adding the TWO exact budgets before using (13), so

It does not replace any of the older2948 conditions. For example,

 u*delta<=Krow*Erow/nu+2*sqrt(Q*Erow/nu).

    b<(a+3)*(C-a-2)/((a+1)*(C+a+2)).

To see the latter equivalence, the direct factorization is

The second factor is positive since C>A and u*A>delta. Substituting
eta=b/(C-A-b) in the positive first factor gives the displayed

## 5. A further matched full-column inequality near equality

In this section v>0 and all constants are the ones already defined.
Let

    dphys=a*b*c*v=(1+v)*z,
    nbar=s*(2C-(C+1)*v)/v>=0,

    Lterm=sqrt((alpha-beta)^2/sigma+1/(1+Hbar)),
    Kterm=(dphys/x)*Lterm*sqrt(nbar), Jfee=a/(2*x),
    Pcost=max(ell*mu*Hpre/cB,ell*mu*beta/c1,c03old/c03,1/k),
    Gcost=max(mu*Lnorm^2,Pcost).

All denominators are strictly positive. If nbar<0, the original
terminal budget (9) already excludes the word; it is never square-rooted.

       +sqrt((sqrt(Q)+Lnorm*y)^2+4*jcoef*(Kterm*y+Jfee*y^2)))
                                                            /(2*jcoef),

            +2*mu*hcoef*Lnorm*tau_max(y)*y+Gcost*y^2.

Here is the full derivation retaining the actual quadratic column.
The vector identity, checked in all five coordinates, is

    pquad=uE-aX-jcoef*B+dphys*e4+ab*e.

The original terminal rows give

    2<E,e4>_M=a*Lambda40-Lambda04,
    2<J,e4>_M=2<E,e4>_M+beta*Lambda40+b*Lambda41-Lambda14.

Since X=Y+alpha E, they imply

 x<B,e4>_M=(alpha-beta)<E,e4>_M+<Y,e4>_M
                              -beta*(beta*Lambda40+b*Lambda41-Lambda14)/2.

Taking the inner product of pquad with B therefore gives

 <B,pquad>_M=(u-a*alpha)<E,B>_M-a<Y,B>_M-jcoef*Tcut
       +(dphys/x)*((alpha-beta)<E,e4>_M+<Y,e4>_M)
       -dphys*beta*(beta*Lambda40+b*Lambda41-Lambda14)/(2*x).       (18)

Let eps_G=sigma*m+(1+Hbar)||Y||_M^2 and let eps_C be the cost sum
in (12). Thus eps=eps_G+eps_C with both nonnegative. Weighted
Cauchy--Schwarz gives

 ||(u-a*alpha)E-aY||_M<=Lnorm*sqrt(eps_G),
 ||(alpha-beta)E+Y||_M<=Lterm*sqrt(eps_G).

Also ||e4||_M^2<=nbar by (9), and
c14=beta*dphys/a. Drop the negative Lambda40,Lambda41 terms in

 jcoef*Tcut<=sqrt(Tcut*Q)
        +(Lnorm*sqrt(Tcut)+Kterm)*sqrt(eps_G)+Jfee*eps_C.          (19)

For the other direction (4) and Avec=(u-a*alpha)E-aY+hcoef*B give

 R<=rcoef*Tcut+2*mu*hcoef*Lnorm*sqrt(Tcut*eps_G)
                                    +mu*Lnorm^2*eps_G+Pcost*eps_C
  <=rcoef*Tcut+2*mu*hcoef*Lnorm*sqrt(Tcut*eps)+Gcost*eps.           (20)

The cost bound follows by comparing each cost in (4) to the cost of
the SAME multiplier in (12); their maximum ratio is precisely Pcost.
Thus costs and energies have not been chosen from different arrays.
Replacing eps_G,eps_C separately by eps in (19) gives, for
tcut=sqrt(Tcut)>=0,

 jcoef*tcut^2-(sqrt(Q)+Lnorm*y)*tcut-(Kterm*y+Jfee*y^2)<=0.

Its nonnegative root is tau_max(y). The right side of (20) increases
with tcut>=0, which proves (17).

This supplies a quantified positive-gap exclusion test. Psi is
continuous and strictly increasing on [0,infinity), tends to infinity,
and

    Psi(0)=rcoef*Q/jcoef^2.

Consequently, if R>rcoef*Q/jcoef^2, there is a unique y0>0 with
Psi(y0)=R, and every full array must have

                           eps>=y0^2>0.                         (21)

Strict increase follows from the explicit nondecreasing positive
coefficients in tau_max and Gcost>0. This is an algebraic definition
and proof; no numerical root finding is used. In particular Q=0,R>0
forces a positive eps, even when delta=0 and the first-row bound

No uniform lower bound as v,ell,R,Q or other factors vanish is claimed.
