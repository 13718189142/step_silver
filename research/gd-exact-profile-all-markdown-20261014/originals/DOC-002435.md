    W_n^simplex(h) = W_n(h).                        (1.1)

## 2. Elementary projection facts and the complete minimum set

For a nonempty compact convex set `D`, the maximizer defining `F_D` is unique:
completing the square identifies it with the unique nearest point `P_D(q)`.
For `v in D`, the exact characterization is

    P_D(q)=v  iff  <q-v,w-v><=0 for every w in D.     (2.1)

Necessity follows by differentiating the squared distance on the segment
from `v` to `w` at zero. For sufficiency, expand

    ||q-w||^2 = ||q-v||^2 + ||w-v||^2
                -2<q-v,w-v> >= ||q-v||^2.

For a finite convex hull it is enough to check every generating point,
because the left side of (2.1) is affine in `w`.

The two normal inequalities for `v=P_D(q)` and `v'=P_D(q')` imply

    <q-q',v-v'> >= ||v-v'||^2.

Thus the projection is 1-Lipschitz. Evaluating the affine maxima at each
other's optimizers gives

    0 <= F_D(q')-F_D(q)-<P_D(q),q'-q>
      <= <P_D(q')-P_D(q),q'-q> <= ||q'-q||^2.

Consequently `F_D` is differentiable with gradient `P_D` and is globally
1-smooth. It is convex as a maximum of affine functions of `q` and finite
because `D` is compact.

When `0 in D`, the minimum is zero and its COMPLETE minimum set is the cone

    C_D = { a : <a,v><=0 for every v in D }.          (2.2)

Membership makes every defining expression nonpositive, while `v=0` gives
zero. Conversely, if `<a,v>>0` for some `v in D`, then `theta v in D` for
`0<=theta<=1`, and

    <a,theta v>-||theta v||^2/2 > 0

for sufficiently small positive `theta`. Thus `a` is not a minimizer.
The polar here always means the cone with threshold ZERO, not a body polar
with threshold one. It is nonempty, closed, and convex, so every point has
a closest point in it. Existence follows, for example, by restricting a
distance-minimizing sequence to a bounded ball and taking a convergent
subsequence.

## 3. Strict smoothness supplies the necessary strict rows

Let `0<ell<1`, let `f` be convex differentiable and ell-smooth, and translate
and subtract its chosen optimum so that `f(0)=min f=0`. Use its ACTUAL GD
samples `i=0,...,n`, including the terminal sample, with

    x_{i+1}=x_i-h_i g_i,
    g_i=grad f(x_i),  f_i=f(x_i).

Add a reference label `*` with `x_*=g_*=f_*=0`. Define

    Q_ji = f_j-f_i+<g_j,x_i-x_j>+||g_j-g_i||^2/2,
    gamma = (1-ell)/(2ell)>0.

The ell-smooth interpolation necessity is

    Q_ji <= -gamma ||g_j-g_i||^2.                   (3.1)

This is the smoothness-scaled necessity part of accepted local fact
`1252b8823bb5a290`; none of its duality or attainment conclusions is used.
One can also derive the precise orientation directly: the convex function
`k(x)=f(x)-<g_j,x>` has a minimum at `x_j`, and an ell-smooth descent step
from `x_i` of length `1/ell` gives

    k(x_j) <= k(x_i-(g_i-g_j)/ell)
             <= k(x_i)-||g_i-g_j||^2/(2ell).

Rearrange and add the `||g_i-g_j||^2/2` term to obtain (3.1).

Put

    eta_i = <x_i,g_i>-f_i-||g_i||^2/2,
    H_i = sum_{t<i} h_t eta_t,
    eta_* = 0.

The optimum row `Q_{i,*}=-eta_i` gives

    eta_i >= gamma ||g_i||^2 >= 0,                 (3.2)

and `Q_{*,i}=-f_i+||g_i||^2/2` gives

    f_i-||g_i||^2/2 >= gamma ||g_i||^2.             (3.3)

In particular every nonzero gradient has STRICTLY positive `eta_i`.
If `g_i=0`, convexity makes `x_i` a minimizer, so `f_i=eta_i=0`.
All later gradients and positions then remain zero-gradient/stationary.

If `g_i=g_j=g`, the two elementary convexity inequalities force

    f_i-f_j=<g,x_i-x_j>.

Therefore `eta_i=eta_j`, and direct substitution gives `Q_ji=0`.
The same conclusion applies to equal zero gradients and to the reference
label. Distinct positions with the same gradient need not be identified as
positions; only their gradient/height vertex labels will be identified.

## 4. Mechanism 1: exact sparse normal-guard construction

For `R>0`, define

    v_i=(g_i,eta_i/R),  v_*=0,
    D_R=conv{0,v_0,...,v_n},
    z_i=(x_i,-R-H_i/R),
    a_R=(0,-R).

This is a compact finite polytope in one extra coordinate, with at most
`n+2` generating points. The point `a_R` belongs to its full minimum cone
because `<a_R,v_i>=-eta_i<=0`. For `z_0=(x_0,-R)`,

    ||z_0-a_R||=||x_0||.                           (4.1)

The claimed identity is exact. For every physical sample `i` and every
`j in {*,0,...,n}`,

    <z_i-v_i,v_j-v_i>
      = <x_i-g_i,g_j-g_i>-(eta_j-eta_i)
        -(H_i+eta_i)(eta_j-eta_i)/R^2
      = Q_ji-(H_i+eta_i)(eta_j-eta_i)/R^2.          (4.2)

For clarity, the middle terms expand to

    <x_i-g_i,g_j-g_i>-(eta_j-eta_i)
      = f_j-f_i+<g_j,x_i-x_j>+||g_j-g_i||^2/2.

This checks both the orientation `Q_ji` and the sign of the vertical
correction. No position associated with the reference `*` appears in the
normal test other than through the zero jet in `Q_*i`.

If `g_i=g_j`, both terms in (4.2) vanish. Otherwise `-Q_ji>0` by (3.1).
Define the data-dependent finite number

    A = max_{i=0,...,n; j in {*,0,...,n}; g_i!=g_j}
          (H_i+eta_i)(eta_i-eta_j)_+ / (-Q_ji),      (4.3)

with maximum over an empty set equal to zero. Finiteness follows from
finitely many finite data and strictly positive denominators. Here
`H_i+eta_i>=0` uses the nonnegative steps.

For `R^2>=A`, all expressions in (4.2) are nonpositive. By (2.1),

    P_{D_R}(z_i)=v_i,  for EVERY physical i=0,...,n. (4.4)

The prospective positions satisfy

    z_{i+1}=z_i-h_i v_i,                            (4.5)

because `H_{i+1}=H_i+h_i eta_i`. Equations (4.4)--(4.5), by induction from
the stated initial point, prove that these are the ACTUAL ordinary GD
iterates on the single fixed function `F_{D_R}`. All original horizontal
positions are reproduced exactly. The added coordinate moves by
`-h_i eta_i/R`; it is not held fixed.

The value at every actual sample is also exact:

    F_{D_R}(z_i)
      = <z_i,v_i>-||v_i||^2/2
      = f_i-C_i/R^2,
    C_i = eta_i H_i+eta_i^2/2 >= 0.                (4.6)

The optimum/reference row is included in the guard; it is not left to a
heuristic value estimate. If `g_i!=0`, (4.4) already implies positive value
via `F_{D_R}(z_i)>=||v_i||^2/2>0`. Taking `R` larger also makes the explicit
correction in (4.6) as small as desired.

This completes Mechanism 1 with no gap found. If only `R^2=A` is used, some
distinct-vertex rows may be equalities; that boundary is insufficient for
the generic perturbation proposal. The next section deliberately chooses
strict quantitative slack instead.

## 5. Quantitative strict margins, including zero-gradient samples

Suppose at least one sampled gradient is nonzero. List the distinct nonzero
gradient values as `g^1,...,g^m`, with their well-defined heights
`eta^1,...,eta^m`. Write

    g^0=0,  eta^0=0,
    a(i) in {0,...,m},
    g_i=g^{a(i)},  eta_i=eta^{a(i)}.

The equality of heights proved above makes this label map unambiguous,
including repeated gradients at different physical times. Let

    delta = min_{0<=a<b<=m} ||g^a-g^b|| > 0.        (5.1)

Choose

    R^2 >= max(1,2A).                              (5.2)

For a physical `i` and a target label `b`, define

    N_{ib}=<z_i-v^{a(i)},v^b-v^{a(i)}>,
    v^b=(g^b,eta^b/R).

When `a(i)!=b`, choose any original representative of `b` (or the reference
label for zero) in (4.2). The possible positive correction is at most
`A(-Q_ji)/R^2<=(-Q_ji)/2`. Thus

    N_{ib} <= Q_ji/2
            <= -(gamma/2)||g_i-g^b||^2
            <= -gamma delta^2/2.                  (5.3)

For equal labels it is identically zero. This retains every PHYSICAL
sample row, even when one vertex label occurs repeatedly; those samples
have different `H_i` and must not be silently collapsed as time indices.

If `g_i=0` and `b!=0`, the exact formula is even more transparent:

    N_{ib}=Q_{ji}-H_i eta^b/R^2<0.

So zero-gradient samples supply strict inequalities against every nonzero
vertex. Their equality against zero itself is a harmless identical-vertex
row, not a missing strictness condition.

The strict margins also show that each distinct nonzero `v^b` is an exposed
vertex of `D_R`: at an occurrence of label `b`, the vector `z_i-v^b`
strictly exposes it among all other generating points. Zero is exposed by
`a_R`, since all `eta^b>0`. Thus, under (5.2), the sparse body already has
exactly `m+1` vertices, although they need not be affinely independent.

## 6. Mechanism 2: an explicit simplex perturbation with exact dynamics

Append `m` new mutually orthogonal coordinates. Let `e_1,...,e_m` be their
standard basis and set `e_0=0`. For a number `epsilon>0`, define

    w^b=(v^b,epsilon e_b),  0<=b<=m,
    D_{R,epsilon}=conv{0,w^1,...,w^m}.

In particular `w^0=0`; a zero-gradient vertex is NOT given a new nonzero
component. Every occurrence of the same nonzero gradient receives exactly
the same component. Define cumulative chronological vectors

    b_i=sum_{t<i} h_t e_{a(t)},
    Z_i=(z_i,-epsilon b_i),
    A_R=(a_R,0).

The INITIAL appended coordinates are zero. Their subsequent values above
are recomputed from all the actual preceding steps, rather than assumed to
stay zero. By construction,

    Z_0=(x_0,-R,0),
    Z_{i+1}=Z_i-h_i w^{a(i)}.                       (6.1)

The normal condition has an explicit perturbation identity. With
`a=a(i)`, for every target label `b`,

    <Z_i-w^a,w^b-w^a>
       = N_{ib}
         -epsilon^2 <b_i+e_a,e_b-e_a>.             (6.2)

There is no term linear in `epsilon`, because old and new coordinate blocks
are orthogonal and the new initial block is zero.

Let `b_{i,a}=<b_i,e_a>` for `a>0`, and take `b_{i,0}=0`. All these numbers
are nonnegative and at most `S`. The coefficient of `epsilon^2` in (6.2)
is exactly

    0                         if a=b;
    b_{i,a}-b_{i,b}+1          if a,b>0 and a!=b;
    b_{i,a}+1                  if a>0 and b=0;
    -b_{i,b}                   if a=0 and b>0.

Hence it is at most `S+1`. Equal-label rows remain exactly zero for every
physical occurrence. At zero-gradient samples the perturbation only makes
the distinct-vertex normal inequality more negative.

Choose, in addition to (5.2),

    0<epsilon^2 <= gamma delta^2/[4(S+1)].          (6.3)

Combining (5.3) and (6.2) gives, for all different labels,

    <Z_i-w^{a(i)},w^b-w^{a(i)}>
         <= -gamma delta^2/4 < 0.                 (6.4)

The identical-label rows are zero. Thus every generator satisfies the normal
condition, and (2.1) proves

    P_{D_{R,epsilon}}(Z_i)=w^{a(i)}

at every physical index, including the terminal. Along with (6.1), this
proves that `Z_i` is the ACTUAL GD run of `F_{D_{R,epsilon}}` under the same
word. This is a quantitative proof of the proposal, not an appeal to
genericity or continuity at a supposedly strict equality.

The vectors `w^1,...,w^m` are linearly independent: projection of a linear
relation to the new coordinate block gives `epsilon` times every coefficient
equal to zero. Therefore this body is a genuine `m`-simplex in its span,
with exactly `m+1` vertices including zero.

The nearby minimum remains exact:

    <A_R,w^b>=-eta^b<0 for b>0,
    ||Z_0-A_R||=||x_0||.                           (6.5)

Thus the whole minimum cone has not been replaced by the origin, and no
large artificial coordinate is paid for in the initial distance.

Finally, every perturbed value has the explicit formula

    F_{D_{R,epsilon}}(Z_i)
      = f_i-C_i/R^2-epsilon^2 beta_i,
    beta_i=<b_i,e_{a(i)}>+||e_{a(i)}||^2/2.         (6.6)

If `a(i)=0`, both `C_i` and `beta_i` are zero and the value is exactly zero.
Otherwise

    beta_i=b_{i,a(i)}+1/2,
    0<beta_i<=S+1/2.

This proves value continuity quantitatively while preserving the original
strict-run horizontal trajectory exactly before compression.

For any desired strict-run terminal value loss `tau>0`, the simultaneous
explicit choices

    R^2 >= max(1,2A,2C_n/tau),
    0<epsilon^2 <= min{
        gamma delta^2/[4(S+1)],
        tau/[2(S+1/2)]
    }                                              (6.7)

make the total loss in (6.6) at most `tau`, while all different-label normal
guards remain strictly negative. Every bound is finite and strictly
positive where a positive perturbation is needed. No uniform lower bound
on `delta`, or uniform upper bound on `A`, is assumed.

## 7. Compression to exactly m dimensions and the own closest radius

The construction so far lives in the original space plus `1+m` coordinates.
Let

    E=span{w^1,...,w^m},  dim E=m,

and let `P_E` be orthogonal projection onto `E`. Since the entire body lies
in `E`, for every `Z` and every `w in D_{R,epsilon}`,

    <Z,w>=<P_E Z,w>,
    ||Z-w||^2=||P_E Z-w||^2+||Z-P_E Z||^2.

Consequently both the value and the selected projection gradient are
preserved:

    F_D(Z)=F_D(P_E Z),
    P_D(Z)=P_D(P_E Z).

Put `q_i=P_E Z_i` and `a=P_E A_R`. Then

    q_{i+1}=q_i-h_i w^{a(i)},
    P_D(q_i)=w^{a(i)},
    F_D(q_i)=F_D(Z_i),
    <a,w^b>=-eta^b<=0.

Thus the compressed points are an actual GD run of the same simplex
function regarded as a function on the `m`-dimensional Euclidean space
`E`; choosing an orthonormal basis identifies it with `R^m`. The point `a`
is a true minimizer, and

    ||q_0-a||=||P_E(Z_0-A_R)||<=||x_0||.            (7.1)

This handles the selected radius. The OWN closest radius is preserved
exactly under the compression as well. Writing `C_E` for the polar cone
inside `E`, the ambient minimum set is

    C_D = C_E + E^perp.

Orthogonal decomposition therefore gives

    dist(Z_0,C_D)=dist(P_E Z_0,C_E).                 (7.2)

If the terminal value is positive, neither starting point is a minimizer:
a zero gradient would leave every following iterate fixed with zero value.
The shared radius in (7.2), denoted `rho`, is then positive, and (7.1) gives

    0<rho<=||x_0||.

For `r=||x_0||>0`, if `tau<f_n`, (6.7) consequently yields

    2 F_D(q_n)/rho^2 >= 2(f_n-tau)/r^2.             (7.3)

The positivity of the lower numerator is explicit; no inequality with a
negative numerator is reversed by changing the denominator. Neither the
selected compressed radius nor the closest radius is asserted to equal the
original `r`. The inequality is sufficient.

The number of distinct sampled positions is at most `p+1`, because a zero
step leaves the position unchanged and each positive step can add at most
one position. Therefore

    m <= p+1,
    number of simplex vertices = m+1 <= p+2.

This count includes the terminal gradient. Repeated gradients can reduce
`m` further, even when their positions differ. It is not legitimate to
remove a distinct terminal gradient merely because it is unqueried by the
last update; it remains part of this value/normal construction.

## 8. Regularization of an arbitrary 1-smooth original run

Return now to an arbitrary convex differentiable 1-smooth `f`, with chosen
minimum zero at zero and initial radius `r>0`. Its original samples are
`x_i,g_i,f_i`. For `0<c<1`, define `f_c=c f` and run the SAME word on it,
starting from the same `x_0`. Denote the new actual positions by `x_i^c`.
Its smoothness bound is `ell=c<1`, and its minimum set is unchanged.

Set

    M=max_{0<=i<=n}||g_i||,
    Pi_i=product_{t<i}(1+h_t),
    B_i=(1-c)M(Pi_i-1).

Subtract the two actual updates and use 1-Lipschitz continuity of `grad f`:

    ||x_{i+1}^c-x_{i+1}||
      <= (1+c h_i)||x_i^c-x_i||+(1-c)h_i||g_i||
      <= (1+h_i)||x_i^c-x_i||+(1-c)h_i M.

Induction gives

    ||x_i^c-x_i||<=B_i.                             (8.1)

Convexity and smoothness at the ORIGINAL terminal point imply

    |f(x_n^c)-f_n|<=||g_n||B_n+B_n^2/2.

Therefore the terminal error after scaling the objective is explicitly

    |f_c(x_n^c)-f_n| <= e_c,
    e_c=(1-c)f_n+c||g_n||B_n+c B_n^2/2 -> 0.        (8.2)

For example, with `K=f_n+M^2(Pi_n-1)+M^2(Pi_n-1)^2/2`, one has
`e_c<=(1-c)K`. For a positive original terminal value, `K>0`, so a finite
choice of `c<1` sufficiently close to one is immediate from this bound.

The exact horizontal reproduction in Sections 4--6 is of the ACTUAL
`f_c` run. There is no assertion that this regularized run is horizontally
identical to the original `f` run.

Given any positive original score and `eta>0`, choose

    0<d<min(f_n,eta r^2/2).

First choose `c<1` so that `e_c<d/2`; the new strict-run terminal value is
positive. Then choose a strict-run loss `0<tau<d/2`, and choose `R,epsilon`
by (6.7) for THAT strict run. Its total value loss relative to the original
is less than `d`, so the resulting simplex run has positive terminal value
and own-closest score

    2 F_D(q_n)/rho^2
      >= 2(f_n-d)/r^2
      > 2f_n/r^2-eta.                              (8.3)

This sequence of choices is important: `c` first, then its finite actual
transcript, then `R`, then a positive `epsilon`, then span compression.
The parameters may depend on the instance, the entire fixed word, and the
tolerance. The number of distinct gradient labels may also depend on `c`,
but it always satisfies the same bound `m<=p+1`.

## 9. Equality of suprema, scale L, and boundary audit

Every simplex function in (1.1) is a legal convex differentiable 1-smooth
function with a minimizer, by Section 2. Thus

    W_n^simplex(h)<=W_n(h).

For every original positive-score instance and every tolerance, (8.3)
produces an admissible simplex instance with no more than `p+2` vertices
and dimension no more than `p+1`. Taking suprema gives the reverse
inequality and proves the candidate identity (1.1).

Original zero-score runs do not require a ratio with zero closest radius.
The restricted class is nonempty and has nonnegative scores. It even has
a positive score for every fixed word: in dimension one take `D=[0,1]`
and start at `q_0=1+S`. Every iterate stays at least one, all projection
gradients equal one, and the terminal value is `1/2`, with closest radius
`1+S`. Its score is `1/(1+S)^2>0`.

For arbitrary `L>0`, replace the original function by

    tilde f(x)=[f(x_*+x)-f(x_*)]/L.

The normalized step is `h_i`, exactly corresponding to the original
`h_i/L` step. Multiply the final simplex function by `L`. Its gradient
is `L P_D`, its algorithm again uses the same word, and its normalized
score is still `2F_D(q_n)/rho^2`.

The complete boundary audit is as follows.

1. **Empty word and all-zero words.** Here `p=0`, positions do not move,
   there is at most one nonzero gradient label, and `H_i=b_i=0`. A positive
   score uses a one-dimensional segment simplex. Equations (4.6) and (6.6)
   reduce respectively to losses `eta_i^2/(2R^2)` and `epsilon^2/2`.
   The regularization error (8.2) still applies with `Pi_n=1`.
2. **Zero steps among positive steps.** Duplicate positions and gradients
   use the same label. Both cumulative vectors simply omit their zero
   contributions, and no strictness between identical vertices is required.
3. **Repeated nonzero gradients at different times.** Convexity forces
   equal heights. Use one vertex and one appended coordinate for the whole
   label, but retain every physical normal row and its actual `H_i,b_i`.
   Equation (6.2) is identically zero for a same-label comparison regardless
   of accumulated time. Different-label comparisons have the explicit bound.
4. **Zero-gradient samples.** Their function values and heights are zero,
   and their vertex remains exactly zero. Against nonzero vertices their
   old rows are strict and their new correction is nonpositive. From such
   a sample onward the original and lifted runs are stationary. If the
   terminal score is positive, no zero-gradient sample can have occurred.
5. **All gradients zero.** The run has score zero. The exact sparse lift
   is the point body `{0}`. A zero-dimensional simplex convention can
   describe it, but no own-closest quotient is formed and this case is
   unnecessary for the positive-score supremum argument.
6. **Origin/optimum rows.** The reference zero vertex is included in `A`
   and in every perturbed normal test. Its perturbation coefficient at a
   nonzero sample is `b_{i,a}+1`, the potentially positive term controlled
   by `S+1`. It is not dropped. The chosen nearby minimizer is checked
   separately by its negative inner products with all nonzero vertices.
7. **Degenerate sparse bodies.** Degeneracy before the orthogonal
   perturbation causes no issue. Positive `epsilon` makes all nonzero
   vertices independent. Compression then gives precisely their span,
   without a full-dimensionality assumption on the original gradient data.
8. **Huge finite steps and small strict margins.** `A`, `S`, and `Pi_n` may
   be huge, while `gamma` and `delta` may be tiny. For a fixed finite run
   they remain finite, with `gamma,delta>0`, so (6.7) permits a finite `R`
   and a strictly positive `epsilon`. No word-uniform conditioning estimate
   is asserted, and no limit is interchanged with a supremum or infimum.
9. **Closest-minimum distance.** Positivity is obtained before dividing;
   compression preserves the own closest radius exactly by (7.2), while
   the explicitly chosen minimum gives its upper bound by the original
   radius. No claim of convergence of these radii is needed.

The scope remains nonnegative finite steps and finite horizons. Negative
steps are not covered by the signs used in `H_i`, `b_i`, and the estimates.

## 10. What this does and does not change

The first mechanism improves the earlier general-polytope representation
