# Global minimizers and a stationary energy identity for full PEP certificates

Status: exploratory, unverified proof package. No extra verifier submission is authorized. The selected initial submission was used for fact dfde2fa326c908bf. This document does not establish the exact-profile target.

## 1. Claim under investigation

For each fixed horizon n>=1, the project minimization over h in [0,infinity)^n has a global minimizer, and every global minimizer has strictly positive steps. At any such minimizer there exist a full interpolation upper certificate (q,lambda), a legal primal Gram matrix G and values f_i, attaining q=W_n^*, for which

    <g_t,z_t>=0, 0<=t<n,                                      (S)
    q=(1/2) sum_{i,j} lambda_ij ||g_i-g_j||^2.                 (E)

Here the Euclidean vectors realize G, x_*=g_*=0, ||x_0||=1, and

    z_t=sum_{i>t}(mu_i+sum_{j<=t}lambda_ij)g_i
        -sum_{i<=t}(sum_{j>t}lambda_ij)g_i,                    (Z)
    mu_i=lambda_{i,*}.

Every unstarred sum ranges over 0,...,n; directed pairs are distinct. The assertion retains the full certificate PSD constraint and the full primal interpolation constraints. It does not claim that these displayed identities alone characterize global minimizers.

The proof below supplies the compactness, zero-step exclusion, strong-duality and multiplier-regularity arguments rather than assuming interiority or nonsingular multipliers.

## 2. Fixed-horizon compactness and positive optimal steps

Normalize L=1, x_*=0 and f_*=0. For any fixed finite h, the estimates ||g_i||<=||x_i|| and ||x_{i+1}||<= (1+h_i)||x_i|| imply W_n(h)<=prod_i(1+h_i)^2. Every fixed legal function and initial point has an error continuous in h, by continuity of its gradient. A supremum of continuous functions is lower semicontinuous, so W_n is lower semicontinuous.

Write Poly_n(z)=prod_{t<n}(1-h_t z). For n+1 distinct nodes a_k=k/(n+1), 1<=k<=n+1, the quadratic f(x)=a_k x^2/2 from x_0=1 gives

    a_k Poly_n(a_k)^2 <= W_n(h).

Define the Lagrange polynomials L_k(z)=prod_{l!=k}(z-a_l)/(a_k-a_l). On the sublevel W_n(h)<=1, interpolation of a polynomial of degree at most n gives

    sum_t h_t = -Poly_n'(0)
      <= sum_k |L_k'(0)|/sqrt(a_k) =: C_n < infinity.

Nonnegativity of the steps therefore bounds each h_t by C_n. The sublevel is closed, bounded, and nonempty because h=0 has W_n=1. Lower semicontinuity on this compact set proves attainment of the global infimum.

For positivity, first note the explicit Huber lower bound. Put S=sum h_t and delta=1/(2S+1). The function f(x)=x^2/2 for |x|<=delta, and delta|x|-delta^2/2 otherwise, is convex and 1-smooth. From x_0=1, every iterate is x_i=1-delta sum_{t<i}h_t>=delta; its terminal error is 2f(x_n)=1/(2S+1)>0.

Suppose a globally minimizing length-n schedule contains a zero. Delete that zero to obtain h' of length n-1 with exactly the same terminal point and worst-case value q>0. Set B=prod_{t<n-1}(1+h'_t), so every normalized legal instance has terminal radius at most B. If its terminal function gap is F, convexity gives F<=B||g||, and a final unit step gives

    F_new <= F-||g||^2/2 <= F-F^2/(2B^2).

We have 0<=F<=q/2<=B^2/2. The displayed scalar function is increasing on this interval. Thus appending a unit step to h' produces another length-n schedule satisfying

    W_n(h',1) <= q-q^2/(4B^2) < q,

contradicting global minimality. Therefore every minimizing step is positive. This argument also handles the all-zero proposed minimizer and uses no differentiability of W_n.

## 3. Exact finite-dimensional primal formulation

Let I={*,0,...,n}. For i!=j define

    Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2.

The primal variables are the Gram matrix G of (x_0,g_0,...,g_n), with G>=0 and G_00<=1, together with the real values f_0,...,f_n. The remaining positions are defined by x_i=x_0-sum_{t<i}h_t g_t, and the optimum sample is zero. Impose Q_ij<=0 for every ordered pair. Maximize 2f_n. Necessity of the interpolation inequalities follows by applying a unit gradient step to the convex 1-smooth function f(x)-<g_i,x>, whose minimum is attained at x_i: its decrease bound from x_j gives f_j-f_i-<g_i,x_j-x_i>>=||g_j-g_i||^2/2.

For completeness, the interpolation sufficiency has the following explicit proof. Given such finite vector/value data, put a_i=<g_i,x_i>-f_i and

    H(v)=max_i {a_i+<x_i,v-g_i>+||v-g_i||^2/2}.

At v=g_i, the i-th expression is a_i and every j-th expression is at most a_i, because that comparison is exactly -Q_ij>=0. Hence H(g_i)=a_i and x_i belongs to the convex subdifferential of H at g_i. The function H is finite and 1-strongly convex, with quadratic growth. Its conjugate f=H^* is finite everywhere, convex, differentiable and 1-smooth, and satisfies f(x_i)=f_i and grad f(x_i)=g_i. Indeed, strong convexity makes the maximizing vector in the conjugate unique; the strong monotonicity of the subgradient relation bounds the distance between two such maximizing vectors by the distance between their arguments. This gives the claimed gradient Lipschitz constant. The zero sample makes zero a minimizer. Thus the primal optimum is exactly W_n(h), with dimension at most n+2.

The feasible primal set is compact: the radius/gradient estimates above bound all Gram entries; the two optimum interpolation inequalities imply 0<=f_i<=||x_i||^2/2. It is closed and nonempty, so its optimum is attained.

For positive h, the primal admits strict interpolation inequalities and G positive definite. Take n+2 distinct numbers theta_k in (0,1), the quadratic with diagonal Hessian diag(theta_k), and an initial vector having every coordinate nonzero and norm less than one. The columns x_0,g_0,...,g_n are evaluations of 1,zPoly_0(z),...,zPoly_n(z) at these nodes, times nonzero coordinate factors. Their distinct degrees and nonzero leading coefficients make this matrix invertible. No two sampled positions agree, because their different prefix polynomials cannot agree at n+2 distinct nodes. None equals zero for the same degree reason. Direct substitution gives

    Q_ij=-(1/2)sum_k theta_k(1-theta_k)(x_{i,k}-x_{j,k})^2 <0

for every distinct pair, including *. The norm inequality is also strict.

Source context: Taylor, Hendrickx and Glineur, arXiv:1502.05666, Theorem 4 and Theorem 6 treat smooth-convex interpolation and strong duality for fixed-step methods using the latest gradient with nonzero coefficient. Their proof uses a full-rank quadratic transcript. The preceding Vandermonde construction and the separation argument below provide the needed hypotheses and conclusion directly here; no general-step extension is presumed.

## 4. Full dual attainment by finite-dimensional separation

Let T_ij(G) denote the gradient/position part of Q_ij, so Q_ij=f_i-f_j+T_ij(G). Consider the open convex set of triples (a,b,c) for which some G>=0 and some f satisfy

    a_ij>Q_ij(f,G), b>G_00-1, c<2f_n.

The point (0,0,q), where q is the finite primal optimum, is outside this set. A separating hyperplane, using the independent upward variations of a,b and downward variation of c, has nonnegative coefficients lambda_ij, q_0, alpha, not all zero, and yields

    sum lambda_ij Q_ij(f,G)+q_0(G_00-1)-alpha*2f_n >= -alpha*q

for all G>=0 and all f. The strict primal point constructed above rules out alpha=0: substituting it would make the left side strictly negative whenever lambda or q_0 is nonzero. Thus normalize alpha=1.

The arbitrary f coordinates force

    out_i-in_i=2*1_{i=n}.

The arbitrary PSD matrix G then forces

    M(q_0,lambda,h)=q_0 E_00+sum lambda_ij T_ij >=0.

Taking G=f=0 gives q_0<=q. Conversely the flow and PSD constraints give an upper certificate W_n(h)<=q_0. Therefore q_0=q and the full dual is attained.

This argument establishes the direction needed here: at a minimizing positive schedule there is a certificate with q exactly equal to its true worst-case value. A feasible upper certificate by itself is never interpreted as a lower bound.

## 5. A strictly feasible certificate at every finite h

A regularity direction is needed because the optimal certificate can have many zero multipliers and singular PSD slack. We now construct a certificate with all directed multipliers positive and M positive definite at the same fixed h.

For each finite i, adding equal weight to lambda_{i,*} and lambda_{*,i} preserves the flow. Their quadratic sum is

    Q_{i,*}+Q_{*,i}=||g_i||^2-<g_i,x_i>.

Let H_max=max_t h_t, r=1/(2H_max+3), and w_i=r^(2i)>0. The gradient block A_w of the weighted sum of these cycles has diagonal w_i and, for i>t, off-diagonal w_i h_t/2. With D=diag(w_i), the matrix D^(-1/2) A_w D^(-1/2) has diagonal one and off-diagonal h_t r^(i-t)/2. Each row's absolute off-diagonal sum is at most H_max*r/(1-r)<1/2. Hence A_w is positive definite, as follows also by bounding each cross term with the two corresponding squared coordinates.

Start with lambda_{n,*}=2, and add weight one to every directed finite-to-finite pair. Those paired edges are flow-balanced. Next add K w_i to each of lambda_{i,*} and lambda_{*,i}. All directed multipliers are now positive and the required flow holds. For sufficiently large finite K, their gradient block A is positive definite, because K A_w dominates the fixed remaining symmetric matrix. The full matrix has form

    M=[[q,-mu^T/2],[-mu/2,A]],  mu_i=lambda_{i,*}.

Choosing q>mu^T A^(-1)mu/4 makes M positive definite. This constructs strict feasibility with no assumptions on which multipliers of the optimum vanish.

## 6. KKT without assumed nondegeneracy

Take a global minimizing positive schedule h and an attained certificate (q,lambda). It is also a global minimizer of q over the joint variables (q,lambda,h), subject to flow, lambda>=0 and M>=0: every such certificate upper-bounds a true W_n(h)>=W_n^*.

Here is the finite-dimensional separation argument giving the needed multipliers. Restrict directions d to the linear flow-null space. Let J be the set of zero lambda entries, and let K_0=ker M. If a direction satisfies

    dq<0, d lambda_e>0 for e in J,
    (DM[d]) restricted to K_0 is positive definite,

then a sufficiently small positive step along it is feasible and improves q. To justify the matrix assertion, decompose into K_0 and its orthogonal complement. The complementary block of M is positive definite, the kernel block has a positive first-order term, and cross blocks are first order. The Schur complement subtracts only a second-order term. The dependence on h and lambda is polynomial, so its Taylor remainder is also second order. Positivity of h persists for sufficiently small steps. Such a descent direction is therefore impossible.

Separating the image of the direction space from the open cone defined by these three strict inequalities gives an objective multiplier alpha>=0, numbers r_e>=0 supported on J, and a PSD matrix G supported on K_0, not all zero, annihilating every flow-null direction:

    alpha*dq - sum r_e*d lambda_e - <G,DM[d]> =0.

Move from the optimal certificate toward the strictly feasible certificate from section 5 while holding h fixed. That direction has every d lambda_e>0 on J and has positive compression of DM to K_0. Substituting it rules out alpha=0, unless r=G=0, which would contradict nontrivial separation. Normalize alpha=1.

Extending the annihilator by flow multipliers written as -f_i gives the coefficient identities

    G_00=1,
    f_i-f_j+T_ij(G)=-r_ij<=0,
    lambda_ij r_ij=0,
    M G=0,
    <G,partial M/partial h_t>=0 for every t.

The PSD matrix G is consequently a legal normalized primal Gram matrix by section 3. The interpolation inequalities and lambda complementarity imply sum lambda_ij Q_ij=0. Taking the trace of M G then gives q=2f_n. Thus G realizes an actual worst-case transcript. Degenerate lambda and singular M were allowed throughout.

If either J or K_0 is empty, omit its corresponding strict-cone factor and multiplier. The same argument applies in these zero-dimensional cases.

## 7. The resulting quantitative identities

Let x_0,g_0,...,g_n be Euclidean vectors with Gram matrix G. Expanding the derivative with respect to h_t gives exactly

    partial/partial h_t [sum lambda_ij T_ij]
      = <g_t,
          sum_{i>t}(mu_i+sum_{j<=t}lambda_ij)g_i
          -sum_{i<=t}(sum_{j>t}lambda_ij)g_i >.

The stationary equations are therefore (S) with z_t defined in (Z), including every edge that crosses the chronological cut t.

The x_0 row of M G=0 and G_00=1 give

    sum_i mu_i <x_0,g_i>=2q.                                  (X)

The full quadratic trace can be expanded as

    0=<M,G>
     =q-sum_i mu_i<x_0,g_i>
       +sum_{t<n}h_t<g_t,z_t>
       +(1/2)sum_{i,j}lambda_ij||g_i-g_j||^2.

Using (S) and (X) yields the announced energy identity (E). The energy is nonnegative, and all chronological cut terms have been justified individually rather than discarded.

These equations constrain both a full feasible dual certificate and a full feasible primal transcript at an actual global minimizer. They do not supply a uniform numerical comparison with the Zhang--Jiang recurrence yet.

## 8. Exact one-step primal-dual check

For n=1, h_0=3/2, q=1/4, take

    lambda_{0,*}=lambda_{1,*}=lambda_{1,0}=1,

with all other multipliers zero. Direct expansion yields

    M = v v^T, v=(1/2,-1,-1).

A matching stationary primal Gram matrix is

    G=[[1,1/3,1/6],
       [1/3,1/6,0],
       [1/6,0,1/12]],
    (f_0,f_1)=(1/4,1/8).

It is the convex combination with weights 8/9 and 1/9 of two legal one-dimensional transcripts, each starting at x_0=1:

* Huber threshold 1/4: (g_0,g_1)=(1/4,1/4), (f_0,f_1)=(7/32,1/8).
* Quadratic f(x)=x^2/2: (g_0,g_1)=(1,-1/2), (f_0,f_1)=(1/2,1/8).

The direct sum after coordinate scaling by the square roots of the weights is itself one legal two-dimensional instance, so the combination is not merely a formal averaging device. In detail, for weights a,b and scalar functions H,Q, use F(u,v)=a H(u/sqrt(a))+b Q(v/sqrt(b)) and initial point (sqrt(a),sqrt(b)); this preserves smoothness one and gives exactly the displayed Gram matrix and values.

The full matrix equality M G=0 holds. The cut vector is z_0=2g_1 and <g_0,g_1>=0, verifying stationarity. The directed energy is

    (1/2)(||g_0||^2+||g_1||^2+||g_1-g_0||^2)
      =1/6+1/12=1/4=q.

Global one-step optimality follows directly: the Huber lower bound is at least 1/4 for h_0<=3/2, and the quadratic lower bound (h_0-1)^2 is at least 1/4 for h_0>=3/2. The displayed certificate attains 1/4.

A hostile check is useful: the pure Huber transcript is worst-case for this very same optimal schedule and dual certificate, but has <g_0,g_1>=1/16 and fails stationarity. Thus it is invalid to assume that an arbitrary active worst-case function supplies the stationary primal matrix. The multiplier-separation construction selects a suitable primal matrix, and an admissible direct sum may be essential.

## 9. Exact remaining obstruction

The current identities do not force q to equal the sum-based Huber level, do not show lambda_{*,i}=0 at a global minimizer, and do not imply that all temporal gradients are orthogonal. Each such additional simplification would require its own proof or counterexample test.

A route to the target would still need a quantitative estimate combining the full PSD slack, all interpolation inequalities, the cut equations (S), and the energy (E), uniformly in horizon, whose constant matches the exact recurrence. Merely defining that missing estimate is not a result. The package above establishes a candidate rigorous interface at genuine global minimizers, with exact feasible-data checks, and records the current obstruction.
