# Generalized primitive seed and symmetric-doubling obstruction

Status: unverified candidate. Max4's initial-wave fact-submit allowance has been used on fact 0be8b54e0a33381c. Do not use this package as a fact without verification.

Candidate exact rational seed and obstruction for a weakened primitive potential. The one-step schedule h=[3/2] satisfies the generalized primitive certificate V_h(3/2)>=0 defined below, and its coefficient 3/2 is sharp for that schedule. It has a larger sum than the saturated primitive one-step seed [sqrt(2)], but a positive potential deficit. Under the explicit symmetric generalized-ConPP rule below, every seed with any fixed positive deficit has total stepsize sum Theta(n), and its true objective worst case is Theta(1/n). Thus this particular attempt to amplify an improved-sum seed loses the silver exponent; it cannot disprove (P). This is an unverified candidate package, reserved for main-agent prioritization because max4 has used the initial-wave verification allowance.

Work at L=1; replacing f by f/L restores arbitrary L without changing normalized steps. For any finite nonnegative schedule h of length n, let s=sum_i h_i and let x_{i+1}=x_i-h_i g_i, g_i=grad f(x_i), f_i=f(x_i). Define
V_h(C)=sum_{i=0}^{n-1} h_i[2(f_i-f_n)+||g_i||^2+2<g_i,x_0-x_i>]-||x_n-x_0||^2-C||g_n||^2.
The certificate V_h(C)>=0 is required for every finite-dimensional convex 1-smooth f and every initial point. Here C>=0. Define Q_{i,j}=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2.

Preliminary interpolation inequality. For completeness Q_{i,j}<=0 follows without an external theorem: apply the descent inequality F(u-grad F(u))<=F(u)-||grad F(u)||^2/2 to F(z)=f(z)-<g_i,z>. Convexity gives that x_i minimizes F, because grad F(x_i)=0. Hence F(x_j)-F(x_i)>=||g_j-g_i||^2/2, which is precisely -Q_{i,j}>=0. The descent inequality itself follows by integrating the gradient along a segment and using 1-Lipschitz continuity. Summing Q_{i,j}<=0 and Q_{j,i}<=0 also gives the usual cocoercivity inequality.

1. Explicit rational seed.
For h=[3/2], direct substitution x_1=x_0-(3/2)g_0 gives
V_h(3/2)=3(f_0-f_1)-(3/4)||g_0||^2-(3/2)||g_1||^2
=4(-Q_{1,0})+(-Q_{0,1})+||g_1+g_0/2||^2>=0.
All conic multipliers are positive rational numbers and the remaining term is an explicit squared norm. On f(z)=z^2/2, x_0=1, x_1=-1/2, equality holds. Increasing C would decrease V by a positive multiple of ||g_1||^2, so C=3/2 is the maximum valid coefficient for this seed.
Its sum s=3/2 exceeds sqrt(2), whereas its deficit from the saturated primitive coefficient is d=s(s+1)-C=9/4>0.

2. Exact generalized composition.
Suppose a and b have sums x and y and certificates V_a(C_a)>=0 and V_b(C_b)>=0, with C_a,C_b>=0. Choose 1<=alpha<y+2 and form c=[a,alpha,b]. Let p be the endpoint index of a and q the endpoint of c; b starts at p+1, with x_{p+1}=x_p-alpha g_p. Define
d0=y+2-alpha,
A0=y+3alpha-2alpha^2,
B0=2C_b+y+alpha.
For b, evaluated with initial point x_{p+1}, the following exact identity holds:
V_b(C_b)-sum_{i=p+1}^{q-1}b_{i-p-1}(Q_{i,p}+Q_{i,q})-[(alpha-1)Q_{p,q}+Q_{q,p}]
=d0(f_p-f_q)-A0||g_p||^2/2-B0||g_q||^2/2.
The left side is nonnegative: V_b>=0, all Q<=0, b_i>=0 and alpha-1>=0. This identity can be checked by setting w=x_{p+1}-x_q=sum b_i g_i: all terms involving internal function values cancel, the w cross terms cancel between the two groups of interpolation inequalities, and the remaining gradient coefficients are the ones displayed.
Consequently
2(f_p-f_q)>=(A0/d0)||g_p||^2+(B0/d0)||g_q||^2.
Direct expansion of V_c gives
V_c(C_c)=V_a(C_a)+V_b(C_b)+2(x+alpha)(f_p-f_q)
+(C_a+alpha-alpha^2)||g_p||^2+(C_b-C_c)||g_q||^2.
Therefore the sufficient conditions
C_a>=alpha^2-alpha-(x+alpha)A0/d0,
C_c=C_b+(x+alpha)B0/d0
yield V_c(C_c)>=0. These conditions provide a constructive exact upper-bound certificate using only the prior two certificates and nonnegative interpolation multipliers.

Write P=alpha^2+(x+y)alpha-(xy+2x+2y+2).
For the equality choice in the first sufficient condition, define d_a=x(x+1)-C_a and d_b=y(y+1)-C_b. Algebra gives
d_a=-(x+alpha)P/(y+2-alpha),
d_c=(x+alpha+y)(x+alpha+y+1)-C_c
=d_a+[(2x+y+alpha+2)/(y+2-alpha)]d_b.
The deficit upper ceiling d>=0 for any valid certificate follows by evaluating V_h(C) on a Huber function with all iterates in its affine region: then all gradients equal a nonzero vector and V_h(C)=[s(s+1)-C]||g||^2. This verifies the role of the Huber-saturated value s(s+1).

3. Symmetric recursive construction and exact dynamics.
Start with any seed h^(0) of length n_0 and sum s_0>0 satisfying V_{h^(0)}(C_0)>=0, 0<=C_0<s_0(s_0+1). In particular the rational seed above qualifies.
At level j put r_j=s_j+1 and
e_j=[s_j(s_j+1)-C_j]/r_j^2,
p_j=1-e_j=(C_j+r_j)/r_j^2.
The initial assumptions give 0<e_0<1 and 0<p_0<1. Let t_j be the unique number in (0,sqrt(2)-1) satisfying
p_j=t_j^2(3+t_j)/(1-t_j).
The right side is continuous and strictly increasing for 0<t<sqrt(2)-1, starts at zero, and equals one at the upper endpoint.
Choose alpha_j=1+r_j t_j and set
h^(j+1)=[h^(j),alpha_j,h^(j)].
Use the equality case of part 2 to define C_{j+1}. The relation defining t_j is exactly the first equality condition: with x=y=s_j and alpha=1+r_j t_j,
P=r_j^2(t_j^2+2t_j-1), x+alpha=r_j(1+t_j), y+2-alpha=r_j(1-t_j),
and hence the required normalized deficit is
e_j=(1+t_j)(1-2t_j-t_j^2)/(1-t_j)
=1-t_j^2(3+t_j)/(1-t_j).
All bridge steps exceed one and remain below y+2. The formula C_{j+1}=C_j+(s_j+alpha_j)(2C_j+s_j+alpha_j)/(s_j+2-alpha_j) is nonnegative. The certificate is consequently valid at every level by part 2. No state other than the current GD iterate is used; the whole finite schedule is fixed in advance.

Lengths and shifted sums obey the exact identities
n_j+1=(n_0+1)2^j,
r_{j+1}=(2+t_j)r_j.
The deficit identity in part 2 gives
e_{j+1}=4e_j/[(1-t_j)(2+t_j)^2].
Equivalently,
p_{j+1}=p_j R(t_j), where R(t)=(3+t)/[(1-t)(2+t)^2].
To check the second identity, use p_j=t_j^2(3+t_j)/(1-t_j) and expand the first identity.

For 0<t<sqrt(2)-1 one has
(1-t)(2+t)^2-(3+t)=1-t-3t^2-t^3
=(1-t)[1-t^2(3+t)/(1-t)]>0,
so 0<R(t)<1. Thus p_{j+1}<p_j and hence t_{j+1}<t_j. Furthermore
d(log R(t))/dt=1/(3+t)+1/(1-t)-2/(2+t)
=2(t^2+4t+1)/[(3+t)(1-t)(2+t)]>0.
Let kappa=R(t_0)<1. Then p_j<=p_0 kappa^j. Since t_j^2(3+t_j)/(1-t_j)>=3t_j^2,
t_j<=sqrt(p_0/3) kappa^(j/2).
Consequently the sum of the t_j is at most sqrt(p_0/3)/(1-sqrt(kappa)), and
r_0 2^j<=r_j
=r_0 2^j product_{i=0}^{j-1}(1+t_i/2)
<=r_0 2^j exp(sqrt(p_0/3)/(2(1-sqrt(kappa)))).
Thus s_j=Theta(n_j), with constants depending only on the fixed seed. This establishes exact failure of silver-exponent amplification for every positive-deficit seed under this specified rule.

4. True objective behavior and comparison with the actual benchmark.
The Huber construction proved in fact_id 0be8b54e0a33381c, proof part 1, applies to every nonnegative schedule and gives W_{n_j}(h^(j))>=1/(2s_j+1). Thus the schedules have a lower bound of order 1/n_j.
They also have an upper bound of order 1/n_j from their certificate. Indeed let beta_j=(1+sqrt(1+4C_j))/2. Cancelling the x_star terms in V_h(C)>=0 gives
||x_0-x_star||^2/2+sum_i h_i Q_{i,star}-s(f_n-f_star)
>=||x_n-x_star||^2/2+C||g_n||^2/2.
Add beta Q_{n,star}-beta(f_n-f_star) and use beta^2=C+beta. The right side becomes ||x_n-x_star-beta g_n||^2/2. Since all Q_{i,star}<=0 and all their coefficients are nonnegative,
W_n(h)<=1/(s+beta).
As beta_j>=1, the upper and lower bounds together give W_{n_j}(h^(j))=Theta(1/n_j).

For an explicit comparison with the exact DP benchmark, set lambda=1+sqrt(2) and rho=log_2(lambda). The primitive DP in fact 0be8b54e0a33381c has shifted join
F(A,B)=(A+B+sqrt(A^2+6AB+B^2))/2, so F(A,A)=lambda A. Its primitive sums are nondecreasing because appending the empty primitive block adds phi(x,0)>=1. Hence S_circle(2^k-1)>=lambda^k-1 by induction. For any n>=1 choose k with 2^k<=n<2^{k+1}. The dominant recurrence at split n-1, with empty terminal block, gives S_bullet(n)>=S_circle(n-1). It follows that
B_n=2S_bullet(n)+1>=2lambda^k-1>=2 n^rho/lambda-1.
Therefore the ratio W_{n_j}(h^(j))/U_{n_j}^ZJ grows at least as a positive constant times n_j^(rho-1) and tends to infinity. This is a failure of the specified scalar-potential amplification route, not a universal lower bound and not an impossibility statement about other richer potentials.
