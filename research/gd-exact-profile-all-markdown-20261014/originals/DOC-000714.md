# A legal multi-cut construction with exact remote-history transfer

Status: verifier-accepted as fact 1fca3e5dd555cb4a. Worker: max3.
The fact statement is the verified construction. This readable research copy
expands two residual compact products from the submitted proof: s_j m_j^2
means s_j m_j^2, and q z_term means q z_term.

This is an actual one-dimensional convex 1-smooth function construction,
not a free choice of curvature at each iterate and not a PEP reformulation.
It keeps remote ray offsets and function-value areas. It gives a lower-bound
functional stronger than the verified single-cut functional K_n, but no
uniform comparison to U_n^ZJ has been proved.

## Inputs, feasibility, and quantitative output

Take any finite nonnegative schedule and select k checkpoint steps. In
chronological order write the schedule as

\[
(\block_0,b_0,\mathcal{L}_1,b_1,\ldots,b_{k-1},\mathcal{L}_k),
\]

where each block_i is a possibly empty list of nonnegative ordinary steps,
s_i is its sum, and b_i is a nonnegative checkpoint step. This preserves
the individual steps and their order; their sums suffice only because the
constructed derivative will be constant during each ordinary block.

For k>=1 define nonterminal gradient magnitudes m_0,...,m_{k-1} and
nonnegative offset sums O_0,...,O_k as follows:

\[
m_0=\frac1{1+s_0},\qquad O_0=0,
\]

and, for i=0,...,k-2,

\[
m_{i+1}=\frac{(b_i-1)m_i-O_i}{1+s_{i+1}},\qquad
O_{i+1}=O_i+s_i m_i.
\tag{1}
\]

Also set O_k=O_{k-1}+s_{k-1}m_{k-1}; its last value is only bookkeeping.
Require

\[
m_i>0\quad(0\leq i<k),\qquad
m_i\geq m_{i-2}\quad(2\leq i<k).
\tag{2}
\]

Define the terminal distance budget and earlier terminal-ray slope by

\[
z_term=(b_{k-1}-1)m_{k-1}-O_{k-1},\qquad
m_{\rm old}=\begin{cases}0,&k=1,\\m_{k-2},&k\geq2.\end{cases}
\]

The terminal feasibility requirement is

\[
z_term\geq(1+s_k)m_{\rm old}.
\tag{3}
\]

Choose any final slope magnitude

\[
q\in\left[m_{\rm old},\frac{z_term}{1+s_k}\right].
\tag{4}
\]

Put e_term=sum of s_i m_i^2 over 0<=i<k with i congruent to k modulo2.
There is a convex differentiable 1-smooth f:R->R with minimizer0 and
f(0)=0 such that GD from x_0=1 with the exact supplied schedule has

\[
2f(x_n)=2 e_term+2q z_term-(2s_k+1)q^2.
\tag{5}
\]

Thus this expression is a legal lower bound on W_n(h). Scaling f by L
preserves the normalized statement at any L>0. The best q within (4) is

\[
q_{\rm opt}=\max\left\{m_{\rm old},\frac{z_term}{2s_k+1}\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]

Indeed the quadratic in (5) is concave, its unconstrained maximum is at
z_term/(2s_k+1), and this point is no larger than the upper endpoint in (4).

For k=0 take z_term=1, e_term=0, m_old=0 and s_k=s_0. Equations (4)--(6) then
give the usual exact Huber value 1/(1+2s_0). For k=1 and b_0>=1, the
terminal old slope is0, and (5)--(6) give exactly
(b_0-1)^2/[(1+s_0)^2(1+2s_1)]. Therefore the maximum over all feasible
selected chains includes every term of K_n in fact b98bb807d514e80d.

## Explicit construction on the two rays

Write a signed point as x=(-1)^i r at stage i, where r>=0 is its distance
from0. On each ray construct a radial derivative magnitude Q_+(r) or Q_-(r),
starting at Q_+(0)=Q_-(0)=0. Let A_+ and A_- denote the largest distances
previously visited on those rays, and let w_+,w_- be their derivative
magnitudes. Initially all four numbers are0.

At a nonterminal stage i the starting distance is a_i. On its active ray
write A_old,w_old for the previous record and slope and d_i=A_old-w_old.
Choose the magnitude m_i and extend that ray's derivative by a slope-1 ramp
from (A_old,w_old) to

\[
(r_i,m_i),\qquad r_i=A_{\rm old}+m_i-w_{\rm old}=d_i+m_i,
\]

followed by a constant segment of value m_i up to a_i. Conditions (2)
give m_i>=w_old; for the first visit to a ray its old slope is0. The
recurrence will give

\[
a_i=d_i+(1+s_i)m_i=r_i+s_i m_i,
\tag{7}
\]

so a_i>=r_i>=A_old. This extension does not change any previously visited
data. During block_i every iterate remains on this flat segment, since its
partial step sums lie between0 and s_i. Thus the stage ends at signed
distance r_i with gradient sign (-1)^i and magnitude m_i.

At the checkpoint b_i the next signed point has opposite sign and distance

\[
a_{i+1}=b_i m_i-r_i=(b_i-1)m_i-d_i.
\tag{8}
\]

The previously unvisited amount will be nonnegative by the next-stage
feasibility condition. The active ray's new outer record is a_i and its
slope there is m_i. Hence its offset on its next visit is

\[
d_{i+2}=a_i-m_i=d_i+s_i m_i.
\tag{9}
\]

Initially d_0=d_1=0. Write O_i=d_i+d_{i+1}. Equations (8)--(9) imply
O_{i+1}=O_i+s_i m_i and
a_{i+1}-d_{i+1}=(b_i-1)m_i-O_i. Equating this last quantity with
(1+s_{i+1})m_{i+1} gives exactly (1). Also a_0=1 and d_0=0 imply the
stated m_0, completing the induction that verifies (7) for each nonterminal
stage. Positive m_i means nonterminal stages do not reach0. The terminal
case z_term=0 is permitted only with zero old slope and causes no difficulty.

At terminal stage k, the starting distance satisfies a_k-d_k=z_term. Extend
its ray by a slope-1 ramp from its old record to slope q and then by a flat
segment to a_k. Condition (4) makes the ramp endpoint d_k+q no smaller
than the old record and no larger than a_k-s_k q. Thus every final-block
iterate stays on its flat segment. The final distance is a_k-s_k q.

For k=0 the same construction begins directly from the initial origin datum,
with a_0=1 and q given by (4).

After the last stage extend each radial derivative constantly to infinity.
Define the global derivative by f'(x)=Q_+(x) for x>=0 and
f'(x)=-Q_-(-x) for x<=0, and define f(x)=integral_0^x f'(t)dt.
Each radial derivative is continuous, nondecreasing and 1-Lipschitz, with
Q_+(0)=Q_-(0)=0. Across0, for x<=0<=y,

\[
0\leq f'(y)-f'(x)=Q_+(y)+Q_-(-x)\leq y-x.
\]

Consequently f' is globally continuous, nondecreasing and 1-Lipschitz.
This proves differentiability, convexity, 1-smoothness, and a minimizer at0.
The earlier stage calculations prove the exact original GD recursion.
In particular no interpolation or dimensional-realizability gap remains
within the candidate construction.

## Retained function values give the exact objective

On one ray, a ramp from magnitude w to v adds area (v^2-w^2)/2. Its
following nonterminal flat segment has length s_i m_i and adds s_i m_i^2.
Therefore at the outer record after any nonterminal stage i, the radial
function value equals

\[
m_i^2/2+\sum_{j\leq i,\ j\equiv i\ ({\rm mod}\ 2)}s_j m_j^2.
\tag{10}
\]

This identity follows by induction on successive visits to that ray: the
squared-gradient contributions telescope and the flat-segment areas remain.
At the final stage the new ramp endpoint has value e_term+q^2/2. The distance
from that endpoint to the final point is z_term-(s_k+1)q, on which the derivative
equals q. The final value is consequently

\[
e_term+q^2/2+q[z_term-(s_k+1)q]
=e_term+q z_term-(s_k+1/2)q^2,
\]

proving (5). The initial distance is exactly1, so the normalization is exact.

## Exact two-cut illustration

For h=(1,5,1,9,1) select checkpoint steps5 and9. Then s_0=s_1=s_2=1,
m_0=1/2, O_0=0, m_1=1, O_1=1/2, z_term=15/2, m_old=1/2,
e_term=1/4, and q_opt=5/2. All feasibility conditions hold. Formula (5) gives

\[
W_5(1,5,1,9,1)\geq2(1/4)+(15/2)^2/3=77/4.
\]

The actual points are 1,1/2,-2,-1,8,11/2. On the positive ray the
derivative is r on [0,1/2], 1/2 on [1/2,1], r-1/2 on [1,3], and5/2
on [3,infinity). On the negative ray its magnitude is min(r,1).
Direct integration gives f(11/2)=77/8, consistent with the formula.
By contrast the exact K_5 value is max{1/35,4/23,1/3}=1/3.

## Interface still missing for the project target

The construction works for each chain satisfying (2)--(4), and these
conditions can be checked from the selected block sums and step values.
It does not establish the existence of a sufficiently strong feasible chain
for every schedule. The assertion that maximizing (5) over feasible chains
always gives (1-o(1))U_n^ZJ would be a new theorem-strength obligation.
No such assertion is assumed. The terms -O_i and e_term are genuine retained
history contributions; discarding them changes the construction and is not
a permitted constant-level approximation without a separate proof.
