## 9. Exact geometric interface needed to use the conditional gain

The following is a conditional implication, not an additional scaling
regime or a claim that the necessary geometry exists. It specifies
which effective costs a future extension must actually prove.

Fix tau>0. Suppose a future construction supplies costs, as m grows,

 epsilon_m=E/m, E>0,

                  log T_m<=alpha*m+O(log m).         (9.1)

Define the SAME increasing-record pattern by the exact rule

 R_m=max{A_m/epsilon_m,T_m-1},
 J_m=max{1,ceil(log(R_m/a)/log c)}.                   (9.2)

If necessary one may include1 in the maximum without changing any
asymptotic assertion. Here a,c are still the exact enclosing-word
parameters, not geometric costs. The bounds(9.1) imply
J_m/m->theta=alpha/log lambda in(0,1), and every selected physical
step meets b_j>=T_m and epsilon_m*b_j>A_m. For the original frozen
threshold, T_m=2(1+epsilon_m^(-2)), this is precisely(1.5).

The REQUIRED geometric theorem must supply, simultaneously:

 (G1) ONE finite-dimensional convex differentiable L-smooth function
      f_m with an attained minimum, fixed before the run; an initial
      point x_0 outside its minimum set; and its actual GD trajectory using EVERY original
      physical step of H_(m,tau), in order, including its actual
      terminal point. Old and future components must remain part of
      that same function. All original directed interpolation rows,
      including the minimum and terminal rows, must hold.

 (G2) Positive scales D_rc_0,...,D_rc_k on that very trajectory, with
      transfers for the exact incoming sums in(2.2) bounded below by

      D_rc_(i+1)>=D_rc_i*(b_i-1)
          /{[chi_m*s_i+max(epsilon_m*b_i,A_m)]*eta_(m,i)},

      eta_(m,i)>=1, sum_(i=0)^(k-1)log eta_(m,i)->ell<infinity. (9.3)

      Thus any norm, gluing, or overlap losses must be included in
      the EFFECTIVE chi_m,epsilon_m,A_m or the SUMMED eta losses.
      Separate local trajectories without their global realization
      do not satisfy this requirement.

 (G3) An actual terminal value bound with the original smoothness L,

      2*(f_m(x_n)-min f_m)>=L*D_rc_k^2/(1+2*S_(m-1)).    (9.4)

 (G4) The TRUE squared initial distance satisfies

      dist(x_0,argmin f_m)^2<=r_rc_m*D_rc_0^2,
                       r_rc_m->r_rc in(0,infinity).         (9.5)

      A declared unit input coordinate alone does not establish(9.5).
      An exhibited minimizer with this distance bound suffices;
      otherwise the actual closest-minimizer argument is required.

These hypotheses are enough: multiply the ACTUAL inequalities(9.3),
use(9.4),(9.5), and normalize by L and the true radius. Then

 W_n(H_(m,tau)) >= F5/[r_rc_m*product_i eta_(m,i)^2].     (9.6)

Here W_n is the original supremum of
2(f(x_n)-min f)/(L dist(x_0,argmin f)^2), equivalently allowing any
minimizer and taking the closest for this instance. A function with
Lipschitz gradient at most L has the required L-smoothness. If the
construction changes its smoothness normalization, that change must
already be reflected in (G1)--(G3), not dropped after this step.

The proof of Sections2--6 applies to these costs as well. To see that
