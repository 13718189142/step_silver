# A quantitative obstruction to chronological-window gluing

Status: complete explicit candidate proof; not yet verifier-accepted.
Project: gd-exact-profile-20260926. Worker: max3.

## Exact scope

An exact conditional completion state for smooth-convex GD histories cannot
consist only of the initial jet, minimizer, prescribed schedule, every past point
and gradient, and any fixed-length chronological window of function values.
There are two legal histories with identical such retained data but conditional
terminal objective maxima differing by a factor of 16. The missing information
in this example is exactly one old function value.

This does not exclude other finite-dimensional state representations, including
states that encode the missing interpolation constraint. It does not determine
the unrestricted worst case of the displayed schedule, nor the minimax target.

## Explicit functions and legal histories

Normalize L=1, x_star=0, and f_star=0. Define

\[
F(x)=\begin{cases}
x^2/2,&x\leq0,\\
0,&0\leq x\leq1,\\
(x-1)^2/2,&1\leq x\leq3,\\
2x-4,&x\geq3.
\end{cases}
\]

Let G(x)=integral from 0 to x of q(t) dt, where

\[
q(t)=\begin{cases}
t,&t\leq1/2,\\
1/2,&1/2\leq t\leq11/6,\\
t-4/3,&11/6\leq t\leq10/3,\\
2,&t\geq10/3.
\end{cases}
\]

The derivatives of F and G are continuous and nondecreasing and have slopes
only 0 or 1. They are globally 1-Lipschitz. Both functions are differentiable,
convex, and have a minimizer at 0 with value 0. The functions coincide on the
negative half-line.

At 4, both functions have value 4 and derivative 2. For G, direct integration
gives G(11/6)=19/24, G(10/3)=19/24+15/8=8/3, and G(4)=4.
At p=3/2 their common derivative is 1/2, but

\[
F(p)=1/8,\qquad G(p)=5/8.
\]

Fix an integer m>=1. With x_0=4 use the predetermined schedule

\[
h_0=5/4,\quad h_1=5,\quad
h_{2+j}=1/2\quad(0\leq j<m),\quad
h_{m+2}=1+(5/4)2^m.
\]

All m+3 steps are strictly positive and finite. Under either function the
prefix iterates and derivatives coincide:

\[
x_1=4-(5/4)2=3/2,
\quad x_2=3/2-5(1/2)=-1,
\quad x_{2+j}=-2^{-j}\quad(0\leq j\leq m).
\]

For the negative tail, the derivative equals the coordinate, so each step
1/2 halves the coordinate. At the cut k=m+2 the common point and derivative
are -2^{-m}. The last step takes both trajectories to

\[
y=-2^{-m}+[1+(5/4)2^m]2^{-m}=5/4.
\]

All sampled prefix function values coincide except the value at x_1=p.
For any prescribed finite chronological window choose m long enough that p
lies outside it. This gives identical retained data even if every sampled
point and gradient, the initial jet, minimizer, radius, and full schedule are
also retained. No repeated points or zero steps are needed.

## Exact conditional terminal maxima

A completion of a prefix means any differentiable convex function on R with
1-Lipschitz derivative that interpolates every point, value and derivative in
that prefix and has f(0)=0 and f'(0)=0. Its final query point is y=5/4 because
the cut data and last step are fixed.

For an F-prefix completion f, smoothness gives

\[
f'(t)\geq f'(p)-(p-t)=t-1\qquad(y\leq t\leq p).
\]

Integrating gives

\[
f(y)=f(p)-\int_y^p f'(t)\,dt
\leq\frac18-\int_{5/4}^{3/2}(t-1)\,dt
=\frac18-\frac3{32}=\frac1{32}.
\]

F attains this bound, hence the conditional maximum is 1/32.

For a G-prefix completion f, smoothness from 0 and monotonicity up to p give

\[
f'(t)\leq\min\{t,1/2\}\qquad(0\leq t\leq p).
\]

The integral of the right side equals 1/8+1/2=5/8, exactly f(p)-f(0).
The continuous nonnegative difference between this upper envelope and f'
therefore has integral zero and is identically zero. Consequently

\[
f(y)=\int_0^{5/4}\min\{t,1/2\}\,dt
=\frac18+\frac12\left(\frac54-\frac12\right)=\frac12.
\]

G is a completion, so the second conditional maximum is exactly 1/2.
Their ratio is 16. Since |x_0-x_star|^2=16, the normalized conditional
objective values 2(f(y)-f_star)/|x_0-x_star|^2 are 1/256 and 1/16.

## Consequence for a split rule

Take the full F prefix and the G continuation. The continuation is legal and
agrees with every retained datum listed above, but no single legal function can
realize their union: the first conditional bound forbids the continuation's
value 1/2 at y. Thus matching those retained data is not a lossless gluing
criterion. Any proposed exact state compression must retain the old value's
constraint, or prove an equivalent replacement. This construction does not
preclude a different compressed state or an argument at the unconditional
maximized value level.
