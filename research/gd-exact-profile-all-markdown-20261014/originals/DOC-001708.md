Current best proof skeleton (two unresolved alternatives, neither promoted to a theorem):
1. Retain the exact phi/psi dynamic-programming benchmark from PROBLEM.md.
2. For an affirmative result, attach a legal finite-dimensional hard instance to each arbitrary schedule.
3. Establish its objective lower bound without schedule-dependent asymptotic errors.
4. Compare that bound to the exact S_bullet(n) profile for every sufficiently large n.
5. Combine it with the benchmark upper bound after checking the source theorem and normalization.
6. Alternatively, construct an explicit infinite family of legal schedules and exact worst-case upper-bound certificates.
7. Prove a fixed relative separation below the exact benchmark along unbounded horizons.
8. Independently attack a completed argument and verify the surviving full proof.

Central missing lemma: the affirmative route requires, for every epsilon > 0, an N_epsilon independent of h such that for every integer n >= N_epsilon and every h in [0,infinity)^n there is a finite dimension, a convex function with L-Lipschitz gradient and a minimizer, and x_0 != x_* for which the normalized objective error is at least (1-epsilon)/(2 S_bullet(n)+1), or a supremum-attainment-free approximation that proves the same W inequality. This is the main theorem-strength obligation itself, not a routine bridge. No reduction currently makes it easier. The negative alternative requires delta>0, n_j -> infinity, and exact certificates W_{n_j}(h^(j)) <= (1-delta) U_{n_j}^ZJ.

# 3. Dangerous heuristic lines and strategies not to pursue

- "A scalar functional satisfies the same concatenation recursion." Conditional only: the source proposes this as a possible mechanism. A relation to arbitrary schedules and legal hard instances must precede any universal conclusion.
- "Optimizing the existing checkpoint parameters removes the loss." Not automatic: the supplied source warns that auxiliary constants deteriorate as parameters shrink. An exponent correction cannot be hidden inside a constant-level o(1).
- "0.4239 is the asymptotic constant." Conflated with a stronger claim: the target is the exact benchmark profile; the decimal is a numerical estimate.
- "A polynomial or finite-field identity proves the lower bound." Conditional only: a real inequality, PSD/realizability and a uniform quantitative comparison remain necessary.
- "A smaller finite-n PEP value disproves (P)." Conflated with a stronger claim: a nonvanishing separation on infinitely many unbounded horizons is required.
- Do not erase chronological cross-state data before optimizing a structural bound.
- Do not treat source claims, shared attempts or old replay counts as verifier-accepted facts.
- Do not assume bounded, ordered, positive or recursively composable schedules.
- Do not confuse a feasible PEP dual upper bound with a lower-bound hard instance.
- Do not call a theorem-equivalent missing minimax lemma routine.
