## 2. The one static function, actual minimum and radius

J is a finite continuous convex nonnegative maximum of affine functions.
For fixed x its defining proximal objective is coercive and strictly
convex, so it has a unique minimizer y(x). Its optimality condition is

    g(x):=x-y(x) in partial J(y(x)).                (8)

For completeness, a minimizer compared to y+t(z-y), convexity of J,
and division by t>0 followed by t down to0 give the subgradient
inequality J(z)>=J(y)+<x-y,z-y>. Conversely that inequality and the
quadratic expansion imply that the objective at z exceeds its value
at y by at least ||z-y||^2/2, proving (8).

Add the two subgradient inequalities at x,x'. With g=g(x),g'=g(x'),
one obtains <g-g',y-y'>>=0 and hence

    ||g-g'||^2<=<g-g',x-x'>.

Thus g is1-Lipschitz. Evaluating the proximal objective for x' at y
gives the upper bound in

    0<=F(x')-F(x)-<g,x'-x><=||x'-x||^2/2.

For the lower bound, apply the subgradient inequality at y to y' and
expand the two quadratic terms; the remainder is ||g'-g||^2/2.
These inequalities prove differentiability with gradient g and convexity
by the supporting affine function at each x. Therefore F is everywhere
defined, convex and1-smooth.

    K={x_1<=0,x_2<=0,-u x_1-v x_2<=kappa}.         (9)
