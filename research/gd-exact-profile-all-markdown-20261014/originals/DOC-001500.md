## 2. Why the exact finite model represents the function class

For a convex differentiable 1-smooth function, the standard smooth-convex inequality, derived in accepted `1252b8823bb5a290`, is exactly `Q_ij<=0`. This includes both minimizer directions. Every actual normalized trajectory is therefore feasible.

Conversely, an exactly PSD finite Gram matrix has a realization by vectors in dimension at most `p+2`. For exactly feasible values and all ordered rows, put `c_i=<g_i,x_i>-f_i` and

`H(v)=max_i [c_i+<x_i,v-g_i>+||v-g_i||^2/2]`, including the zero minimizer jet.

Then `H(v)-||v||^2/2` is convex. Its conjugate `F(x)=sup_v(<x,v>-H(v))` is finite everywhere, convex, differentiable, and has 1-Lipschitz gradient: strong convexity gives a unique maximizer `g(x)`, and the two strong subgradient inequalities give `<x-x',g(x)-g(x')> >= ||g(x)-g(x')||^2`. The conjugate difference bounds then give differentiability with this gradient.

At a supplied jet `i`, the difference between the `j`th quadratic and `c_i` at `g_i` is exactly `Q_ij<=0`; its own quadratic is active and has derivative `x_i`. Thus `F(x_i)=f_i` and `grad F(x_i)=g_i`. The zero jet gives `F(0)=min F=0`. The supplied GD relations are consequently the actual updates for this same function. This construction uses all rows, including the final prefix query; no terminal deletion is invoked.

The exact feasible set is bounded by the fixed-word bounds `||x_i||,||g_i|| <= product_(t<i)(1+k_t)` and `0<=f_i<=||x_i||^2/2`, so its closed Gram/value formulation attains the maximum. Scaling vectors by a positive radius and values by its square establishes the original-radius normalization; a zero-radius feasible trajectory has zero criterion. This exact correspondence does not make a numerically indefinite or interpolation-defective Gram matrix realizable. Raw numerical outputs are not exact witnesses.
