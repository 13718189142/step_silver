### 8.1 Exact shortest distances and the attainable price

The isolated slow distances are still(10). The isolated fast distances

    d_i^f=B*[t_j^2+sum_(k=i)^(j-1)(t_k-t_(k+1))^2] for i<=j,
    d_(j+1)^f=B*m^2*t_j^2,
    d_i^f=0 for i>=j+2.                             (36)

To prove these expressions, insert the source0 into the sorted scalar

single negative value-m*t_j is on the other side. The ordered-gap proof
of(10) applies on each side, and detouring through the opposite side
adds a nonnegative cost. This includes zero products and ties.

Write d_i^sep for the sum of(36) and all slow scalar distances. For
i>=j+1 the chronological path n,...,i attains this sum, so the joint
distance equals d_i^sep. For each i<=j, there are exactly two relevant
paths: the chronological path, and the chronological path that bypasses
the single vertex j+1. Their costs are

    d_i^sep+2B*t_j^2*m*(1+m),
    d_i^sep+2A_j.                                  (37)

Here is an all-directed-pair proof that no omitted path can be shorter.
Set delta=2min(A_j,B*t_j^2*m*(1+m)). Propose distances d_i^sep+delta
on i<=j, and d_i^sep on i>=j+1. Sums of the separate scalar distance
potentials always obey d_i^sep-d_k^sep<=w_(k,i), since each scalar
potential obeys its own distance inequality and the edge costs add.

Within either index group, the proposed constant shift cancels. From
a prefix index to a later index, it makes the distance inequality
weaker. Only inequalities bounding a prefix distance by a later
distance plus an edge need checking. For i<=j and k>=j+1, the scaled
position of k is coordinatewise at most that of j, and the positions
of j,j-1,...,i are coordinatewise nondecreasing. Expanding the sums of
nonnegative coordinate gaps gives

    w_(k,i)>=w_(k,j)+sum_(l=i)^(j-1)w_(l+1,l),
    d_i^sep=d_j^sep+sum_(l=i)^(j-1)w_(l+1,l).

    d_k^sep+w_(k,j)>=d_(j+2)^sep+w_(j+2,j)

### 8.3 The complete slow-mode budget at an arbitrary reversal
