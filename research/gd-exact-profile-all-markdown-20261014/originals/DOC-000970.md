For an integer m>=2 set lambda=1+sqrt(2), beta=1-1/m,
c=lambda(1+1/m), a=beta/c, r=2/c, w_j=r^j/sum_(k<m)r^k.
Keep this same enclosing m at every level: H_0=(),
H_(j+1)=(H_j,1+a*c^j,H_j), n=2^m-1, S=sum H_m. The actual step at
zero-based index i is h_i=1+a*c^(v2(i+1)), by this recursion.
Here v2 counts factors of two. For the selected positive zeta put
delta=zeta/(1+2S). The initial vector is NOT scaled.

There is an orthonormal coordinate e_v for each binary word v of length
less than m. For a leaf s of length m define
v_s=sum_(j<m)(-1)^(s_(j+1))*sqrt(w_j)*e_(s_1...s_j).
The full body K_m is the convex hull of these 2^m vectors, in dimension
2^m-1. Each leaf has squared norm sum w_j=1. The feature matrix rows
have zero sum and are pairwise orthogonal: disjoint subtrees have zero
product; a descendant's two signs cancel on an ancestor's constant
sign region. Every row is nonzero. Thus the matrix has rank 2^m-1 and
kernel the constant leaf vector. The leaves are affinely independent,
their average is zero, and zero is interior to K_m. In particular this
is the CENTERED simplex without a carrier or an extra dimension.

Let K_scaled=delta*K_m and
F_scaled(x)=max_(g in K_scaled)(<x,g>-||g||^2/2).
Strict convexity of squared distance gives a unique maximizer g, and
completing the square identifies
g=P_(delta*K_m)(x)=delta*P_(K_m)(x/delta).
The projection characterization is <x-g,v-g><=0 for every vertex v.
Necessity follows by a derivative along the segment from g to v;
sufficiency follows by expanding ||x-v||^2-||x-g||^2. Adding the two
normal inequalities at x,x' gives ||g-g'||^2<=<g-g',x-x'>, proving
1-Lipschitz continuity. The maximizing formulas sandwich
F_scaled(x+d)-F_scaled(x)-<g,d> between zero and
<P_(delta*K_m)(x+d)-g,d>, bounded by ||d||^2. Hence F_scaled is convex
and differentiable with the stated gradient and global smoothness L=1.
Inside the body the gradient equals x; an open ball is contained there,
so no smaller global Lipschitz constant is possible. Every gradient has
norm at most delta by convexity and the unit leaf norms. Along t*v_left
with t>=delta its norm equals delta, so this cap is sharp.

The zero gradient is feasible, hence F_scaled>=0 and F_scaled(0)=0.
For x!=0 a sufficiently small positive multiple t*x belongs to the
interior body and has <x,t*x>-||t*x||^2/2>0. Thus the WHOLE minimum set
is {0}. With x0=v_left, the unscaled all-left leaf, the true squared
closest-minimizer distance is exactly R^2=1. Reported V=2F_scaled/(LR^2)
therefore equals 2F_scaled. Neither x0 nor H_m nor L is recalibrated.
Although F_scaled(x)=delta^2 F_K_m(x/delta), its equivalent unit-body
start would be v_left/delta, which differs from the old pilot's start.

Off-spine tree automorphisms act by orthogonal signed coordinate
permutations, preserve K_m, and fix x0. Projection commutes with them
by uniqueness. A vector fixed by all these automorphisms has zero
off-spine coordinates, since the child swap at an off-spine node reverses
that coordinate. Induction in the actual GD update keeps every x_i in
the m-dimensional spine. Group the leaves by first-right depth k<m,
and retain the all-left leaf as group m. Uniform averaging gives b_k
with j coordinate sqrt(w_j) for j<k, -sqrt(w_k) for j=k<m, and zero
for j>k; b_m has all coordinates sqrt(w_j). Averaging representations
proves the fixed section is exactly conv{b_0,...,b_m}. Group masses p_k
lift uniformly over 2^(m-k-1) leaves for k<m, and p_m is the last mass.
For a spine residual, its inner product with every full leaf in a group
equals the product with b_k. Thus ALL reduced vertex normals give ALL
original full-leaf normals, with no full-space diagnostic sampling.

For physical x_j=sqrt(w_j) z_j and group tail masses q_j=sum_(k>=j)p_k,
q_0=1, the gradient coordinate is
g_j=delta*sqrt(w_j)*(2q_(j+1)-q_j).
Projection minimizes sum_j w_j*(z_j/delta-(2q_(j+1)-q_j))^2 over
1=q_0>=q_1>=...>=q_m>=0. These are the actual Haar metric and physical
gradient, not Euclidean probability mass thresholding. To test a fresh
core query, set t_j=x_j/(delta*sqrt(w_j)), q_0=1 and
q_(j+1)=(q_j+t_j)/2; masses are p_j=q_j-q_(j+1),p_m=q_m.
The independent inverse is
p_k=2^(-k-1)*(1+sum_(j<k)2^j*t_j-2^k*t_k), k<m,
p_m=2^(-m)*(1+sum_(j<m)2^j*t_j).
The core test is that every mass is nonnegative. It is repeated at
every query and has no automatic implication about future queries.

For a proposed face I choose its first vertex v0 and independent edges

input X=x/delta. The Gram is positive definite. An independent formula
solves [V^T V,1;1^T,0]*(p,nu)=(V^T X,1). Its kernel is zero: a kernel
vector p has sum zero and ||Vp||^2=0, forcing p=0 by affine independence,
then nu=0. A feasible affine solution satisfying all vertex normals is
the projection by the distance expansion. A bounded active-set procedure
adds violated normals and moves toward affine optima until a first
blocking mass is zero; failure of any feasibility/normal/linear solve
test invalidates the case. No iteration-complexity theorem is assumed.
