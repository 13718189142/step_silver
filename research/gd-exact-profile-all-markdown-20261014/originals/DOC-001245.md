## 7. True closest-minimizer normalization

The minimizer set is nonempty and closed. Its distance from X_0 is attained:
a minimizing sequence may be restricted to a bounded closed ball in the
finite-dimensional Euclidean space and hence has a convergent subsequence.
Take a closest minimizer X_star, without assuming it is the origin. Since
grad F(X_star)=0, smoothness implies

    Gamma_0<=R_dist.                                      (33)

    M_chr=1+sum_(i<n)h_i
         =N+a_chr(c_chr^m-N)/(c_chr-2)<c_chr^m.             (34)

## 8. Two explicit weight laws and an actual vanishing-support sequence

All formulas below are probabilities on the signs (B,C_1,...,C_(m-1)),
converted uniquely to weights w_s by the bijection in Section1. These are
finite real vectors (2), not an arbitrary unspecified Gram.

First, the conditional-product proposal is exactly

    w_s=[(1+(1-epsilon)B(s))/2]
          product_(j=1)^(m-1)[(1+C_j(s)(1/10-4B(s)/5))/2].  (37)

The first moment identities (1) follow by conditioning on B. Given B=1,
each C_j has mean -7/10; given B=-1, its mean is9/10. Directly,

    E C_1 C_2=49/100+4epsilon/25<=1/2,
    E B C_1 C_2=49/100-13epsilon/20<=1/2.

Thus (37) satisfies every hypothesis for ANY epsilon in [0,10^(-6)]. In
particular it covers the original specified sequence epsilon=N^(-1), any
other sequence epsilon_m eventually in this interval, and the exact boundary
epsilon=0. For positive epsilon all weights are positive. The proof is
uniform over all of these choices without using continuity.

Second, here is a genuinely nonproduct low-prefix distribution and a highly
dependent coarse-tail mixture. Choose eta in [0,1]. For b in {-1,1} set

    mu_1=-7/10, mu_(-1)=9/10,
    tau_1=1/2-epsilon, tau_(-1)=9/10,
    pi_b(c_1,c_2)=[1+mu_b(c_1+c_2)+tau_b c_1 c_2]/4.       (38)

These four probabilities are explicit. For b=1 they are
(1/10-epsilon)/4 for(++), (1/2+epsilon)/4 for each mixed pair, and
(29/10-epsilon)/4 for(--). For b=-1 they are37/40 for(++) and1/40 for
each of the other three pairs. All are positive in the stated epsilon range,
and their sum is one. Their two means are mu_b and their product mean is
tau_b, as follows by summing these four values.

Let c_tail=(c_3,...,c_(m-1)); m>=1024 ensures this tail is nonempty. Define

    T_b(c_tail)=(1-eta)[(1+mu_b)/2 * 1_(all tail signs=1)
                        +(1-mu_b)/2 * 1_(all tail signs=-1)]
                    +eta product_(j=3)^(m-1)(1+mu_b c_j)/2. (39)

Here 1_(condition) is1 if the condition holds and0 otherwise. The first
part assigns one common sign to EVERY tail prefix. The second part is a
finite product distribution with full support. Both parts give each tail
sign mean mu_b; their probabilities sum to one. The complete weight is

    w_s=[(1+(1-epsilon)B(s))/2]
             pi_(B(s))(C_1(s),C_2(s))
             T_(B(s))(C_3(s),...,C_(m-1)(s)).              (40)

This specifies every atom. Each prefix has the conditional mean mu_b, so
the first moments in (1) are identical to those for (37). The low joint
moments are now

    E C_1 C_2=1/2-(4/5)epsilon+epsilon^2/2<=1/2,
    E B C_1 C_2=1/2-(17/10)epsilon+epsilon^2/2<=1/2.        (41)

Thus (40) satisfies the theorem for EVERY eta in [0,1], regardless of
how strongly its tail prefixes depend on one another. It is not the
conditional-product policy (37): already pi_b has a different product mean,
and for eta<1 the tail variables share a common component.

One definite original-horizon policy is

    epsilon=N^(-1), eta=N^(-2), standard chronological labels. (42)

For every finite N=2^m all weights in (40),(42) are strictly positive:
P(B=b)>0, all four pi_b are positive, and the eta product component is
positive at every tail pattern. Its full-support tail component tends to
zero at the specified rate, and its low-bit discrepancy also tends to
zero. Nevertheless EVERY positive-amplitude realization on actual H_m that
is legal for one global function has the bound (4),(5). No tuning of a
nonsaturating amplitude or choice of common anchor is left out of that bound.

The exact singular boundary epsilon=eta=0 also satisfies (1): at each
finite m it is supported on eight sign patterns (B=1, four low pairs,
two constant tails). The same inequalities (12),(20),(28),(35) apply
there directly and give the same bound. This makes no continuity assertion
between ambient spaces or between inverse Gram matrices.

## 9. What is closed and what is still open

The proof excludes the two explicit policies (37),(42), all rates at which
their displayed epsilon and eta approach zero, and more generally every
finite sign measure satisfying the moment hypotheses (1). It allows zero
weights, unbounded conditioning, all positive amplitudes with arbitrary
slack, every common anchor, and nonzero closest minimizers. The quantitative
loss pays for every repeated low block and all high bridges at the actual
original horizon. C62 and any proposed Gram-inverse distance formula are not
used. The accepted fixed C59 witness is not constrained by (1) and is not
contradicted.

This is NOT an obstruction to all dependent character constructions. For
example, keep the same law of B, choose ONE sign V conditionally of mean
mu_B, and put C_j=V for every j. Its four atom probabilities are
P(B=b)(1+mu_b v)/2 for b,v in {-1,1}. It has all the required FIRST moments
but E C_1 C_2=1 and E B C_1 C_2=1-epsilon, so it violates the two joint
moment upper bounds. Mixing this law with (37) with coefficient eta=N^(-2)
retains full support at positive epsilon and keeps the first joint moment
strictly above1/2. No legal trajectory, amplitude rule, useful payoff, or
common anchor for that different policy is asserted here. It is a precise
unresolved escape from this theorem's hypotheses, not a counterexample to
its conclusion.

Within the fixed first-moment chart and positive-amplitude convention,
a competitive construction must therefore violate at least one of
E C_1 C_2<=1/2 and E B C_1 C_2<=1/2, or change its labels or the stated
first moments. Arbitrary further dependence of the higher prefixes alone
cannot escape this result while (1) is retained. Changing the two lowest
prefix joint moments still needs a new full interpolation and true-distance
argument; Gram positivity alone says nothing about that missing feasibility.
