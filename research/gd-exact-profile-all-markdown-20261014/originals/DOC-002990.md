# Extra storage from shortening the primitive pair

Status: complete candidate identity, not verifier-accepted. This is a proposed
input for the remaining joint boundary transport, not an improved schedule.
No solver or verifier call is used.

For 0<=u<=3/2 consider the physical pair h=(u,u). Write
p=g_0, m=g_1, q=g_2 and define

    K=1+2u,
    V=2u(f_0+f_1-2f_2)+u(1-u)(||p||^2+||m||^2)
        -2u(1+2u)||q||^2,
    epsilon=(3/2-u)(u+1/2)>=0,
    I_ij=f_j-f_i-<g_i,x_j-x_i>-||g_i-g_j||^2/2.

On samples of one convex 1-smooth function I_ij>=0. The following exact
identity retains two independent gradient differences:

    V=I_02+(1+2u)I_10+(3+4u)I_12+(4+8u)I_21
       +(1/4)||p+m-2q||^2
       +epsilon[||p-m||^2+4||m-q||^2].               (1)

All four actual interpolation coefficients are nonnegative, and every square
coefficient is nonnegative in the stated range. Thus this is a full candidate
primitive certificate for the pair. At u=3/2 it reduces to the existing pair
identity in finding b750ce63e2821fa8; the epsilon term is the new retained
slack from changing the physical steps, not the proof weights.

For completeness, here is a coefficient proof of (1). Use x_1=x_0-u p and
x_2=x_0-u(p+m). Then

    I_02=f_2-f_0+(u-1/2)||p||^2+u<p,m>+<p,q>-(1/2)||q||^2,
    I_10=f_0-f_1-(1/2)||p||^2-(1/2)||m||^2+(1-u)<p,m>,
    I_12=f_2-f_1+(u-1/2)||m||^2+<m,q>-(1/2)||q||^2,
    I_21=f_1-f_2-(1/2)||m||^2-(1/2)||q||^2+(1-u)<m,q>.

The weighted sum in (1) has function coefficients(2u,2u,-4u). Subtracting
its gradient part from V leaves a quadratic form on a=p-q and b=m-q:

    (a,b)^T R_u(a,b),
    R_u=[[alpha,1/2-alpha],[1/2-alpha,5alpha-1]],
    alpha=1+u-u^2=1/4+epsilon.

Therefore

    R_u=(1/4)[[1,1],[1,1]]+epsilon[[1,-1],[-1,5]],

which is the sum of squares in (1). Its determinant is
4alpha^2-1/4 and its trace is6alpha-1. At u<3/2 it is positive definite,
because alpha>1/4; at u=3/2 it has rank1. More explicitly,
[[1,-1],[-1,5]] has determinant4 and positive upper-left entry1, so its
quadratic form is ||a-b||^2+4||b||^2.

## Quadratic slack and the precise changed hypothesis

The pair's quadratic response is Pi_u=(1-u)^2. Its quadratic-balance ratio is

    kappa(u)=K Pi_u=(1+2u)(1-u)^2.

For 0<u<3/2, kappa(u)<1, since

    1-kappa(u)=u^2(3-2u)>0.

At u=3/2, kappa=1. If u=3/2-delta with delta small and positive,

    K=4-2delta,
    kappa=1-(9/2)delta+6delta^2-2delta^3,
    epsilon=2delta-delta^2.

Thus shortening the pair supplies explicit positive gradient-variation
storage and strictly weakens the quadratic product test. The unequal-block
obstruction requiring exact quadratic balance cannot be applied to these
inputs without changing its hypotheses.

For three equal copies with bridges1+cK and1+dK, put M=3+c+d. The two
necessary cut tests remain

    Mc<=2+d, Md<=2+c,

while the quadratic test becomes

    Mcd<=kappa(u)^(-3)                              (2)

when kappa(u)>0; at u=1 the quadratic response is zero and this test is
vacuous. Passing (2) is not sufficient for a primitive certificate. It only
identifies a concrete unresolved regime not closed by the balanced result.

## Joint objective and primitive-dual storage

There is also an exact correlated-slack identity throughout the explicit
interval 29/20<=u<=3/2. Define

    H=2(f_0-f_2)-4u||q||^2
         -||p-(u(p+m)+q)/K||^2.

First, direct expansion gives the primitive-dual identity

    H=(4u+1)V/K^2+(2u/K)I_10+(2/K)I_20
                          +(4u^2/K^2)(I_02+I_12).   (3)

Together with (1), it proves H>=0 for this actual pair. More information is
retained by the following identity:

    V-(4/5)H
      =(2/5)I_01+(2u-6/5)I_10
        +(4u-4/5)I_12+(8u-12/5)I_21
        +(1/4)[P||z||^2+2D<z,w>+Q||w||^2],         (4)

where the ACTUAL gradient combinations and scalar coefficients are

    z=p+m-2q, w=p-m,
    P=(-20u^2+36u-8+4/K^2)/5,
    Q=4(3-2u)(5u-1)/5,
    D=-4(3-2u)(5u^2+u-1)/(5K).

The four interpolation coefficients in (4) are nonnegative on the stated
interval. The quadratic matrix (1/4)[[P,D],[D,Q]] is PSD, and is positive
definite when u<3/2. Here is an explicit check. Put delta=3/2-u, so
0<=delta<=1/20. Since K=4-2delta,

    P=[1+24delta-20delta^2+4/K^2]/5>=1/4.

For delta>0, Q>0 and

    D^2/Q=4(3-2u)(5u^2+u-1)^2/[5K^2(5u-1)]
          <=4418/38025 <1/4 <=P.

The displayed upper bound uses 3-2u<=1/10,
5u^2+u-1<=47/4, K>=39/10, and 5u-1>=25/4. Thus its determinant is strictly
positive. At delta=0, P=1/4 and D=Q=0, leaving ||z||^2/16. This is exactly
the older u=3/2 correlated storage, and the second eigenvalue becomes
positive on shortening the physical steps.

For a full matrix on the distinct variables(p,m,q), set

    t11=(P+Q+2D)/4, t12=(P-Q)/4, t22=(P+Q-2D)/4.

Then the remainder in (4) is

    (p,m,q)^T J_u(p,m,q),
    J_u=[[t11,t12,-t11-t12],
         [t12,t22,-t12-t22],
         [-t11-t12,-t12-t22,t11+2t12+t22]].          (5)

It is PSD by (4); its kernel is the constant-gradient subspace when
u<3/2. Equivalently, one may replace m by d_block/u-p to carry the actual
displacement d_block=u(p+m). No initial or terminal gradient of a neighboring
block is identified with these variables.

For a coefficient audit of (4), the following two-parameter identity is
convenient. Choose scalars b0,e0 and put

    a0=2-2b0, c0=2u+2-b0-e0,
    d0=4+4u-b0-e0, f0=4+8u-2e0.

These lower-case subscripted letters in this audit are interpolation
coefficients, not function values or block capacities. The exact expansion is

    V=a0 I_01+b0 I_02+c0 I_10+d0 I_12+e0 I_20+f0 I_21
       +(p-q,m-q)^T R(p-q,m-q),

    R11=2-u^2-(1-u)b0,
    R22=6+4u-5u^2-(2-u)(b0+e0),
    2R12=-[4-2u^2+(2u-3)b0-(1-u)e0].

Set L0=20u^2+4u+1, b0=16u^2/L0 and e0=8K/L0. Substitute this expansion
in (1-(4/5)(4u+1)/K^2)V and then subtract the other terms of (4/5)H from
(3). The I_02 and I_20 coefficients cancel exactly; the four remaining
coefficients are those in (4). Rewriting the remainder using z,w gives P,D,Q
as displayed. These are algebraic identities, not appeals to an unverified
primitive residual lemma.

## What a next join must prove

The retained variables in (1) and (4) are the ACTUAL p,m,q of each block. Since
d_block=u(p+m), they can also be stored as(p,d_block,q) for u>0, without
identifying the next block's start gradient with the bridge gradient. An
attempt to spend the epsilon term across two bridges must preserve these
distinct variables and the four displayed interpolation coefficients. It
must finish with a nonnegative full matrix and an original-objective bound.
No such two-bridge completion or asymptotic improvement is proved here.
