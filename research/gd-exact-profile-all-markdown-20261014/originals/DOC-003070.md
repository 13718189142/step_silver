## 1. Original word and the claim

Fix an integer m>=4. Set

    N=2^m, n=N-1, sigma=1+sqrt(2), delta=1-1/m,
    c_chr=sigma(1+1/m), a_chr=delta/c_chr.

Use the actual chronological words

    H_chr,0=(),
    H_chr,j+1=(H_chr,j,1+a_chr*c_chr^j,H_chr,j), 0<=j<m.

Thus h=H_chr,m has exactly n steps and

    h_i=1+a_chr*c_chr^(nu_2(i+1)), 0<=i<n,

where nu_2(v) is the exponent of2 dividing the positive integer v. Every
subword retains the displayed enclosing-m parameters. There is no reset,
padding, or permutation of physical steps. In particular its last physical
step is h_(n-1)=1+a_chr in(1,2).

Section2 specifies strictly positive probabilities w_s on s in{0,1}^m.
Write chi_s(i)=(-1)^(sum_l s_l i_l), using the ordinary binary digits i_l
of the original sample index. Prescribe ONLY the queried gradients by

    g_i=(Gamma_i*sqrt(w_s)*chi_s(i))_s, 0<=i<n,             (1)

where every Gamma_i is arbitrary and positive. All Euclidean realizations
of the same Gram matrix are included. The amplitudes may depend on the
whole word and the enclosing horizon. There is no adjacent saturation
assumption and no common amplitude profile in repeated subwords.

Suppose ONE finite-dimensional convex differentiable1-smooth function F
with a minimizer realizes these gradients along

    X_(i+1)=X_i-h_i*g_i, 0<=i<n.

The anchor, the values, the function, and the terminal gradient are otherwise
unrestricted. Let X_star be a closest minimizer, and define

    R=||X_0-X_star||, F_star=min F,
    V_C89=2(F(X_n)-F_star)/R^2.

The claim is

    R>0,
    V_C89 <= beta_(N/4-1) <= 128/N^2,                    (2)

where the accepted notation is

    c_0=0, c_(k+1)=c_k+2+2sqrt(c_k+1),
    beta_k=4/(1+sqrt(1+2c_k))^2.                         (3)

Consequently, for the ORIGINAL exact benchmark B_n=2S_bullet(n)+1,

    0 <= B_n V_C89
       <= 128(1+4n^p)/(n+1)^2 ->0,
    p=log_2(3)<2.                                      (4)

The bound B_n<=1+4n^p is from `b98bb807d514e80d`. This is an obstruction

## 2. Every probability atom, its correlations, and the removed hypotheses

Put p_bias=1-1/N. For s in{0,1}^m let

    B_sig=(-1)^s_0, C_sig=(-1)^s_1, D_sig=(-1)^s_2,
    mu_+=1/2, mu_-=1/4.

The exact joint law is as follows. B_sig has mean p_bias; conditional on
B_sig, C_sig is unbiased; conditional on B_sig=b and C_sig=c, the sign
D_sig has mean mu_b*c. All bits s_3,...,s_(m-1) are independent unbiased
bits, independent of the first three signs. Explicitly,

    w_s=2^(-(m-3)) * (1+p_bias*B_sig)/2
                         * (1/2) * (1+mu_(B_sig)*C_sig*D_sig)/2.       (5)

All factors are strictly positive. Summing in the order D_sig,C_sig,B_sig,
then in the higher bits, proves sum_s w_s=1. This is a real probability
law; no assertion of real positivity is inferred from finite-field algebra.

Its only nonzero nonconstant character moments are

    E B_sig=p_bias,
    E(C_sig D_sig)=q_mix=(3+p_bias)/8,
    E(B_sig C_sig D_sig)=r_mix=(1+3p_bias)/8.               (6)

Indeed conditional averaging first gives E(D_sig|B_sig,C_sig)=mu_(B_sig)
C_sig and E(C_sig|B_sig)=0. Also mu_b=(3+b)/8. These formulas give (6),
while E C_sig=E D_sig=E(B_sig C_sig)=E(B_sig D_sig)=0. A nonempty product
of higher signs has expectation0, independently of the first three signs.
Equivalently the density is exactly

    w_s=N^(-1)[1+p_bias B_sig+q_mix C_sig D_sig
                                      +r_mix B_sig C_sig D_sig].      (7)

Let XOR mean bitwise addition modulo2 of label vectors, and let

    H_corr={0,1,6,7}=span_(F_2){1,6}.                     (8)

Here 1 is binary001, 6 is binary110, and any higher bits are zero.
The real normalized correlation kernel is

    k(0)=1, k(1)=p_bias, k(6)=q_mix, k(7)=r_mix,
    k(d)=0 for d outside H_corr,
    <g_i,g_j>=Gamma_i Gamma_j k(i XOR j).                 (9)

This follows by multiplying the two real characters, and using (6).
In particular every queried Gram entry is nonnegative. The matrix is
positive semidefinite because (1) is an actual real vector realization.
It is positive definite at distinct labels: for any nonzero coefficients
a_i, the squared norm of sum_i a_i g_i equals
sum_s w_s(sum_i a_i Gamma_i chi_s(i))^2>0. For the strict inequality,
character orthogonality gives independence of the distinct character rows.
That orthogonality follows by pairing s with s+e_l at a bit where two
labels differ, so their signed sum is zero.

The law is nonproduct even after an invertible linear change of sign
coordinates. To see this, its nonzero Fourier support is the rank2 subgroup
H_corr. A product law in some linear sign coordinates with this support
would have exactly two nonzero coordinate means, and one of the three
nonconstant moments on H_corr would be the product of the other two.
None is:

    r_mix-p_bias*q_mix=(1-p_bias^2)/8>0,
    q_mix-p_bias*r_mix=3(1-p_bias^2)/8>0,
    p_bias-q_mix*r_mix>0.                               (10)

The last inequality uses p_bias>=15/16 and q_mix,r_mix<1/2. A product law
with more nonzero means would have larger Fourier support, and a product
law with fewer would have smaller support. Full support of the probability
also rules out a deterministic coordinate introduced by a change of basis.

The differences from the earlier laws are visible in the ORIGINAL pairs.
Every leaf has correlation p_bias approaching1, instead of the old fixed
root correlation -1/2. At the first level2 bridge, samples2,3,4 have

    corr(g_2,g_3)=p_bias,
    corr(g_2,g_4)=q_mix,
    corr(g_3,g_4)=r_mix != p_bias*q_mix.                  (11)

Thus the product triangle identity with the third correlation equal to
the product of the first two cannot be imported. At a level-k bridge
with k>=3, the three samples 2^k-2,2^k-1,2^k instead have correlations
p_bias,0,0: the two label differences crossing the bridge contain a
nonzero high bit. The old near-unit outer correlation is absent.

The common-prefix return mechanism is also absent. For m>=4,
corr(g_0,g_4)=k(4)=0, whereas the old common-prefix component repeats its
first coarse direction after four samples and its full-law correlation
there is close to1. In (5), higher prefix characters are not forced to
share a common sign. These are changes of actual queried Gram entries,
not scalar amplitude reweighting of the old fixed law.

Removing these hypotheses does not establish interpolation. The exact
obstruction below uses a different property: the many orthogonal selections
left by sparse, nonnegative correlations.

## 3. Original queried selections with nonnegative past correlations

Here is the general calculation used in this joint proof. Work on one
actual trajectory of a convex differentiable1-smooth F, translated so that
a true closest minimizer is0 and F_star=0. A closest minimizer exists:
argmin F is closed and nonempty, and a bounded sequence approaching its
distance from X_0 has a convergent subsequence in finite dimension.

Choose original queried indices

    j_0<...<j_(M-1),

whose gradients v_r=g_(j_r) are nonzero and pairwise orthogonal. Assume
that each selected gradient has nonnegative scalar product with every
earlier queried gradient:

    <v_r,g_t> >=0 whenever t<j_r.                       (12)

Let f_r=F(X_(j_r)), q_r=||v_r||^2>0, and D_0=X_0-X_star. These f_r and
q_r are local names for the selected values and energies in this section.
No physical query or step has been deleted or compressed.

Accepted `1252b8823bb5a290` gives, on every actual ordered pair,

    Q_ij=F(X_i)-F(X_j)+<g_i,X_j-X_i>
                                      +||g_i-g_j||^2/2 <=0.          (13)

For successive selected indices, the inequality with the LATER gradient
and the earlier point gives

    f_r >= f_(r+1) + (q_r+q_(r+1))/2
                + sum_(t=j_r)^(j_(r+1)-1) h_t <v_(r+1),g_t>
        >= f_(r+1)+(q_r+q_(r+1))/2.                    (14)

All terms in the displayed original displacement are present. Their
nonnegativity, not a fictitious compressed GD identity, justifies the
second line. Orthogonality is used only for the squared gradient difference.

The original optimum comparison similarly gives

    <D_0,v_r> = <X_(j_r)-X_star,v_r>
                       +sum_(t<j_r)h_t <g_t,v_r>
              >= f_r+q_r/2.                            (15)

Also f_(M-1)>=q_(M-1)/2. Put b=f_(M-1)-q_(M-1)/2>=0 and

    T_r=b+sum_(s=r)^(M-1)q_s.

Summing (14) and using (15) gives <D_0,v_r> >= T_r>0 for every r.
Expansion of the nonnegative squared norm
||D_0-sum_r(<D_0,v_r>/q_r)v_r||^2 now gives

    R^2=||D_0||^2 >= sum_(r=0)^(M-1) T_r^2/q_r.         (16)

This is the TRUE initial-distance norm of the given legal function.
No inverse Gram anchor or distance to an arbitrary minimizer replaces it.

For completeness the exact scalar elimination from the accepted
orthogonal-moment proof is reproduced. Inductively,

    sum_(r=0)^(k-1) T_r^2/q_r >= c_k T_k,
    0<=k<=M-1.                                        (17)

The initial case is0>=0. Since T_k=q_k+T_(k+1), adding the next term to
the inductive bound gives

    c_k T_k+T_k^2/q_k
       =(c_k+1)q_k+(c_k+2)T_(k+1)+T_(k+1)^2/q_k
       >=[c_k+2+2sqrt(c_k+1)]T_(k+1).

The last inequality is the nonnegativity of
(sqrt(c_k+1)*sqrt(q_k)-T_(k+1)/sqrt(q_k))^2. This proves (17).
Let c=c_(M-1), z=T_(M-1)/q_(M-1)>=1, and
r_cap=(1+sqrt(1+2c))/2. Equations (16)-(17) imply

    R^2 >= q_(M-1) z(z+c),
    2f_(M-1)=q_(M-1)(2z-1).

Because c=2r_cap(r_cap-1),

    z(z+c)-r_cap^2(2z-1)=(z-r_cap)^2>=0.

Therefore

    2F(X_(j_(M-1)))/R^2 <= beta_(M-1).                 (18)

The formula includes M=1, where beta_0=1. If a selected gradient were
zero, its point would be a minimizer, every subsequent iterate would
remain there, and the terminal objective would be0 instead.

For k>=1, induction in (3) gives c_k>=k^2. Since
c_k=2r_cap(r_cap-1)<=2r_cap^2, this also proves

    beta_k <= 2/k^2.                                   (19)

The new content relative to the accepted all-gradients-orthogonal fact is
precisely (14)-(15): nonorthogonal gradients at the skipped original samples
are allowed, with their full chronological contributions retained.

## 6. Nonvacuity: an explicit common anchor and one global function

This part only establishes nonemptiness; it is not a competitive payoff
claim. In fact it works for every finite schedule with h_i>1 and the same
kind of nonnegative kernel. For the dyadic word above retain its exact
weights and additionally prescribe a character gradient at the terminal.
Write

    H_max=max_(i<n) h_i,
    kappa=min(1,(min_(i<n)h_i-1)/4)>0,
    eta=kappa/[8(H_max+kappa+1)],
    Gamma_i=eta^i, f_i=(kappa+1/2)Gamma_i^2, 0<=i<=n,
    K_ij=<g_i,g_j>.

Here eta<1/8 and h_i>=1+4kappa. Define

    b_i=(kappa+1)Gamma_i^2+sum_(t<i)h_t K_ti,
    (X_0)_s=[1/(N sqrt(w_s))]
                              sum_(i=0)^n (b_i/Gamma_i)chi_s(i),
    X_i=X_0-sum_(t<i)h_t g_t.                           (23)

Character orthogonality proves <X_0,g_i>=b_i, hence

    <X_i,g_i>=(kappa+1)Gamma_i^2.                       (24)

For i<j, direct expansion of the ORIGINAL residuals gives

    Q_ij=(kappa+1-h_i)Gamma_i^2-kappa Gamma_j^2
                          -sum_(i<t<j)h_t K_it-K_ij,

    Q_ji=(kappa+1)Gamma_j^2-kappa Gamma_i^2
                          +(h_i-1)K_ij+sum_(i<t<j)h_t K_jt.          (25)

By (9), 0<=K_it<=Gamma_i Gamma_t. The first line of (25) is at most
-3kappa Gamma_i^2. The second, divided by Gamma_i^2, is at most

    -kappa+(kappa+1)eta^2+H_max*eta/(1-eta)
       <=-kappa+(2H_max+kappa+1)eta
       <-3kappa/4.                                    (26)

The second line uses eta<1/2; the final inequality follows on inserting
the exact eta and using2H_max+kappa+1<2(H_max+kappa+1).
These inequalities include the first actual cross-cycle and both
orientations of every remote queried or terminal pair. With the optimum
jet (0,0,0), (24) gives exactly

    Q_(i,star)=0, Q_(star,i)=-kappa Gamma_i^2<0.          (27)

For a concrete ONE static function, fixed before the run, let

    C_grad=conv(0,g_0,...,g_n),
    F_C89(X)=max_(v in C_grad)(<X,v>-||v||^2/2).          (28)

Completing the square identifies its unique maximizing vector with the
Euclidean projection P_C_grad(X). Projection onto this compact convex set
is characterized by <X-P_C_grad(X),v-P_C_grad(X)><=0 for every v in C_grad.
Adding those normal inequalities at two points proves
||P_C_grad(X)-P_C_grad(Y)||^2<=<X-Y,P_C_grad(X)-P_C_grad(Y)>;
hence projection is1-Lipschitz. Comparing the two maximizing expressions
in (28) bounds its first-order remainder by||X-Y||^2, proving that F_C89
is differentiable with gradient P_C_grad. It is convex as a supremum of
affine functions and is everywhere finite.

The values in (23)-(24) obey

    <X_i-g_i,g_j-g_i>=Q_ji<=0,
    <X_i-g_i,-g_i>=-kappa Gamma_i^2<0.

They check every vertex of C_grad, so P_C_grad(X_i)=g_i and
F_C89(X_i)=f_i. Thus actual GD on the single function (28), with the

The function has min0 and minimizer set

    C_grad^polar={Z:<Z,g_i><=0 for every i}.

This follows from the projection condition at0. The true initial distance

## 7. Non-dyadic scope and the broader remaining interface

The construction of the probability law does not require a dyadic number
of queries. For any integer n>=1, choose d>=3 with D=2^d>=n+1, set
p_bias=1-1/(n+1), and use the same conditional law (5) in d bits. Use only
the actual labels0,...,n-1 for the queried gradients. This enlarges the
coordinate space, not the physical schedule. There is no fictitious query
or algorithm step.

Choose one maximal queried label from each occupied H_corr-coset. The
number M of occupied cosets satisfies M>=ceil(n/4), and this selection
contains n-1. For ANY actual nonnegative schedule h and every realizing
function with positive queried amplitudes, the same proof gives

    V_C89 <= A_last*beta_(M-1),
    A_last=max{1,(h_(n-1)-1)^2}.                        (29)

For0<=h_(n-1)<=2 the descent estimate gives A_last=1. For h_(n-1)>2,
smoothness gives F(X_n)<=F(X_(n-1))+h_(n-1)(h_(n-1)-2)||g_(n-1)||^2/2.
The optimum inequality gives||g_(n-1)||^2<=2(F(X_(n-1))-F_star), yielding
the stated factor. Nothing is hidden in an asymptotic remainder.
The nonvacuity construction also works for non-dyadic n and all h_i>1:
replace N in (23) by the ambient D and sum only over i=0,...,n.
Distinct-row character orthogonality still proves (24)-(28).

More generally, suppose a character correlation kernel has nonnegative
values, is zero outside a subgroup of size K_corr, and the original
queried labels occupy M cosets, one containing the final query. No product
assumption on its weights is needed. Representatives from those cosets
satisfy exactly (12), so (29) holds. On a complete dyadic set with one
unqueried label, if all N/K_corr cosets remain represented and the last
physical step is at most2, then

    V <= beta_(N/K_corr-1) <= 8 K_corr^2/N^2             (30)

whenever N/K_corr>=2. For any K_corr=o(N/sqrt(B_(N-1))), the original-B
normalized payoff of that class tends to0. In particular a fixed-size
nonnegative correlation support cannot repair this route merely by making
the conditional probability law more intricate within its support.

This does NOT exclude arbitrary character measures. The proof can fail
when there is no sufficiently large orthogonal selection containing the
last query, or when earlier gradients make negative chronological scalar
products with a selected gradient. Growing correlation support and signed
long-range correlations are unexcluded freedoms, not automatic legal
constructions. They must still resolve all original cycles, one common
optimum, the bridge timing, values, and true initial distance.

## 8. Exact scope attack: orthogonal selected gradients alone are insufficient

The nonnegative-history hypothesis in (12) cannot be silently discarded.
Consider the explicit convex1-smooth quadratic

    F(x,y)=x^2/2+y^2/4, X_0=(1,1).

Its unique minimizer is0 and R^2=2. Use the three actual GD steps
h=(10,20/19,1/2), so that samples0 and2 are both queried. They give

    g_0=(1,1/2), X_1=(-9,-4), g_1=(-9,-2),
    X_2=(9/19,-36/19), g_2=(9/19,-18/19).
    X_3=(9/38,-27/19).

The selected gradients g_0 and g_2 are orthogonal, but

    <g_2,g_1>=-45/19<0,
    2F(X_2)/R^2=729/722>1/4=beta_1.
    2F(X_3)/R^2=1539/2888>1/4.

Thus the selected-gradient conclusion and its terminal consequence fail
without the chronological sign condition, even with a final step below2
on an explicit static quadratic. Its full ordered
interpolation inequalities hold: writing A=diag(1,1/2), their residual is
-<X_i-X_j,A(I-A)(X_i-X_j)>/2<=0. This is a symbolic hypothesis test, not
a numerical model evaluation or a proposed improving word.
