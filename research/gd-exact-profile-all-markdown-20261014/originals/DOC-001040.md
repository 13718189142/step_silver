## 2. Legality, energies, and elementary payments

    kappa*r       for 0<=r<=1,

    B_sigma       for r>=v+B_sigma.

The signed derivative is continuous, nondecreasing, and piecewise has

    2f(sigma*r)=kappa*r^2                         (r<=1),

    0<=G_sigma(r)<=min(r,B_sigma).                  (2.1)

## 5. Exactly two crossings: all positions, all original ancestors

Assume the positive-terminal history has exactly two crossings, at
levels u and v in chronological order. Let P,M,T be respectively the
full original mass before, between and after these two physical steps,
and put X=1+P,Y=1+M,Z=1+T. If the source gradients are p_1,p_2, then
(2.2)-(2.3) give

    X*p_1<=R, Y*p_2<=d_u*p_1, Z*p_n<=d_v*p_2,
    p_n/R<=d_u*d_v/(XYZ).                         (5.1)

This is a valid upper bound on the ACTUAL history. We do not assume it
is sharp. The tree gives two exhaustive cases.

Choose the smallest literal subtree H_(l+1) containing BOTH crossing
steps. Its center has level l. Either that center is one of the two
crossings, or the two crossings lie in its different children and the
center does not cross. These alternatives also cover equal crossing
levels in disjoint children.

Let E_L,E_R be the physical masses before and after this whole subtree
in the original H_j; E=E_L+E_R=A_j-A_(l+1). These exterior portions have
no crossings because the two selected ones are all the crossings.
They add to X and Z, not to the middle gap Y.

### 5A. One crossing is the center of the smallest subtree

First suppose the second crossing is the center, of level l, and the
first, of level k<l, lies in its left H_l. Within that left H_l let
X_0,Y_0 be one plus the masses before and after the level-k crossing.
Then X_0,Y_0<=A_l, and (3.4) gives

    X_0*Y_0>d_l*d_k/gamma.

In the whole original history,

    X=X_0+E_L, Y=Y_0, Z=A_l+E_R.

Since X_0<=A_l, multiplication of these nonnegative quantities yields

    XYZ >=X_0*Y_0*(A_l+E_L+E_R)
        > (d_l*d_k/gamma)*(A_l+E)
        > d_l*d_k*A_j/(c*gamma).

If instead the first crossing is the center and the second is in its
right H_l, interchange the left and right roles: the same proof uses
X=A_l+E_L, Y=Y_0, Z=Z_0+E_R, with Y_0*Z_0>d_l*d_k/gamma and Z_0<=A_l.
This is a comparison of physical masses; it does not reverse the actual
trajectory or assume equal gradients on the two rays.

Combining with (5.1) and A_j>d_j/b gives

    p_n/R <c*gamma/A_j <c^2*b^3/d_j <1/(2d_j).      (5.2)

For the last comparison use

    c^2*b^3 <(17/7)^2*(3/7)^3=7803/16807<1/2.

### 5B. The center between the two crossings does not cross

Now the first crossing is in the left H_l and the second in the right
H_l. Let X_0,Z_0 be one plus the masses before the first and after the
second, within this smallest subtree. Thus

    A_u<=X_0<=A_l, A_v<=Z_0<=A_l.

The middle gap includes the entire immediate right H_u of the first
crossing, the level-l center, and the entire immediate left H_v of the
second. These three pieces are disjoint physical pieces. Therefore

    Y>=A_u+A_v+d_l>d_l.

The original gaps are X=X_0+E_L and Z=Z_0+E_R. Since X_0,Z_0<=A_l,

    XZ>=X_0*Z_0*(1+E/A_l)
       >(d_u*d_v/b^2)*(1+E/A_l).

Using d_l/A_l>=a and (3.5) then gives

    XYZ >(d_u*d_v/b^2)*(d_l/A_l)*(A_l+E)
        >a*d_u*d_v*A_j/(c*b^2)
        >a*d_u*d_v*d_j/(c*b^3).

Consequently

    p_n/R <c*b^3/(a*d_j)<1/(2d_j),                (5.3)

because

    c*b^3/a <(17/7)*(3/7)^3*(5/2)=2295/4802<1/2.

## 6. Arbitrarily many crossings: quantitative last-two capture

    p_n<=d_k*K/(1+T)<=d_k*K/A_k<b*K.                (6.1)

Suppose now that there are at least two crossings and the terminal is

    p_n<=d_k*p/A_k <b*p <b^2*K <gamma*K.            (6.2)

    (1+M)*p<=L<=d_l*K.

    (1+T)*p_n<=z<=d_k*p.

    p_n<=d_l*d_k*K/((1+M)*(1+N))<gamma*K.           (6.3)

The case l=0 has an empty immediate child and only the first alternative
