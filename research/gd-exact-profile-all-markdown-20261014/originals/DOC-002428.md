The conclusion is the following normalized statement. Fix a finite integer

normalized terminal score can be approached from below by scores from functions

    F_D(z) = max_{v in D} { <z,v> - ||v||^2/2 },

## 2. Interpolation anchors: signs and exact identities

Use the original samples `i=0,...,n`, including the terminal sample, and the
optimum sample `*` with `(x_*,g_*,f_*)=(0,0,0)`. The only accepted fact used is
the necessity of the ordered interpolation inequalities in local fact
`1252b8823bb5a290`:

    Q_ij = f_i-f_j + <g_i,x_j-x_i> + ||g_i-g_j||^2/2 <= 0.

The local fact file was read, and its exact verification record has
`verdict="correct"`, `write_error=null`, record ID `5480845b8a751e16`.
No other part of that large fact, including its SDP or attainment statements,
is needed here.

For completeness, the required necessity has a direct short proof. For fixed
`i`, the function `k(x)=f(x)-<g_i,x>` is convex and 1-smooth and has a minimum
at `x_i`. The segment-integral smoothness estimate at `x_j`, with step
`-(g_j-g_i)`, gives

    k(x_i) <= k(x_j-(g_j-g_i))
           <= k(x_j) - ||g_j-g_i||^2/2.

Rearranging gives exactly the stated orientation of `Q_ij`.

Put

    delta_i = f_i-||g_i||^2/2,
    ell_i(g) = <x_i-g_i,g> - delta_i,
    p(g) = max(0,ell_0(g),...,ell_n(g)),
    K = conv{0,g_0,...,g_n}.

The two optimum rows have different roles:

    Q_*i <= 0  gives  delta_i >= 0,
    Q_i* <= 0  gives  ell_i(g_i) >= 0.

Thus `p(0)=0`, while direct expansion, with no change of pair orientation,
gives

    ell_j(g_i)-ell_i(g_i)
      = f_i-f_j + <g_i,x_j-x_i> + ||g_i-g_j||^2/2
      = Q_ij <= 0.

It follows that `p(g_i)=ell_i(g_i)` for every sample, including `i=n`.
The nonnegative maximum with zero does not spoil this equality precisely
because the outgoing optimum row gives `ell_i(g_i)>=0`.

For any `g in K`, the active-anchor inequality `p(g)>=ell_i(g)` gives

    <x_i,g> - ||g||^2/2 - p(g)
      <= f_i - ||g-g_i||^2/2.                         (2.1)

At `g=g_i` equality holds. Therefore the finite dual interpolant

    phi(x) = max_{g in K} { <x,g>-||g||^2/2-p(g) }

has value `f_i` and unique maximizer `g_i` at `x_i`. It will follow below that
this maximizer is its gradient. Since `0 in K` and `p(0)=0`, `phi>=0`;
at zero all the maximized expressions are nonpositive, so `phi(0)=0`.

Every element of `K` is a convex combination of its listed generators. With

    M = max_i ||g_i||,
    Z = max(0,p(g_0),...,p(g_n)),

convexity of `p` gives `0<=p(g)<=Z` on `K`, and the triangle inequality gives
`||g||<=M`. This uses finite known values, not a hidden unbounded maximization.
Maximizing the difference of the affine expressions also gives

    |phi(x)-phi(y)| <= M ||x-y||.                    (2.2)

## 3. Smoothness with a compact, possibly degenerate dual domain

Here is an elementary argument that avoids assumptions of differentiability
of `p`, full dimension of `K`, or interior optimality. Write

    A_x(g)=<x,g>-||g||^2/2-p(g),
    a(x)=argmax_{g in K} A_x(g).

Continuity and compactness give existence, and strict concavity gives
uniqueness. Strong concavity on a line segment from the maximizer to any
`b in K`, followed by division by the segment parameter and its limit to zero,
gives

    A_x(a(x))-A_x(b) >= ||a(x)-b||^2/2.              (3.1)

This argument works at a boundary or in a lower dimensional affine hull.
Use (3.1) for `(x,a(y))` and `(y,a(x))`, and add. The result is

    <x-y,a(x)-a(y)> >= ||a(x)-a(y)||^2.              (3.2)

Thus `a` is firmly nonexpansive and in particular 1-Lipschitz. Evaluating
each maximum at the other point's maximizer gives

    0 <= phi(y)-phi(x)-<a(x),y-x>
      <= <a(y)-a(x),y-x> <= ||y-x||^2.

Consequently `phi` is differentiable with `grad phi=a`, and is globally
1-smooth. Its convexity follows directly from the maximum of affine functions
in `x`. Equation (2.1) now proves both exact values and exact gradients at
ALL the original samples. Induction in the ordinary GD recurrence therefore
reproduces the original finite run for `phi`.

The same elementary argument, with `p=0` and any compact convex domain `D`,
proves differentiability and 1-smoothness of `F_D`. Completing the square in
its defining maximum identifies its unique maximizing vector as `P_D(z)`:

    F_D(z)=||z||^2/2-dist(z,D)^2/2,
    grad F_D(z)=P_D(z).

Convexity here comes from the affine maximum, not from an unjustified claim
that a difference of two convex functions is convex.

## 4. Finite polytope, complete minimum set, and nearby minimizer

For every `R>0`, set

    D_R = { (g,u): g in K, p(g)/R <= u <= Z/R }.

The lower and upper bounds are compatible because `p<=Z` on `K`.
The set is nonempty, closed, bounded, convex, and contains `(0,0)`.
For an explicit finite-polytope justification, start from the finite convex
hull `K x [0,Z/R]`, and intersect successively with the finitely many
halfspaces

    R u >= ell_i(g),  i=0,...,n.

The condition `u>=0` is already imposed by the product. Cutting a finite
convex hull by a halfspace leaves a finite convex hull: retain its feasible
generators and add intersections with the cutting hyperplane of segments
joining feasible and infeasible generators. One can see this by pairing the
positive and negative signed contributions to the hyperplane inequality in
a convex combination. Repeating the finite operation proves the claim
without a full-dimensionality hypothesis. When `Z=0`, the result is exactly
`K x {0}` because `p=0` throughout `K`. If `K={0}`, it is simply `{(0,0)}`.

For any compact convex `D` containing zero, the entire minimum set of `F_D`
is the cone

    C_D = { z : <z,v> <= 0 for all v in D }.          (4.1)

This is the zero-threshold polar CONE, not the body polar with threshold one.
Indeed, `F_D>=0`, and membership in this cone makes all the candidate values
nonpositive. Conversely, if `<z,v> > 0` for some `v in D`, then `v!=0` and
`tau v in D` for `0<=tau<=1`. For sufficiently small positive `tau`,

    <z,tau v>-||tau v||^2/2
      = tau <z,v> - tau^2 ||v||^2/2 > 0.

Hence no such `z` can be a minimizer. This proves (4.1) in both directions.

All elements of `D_R` have `u>=0`, so the fixed point

    z_star=(0,-R)

belongs to `C_{D_R}`. For `z_0=(x_0,-R)`, the exact initial distance to this
actual minimizer is `||z_0-z_star||=||x_0||`. A large coordinate has not been
charged to the denominator. The proof does not replace the actual minimum
set by the singleton `{0}`.

## 5. Actual GD dynamics and the moving added coordinate

At a point `(X,-Rt)` with `t>=1`, for fixed `g` the derivative of the objective
with respect to `u>=0` is `-Rt-u<0`. The optimizing `u` is therefore exactly
`p(g)/R`. The maximization becomes

    F_R(X,-Rt)
      = max_{g in K} { A_X(g) - d_{R,t}(g) },
    d_{R,t}(g) = (t-1)p(g) + p(g)^2/(2R^2).

Let its maximizing horizontal coordinate be `b_R(X,t)`. Uniqueness also
follows from uniqueness of the full projection onto `D_R`. Its full gradient
is

    ( b_R(X,t), p(b_R(X,t))/R ).

Starting with `X_0=x_0`, `t_0=1`, the SAME ordinary GD word gives

    X_{i+1}=X_i-h_i b_R(X_i,t_i),
    t_{i+1}=t_i+h_i p(b_R(X_i,t_i))/R^2.              (5.1)

The plus sign in the second equation is required: the vertical position
becomes more negative. If `S=sum_{i<n} h_i`, induction using `h_i>=0` gives

    1 <= t_i <= 1+SZ/R^2,  0<=i<=n.                 (5.2)

This proves at the same time that the region `t>=1` used to eliminate `u`
contains every actual iterate; there is no circular fixed-slice assumption.
The absolute displacement of the added coordinate from its INITIAL value is
at most `SZ/R`, not zero. It vanishes as `R` grows for this fixed run.

No projection step is substituted for GD: projection is the gradient of the
single static function `F_R`, and the algorithm still subtracts `h_i` times
that full gradient. Neither the word nor the body changes during the run.

## 6. Independent whole-run error derivation

On every visited `t_i`, (5.2) and `0<=p<=Z` give the uniform bound

    0 <= d_{R,t_i}(g) <= Delta_R := (S+1/2)Z^2/R^2,
    for every g in K.                               (6.1)

Fix such a visited pair `(X,t)`, put `a=grad phi(X)` and `b=b_R(X,t)`.
Optimality in the PERTURBED maximum gives

    A_X(b)-d(b) >= A_X(a)-d(a).

Combining this with (3.1) yields, with the indicated sign,

    ||a-b||^2/2 <= A_X(a)-A_X(b)
                <= d(a)-d(b) <= Delta_R.            (6.2)

In particular, the source does not need the looser bound `2 Delta_R` on the
right: the nonnegative perturbation lies in `[0,Delta_R]`, whose oscillation
is at most `Delta_R`. With `A=Z sqrt(2S+1)`, (6.2) gives

    ||b_R(X_i,t_i)-grad phi(X_i)|| <= A/R.

Let `E_i=||X_i-x_i||` and `Pi_i=product_{j<i}(1+h_j)`. Using (5.1), the original
GD update, interpolation of `g_i`, and 1-Lipschitz continuity of `grad phi`,

    E_{i+1} <= (1+h_i)E_i + h_i A/R,
    E_0=0.

Induction, including `h_i=0`, gives exactly

    E_i <= (A/R)(Pi_i-1).                           (6.3)

In fact the full iterates, after subtracting the nearby minimizer, satisfy

    ||(z_i-z_star)-(x_i,0)|| <= (A/R)(Pi_i-1)+SZ/R.

Thus the moving extra coordinate also converges to zero in the translated
trajectory; there is no hidden order-one displacement in that direction.

Taking maxima in (6.1) gives

    phi(X_i)-Delta_R <= F_R(z_i) <= phi(X_i).

Use (2.2), (6.3), and `phi(x_i)=f_i` to obtain

    |F_R(z_i)-f_i|
       <= M A(Pi_i-1)/R + Delta_R.

At the actual terminal iterate this is exactly the source's estimate

    e_R = M Z sqrt(2S+1)(Pi_n-1)/R
          + (S+1/2)Z^2/R^2.                        (6.4)

All constants are finite for each fixed finite actual run and each finite
word, however large its entries. Given `epsilon>0`, write

    C=M Z sqrt(2S+1)(Pi_n-1),  E=(S+1/2)Z^2.

Then `R>=max(1,2C/epsilon,sqrt(2E/epsilon))` makes each term of (6.4) at most
`epsilon/2`. This also works when either or both constants vanish. It proves
convergence without invoking an asymptotic interchange of unbounded words.

## 7. Own closest radius and equality of suprema

Set `r=||x_0||>0`. If `f_n>0`, choose `R` with `e_R<f_n`, so `F_R(z_n)>0`.
If `z_0` were a minimizer, its gradient would be zero and every GD step would
leave it fixed, for ANY finite prescribed steps. That would give terminal
value zero, a contradiction. Thus `z_0` is outside the closed cone `C_{D_R}`.

The distance

    r_R=dist(z_0,C_{D_R})

is positive. A closest point exists: zero belongs to the closed set, so a
minimizing sequence can be confined to a bounded ball, and finite-dimensional
compactness gives a minimizing limit. Because `z_star` is another member,

    0 < r_R <= r.

The lower numerator `f_n-e_R` is strictly positive. This sign matters when
comparing denominators, and it gives

    2 F_R(z_n)/r_R^2
      >= 2(f_n-e_R)/r_R^2
      >= 2(f_n-e_R)/r^2.                            (7.1)

Choosing `e_R<min(f_n,eta r^2/2)` makes (7.1) exceed
`2f_n/r^2-eta`. No claim that `r_R` equals or tends to `r` is needed.
Indeed, the normalized scores themselves need not converge to the original
score; the one-sided comparison is the correct assertion. One could already
obtain the required comparison using the selected minimizer `z_star`; the
own-closest comparison is a valid strengthening.

For `f_n=0`, the original score is zero, and the restricted supremum is
nonnegative. No ratio with `r_R=0` is necessary. In the original definition,
a start that is itself a minimizer but differs from the selected minimizer
also has score zero and causes no problem.

For precision, at smoothness one define the restricted supremum over every
finite dimension, every compact convex finite polytope `D` containing zero,
every `z_* in C_D`, and every `z_0!=z_*`. Every such `F_D` is a legal original
instance, so `W_n^poly(h)<=W_n(h)`. Conversely, (7.1) applies to each original
positive-score instance and each `eta>0`; its lifted instance is among the
restricted choices. Taking the two suprema yields

    W_n^poly(h)=W_n(h).

The quantifier order used is

    for every finite n and finite h>=0,
    for every original actual instance and every eta>0,
    there exists a finite R and one admissible D_R.

It is NOT a single choice of `R` for all instances or all words. Equality is
pointwise in `h`; no interchange of `inf_h` and an approximation limit is
used. Taking `inf_h` AFTER this equality gives equality of the corresponding
minimax formulations, but supplies no numerical or asymptotic bound for them.

There is also no hidden infinite-score issue. For any original instance,
Lipschitz continuity of the gradient at a chosen minimizer gives
`||x_i-x_*||<=Pi_i||x_0-x_*||`; smoothness at that minimizer then gives
`2(f_n-f_*)<=||x_n-x_*||^2`. Thus `W_n(h)<=Pi_n^2<infinity` at smoothness one.
The same bound applies to each lifted function with ITS closest minimizer.

## 8. Normalization and boundary cases

For an original `L>0`, chosen minimizer `x_*`, and minimum value `f_*`, apply
the preceding argument to

    tilde f(x)=(f(x_*+x)-f_*)/L.

Its gradient is `grad f(x_*+x)/L`, so its step `h_i` is exactly the original
step `h_i/L` after translation. Restore scale by using `L F_D` in the
original problem: its gradient is `L P_D`, and the update `h_i/L` again
gives the same lifted run. Its score is

    2[L F_D(z_n)]/[L ||z_0-z_*||^2]
      =2F_D(z_n)/||z_0-z_*||^2.

Accordingly, for general `L` the restricted functions are `L F_D`. A bare
`F_D` has gradient `P_D` and belongs to the normalized `L=1` statement.
The draft's final scaling paragraph supplies this qualification; it should
remain explicit in any standalone theorem statement.

The following cases require no additional hypothesis:

- `n=0`: `S=0`, `Pi_0=1`, horizontal error is zero and the value error is at
  most `Z^2/(2R^2)`. The single sampled point is also the terminal point.
- All steps zero: positions and `t_i` stay fixed; repeated sample anchors are
  harmless. The same estimate applies.
- Some zero steps: (5.1) and (6.3) simply leave the corresponding errors
  unchanged; no strict-Slater or distinct-point condition appears.
- Arbitrarily huge but finite steps: `S` and `Pi_n` may be very large, but
  remain finite, and the explicit choice of `R` remains valid.
- `Z=0`: convexity and nonnegativity force `p=0` throughout `K`; the lifted
  body is `K x {0}`, every perturbation is zero, and the run is exact.
- `M=0`: all sample gradients vanish. Each actual sampled point is a
  minimizer, all sample values are zero, and `D_R={0}`. The zero-score clause
  handles the closest-radius degeneracy.
- Lower dimensional `K` or `D_R`: all uniqueness and concavity arguments
  are in the ambient Euclidean norm restricted to a convex set, so no
  affine-span interior assumption is used.

Negative steps and an infinite horizon are outside the declared scope.
The present proof specifically uses nonnegativity to maintain `t_i>=1`.

## 9. Positive slack: analytic structural attacks

For `v=P_D(z)`, the defining maximum gives

    F_D(z)=<z,v>-||v||^2/2.

At any minimizer `a in C_D`, the outgoing optimum slack is therefore

    <z-a,v>-F_D(z)-||v||^2/2 = -<a,v> >= 0.          (9.1)

It is zero at `a=0`, but need not be zero at another minimizer. At the
specific nearby `z_star=(0,-R)`, use the exact second gradient component
from Section 5 to get

    -<z_star,grad F_R(z_i)>=p(b_R(X_i,t_i)).          (9.2)

The positive slack is thus retained exactly as stated. Moreover, `p` is
Lipschitz with constant `B=max_i||x_i-g_i||`, since it is a finite maximum
of affine functions with those slopes and the zero function. From (6.3),

    ||b_R(X_i,t_i)-g_i|| <= A Pi_i/R,
    |p(b_R(X_i,t_i))-p(g_i)| <= B A Pi_i/R.

Whenever the original outgoing optimum slack `p(g_i)` is positive, it stays
positive at this nearby minimizer for all sufficiently large `R`.

Here is a fully analytic attack on the possibility that choosing the OWN
closest minimizer might secretly force slack to zero. Take `n=0`,

    f(x)=x^2/4,  x_0=1,  g_0=1/2,  f_0=1/4.

Then

    K=[0,1/2],
    p(g)=max(0,g/2-1/8),
    Z=1/8.

The body lies in the nonnegative quadrant and contains nonzero horizontal
and vertical axis segments: `(g,0)` for `0<=g<=1/4` and `(0,u)` for
`0<=u<=1/(8R)`. Consequently its full polar cone is exactly the negative
quadrant. For `z_0=(1,-R)`, its unique closest minimizer is `(0,-R)`, at
distance one.

On the active segment `1/4<g<1/2`, the reduced objective derivative is

    1/2-g-(g/2-1/8)/(2R^2).

It is positive at the left endpoint and negative at the right endpoint.
Solving its linear zero gives the actual horizontal projection

    g_R=(8R^2+1)/[4(4R^2+1)],
    p(g_R)=R^2/[2(4R^2+1)] > 0.

Both inequalities `1/4<g_R<1/2` hold for every `R>0`, so this branch check
is exact. Direct substitution, using (2.1) with equality on that segment,
gives

    F_R(1,-R)=1/4-1/[32(4R^2+1)].

Thus the own-closest normalized score tends to `1/2`, while the outgoing
optimum slack at that OWN closest minimizer tends to `1/8`, not to zero.
This example supports the declared distinction; it is not a counterexample
to the reduction. All formulas in this example were derived analytically,
with no arithmetic program or solver.

A second structural test is `f(x)=||x||^2/2` under ANY finite word, including
overshooting words. Here `x_i=g_i`, every `delta_i=0`, every anchor is zero,
and `Z=0`. The resulting body `K x {0}` reproduces the entire finite run
exactly because it contains every `g_i=x_i`. This confirms that the
zero-height degeneration is compatible with large-step trajectories.

Finally, if `0` is in the relative interior of `D`, every vector in its polar
cone is orthogonal to `span(D)`: small positive and negative multiples of
every direction in that span occur in `D`, forcing both polar inequalities.
Equation (9.1) is then zero for EVERY minimizer. The positive-slack example
shows why the present construction cannot simply be declared a reduction to
that restricted representation. It does not disprove some separate equality
of suprema for a different restricted family; it establishes that such a
claim is absent from, and unsupported by, this proof.
