## 2. Static function, original normals, and terminal budget

Consequently the N frames w_s and their positive radial rescalings are

## 3. Exact binary factors and exact summation of all mixed characters

For an original index i, the number of level-l transitions before i is

    c_l(i)=floor(i/2^l)-floor(i/2^(l+1))
          =floor(i/2^(l+1))+bit_l(i).

Expanding the floor in the higher binary digits proves, without any
trajectory replay,

    Q_k=r_k product_(l<k) r_l^(2^(k-l-1)),
    sigma_i=product_(k<m) Q_k^bit_k(i).                (3.1)

For every integer exponent b, including negative b, and every binary
character xi, direct distributivity therefore gives

    sum_(i<N) sigma_i^b (-1)^(xi dot bits(i))
      =product_(k<m)[1+(-1)^xi_k Q_k^b].              (3.2)

All factors are positive before the character sign, so negative powers
are well defined. Equation (3.2) is an exact finite identity.

Gray bit l equals i_l xor i_(l+1), with i_m=0. Xoring from k to m-1
telescopes, proving

    chi_(tau_k)(Gamma(i))=(-1)^bit_k(i).               (3.3)

The Gray map is invertible and linear over binary xor. Consequently its
remaining nonzero characters are precisely the binary characters other
than the m singletons. Summing their COMPLEMENT by Walsh orthogonality
gives the exact full Gram kernel

    <w_i,w_j>=v-eta
       +sum_(k<m)[2^k/(N-1)-eta](-1)^(i_k+j_k)
       +eta N 1_(i=j).                               (3.4)

In particular the tiny terms are neither deleted nor replaced by an
unspecified perturbation. The diagonal in (3.4) is exactly T.

There is a useful exact original-tail identity. Partition i>t by the
highest bit k at which i and t differ. Such a block occurs exactly when
t_k=0, has 2^k elements, has equal higher bits, opposite bit k, and
uniform lower bits. The mean of (3.4) on this block is

    V_k=v+(N-3*2^k)/(N-1)-eta(m-k-1).

Hence

    C_t:=sum_(i>t)<w_t,w_i>
       =sum_(k:bit_k(t)=0) 2^k V_k.                  (3.5)

This partitions the actual chronological suffix; it does not reorder
the physical word.

For later use, the worst V_k is the top one:

    V_(m-1)=v-N/[2(N-1)],
    V_k>0 for k<=m-2 whenever v>=M/2.                 (3.6)

Indeed 2^k<=N/4 in the latter range, and
V_k>=v+N/[4(N-1)]-eta m>0. Here Kmix>=m and eta m<=1/N^2.
Thus all C_t are nonnegative if v>=N/[2(N-1)]. If only v>=M/2,
the sole possibly negative summand in (3.5) is the top one, giving

    C_t >= -N/[4(N-1)] >= -1/2.                      (3.7)

The small distinction between M/2 and N/[2(N-1)] is retained.

## 4. Closed finite product formula for the entire budget

The following exact formula applies to the whole law, including ALL
0<delta_0,delta_1<=16, all theta in [0,1], and all R>0. It is not
restricted to the sign regions proved later.

Define empty products as 1 and put

    P_l=product_(j<l)Q_j,
    D_l=product_(j<l)(1+Q_j),
    J_l=product_(j<l)(1+Q_j^(-1)),
    F_l=product_(j>l)(1+Q_j).

For every transition of level l, the lower bits of t are 1, bit l is
0, and the higher bits are free. By (3.1) and (3.5),

    X_l:=sum_(t:nu_2(t+1)=l) sigma_t C_t
       =P_l F_l [2^l V_l
                    +sum_(k>l) 2^k V_k/(1+Q_k)].     (4.1)

The positive step-weight sum is also a closed finite product expression:

    W_l:=sum_(t:nu_2(t+1)=l) B_t sigma_t^2
       =P_l D_l F_l
        +P_l^2 sum_(k>l) Q_k^2 J_k F_k
                         product_(l<j<k)(1+Q_j^2).   (4.2)

To derive (4.2), expand B_t sigma_t^2=sum_(i<=t)sigma_t^2/sigma_i.
The term i=t and terms whose highest differing bit is k<l sum to
P_l D_l F_l, using
1+sum_(k<l)Q_k product_(j<k)(1+Q_j)=D_l.
For highest differing bit k>l, t_k=1 and i_k=0. Higher common bits
give F_k, free lower i bits give J_k, fixed lower t bits give P_l^2,
and its free bits between l and k give product(1+Q_j^2). This is
exactly the second term. Highest differing bit l is impossible.

Set

    ell_l=(T/2)(1+r_l^2)+(H_l-1)r_l G_l
         =H_l r_l G_l+(T/2)(1-r_l)^2+2r_l E_l.

Then L_t=sigma_t^2 ell_l at level l. The whole terminal budget and
its denominator are therefore

    Z=(T/2)D_m+sum_(l<m) H_l X_l+sum_(l<m)ell_l W_l,
    H=J_m.                                           (4.3)

Here D_m in (4.3) means the local product product_(j<m)(1+Q_j),
NOT the unrelated word scalar 1+2S. Equation (4.3), together with
the explicit roots in (1.4), contains no evolving recurrence and at
most double sums over levels. It retains all physical steps, original
terminal rows, radial weights, and mixed characters. The proofs below
evaluate its sign over full infinite parameter domains; they do not
merely display it as a proposed future computation.

## 8. Mechanism II: the true later-child budget pays a large center jump

This mechanism extends the evaluated binary budget into genuinely
order-1/m theta. It uses the original law and dictionary unchanged.
Its new ingredient is the quadratic weight of the child AFTER the
center jump, rather than a small perturbation of theta=0.

Assume

    v>=v_c:=N/[2(N-1)], v<M.                          (8.1)

Then v>=M/2, since zeta/N^2<1/(N-1). All V_k, hence all C_t, are
nonnegative by (3.6). Thus the first part of Z is at least
(T/2)sum_i sigma_i, without needing an upper bound on any radius.
Every G_l with l<=m-2 is positive. Only the center may have negative
G_(m-1). Put

    g=1-v-eta(m-1), so G_(m-1)=-g.

If g<=0 every L_t is nonnegative and exclusion follows immediately.
Suppose g>0. Write d=N/4 and retain the ORIGINAL center and the
right quarter bridge, at t=2d-1 and t=3d-1. Let

    s=sigma_(d-1), q=Q_(m-2), Q=Q_(m-1),
    r_-=r_(m-2), r_+=r_(m-1).

The binary factors give exactly

    sigma_(2d-1)=q s,
    sigma_(3d-1)=Q s,
    Q=(r_+/r_-)q^2.                                  (8.2)

The true inverse-radius prefix satisfies B_(3d-1)>B_(2d-1), so

    W(3d-1)/W(2d-1)>(Q/q)^2,
    r_- W(3d-1)>r_+ Q W(2d-1).                       (8.3)

There is no reset of B, no deletion of the right child, and no
assumption that its radii remain bounded above.

Since G_(m-2)>v and H_(m-2)>H_(m-1)/c, (8.3) gives

    B_(3d-1)L_(3d-1)
      >[H_(m-1)/c] v r_+ Q W(2d-1),
    B_(2d-1)L_(2d-1)
      >=-H_(m-1)g r_+ W(2d-1).                      (8.4)

The omitted squared-distance terms are nonnegative. Hence the one
later original bridge pays the ENTIRE negative center term whenever

    Q>=c g/v.                                        (8.5)

All other B_t L_t are then nonnegative. This is an evaluated sufficient
condition, involving a ratio whose lower bound is supplied next.
