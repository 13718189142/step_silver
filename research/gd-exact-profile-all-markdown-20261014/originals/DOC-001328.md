## 2. The actual body and its support

Write [d]={1,...,d}, let e be the all-ones vector, and put

    E_d={v in R^d: <e,v>=0},
    a_i=(d+1-2i)/d,       1<=i<=d,
    K_d={g in E_d: sum_(i in A) g_i <= F_d(A) for every A subset [d]}.

The vectors a, K_d, and all norms in this document use the ordinary
coordinate Euclidean metric. No graph metric or rank-weighted metric is
substituted. If A and B have disjoint differences of sizes p and q, expanding
the quadratic cardinality formula gives

    F_d(A)+F_d(B)-F_d(A union B)-F_d(A intersection B)=2pq/d>=0.

Thus F_d is submodular. It is zero at the empty and full sets, and strictly
positive at every proper nonempty set. Its successive cardinality increments
are exactly a_i, with sum_i a_i=0 and

    sum_(i=1)^k a_i = k(d-k)/d.                       (2)

Every permutation of a is in K_d: its largest k-coordinate sum is (2).
The singleton and complementary-singleton inequalities give
|g_i|<=(d-1)/d for g in K_d. Thus K_d is nonempty, closed and compact.

For any w, take a permutation pi with
w_(pi(1))>=...>=w_(pi(d)). Summation by parts gives, for g in K_d,

    <w,g>
      = sum_(k<d) (w_(pi(k))-w_(pi(k+1))) sum_(i<=k)g_(pi(i))
      <= sum_(k<d) (w_(pi(k))-w_(pi(k+1))) k(d-k)/d
      = sum_i a_i w_(pi(i)).                         (3)

The permutation g_(pi(i))=a_i attains equality. Consequently

    K_d = conv{all permutations of a},
    sigma_d(w):=max_(g in K_d)<w,g>
              =sum_i a_i w_(pi(i))
              =(1/d) sum_(i<j)|w_i-w_j|.             (4)

For completeness, equality of the two bodies follows without assuming a
majorization theorem. If a point g in K_d were outside the convex hull of
the permutations, let v be its closest point in that compact convex hull.
The vector w=g-v satisfies <w,t-v><=0 for every point t of the hull, by
one-sided differentiation of squared distance. Hence <w,g> exceeds the
hull's support value, contradicting (3) and attainment by a permutation.
The last formula in (4) follows by counting that a sorted coordinate at
rank i has coefficient (d-i)-(i-1)=d+1-2i in the pairwise differences.

Because -a is a permutation of a, K_d=-K_d. It has nonempty relative
interior in E_d. Indeed, for g in E_d and |A|=k,

    |sum_(i in A)g_i|
        <= ||g|| sqrt(k(d-k)/d).

Since k(d-k)>=d-1 for 1<=k<d, every centered g with
||g||<sqrt((d-1)/d) satisfies every proper constraint strictly.

## 3. One static function, paid smoothness, and the closest radius

Freeze the single function

    Phi_d(x)=max_(g in K_d){<x,g>-||g||^2/2}.          (5)

This is the joint quadratic smoothing of (4). There is one common dual
vector g constrained to K_d; no separate smoothing of its individual edges
is substituted. Let P_d(x) denote the unique closest point of x in K_d.
Completing the square proves

    grad Phi_d(x)=P_d(x),
    Phi_d(x)=(||x||^2-||x-P_d(x)||^2)/2.              (6)

Here is a direct justification including differentiability. Compactness
and strict convexity of squared distance give a unique P_d(x). Its
optimality is equivalent to

    <x-P_d(x),g-P_d(x)><=0    for all g in K_d.       (7)

Sufficiency follows by expanding ||x-g||^2; necessity follows by taking
a small segment toward g. Applying (7) at x and x' and adding proves

    <x-x',P_d(x)-P_d(x')> >= ||P_d(x)-P_d(x')||^2.

Thus P_d is 1-Lipschitz. Evaluating the maximum (5) at each other's
maximizers shows that

    0 <= Phi_d(x')-Phi_d(x)-<P_d(x),x'-x>
       <= <P_d(x')-P_d(x),x'-x> <= ||x'-x||^2.

This proves differentiability, (6), and 1-smoothness. Convexity follows
from (5). Smoothness has no missing dimension factor: near zero on E_d,
P_d(x)=x, so the optimal global Lipschitz constant is exactly 1 for d>=2.
For prescribed L>0 use f=L Phi_d. Original GD with h_t/L then has exactly
the updates x_(t+1)=x_t-h_t P_d(x_t).

The complete minimizer set and its distance are

    argmin Phi_d = R e,
    min Phi_d=0,
    dist(x,argmin Phi_d)^2=||x-bar(x)e||^2,
    bar(x)=d^(-1)sum_i x_i.                          (8)

To prove the first equality, K_d subset E_d and 0 in K_d imply
Phi_d(te)=0 and Phi_d>=0. If z=x-bar(x)e is nonzero, choose small
epsilon in (0,2) such that epsilon z lies in K_d, using the relative
interior just proved. Its value in (5) is
(epsilon-epsilon^2/2)||z||^2>0. This excludes every other minimizer.
The closest point is the orthogonal projection bar(x)e, proving (8).
The mean of every GD trajectory is constant because P_d(x) is centered.

## 4. Exact projection from interval averages, with no projection oracle

Let y_1>=...>=y_d be the sorted coordinates of x and set z_i=y_i-a_i.
For 1<=p<=q<=d write

    av_(p,q)(z)=(sum_(j=p)^q z_j)/(q-p+1).

Define an explicit scalar array

    u_i = min_(1<=p<=i) max_(i<=q<=d) av_(p,q)(z).    (9)

Then the exact projection and residual, in the same sorted coordinates,
are

    g_i=y_i-u_i,      y-g=u.                         (10)

All minima and maxima in (9) are over the displayed finite integer sets.
There is no convex program, numerical projection, or unreported root solve
in (9). We prove the formula, including why this ordered calculation
satisfies ALL subset inequalities of the original body.

First consider minimizing ||u-z||^2/2 over the closed cone
u_1>=...>=u_d. Existence follows from coercivity and uniqueness from strict
convexity. For this paragraph let u denote that minimizer. Put

    R_k=sum_(i<=k)(u_i-z_i), R_0=0.

Perturbing all coordinates equally gives R_d=0. Increasing a prefix is a
feasible direction, so R_k>=0. At a strict jump u_k>u_(k+1), the same
prefix can also be decreased slightly, so R_k=0. Thus

    R_k>=0, R_d=0, R_k(u_k-u_(k+1))=0.               (11)

Conversely, a decreasing u satisfying (11) is the unique minimizer.
For any decreasing v, summation by parts gives

    <u-z,v-u>
       =sum_(k<d) R_k[(v_k-v_(k+1))-(u_k-u_(k+1))]>=0.

Adding ||v-u||^2/2 to this expression gives the squared-distance
objective difference, proving the converse.

Let [b,c] be a maximal constant block of u, with level t. At both
boundaries R_(b-1)=R_c=0, including the endpoints 0 and d. Hence

    t=av_(b,c)(z),
    sum_(j=b)^k z_j <= (k-b+1)t       (b<=k<=c),
    sum_(j=k)^c z_j >= (c-k+1)t       (b<=k<=c).      (12)

For i in this block, choose p=b in (9). Every average av_(b,q) with
q>=i is <=t: before c this follows from the prefix inequality in (12),
and after c it follows by adding full later blocks and a prefix of a
later block, all of whose levels are <=t. Equality occurs at q=c.
This gives the upper bound t in (9). Conversely, for every p<=i take
q=c. If p lies in this block, (12) gives av_(p,c)>=t. If p is earlier,
decompose [p,c] into a suffix of its first block and successive full
blocks; all their levels are >=t. This again gives av_(p,c)>=t.
Thus (9) equals t. This proves (9) for every i.

Now put g=y-u. By (2) and (11),

    sum_(i<=k)g_i=F_d({1,...,k})-R_k<=k(d-k)/d,
    sum_i g_i=0.                                    (13)

We also need g to be decreasing. Inside a constant block of u, its
differences are exactly those of y, so they are nonnegative. At the end
c of a block, (12) gives z_c>=u_c; at the first coordinate c+1 of the
next block it gives z_(c+1)<=u_(c+1). Therefore

    g_c=a_c+z_c-u_c >= a_c > a_(c+1)
          >=a_(c+1)+z_(c+1)-u_(c+1)=g_(c+1).

Consequently the prefix bounds (13) imply EVERY subset bound: the sum
over any k coordinates is no larger than the first k sorted coordinates.
So g belongs to K_d, in the real Euclidean metric.

Finally, for every v in K_d, summation by parts yields

    <u,v-g>
      =sum_(k<d)(u_k-u_(k+1)) sum_(i<=k)(v_i-g_i)<=0. (14)

At a nonzero jump in u, (11) says R_k=0, so the prefix of g in (13)
equals the body's bound, whereas that of v is at most that bound.
The total-sum term is zero. Formula (14) is exactly (7), hence g is
the true projection. Undoing the sorting permutation proves (10) for x.
Ties cause no ambiguity in the physical gradient: exchanging equal
coordinates of x preserves the problem and uniqueness fixes its projection.

An explicit form of the state on a maximal block [b,c] is

    u_i = av_(b,c)(y) - (d+1-b-c)/d,
    g_i = y_i-av_(b,c)(y)+(d+1-b-c)/d,    b<=i<=c.    (15)

Thus block coordinate differences in the projection equal the corresponding
differences of y. At a physical GD step h they change in the iterate by
the factor 1-h. In particular h>1 reverses every nonconstant old block.
Blocks may also interleave across their former boundaries. A claim that
the chronological GD blocks can only merge would therefore require an
additional argument, not a proximal-path monotonicity citation.

## 5. Explicit all-word trajectory, values, and all interpolation rows

Fix ANY finite nonnegative word h=(h_0,...,h_(n-1)), any d>=2 and any x_0
with R^2=||x_0-bar(x_0)e||^2>0. Let pi_0 list original coordinate labels
in decreasing order of x_0. At each physical t perform the following
mathematical recurrence:

    y_(t,i)=x_(t,pi_t(i)),
    z_(t,i)=y_(t,i)-a_i,
    u_(t,i)=min_(p<=i) max_(q>=i) av_(p,q)(z_t),
    v_(t,i)=(1-h_t)y_(t,i)+h_t u_(t,i),
    y_(t+1,k)=v_(t,sigma_t(k)),
    pi_(t+1)(k)=pi_t(sigma_t(k)).                     (16)

Here sigma_t sorts v_t in decreasing order; ties may be broken by the
original coordinate labels pi_t(i). For the terminal time evaluate u_n
by the same formula but apply no extra step. The gradient in physical
coordinates is

    g_(t,pi_t(i))=y_(t,i)-u_(t,i).

Equations (9)--(10) show inductively that (16) is EXACTLY
x_(t+1)=x_t-h_t grad Phi_d(x_t), with its original h_t. Sorting is an
orthogonal relabeling for that state only; pi_t lifts every coordinate
back to its physical label. No chronological step or enclosing parameter
is reordered. Means are preserved since sum u_t=sum y_t.

The true-distance normalized payoff of this single actual function is

    V_d(h,x_0)
       = [sum_i y_(n,i)^2-sum_i u_(n,i)^2]/R^2.       (17)

This is an all-word recurrence and payoff law with no remaining continuous
optimization. It is valid even if the number of blocks grows, shrinks or
changes nonmonotonically. It is not asserted to optimize over x_0 or d.
As a legal instance it gives V_d(h,x_0)<=W_n(h).

All ordered interpolation inequalities follow from the actual function,
not merely from adjacent consistency. For physical samples let

    G_i=P_d(x_i), Y_i=x_i-G_i, F_i=Phi_d(x_i).

Add the closest minimizer sample x_star=bar(x_0)e, G_star=0, F_star=0.
For EVERY ordered pair, direct substitution of
F_i=<x_i,G_i>-||G_i||^2/2 gives

    Q_ij=F_i-F_j+<G_i,x_j-x_i>+||G_i-G_j||^2/2
        =<G_i-G_j,Y_j><=0.                          (18)

The inequality is (7) at j because G_i belongs to K_d, including G_star.
At the optimum Y_star=x_star is orthogonal to K_d. Thus (18) includes
all remote, incoming-terminal, outgoing-terminal and both optimum rows.

## 6. One frozen growing calibration: every pair reverses, then enters the core

Let d=2r>=4. Define centered orthogonal vectors A and b by

    A_(2j-1)=A_(2j)=Abar_j=(d+2-4j)/d,
    b_(2j-1)=1/(2d), b_(2j)=-1/(2d),   1<=j<=r.

Freeze the primary initial point

    x_0=2A+b.                                       (19)

The body is still precisely K_d. For a useful scope extension of this
same body and pair pattern, we also prove the formulas for

    x_0=gamma A+b,             1<=gamma<=2.           (20)

This is a symbolic amplitude interval, not an executed parameter search
or a change of submodular structure. Formula (19) remains the selected
nonlinear calibration. Centering is exact. Therefore the closest initial
minimizer is zero, despite the full line of minimizers in (8).

The rank vector has a_(2j-1)=Abar_j+1/d and a_(2j)=Abar_j-1/d.
The initial point is decreasing: inside a pair its gap is 1/d, and
between pairs the gap is (4gamma-1)/d>0. Its z=x_0-a has entries

    z_(2j-1)=(gamma-1)Abar_j-1/(2d),
    z_(2j)=(gamma-1)Abar_j+1/(2d).

For gamma>1 the exact decreasing isotonic residual has r distinct blocks,
each equal to one adjacent pair, with levels (gamma-1)Abar_j. Indeed its
R_(2j-1)=1/(2d)>0, R_(2j)=0, and its block levels strictly decrease.
These are the full conditions (11). For gamma=1 all levels merge to zero.
It follows that

    G_0=A+b, Y_0=(gamma-1)A,
    F_0=(gamma-1/2)||A||^2+||b||^2/2.                (21)

For gamma>1 this is an actual nonquadratic query. Its residual Y_0 exposes
a face with r independent pair directions: the convex hull of the 2^r
vertices obtained by swapping the two ranks independently in every pair.
Each projected pair Abar_j plus/minus 1/(2d) is strictly between that
pair's endpoint ranks Abar_j plus/minus 1/d. The block state is therefore
not a formal or degenerate active-set prescription.

Let the first step be 1+alpha, with 0<alpha<1/2. The actual first iterate is

    X_1=(gamma-1-alpha)A-alpha b.                    (22)

For the frozen gamma=2, each pair has reversed. Sorting only exchanges
the two original labels in each pair, because the gap between successive
sorted pairs is (4-5alpha)/d>0. The sorted first point has entries

    y_(1,2j-1)=(1-alpha)Abar_j+alpha/(2d),
    y_(1,2j)=(1-alpha)Abar_j-alpha/(2d).

For every proper even k and odd k respectively, its exact prefix sums are

    sum_(i<=k)y_(1,i)=(1-alpha) k(d-k)/d,                     k even,
    sum_(i<=k)y_(1,i)=(1-alpha) k(d-k)/d+(-1+3alpha/2)/d,      k odd.
                                                               (23)

The identities follow by summing Abar_1,...,Abar_j, whose sum is
j(d-2j)/d, and then the possible first member of the next pair.
Since -1+3alpha/2<0, (23) puts X_1 in (1-alpha)K_d and strictly in the
relative interior of K_d. Thus G_1=X_1 and Y_1=0: all residual blocks
have merged into a single block. This is the exact nonlinear face-to-core
transition in dimension d, with a real rank reversal in every old pair.

The entire interval (20) also enters the core. The vector A is the
average of a and the permutation swapping each adjacent pair, hence A in
K_d. Also b in K_d: its total is zero and every k-coordinate sum is at
most k/(2d)<=k(d-k)/d for 1<=k<d. With t=gamma-1-alpha we have

    |t|<=1-alpha,       |t|+alpha<=1.

Central symmetry, convexity and 0 in K_d imply
tA-alpha b in K_d, proving the claim for (22). This proof does not
assume the same sorting permutation throughout the amplitude interval.

## 7. Unchanged physical hierarchy and every remaining update

For an integer m>=2 and a real tau with 0<tau<m, set

    lambda=1+sqrt(2), beta=1-tau/m,
    c=lambda(1+tau/m), alpha=beta/c,
    H_(0;m,tau)=(),
    H_(j+1;m,tau)=(H_(j;m,tau),1+alpha c^j,H_(j;m,tau)), 0<=j<m.

Every child uses the SAME enclosing (m,tau). The physical word is
H_(m;m,tau), of length n=2^m-1. Tau=1 is the original ruler family;
tau=1/4 is the preferred diagnostic. No shorter relabeled word is used.
For growing dimension take d=2^m in (19), although all conclusions below
hold independently for any even d>=4.

Let nu_2(k) be the exponent of 2 dividing a positive integer k, and let
s_2(i) be the number of ones in the binary expansion of a nonnegative
integer i. The concatenation gives the exact physical step at zero-based
index t:

    h_t=1+alpha c^(nu_2(t+1)),          0<=t<n.       (24)

To check this by induction, the bridge joining two length-(2^j-1) words
is at t=2^j-1 and has nu_2(t+1)=j. The left child keeps its indices.
An index of the right child has t+1=2^j+s for 1<=s<2^j, and
nu_2(2^j+s)=nu_2(s), since the power of 2 dividing s is smaller than 2^j.
This reproduces the same child steps with the same enclosing parameters;
the base case is the empty word followed by its first bridge.

For a prefix of i steps, count each power of 2 dividing the integers
1,...,i. This gives

    sum_(k=1)^i nu_2(k)=sum_(ell>=1)floor(i/2^ell)=i-s_2(i).

For the second equality, write i as sum_j epsilon_j 2^j; each binary
digit contributes epsilon_j(2^j-1) to the sum of floors. Therefore the
exact unit-quadratic prefix product is

    P_i=product_(t<i)(1-h_t)=(-1)^i beta^i c^(-s_2(i)).       (25)

In particular P_1=-alpha. For i>=1 define

    sigma_i=P_i/P_1=(-1)^(i-1) beta^(i-1)c^(1-s_2(i)).        (26)

Every i>=1 has s_2(i)>=1. Since 0<beta<1 and c>1,

    |sigma_i|<=1                 for every 1<=i<=n.          (27)

This is a statement about EVERY physical prefix, including those that
cross a large checkpoint. It is not merely a terminal product estimate.

Section 6 proved X_1 in K_d and the true first update. Equations (26)--(27)
and the central symmetry of K_d now prove the complete remaining trajectory
by induction:

    x_0=gamma A+b,  G_0=A+b,
    x_i=sigma_i X_1, G_i=x_i, F_i=||x_i||^2/2, Y_i=0,
                                                1<=i<=n.  (28)

Indeed x_i in K_d implies P_d(x_i)=x_i; the next original update is
(1-h_i)x_i=sigma_(i+1)X_1, and (27) puts that next point in K_d too.
There is no rescaling or forgetting of old planes, and no induction on a
surrogate child parameter. For the nonlinear gamma=2 start there are
exactly r initial residual blocks, a first pairwise rank reversal, and
then a single residual block for the rest of the actual word.

One can check every original interpolation row even more explicitly.
For i>=1, or i=star, rows whose second index j is >=1 or star have
Q_ij=0, and the same holds for Q_(0,j) at those j. The remaining rows are

    Q_(i,0)=(gamma-1)[sigma_i(gamma-1-alpha)-1]||A||^2<=0,
                                                   1<=i<=n,
    Q_(star,0)=-(gamma-1)||A||^2<=0.                         (29)

Here |sigma_i|<=1 and |gamma-1-alpha|<=1-alpha<1. Formula (29), together
with (18), pays all remote and terminal interpolation conditions.

## 8. Exact radius, payoff, and same-word obstruction

Orthogonality of A and b follows separately in each pair. Summing their
squares gives

    ||A||^2=(d^2-4)/(3d),  ||b||^2=1/(4d),
    R_gamma^2=gamma^2||A||^2+||b||^2
             =[4gamma^2(d^2-4)+3]/(12d).                    (30)

For the first identity, substitute d=2r and use
sum_(j=1)^r(r+1-2j)^2=r(r^2-1)/3, obtained by expanding the square and
the finite sums of j and j^2. This is textual algebra, not an executed
arithmetic check. R_gamma in (30) is the distance to the ENTIRE minimum
line in (8), not the norm to an arbitrary designated minimizer.

Let

    J_(d,gamma)(alpha)
      = [4(gamma-1-alpha)^2(d^2-4)+3alpha^2]
          /[4gamma^2(d^2-4)+3].                            (31)

This is exactly ||X_1||^2/R_gamma^2 by (22) and (30).
Since s_2(n)=m, (26) and (28) give the actual normalized terminal payoff

    V_(m,tau,d,gamma)
       =J_(d,gamma)(alpha) beta^(2n-2)c^(2-2m).              (32)

For the frozen initial point gamma=2 the explicit expressions are

    R_2^2=(16d^2-61)/(12d),
    J_(d,2)(alpha)
       =[4(1-alpha)^2(d^2-4)+3alpha^2]/(16d^2-61).           (33)

These are exact finite formulas for unbounded d and m; in particular
they apply to d=2^m without a dimension-dependent smoothness loss.

On the EXACT SAME physical word, the scalar function q(x)=x^2/2,
started at x_0=1, is a legal normalized instance with payoff

    Qunit_(m,tau)=P_n^2=beta^(2n)c^(-2m).                   (34)

There is no appeal to an exponent proxy or a reference on a different
word. Dividing (32) by (34) gives the exact comparison

    V_(m,tau,d,gamma)/Qunit_(m,tau)
