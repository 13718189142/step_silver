# Quantitative limit of the exact two-cut inner-return objective witness

Status: complete scoped candidate proof, not verifier-accepted. No new verifier
call. This bounds the explicit two-cut construction, not the entire scalar
class and not the unrestricted smooth-convex worst case.

## 1. The construction being bounded

Take a schedule split as (prefix,b,middle,d,tail), with nonnegative ordinary
block sums P,M,T and b,d>1. Choose 0<w<=1 and R>=w. The fixed derivative is

    g(x)=-v                 when x<=-v,
    g(x)=x                  when -v<=x<=w,
    g(x)=w                  when w<=x<=R,
    g(x)=x-R+w              when R<=x<=R+1-w,
    g(x)=1                  when x>=R+1-w,

where v=(b-1-R+w)/(1+M)>0. Start at X=P+R+1-w, and put z=(d-1)v.
Require (1+T)w<=z<=R. As in the complete construction proof, every ordinary
block stays on its indicated plateau, the checkpoints cross the minimum,
and the normalized terminal objective is

    J=[2wz-(2T+1)w^2]/X^2.                                  (1)

All inequalities below use just these displayed definitions. In particular
no unverified result is imported as a proof premise. An overall scaling of
the gradient/radii is immaterial to (1).

Since X>=P+R, z<=R, and completing the square gives
2wz-(2T+1)w^2<=z^2/(2T+1), we have

    J <= [z/(P+R)]^2/(2T+1) <= 1/(2T+1).                    (2)

Also w<=1 and R>=w imply b-1-R+w<=b, so z<=(d-1)b/(1+M).
The strict crossing and z<=R moreover give R<b and z<b: the latter follows
from z<=min(R,(d-1)(b-R)/(1+M)), where w<=1 was used. Consequently

    J <=2z/X^2 <=2b/P^2                                    (3)

when P>0. Combining the first inequality in (2) with the preceding bound on z
gives

    J <= [b(d-1)/(P(1+M))]^2/(2T+1)                        (4)

when P>0. These are distinct valid relaxations; the w<=1 restriction in (3)
is essential near the end of the schedule.

## 2. Uniform bound on the recursive hostile family

Put c=13/5, a=c^(-17), b_j=a c^j, and

    H_0=(),  H_(m+1)=(H_m,b_m,H_m),
    s_m=sum H_m=a(c^m-2^m)/(c-2).

For m>=19 let B=b_(m-1) be the central, largest step. Write eta=13/8.
Since (2/c)^17<1/40, direct substitution gives s_u>=eta b_u for all u>=17.
Any eligible selected step greater than1 has level u>=18. An occurrence of
b_u has a complete H_u immediately before and immediately after it, so its
entire prefix and tail sums are at least eta b_u.

Consider ANY feasible pair of selected checkpoints in H_m and ANY feasible
w,R in Section1. There are three exhaustive cases.

1. The first selected checkpoint is at or after the central B-step. Then
P>=s_(m-1)>=eta B and b<=B. Equation (3) gives

    J <=2/(eta^2 B).

2. The second selected checkpoint is at or before the central B-step. Then
T>=s_(m-1)>=eta B. Equation (2) gives

    J <=1/(1+2eta B) <=1/(2eta B).

3. The first selected checkpoint is strictly before the central step and the
second is strictly after it. The middle sum contains the central step, hence
M>=B. Also P>=eta b, T>=eta d, and d<=B/c. Equation (4) yields

    J <=d^2/[eta^2 B^2(1+2eta d)]
       <=d/(2eta^3 B^2)
       <=1/(2eta^3 c B).

Each bound is at most2/(eta^2 B). Thus, uniformly over every feasible pair,
every threshold, and every choice of ordinary blocks within H_m,

    sup J <=2/(eta^2 a c^(m-1))
           =[2c/(eta^2 a)] c^(-m).                        (5)

This covers the arbitrary lower-positive-plateau height and the retained old
positive ramp/area in the exact C10 object. It does not cover more than two
checkpoints, visits inside nonconstant ordinary blocks, or arbitrary core
curvatures; no such generalization is asserted.

## 3. The exact benchmark comparison, with an elementary bound included

Let C_n=S_circle(n)+1 and B_n=2S_bullet(n)+1 for the exact project recursions.
Their shifted joins are

    Omega(A,D)=(A+D+sqrt((A+D)^2+4AD))/2,
    G(A,D)=2A+D/2+sqrt(D^2+8AD)/2.

We prove the sufficient rough bounds C_n<=(n+1)^(4/3) and
B_n<=1+4n^(4/3) directly here. For positive A,D put
Z=(A^(3/4)+D^(3/4))^(4/3). The inequality Z>=Omega(A,D) is equivalent,
after normalizing A^(3/4)+D^(3/4), to

    t^(4/3)+(1-t)^(4/3)+[t(1-t)]^(4/3)<=1,  0<=t<=1.

To prove it set x=t^(1/3), y=(1-t)^(1/3), so x^3+y^3=1. The difference
between the right and left sides equals

    x^3 y^3 [1/(1+x+x^2)+1/(1+y+y^2)-xy].

Each reciprocal is at least1/3, and xy<=2^(-2/3)<2/3, so the bracket is
nonnegative. This proves the join inequality and, by induction in the exact
primitive recursion, C_n<=(n+1)^(4/3).

Next G(A,D)<=D+4A follows from sqrt(D^2+8AD)<=D+4A. In the dominant recursion
each left subtree has k+1 leaves for k steps and the right subtree has n-k-1
steps. Induction, together with u^(4/3)+v^(4/3)<=(u+v)^(4/3), yields
B_n<=1+4n^(4/3). The empty-horizon initial values are C_0=B_0=1.

At n=2^m-1, (5) and U_n^ZJ=1/B_n imply

    (sup J)/U_(2^m-1)^ZJ
       <=[2c/(eta^2 a)] c^(-m) [1+4(2^m-1)^(4/3)] ->0,

because c^3=2197/125>16. Therefore the exact two-cut inner-return objective
channel also cannot repair the recursive-family profile gap.

This does not undo its primitive-energy counterexample: the latter includes
weighted old-area residuals absent from the last-iterate objective. Nor does
it imply that H_m has a small unrestricted worst case; the coupled-coordinate
construction already shared provides a genuine divergent lower bound there.
