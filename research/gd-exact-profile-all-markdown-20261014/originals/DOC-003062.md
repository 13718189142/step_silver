## 15. A backward extension strengthens the middle triangle

This is a new joint DRAFT calculation in the seventh launch's final round.
It is not a fact and consumes no verifier or numerical allowance. All four
steps retain their order. Write

    h=(1+a,1+b,1+c,1+d), a,b,c,d>0,
    C=5+a+b+c+d, P=2+a, D=2+d, U=C-P,
    H=U-b=1+c+D, eta=b/H,
    kappa(a)=(1+a)(a^2+2a-1)/a,
    kappa_plus=max(kappa(a),0),
    R=P^2-kappa_plus, J=P^2-kappa_plus*(1+eta).

The preceding full-domain derivation gives a<1 and the b-cut gives
eta<=P/C, hence a*eta<1 (indeed a*P/C<3/7). Then a stronger necessary
inequality than the middle adjacent triangle is

    b*c*[R+b(P+C)] <= D*J.                           (43)

For a<=sqrt(2)-1 this is exactly(34). For a>sqrt(2)-1 the positive
term subtracted from the old triangle slack is strictly positive.
No relation between c and b, or saturation assumption, is imposed.

Here is the entire new interpolation check. The case D<=b*c is already
excluded by the explicit capped function in Section11. Thus D>b*c. Set

    K=b^2*c+U*(D-b*c)=U*D-b*c*H,
    r=b^2*c/K,
    u=(1,0,0), w=(r,sqrt(r*(1-r)),0),
    v=-(b*u+D*w)/(1+c).

We have 0<r<1, <u,w>=||w||^2=r, and 0 lies in conv{u,v,w}.
These are exactly the middle triangle vectors, but its initial prefix
will now change. Choose t in[-eta,1] and put

    q=(1+(a-1)*t)/a,
    z=t*u+sqrt(q-t^2)*(0,0,1).

The radicand is (1-t)(1+a*t)/a>=0 because a*eta<1. Define the actual
positions and gradients by

    x=(u+(1+a)*z, u, -b*u, D*w, w),
    g=(z,u,v,w,w),
    f_i=<x_i,g_i>-||g_i||^2/2.

The four GD equalities hold. Add the optimum jet(0,0,0). Each proximal
point y_i=x_i-g_i must support conv{z,u,v,w} at g_i. This suffices for
all ordered Q because Q_ij=<g_i-g_j,y_j>.

At i=0, y_0=u+a*z. Since t+a*q=1+a*t and every old vertex y in
{0,u,v,w} has <u,y><=1,

    <y-z,y_0>=(1+a*t)(<u,y>-1)<=0.

At i=1 and i=4 the proximal point is zero. At i=3 it equals(1+d)*w.
Its pairing with z-w is (1+d)*r*(t-1)<=0; pairings with u-w and v-w
are respectively zero and negative, as explicitly computed in Section11.

At i=2 let n=-b*u-v. The two old vertex comparisons are

    <w-v,n>=0, <u-v,n>=-b*(1-r)*c/(1+c)<0.

The remaining comparison with z is exact. Put A_num=D*r-b*c. Then

    <u,n>=A_num/(1+c),
    <v,n>=r*(D-b*c)/(1+c),
    A_num=-b*c*H*(D-b*c)/K<0,
    r*(D-b*c)=-eta*A_num.

The fresh coordinate of z is orthogonal to n. Consequently

    <z-v,n>=(t+eta)*A_num/(1+c)<=0.

The optimum comparisons follow as 0 is already a convex combination
of u,v,w. This checks every original ordered pair, including those
involving the modified initial gradient. Fact1252b8823bb5a290 realizes
the complete array by one global convex1-smooth function with minimizer0.
No gradient Gram from any other schedule has been reused.

The energy is ||x_0||^2-C^2*r by the directly derived identity(3), and

    ||x_0||^2
      =1+2(1+a)t+(1+a)^2*q
      =P^2+kappa(a)*(t-1).

Choose t=1 if kappa(a)<=0 and t=-eta otherwise. The initial squared
norm is exactly J. Nonnegative energy therefore requires

    C^2*b^2*c <= J*K.

Using K=U*D-b*c*H, C-P=U, and J*H=P^2*H-kappa_plus*U gives

    C^2*b^2*c-J*K
      =U*{b*c*[P^2-kappa_plus+b(P+C)]-D*J},

which proves(43). Compared to the original triangle's forbidden-side
expression, the new left-minus-right expression increases by

    kappa_plus*[D*(1+eta)-b*c]
      =kappa_plus*K/H.

This is strictly positive whenever a>sqrt(2)-1. Thus(43) is a genuine
strengthening throughout that region, rather than a relabeling of the
old test. This last algebraic comparison is a strength comparison only;
it does not by itself prove there exists a word passing every old test
and failing the new one.

For rational elimination on the a>sqrt(2)-1 branch, use

    R=a+3+1/a,
    J=R-kappa(a)*b/(C-2-a-b),
    c <= J*(C-3-a-b)/(J+b*[R+b(C+2+a)]),              (44)

where c+d=C-5-a-b and D=2+d. All denominators are positive: H>0,
J=||x_0||^2>0, b>0, R>0. The strict positivity of J follows also
because its fresh coordinate is nonzero at t=-eta. Equations(43),(44)
retain the independent variables C,a,b,d (with c recovered by the sum).
