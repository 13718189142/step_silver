# Exact boundedness threshold for small-step blocks followed by one long step
Status: complete candidate, UNVERIFIED. No additional verifier call.
This is a scoped matching upper/lower classification, not the unrestricted exact-profile target.

## 1. Statement
Let A=(a_1,...,a_l) be a finite list with0<=a_j<=1 and total s. Repeat the block(A,b) with fixed b>=0.
For every finite-dimensional convex differentiable1-smooth function with a minimizer, one complete block never increases the distance to that minimizer whenever
b<=s+2.                                                 (1)
Consequently W_(m(l+1))((A,b)^m)<=1 for every m>=1 in this range.

At b=s+2, a single fixed symmetric Huber objective gives, at every block endpoint, normalized error
(2s+1)/(s+1)^2>0.                                        (2)
For every b>s+2 and every m>=1, an explicit nonseparable convex1-smooth function in R^(m+1) realizes
W_(m(l+1))((A,b)^m)
 >=[(2s+1)/(s+1)^2][(b-1)/(s+1)]^m,                     (3)
which grows exponentially.
The lower constructions only require the ordinary steps to be nonnegative with total s; the upper implication(1) additionally requires each a_j<=1. The distinction is retained.

## 2. Self-contained inequalities for the upper bound
Normalize the chosen minimizer to0 and minimum to0.
The descent estimate follows by integrating the1-Lipschitz gradient along a segment. Applied to the convex function f(z)-<grad f(y),z>, which is minimized at y, it gives
f(x)>=f(y)+<grad f(y),x-y>+||grad f(x)-grad f(y)||^2/2.    (4)
Adding its two orientations gives cocoercivity.
Against a minimizer, (4) yields
<x,g(x)>>=f(x)+||g(x)||^2/2, f(x)>=||g(x)||^2/2.          (5)

Consider a small step x_next=x-alpha g with0<alpha<=1 and let v=g(x_next). Cocoercivity implies
alpha<g-v,g>>=||g-v||^2,
and hence <g-v,v>>=(1/alpha-1)||g-v||^2>=0.
Thus <g,v>>=||v||^2 and ||g||>=||v||. Formula(4) also gives
f(x)-f(x_next)>=alpha||v||^2.
A zero step has the same properties directly.

Let x_t be the ordinary-block points, y its endpoint, and k=g(y). Put P_t equal to the sum of ordinary steps preceding x_t. Summing the preceding estimates gives
||g(x_t)||>=||k||,
f(x_t)>=f(y)+(s-P_t)||k||^2.                             (6)
For one ordinary step of size alpha, (5) gives
||x_next||^2-||x||^2
<=-2alpha f(x)+alpha(alpha-1)||g(x)||^2.
Because alpha(alpha-1)<=0, (6) may be substituted into both terms. Summing all ordinary steps and using
2 sum_t a_t(s-P_t)=s^2+sum_t a_t^2
yields
||y||^2-||x_0||^2<=-2s f(y)-s(s+1)||k||^2.              (7)
For the long step z=y-bk, (5) gives
||z||^2-||y||^2<=-2b f(y)+b(b-1)||k||^2.
Together with(7), this is the precise block inequality
||z||^2-||x_0||^2
<= -2(s+b)[f(y)-||k||^2/2]
   +(b-s-2)(b+s)||k||^2.                                (8)
Both terms are nonpositive under(1), proving the distance assertion. Applying it at every block keeps the endpoint distance bounded by its initial value. Integrating the gradient from a minimizer also gives f(z)<=||z||^2/2, so the normalized endpoint objective is at most1.

No last-iterate convergence rate below the threshold is asserted here; (8) proves boundedness and its exact boundary.

## 3. Boundary witness
Let tau=1/(s+1) and
f(x)=x^2/2 for |x|<=tau,
f(x)=tau|x|-tau^2/2 for |x|>=tau.
Its derivative clips x to[-tau,tau], so it is convex differentiable1-smooth.
Start at x_0=1. Through an ordinary block the derivative is constantly tau and the points decrease from1 to tau, because the cumulative ordinary sum lies in[0,s]. The step b=s+2 then sends tau to
tau-(s+2)tau=-1.
On the next block the signs reverse. Thus block endpoints alternate between1 and-1 for all repetitions. Their normalized objective is
2tau-tau^2=(2s+1)/(s+1)^2,
establishing(2) on one fixed scalar function.

## 4. Exponential high-dimensional witness above the boundary
Assume b>s+2 and put
T=s+1, q=sqrt((b-1)/(s+1))>1, a=1/q in(0,1).
Construct independent Markov directions in R^(m+1):
zeta_0=e_0,
zeta_(i+1)=-a zeta_i+sqrt(1-a^2)e_(i+1).
Let
alpha_i=q^i/T, omega_i=alpha_i zeta_i,
J(z)=max{0,max_(0<=i<=m)<omega_i,z>}, f=env_1 J.
Start at X_0=T omega_0=e_0 and define
X_(i+1)=X_i-(s+b)omega_i.
We verify that every query in ordinary block i and its checkpoint has gradient omega_i, and that the terminal gradient is omega_m.

Set Z_i=X_i-T omega_i. A direct update gives
Z_0=0,
Z_(i+1)=Z_i-B_i(zeta_i+a zeta_(i+1)),
B_i=(b-1)alpha_i=Tq^2 alpha_i.
Write D_i=B_i(1-a^2)=T(q^2-1)alpha_i.
Then D_(i+1)=q D_i, so D_i=aD_(i+1).
Taking Markov inner products shows
<Z_i,zeta_j>=0 for j>=i,
<Z_i,zeta_j>=-sum_(l=j)^(i-1)D_l(-a)^(l-j)<=0 for j<i.
For the last inequality, consecutive terms cancel exactly because aD_(l+1)=D_l; an odd-length sum has a positive final singleton. Thus every old residual is nonpositive, and this fact has been proved for all lags.

At a query after ordinary cumulative sum u in[0,s], the proposed proximal point is
X_i-(u+1)omega_i=Z_i+d omega_i,
where d=T-u-1=s-u>=0.
The current plane has value d alpha_i^2>=0.
For a future plane j>i,
<omega_j,omega_i>/alpha_i^2=(-a)^(j-i)alpha_j/alpha_i=(-1)^(j-i),
so its value is at most the current value, with equality at even separation.
For a past plane j<i, its residual term is nonpositive and
<omega_j,omega_i>/alpha_i^2=(-1)^(i-j)q^(-2(i-j))<=1;
again it is at most the current value. Thus ALL planes, and the zero plane, are dominated. The active-plane subgradient condition gives grad f=omega_i, and the block update is exact.

At the terminal point X_m the same check has d=T-1=s>=0, giving
f(X_m)=(T-1/2)alpha_m^2.
The Moreau envelope is globally convex differentiable1-smooth by the proximal optimality and monotonicity argument. Its minimum is0 on the cone where every normal has nonpositive inner product. The origin is the closest minimizer to X_0: X_0=T omega_0 is a positive multiple of an active normal at0, so for any minimizing z,
<X_0,z><=0 and ||X_0-z||^2>=||X_0||^2=1.
Consequently
2f(X_m)=(2T-1)q^(2m)/T^2,
which is exactly(3).

The omega_i are independent, with nonzero adjacent inner products. Isolated hinges against the zero plane have noncommuting rank-one Hessian projectors, so this is nonseparable in any fixed orthonormal basis and uses genuinely growing dimension. The ordinary gradients are constant on each block, but the energies grow geometrically from block to block. This is not a sequence of independently selected Hessians.

## 5. Relation to the central target
The threshold b=s+2 is exact for boundedness of the repeated prescribed block with ordinary steps in[0,1]. It is not an exact formula for W at a finite horizon, and it does not cover arbitrary long-step chronology. In particular it cannot substitute for a uniform lower bound matching U_n^ZJ. It supplies a sharp structural test for one class, with matching global upper and high-dimensional lower arguments.
