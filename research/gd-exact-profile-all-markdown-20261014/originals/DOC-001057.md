Status: complete candidate proof, NOT verifier-accepted. No solver or new
fact_submit is used. Accepted facts1252b8823bb5a290 and b98bb807d514e80d
supply the interpolation convention and the benchmark definition/bound.
Every new phase, averaging, polynomial and conditioning estimate is proved

## 1. The actual assigned schedule and the entire parameter regime

For each integer m>=128 put

    s=1+sqrt(2),      c=c_m=s(1+1/m),
    a=a_m=(1-1/m)/c,  d_j=1+a c^j,
    H_0=(),          H_(j+1)=(H_j,d_j,H_j),
    n=2^m-1.

All parameters remain fixed as j varies within this horizon. Let h_pre be
H_m with its last d_0 removed, so it has n-1 steps. All n queried prefix
points, including the last point x_(n-1), will be retained.

Fix arbitrary finite constants U>0 and 0<v_-<=v_+. For each m allow ANY
real u_m and v_m with |u_m|<=U and v_-<=v_m<=v_+, and set

    alpha_m=1-u_m/m,     beta_m=v_m/sqrt(m),
    z_m=alpha_m+i beta_m.

Choose ANY real invertible2x2 matrix T_m with ||T_m e1||=1. Its norm and
condition number may depend arbitrarily on m. At the actual prefix query
indices use the elliptic polynomial data

    x_i=T_m(Re P_i(z_m),Im P_i(z_m)),
    g_i=T_m(Re[z_m P_i(z_m)],Im[z_m P_i(z_m)]),
    P_i(z)=product_(t<i)(1-h_pre,t z).

Assume that SOME values f_i make these same real jets and(0,0,0) fully
interpolable by a convex differentiable1-smooth function. For every such
interpolant F, with F(0)=min F=0, and every final step b in[0,2], we prove
the uniform estimate, for all sufficiently large m,

    2F(x_(n-1)-b g_(n-1))
      <= (2304/v_-^2)m exp[-2^(m+1)/(3m)].                (1)

The threshold in m depends only on U,v_-,v_+. Thus even with arbitrary
horizon-dependent metrics, values and free terminal completion, this
ENTIRE compact parabolic regime has payoff o(U_n^ZJ). The interval[0,2]
includes both the common comparison step1 and the original final d_0.

A necessary condition for legality, uniformly over the same regime, is

    u_m >= (1+A0)v_m^2-o(1),
    A0=sqrt(2)/(3-sqrt(2)).                              (2)

This is not an exclusion of all elliptic frequencies, an upper bound on
unrestricted W, or a resolution of the original exact-profile problem.

## 2. Complete-cycle averaging over actual early copies

For this proof abbreviate alpha=alpha_m, beta=beta_m, z=z_m and
M=T_m^T T_m. Then M is positive definite and M_11=1. Multiplication by z
has real matrix B=[[alpha,-beta],[beta,alpha]]. Define the full partial
word polynomials and copy multipliers

    p_j(z)=product_(ell<j)(1-d_ell z)^(2^(j-ell-1)),
    R_j(z)=p_j(z)(1-d_j z).

Every query position in H_j, in complex coordinates, is a product of a
subset of R_0,...,R_(j-1). Indeed the right copy in H_(j+1) multiplies
every old position by R_j, whereas the left copy leaves it unchanged.

The entire first H_(m-1) is contained in h_pre. It contains2^(m-2)
copies of the two-query word H_1=(d_0), with starting multipliers equal
to all subset products of R_1,...,R_(m-2). All these multipliers are
nonzero because z is nonreal and all d_j are real.

Consider the directed triangle star -> next query -> first query -> star
in any one of these copies. Sum the full interpolation inequalities on
that triangle, eliminating the values. Divide by the squared modulus of
its starting multiplier. Its legality becomes the same base triangle
tested with the rotated metric R_theta^T M R_theta, where theta is the
argument of the starting multiplier and R_theta is planar rotation.
Averaging all these ACTUAL cycle inequalities therefore gives legality
of the base triangle under one effective metric M_bar.

Put tau=tr(M)/2. Rotation averaging preserves its scalar part tau I. On
the traceless part, each binary copy average multiplies its norm by
|cos(arg R_j)|. To verify this directly, identify a traceless symmetric
matrix[[q,r],[r,-q]] with q+i r. Rotation conjugation multiplies it by
exp(+/-2i theta), and averaging identity and this rotation multiplies
its modulus by |(1+exp(2i theta))/2|=|cos theta|. Consequently

    M_bar=tau I+E,
    ||E||_op<=tau Gamma_m,
    Gamma_m=product_(j=1)^(m-2)|cos(arg R_j(z))|.          (3)

The bound on the initial traceless part uses only positive definiteness:
its eigenvalues have magnitude at most tau. It is uniform in conditioning.

## 3. A finite-horizon averaging estimate that includes resonances

For m>=128, the inequalities12/5<s<17/7 give
c<=2193/896<5/2 and a>=(127/128)/(5/2)=127/320>3/8;
also c>12/5. The bounds on s follow by squaring7/5 and10/7 to bound
sqrt(2). For all sufficiently large m, uniformly in our parameter range, alpha>=3/4,
alpha<=2, beta<=1/8, and |1-alpha|<=a/4. Thus

    alpha d_j-1=alpha a c^j-(1-alpha)>=a c^j/2>0.

Choose the principal factor phases

    phi_j=arg(1-d_j z)
          =-pi+arctan[beta d_j/(alpha d_j-1)],
    Phi=sum_(j>=0)2^(-j-1)phi_j=-pi+delta.

The series converges by the bounded phases and summable weights. Since
alpha<=2, alpha>=3/4 and a>=3/8, the arctangent arguments are bounded
between beta/2 and8beta. For sufficiently small beta, arctan t is between
t/2 and t for this range. Therefore

    beta/4<=delta<=8beta.                                (4)

As j grows, phi_j decreases to phi_infinity=-pi+arctan(beta/alpha).
The difference of its two arctangent arguments is exactly

    beta[d_j/(alpha d_j-1)-1/alpha]
      =beta/[alpha(alpha d_j-1)]<=8beta c^(-j).

Since arctan is1-Lipschitz,
0<=phi_j-phi_infinity<=8beta c^(-j). Direct summation of polynomial
multiplicities gives an unwrapped phase identity

    arg R_j=2^j Phi+epsilon_j,
    epsilon_j=phi_j-sum_(ell>=0)2^(-ell-1)phi_(j+ell),
    0<=epsilon_j<=8beta c^(-j).                          (5)

Choose k so that2^k delta lies in[pi/4,pi/2). For large m this integer
satisfies1<=k<=m-2 and2^k<=8/beta, by(4). Discarding earlier cosine
factors only increases the product. Because2^j Phi differs from2^j delta
by an integer multiple of pi, the1-Lipschitz property of |cos| and a
telescoping difference of products of numbers in[0,1] give

    Gamma_m
      <=product_(j=k)^(m-2)|cos(2^j delta)|
                          +sum_(j=k)^(m-2)epsilon_j
      <=[1/(2^(m-1-k)|sin(2^k delta)|)]
                          +8beta c^(-k)/(1-c^(-1)).      (6)

The product identity in the last line follows by iterating
sin(2t)=2sin(t)cos(t); its denominator is nonzero because
2^k delta is in[pi/4,pi/2). This calculation works unchanged at exact
dyadic resonance, where the numerator may vanish.

Let r0=log_2(12/5)>0. Since2^(-k)<=4delta/pi<=11beta, c<=5/2 and
c>=12/5, the bounds above imply, for an absolute constant C,

    Gamma_m<=C[2^(-m)/beta+beta^(1+r0)].                  (7)

For example c^(-k)=(2^(-k))^(log_2 c)<=C beta^r0, since beta<1 and
log_2 c lies in a fixed compact interval. Because beta is bounded above
and below by positive constants times m^(-1/2), (7) yields

    Gamma_m/beta ->0                                    (8)

uniformly in all the stated parameters. This is a FINITE-HORIZON bound
in this specific regime, not the false assertion that every fixed nonreal
parameter has a vanishing infinite tail product.

## 4. The averaged triangle forces the parabolic legality constant

Write d=d_0, e1=(1,0), v0=B e1, r1=e1-d v0 and v1=B r1. The base
triangle weight under a metric M is tr(M Z), with the symmetric matrix

    Z=(v1 v1^T)/2+d sym(v0 v1^T)
          +(v0-v1)(v0-v1)^T/2-sym(e1 v0^T)+(v0 v0^T)/2,
    sym(C)=(C+C^T)/2.                                  (9)

This follows by writing its three edge weights: ||v1||_M^2/2,
d<v1,v0>_M+||v0-v1||_M^2/2, and-<v0,e1>_M+||v0||_M^2/2.
For M=I, direct expansion gives

    tr(Z)=d beta^2-(alpha-|z|^2)(1-d alpha+d^2|z|^2).    (10)

We also need an error bound uniform in M. At z=1, Z=0 for every d.
When |z|<=2 and1<=d<=2, put w=z-1. Then
||v0-e1||<=|w|, ||r1-(1-d)e1||<=2|w|, and
||v1-(1-d)e1||<=7|w|; also||v0||<=2, ||v1||<=10.
Applying ||aa^T-bb^T||_op<=(||a||+||b||)||a-b|| to(9), and expanding
its mixed product, bounds its five differences from z=1 by respectively
38.5|w|,34|w|,56|w|,|w| and1.5|w|. Thus

    ||Z||_op<=132|w|<=264beta                            (11)

for all sufficiently large m, since |alpha-1|=O(m^(-1))=o(beta).

The averaged legal triangle from Section2 satisfies tr(M_bar Z)<=0.
Using(3),(11), and |tr(E Z)|<=2||E||_op||Z||_op, gives

    d beta^2-(alpha-|z|^2)(1-d alpha+d^2|z|^2)
                  <=528 Gamma_m beta=o(beta^2).         (12)

Here tau>0 cancels; no condition-number loss occurs. Now d tends to
sqrt(2), alpha tends to1, and |z|^2 tends to1, so the denominator
1-d alpha+d^2|z|^2 tends to3-sqrt(2)>0. Moreover

    alpha-|z|^2=(u_m-v_m^2)/m-u_m^2/m^2.

Divide(12) by beta^2=v_m^2/m. This proves precisely the uniform
necessary condition(2).

## 5. The full polynomial potential, with a uniform remainder

Put w=z-1, t_j=d_j/(d_j-1)=1+1/(a c^j), and N=2^m-1. For m>=128,
1<=t_j<4. Factor each complex magnitude exactly as

    |1-d_j z|=a c^j |1+t_j w|.

The level multiplicities satisfy sum_j2^(m-j-1)=N and
sum_j j2^(m-j-1)=N-m. Define

    L_m(w)=log(ac)+Re sum_(j>=0)2^(-j-1)log(1+t_j w),

where the logarithms use their convergent power series near1. For
|w|<=1/8, their cubic remainders are at most128|w|^3: bound the terms
of degree>=3 by2|t_j w|^3. Summing the linear and quadratic terms yields

    L_m(w)=log(1-1/m)+D_m Re(w)-(J_m/2)Re(w^2)+O(|w|^3),
    D_m=1+1/[a(2-c^(-1))],
    J_m=1+2/[a(2-c^(-1))]+1/[a^2(2-c^(-2))].            (13)

All remainder constants are uniform over the compact parameter regime.
Because w=-u_m/m+i v_m/sqrt(m),

    L_m(w)=[-1-u_m D_m+(v_m^2/2)J_m]/m+O(m^(-3/2)).     (14)

At the limits c=s, a=1/s, use s^2=2s+1 to obtain

    D=lim D_m=2+2/(2s-1)>5/2,
    J=lim J_m=6+4/(2s-1)+2/(4s+1)<15/2.                (15)

For the inequalities,12/5<s<5/2 implies2s-1<4 and
4/(2s-1)+2/(4s+1)<20/19+10/53<3/2. Also

    1+A0=3/(4-s)>15/8.

By(2),(15), the coefficient in(14) is at most

    -1-[(1+A0)D-J/2]v_m^2+o(1)
          <=-1-(15/16)v_m^2+o(1).

In particular legality forces L_m(w)<=-1/(2m) for sufficiently large m.

The exact finite product differs from its infinite weighted sum only by
a controlled tail:

    log|p_m(z)|
      =2^m L_m(w)-log(ac)-m log c
             -2^m Re sum_(j>=m)2^(-j-1)log(1+t_j w).

The absolute value of the last term is at most8|w|, since
|log(1+t_j w)|<=2t_j|w|<=8|w|. Therefore, uniformly over all legal
parameters under consideration,

    log|p_m(z)|<=-2^m/(3m)                              (16)

for all sufficiently large m. This includes zeros without a division;
in fact nonreal z makes every factor nonzero here.

## 6. Conditioning, the deleted last step, and free completion

The two initial optimum interpolation inequalities give
<x0,g0>>=||g0||^2. Since ||x0||=1, this implies ||g0||<=1. But

    g0=alpha T_m e1+beta T_m e2,

so ||T_m e2||<=(1+|alpha|)/beta<=3/beta. Hence

    ||T_m||_op<=1+3/beta<=4/beta.                       (17)

This bounds the actual two-vector realization despite arbitrary initial
conditioning. Let p_pre(z)=p_m(z)/(1-d_0 z), the polynomial of the
actual queried prefix. From Section3,
|1-d_0 z|>=alpha d_0-1>=a/2>=3/16, so

    |p_pre(z)|<=6|p_m(z)|,
    ||x_(n-1)||<=4|p_pre(z)|/beta,
    ||g_(n-1)||<=8|p_pre(z)|/beta.                      (18)

No condition at a later gradient is used. For any convex1-smooth
interpolant of these queried data, optimum interpolation gives
f_(n-1)<=<x_(n-1),g_(n-1)>-||g_(n-1)||^2/2. Smoothness along a final
step b in[0,2] gives F(x-bg)<=f_(n-1)-b(1-b/2)||g||^2<=f_(n-1).
Equations(16)--(18) now imply

    2F(x-bg)<=2||x||||g||
       <=2304|p_m(z)|^2/beta^2
       <=(2304/v_-^2)m exp[-2^(m+1)/(3m)],

proving(1) for EVERY completion and every final b in the stated interval.

## 7. Exact benchmark comparison and remaining scope

Acceptedb98bb807d514e80d gives B_n<=1+4n^(log_2 3). Since n<2^m,
the ratio of(1) to U_n^ZJ=1/B_n is at most

    (2304/v_-^2)m(1+4*3^m) exp[-2^(m+1)/(3m)] ->0.

The exponential dominates because2^m/m grows faster than any constant
multiple of m, which follows for example by successive doubling after
a fixed sufficiently large index. This uses the actual accepted B_n
bound, not a guessed dyadic benchmark constant.

The obstruction covers all horizon-dependent elliptic metrics in the
entire compact regime alpha=1-O(1/m), beta comparable to m^(-1/2).
It specifically closes the natural near-unit polynomial-growth attempt:
the same cycles that allow a free terminal completion force damping of
the chronological polynomial before that completion. Other imaginary
scales, other real parts, multiple modes and changing generators are not
covered. The arbitrary-schedule exact-profile target is unchanged.
