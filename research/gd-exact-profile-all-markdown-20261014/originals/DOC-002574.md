# Quantitative constraints on the full PEP certificate matrix

Status: new algebraic derivation under the updated assignment; not verifier-submitted. The initial selected submission was already used for fact dfde2fa326c908bf.

## 1. The actual certificate, including PSD

Normalize L=1, x_*=0 and f_*=0. Fix n>=1 and h in [0,infinity)^n. Put S=s(h), P_i=sum_{t<i}h_t for 0<=i<=n, P_*=0, and extend h by h_n=0. The P_i are prefix sums, not the polynomial notation Poly_i.

Use the signed interpolation inequalities

    Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2 <= 0,

with g_*=x_*=0. Let lambda_ij>=0 for all unequal indices i,j in {*,0,...,n}; put lambda_ii=0. A standard full PEP upper certificate at level q consists of

    out_i-in_i=2*1_{i=n}, 0<=i<=n,                                 (F)
    M_cert(q,lambda,h) >= 0,                                       (PSD)

where out_i=sum_j lambda_ij, in_i=sum_j lambda_ji, and M_cert is the matrix of the quadratic expression

    q||x_0||^2-2f_n+sum_{i,j}lambda_ij Q_ij

after substitution x_i=x_0-sum_{t<i}h_t g_t. Condition (F) cancels every f_i. The matrix is evaluated on (x_0,g_0,...,g_n). Because every Q_ij<=0, (PSD) proves W_n(h)<=q. This statement is only the sufficient certificate direction; it does not assume strong duality.

Set mu_i=lambda_{i,*}, nu_i=lambda_{*,i}, Nu=sum_i nu_i and U=sum_i mu_i. Summing (F) gives U=2+Nu. The matrix has block form

    M_cert=[[q,-mu^T/2],[-mu/2,A]].                                (M)

Here A is the full gradient block; it is not replaced by its diagonal or row sums. Positive semidefiniteness forces q>0, because q=0 would force mu=0, contradicting U>=2. Equivalently,

    Z_cert=A-mu mu^T/(4q) >=0.                                     (SC)

The equivalence follows by completing the square in the x_0 coordinate.

## 2. Exact chronological entries

For 0<=i<n define Forw_i=sum_{j=i+1}^n lambda_ij, and Forw_n=0. Direct expansion gives

    A_ii=out_i-1_{i=n}-h_i Forw_i.                                (D)

For 0<=i<j<=n,

    2A_ij=-(lambda_ij+lambda_ji)
          -h_j sum_{k=j+1}^n lambda_{ik}
          +h_i(mu_j+sum_{k=0}^i lambda_{jk}).                      (O)

Explanation: the squared-gradient differences supply minus the two directed edge weights off the diagonal, and half of out_i+in_i on the diagonal. In <g_i,x_j-x_i>, the coefficient of ||g_i||^2 is -h_i exactly when j>i. For the cross term <g_i,g_j>, i<j, a term based at i contributes -h_j if its destination is after j, while a term based at j contributes h_i if its destination is at most i or is *. Using (F) simplifies the diagonal.

Thus every feasible certificate satisfies, with z_i=A_ii-mu_i^2/(4q),

    z_i>=0,
    [A_ij-mu_i mu_j/(4q)]^2<=z_i z_j for i<j.                    (P2)

These are necessary consequences of the full Schur-complement PSD condition. They are not asserted to replace higher principal minors.

## 3. A flow-weighted quantitative Huber inequality

Let e denote the all-ones vector in R^{n+1}. Then the exact scalar identity is

    e^T A e=2S+1+sum_i nu_i(P_i+1).                               (E)

One way to check it is to substitute all gradients equal to a scalar t and x_0=1. Formally take f_i=t-(P_i+1/2)t^2. Then every Q_ij with finite i,j and every Q_{i,*} vanishes, while Q_{*,i}=-t+(P_i+1)t^2. The resulting polynomial identity in t is

    (1,t e)^T M_cert (1,t e)
      =q-(2+Nu)t+[2S+1+sum_i nu_i(P_i+1)]t^2.                    (T)

This is an identity in formal data; no assertion of interpolation for every t is needed to evaluate a PSD quadratic form. Its minimum over real t is nonnegative, so

    q >= (2+Nu)^2/[4(2S+1+sum_i nu_i(P_i+1))]
      >= (2+Nu)^2/[4(2S+1+(S+1)Nu)]
      >= 1/(2S+1).                                              (H)

The last inequality follows on multiplying through: with w=2S+1, the numerator difference is
w(2+Nu)^2-4[w+(S+1)Nu]=4S Nu+w Nu^2>=0.
All steps are exact for arbitrary nonnegative real h, including zero entries.

A more local version retains chronological residuals. Define

    drift_i=sum_{j finite or *}lambda_ij(P_i-P_j),
    tailnu_i=sum_{j=i+1}^n nu_j,
    R_i=2h_i+nu_i+drift_i+h_i tailnu_i-2S mu_i.                    (R)

The exact row identity is

    2Ae=2h+mu+nu+drift+h .* tailnu.                               (Rows)

The entrywise product term h .* tailnu is necessary when multipliers leave the optimum. It must not be discarded.
For a derivation, write c_i=-sum_{t<i}h_t e_t and c_*=0. The gradient part of each interpolation term is sym(e_i(c_j-c_i)^T), for finite i; the i=* terms contribute only squared gradients. Its product with e has first part drift/2. The second part is half of
sum_{i finite,j}lambda_ij(c_j-c_i)
 = -2c_n-sum_j nu_j c_j
 = 2h+h .* tailnu,
by the flow equations. The squared-gradient terms supply (mu+nu)/2. This proves (Rows).

Put tau=1/(2S+1), and define the nonnegative PSD evaluation

    eta=(1,tau e)^T M_cert (1,tau e)
       =q-tau-tau sum_i nu_i[1-tau(P_i+1)] >=0.                   (eta)

For S>0, P_i<=S implies

    Nu <= (q-tau)/(tau^2 S).                                     (Leak)

Furthermore, the i-th gradient coordinate of M_cert(1,tau e) is tau R_i/2. Cauchy--Schwarz for a PSD quadratic form, applied to this vector and the i-th coordinate vector, yields

    R_i^2 <= 4 A_ii eta/tau^2.                                   (Residual)

This is a quantitative restriction on the actual multipliers and PSD diagonal, not merely a recurrence for a scalar schedule statistic.

## 4. The exact Huber face

Assume S>0 and q=tau. The positivity of 1-tau(P_i+1)>=tau S implies nu=0 from (eta). The PSD quadratic form then vanishes on (1,tau e), so its entire matrix product with that vector vanishes. Consequently

    sum_i mu_i=2,
    2S mu_i=2h_i+sum_j lambda_ij(P_i-P_j),                        (B)
    Ae=(2S+1)mu/2,
    Z_cert e=0,    Z_cert>=0.                                    (Face)

The last PSD constraint is essential. The balance equations (B) alone are not a valid certificate.

If S=0, all steps vanish and W_n(h)=1, witnessed by f(x)=||x||^2/2. The division by S in (Leak) and the assertion nu=0 are not used in this degenerate case. Equations (F), (M), (D), (O), (E), (T), (Rows) and (eta) remain valid.

## 5. An exact pseudo-certificate test, and a feasible check

Take one step h_0=a>0 and demand q=1/(2a+1). If a full certificate exists, the exact-face formulas force nu=0. Put t=mu_0. Flow and (B) then give

    mu_1=2-t,
    lambda_01=2(1-t),
    lambda_10=2-t,
    0<=t<=1.

The two-dimensional Schur complement has zero row sums. Its first diagonal is

    Z_00=(2-2a)+(2a-1)t-(2a+1)t^2/4.                            (Z1)

For a>=3/2 this is increasing on 0<=t<=1: its derivative is
2a-1-(2a+1)t/2 >= a-3/2. Its maximum is therefore

    Z_00|_{t=1}=(3-2a)/4.

For a>3/2 it is negative, proving that no full PSD certificate at the Huber level exists, despite the nonnegative flow and row equations admitting t=1 for every a.

For 0<a<=3/2, t=1 gives

    mu=(1,1), lambda_10=1, lambda_01=0, nu=0,
    Z_cert=((3-2a)/4)[[1,-1],[-1,1]] >=0.

Thus the full matrix really is feasible and proves W_1(a)<=1/(2a+1); the canonical Huber witness makes this equality. At a=3/2 the Schur complement is exactly zero, reproducing the exact 1/4 objective bound.

For a=2, the same reduced data t=1 pass flow, all nonnegativity conditions and the Huber row equations, yet Z_00=-1/4. They would falsely claim W_1(2)<=1/5 if PSD were discarded; the legal quadratic f(x)=x^2/2 from x_0=1 has normalized terminal error 1. This is an exact hostile test of the scalar-only reduction.

## 6. Scope and next obstruction

The inequalities (H), (Residual) and (P2) are quantitative necessary conditions for every feasible full interpolation certificate. They do not rely on a global minimizer of the stepsize problem, differentiability of W, or stationarity, and therefore do not exclude unbounded or zero steps.

The all-schedule exact-profile problem is still open. Inequality (H) alone is only a refined sum-based Huber test. The mechanism worth pursuing is the interaction of the residual equations with the complete PSD matrix (SC), including the chronological entries (O). No claim is made that its row equations or its 2-by-2 minors suffice. Declaring the existence of an additional separating inequality of target strength would not close the problem.
