## 8. Arbitrary suffix and normalized lower bound
Run any prescribed finite suffix steps from X_m. After t suffix steps, induction using(17) shows
x^(t) belongs to X_m+V_(m-t L_prop)
as long as m-t L_prop>=2600. Indeed each update adds a scalar multiple of a gradient in the displayed larger span; the scalar can be arbitrarily large.

The guard G_0 uses normals0 and51, and is orthogonal to every omega_i with i>=51. Hence it remains CONSTANT throughout every allowed suffix, with value d_guard>=2/25. Its normal satisfies ||w_0||^2<=chi^2=36/25.
To transfer this affine lower bound through the envelope, note that J is at least both0 and G_0. For any lambda in[0,1], completing the square gives
f(x)>=lambda G_0(x)-lambda^2||w_0||^2/2.
Take lambda=(2/25)/(36/25)=1/18. At the terminal point this yields
2f(x)>= (2/25)^2/(36/25)=1/225.

2f(x)/||X_0-y_star||^2 >=(1/225)/(4356/625)

This proves(2) for every allowed suffix, including zero steps and arbitrarily large finite entries.

Independence of the omega_i and their nonzero adjacent correlations also show nonseparability: isolate each zero/plane hinge, whose envelope Hessian is the rank-one projector onto omega_i. Adjacent such projectors do not commute. Thus no fixed orthonormal coordinates separate the function.

## 9. Scope and exact-profile relevance
This gives a full-suffix construction on a precise unbounded-horizon class, with a number of arbitrary repair steps proportional to the prefix length. It replaces, rather than assumes robustness of, the witness refuted by the two-step annihilator.

Using the verified benchmark bound B_n>=2n+1 from fact0be8b54e0a33381c, the candidate implies W_n/U_n^ZJ>= (25/39204)(2n+1) on this padded-prefix family, tending to infinity. That consequence remains conditional on verification of this new candidate.

The unrestricted problem is still open here. No assertion says an arbitrary near-optimal schedule contains this periodic prefix, or can be transformed to it without cost. The new reusable mechanism is the explicit affine guards and the finite backward propagation estimate(17), with all matrix, value, minimum and suffix conditions included.
