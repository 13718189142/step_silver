## Lower bounds

Ye--Liu, [Silver Rate Is (Almost) Optimal for Gradient Descent,
v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09152v2), was reopened on September30.
Theorem1.1 bounds every nonnegative finite-horizon word, but its displayed
exponent includes an additional positive term of order
sqrt(log log n/log n). Equation1 uses this project's W normalization.
Its stated result therefore does not supply the requested leading
constant or exact B_n profile. This is the same source already scoped
in SOURCE_NOTES.md, not a newly discovered target-resolution theorem.
Sections2 and3.1 identify checkpoint selection, changing directions and
combining local functions as the construction interfaces; AppendixB.3
retains compactness, zero coordinates and splitting at a tight checkpoint.
Any renewed route must overcome the actual quantitative loss with one
static function and true initial distance, rather than merely restating
the exponent theorem. This last sentence is a research requirement, not
a claimed mathematical improvement.

## Recursive benchmark

The primary abstract for Liu--Chen--Jiang--Wang,
[Optimal Recursive Composition and Dyadic Phase Laws for Gradient Descent
with Predetermined Stepsizes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11788), still
describes optimization inside recursive frameworks and log-periodic
profiles. Its scope supplies no automatic identification with arbitrary
predetermined schedules. The prior direct HTML scope check in
SOURCE_NOTES.md remains the relevant warning about ambient certificate
classes versus recursively generated classes.
