## 2. Every atomic row, one global function, and true distance

The residuals are

 -(m-1), -a, -a-(1-rho)z+E.

At receiving sample1 the vertices0,V,W give

 -z, 0, -(1-rho)z+E.

At receiving sample2 the vertices0,U,W give

 -e, -e, -(1-rho)e+E.

The omitted repeated U vertex has the same normal, the receiving vertex

audit, including the positive E charge from the third coordinate.

For any finite collection of such projection jets, define

 K_body=conv(0,g_0,...,g_n),

 ||P_K_body(x)-P_K_body(x')||^2
       <=<P_K_body(x)-P_K_body(x'),x-x'>.

## 5. An original-row condition at every large bridge

Consider a node whose two children have at least two atomic leaves.
Let the LAST leaf of the left child have parameters with subscript L,
and let the FIRST leaf of the right child have subscript R. Write the
last leaf's overall scale inside the left child as b_scale>0. Its
endpoint and penultimate residual are isometric copies of

 b_scale W_L, b_scale y2_L,
 y2_L=c_L V_L+W_L.

The receiving energy is e_rec=b_scale^2 e_L>0. Direct projection onto
the W_L axis gives

 <W_L,y2_L>/e_L=sigma_L,
 ||(y2_L)_perp W_L||=c_L epsilon_L sqrt(eta_L/N_L).

Within the right child, the FIRST bridge joins its first two leaves.
If its excess is r_first and the second leaf's mass is A_second, the
source identity p_second=m_second U_second+a_second V_second+W_second
and the bridge alignment give

 m_second G4+a_second G6+G7=-r_first W_R.

Here W_R is embedded from the right child's first leaf, and G4,G6,G7
are its actual retained gradients, including all intervening scales.
Dividing by A_second produces a convex combination v_R of these three
gradients, because m_second+a_second+1=A_second. Thus

                 v_R=-tau W_R,
                 tau=r_first/A_second>0.             (17)

This vector identity is retained by EVERY subsequent left embedding,
regardless of earlier transverse choices. The initial vector of the
whole right child is the same isometric copy of p_R from its first leaf.

Apply the proposed parent alignment(3). The component of the weighted
new right gradient along the left endpoint gives exactly
r e_rec tau sigma_L d_R. Cauchy--Schwarz bounds its perpendicular
component below by-r e_rec tau u_L t_perp,R. Subtract the receiving
energy and use(7): the corresponding convex combination of ORIGINAL

        >= r tau [sigma_L d_R-u_L t_perp,R]-1.         (18)

This argument permits every real transverse contraction, every ambient
dimension and all earlier maps. It has not optimized a reduced Gram
matrix or deleted any interpolation rows.

For a node containing N_node leaves, the three rows in(18) have actual
indices Q_(2N_node+4,2N_node-2), Q_(2N_node+6,2N_node-2), and

## 9. Scope test: a different atom iterates with growing energy

It is false that every isometrically retained source-matched projection
tree must have vanishing core energy. Take the single global quadratic
F(x)=x^2/2 on R, x_0=1, and the atom h0=(2,0,0). Every sample has
g_i=x_i, y_i=0, f_i=x_i^2/2. Its endpoint is-1 and its mass is A0=3.
All original Q rows equal0, including the optimum. If desired, it is
also realized by(6), taking the finite convex hull of the gradients of
the final chosen trajectory; all sample points belong to this interval.

Define h_(j+1)=(h_j,1+r_j,h_j), A_j=3lambda^j,
r_j=theta A_j. The gradient-descent endpoint w_j from initial1 obeys

 w_0=-1, w_(j+1)=-r_j w_j^2.

The first equality is the literal atom. The second follows because the
left copy multiplies the initial point by w_j, the bridge by-r_j, and
the right copy again by w_j. Thus every child is an actual scaled
isometric copy on the SAME quadratic and the bridge obeys(2)-(3).
The true initial distance remains1. Its energy satisfies

 kappa_0=9, kappa_(j+1)=kappa_j^2,
                       kappa_j=9^(2^j).

This example violates any proposed extension of(4) to unrestricted
atomic projection trajectories. It does not violate the stated result:
its word, mass, and zero receiving residuals fall outside Section1.
It also supplies no improved schedule or uniform minimax converse.

## 10. A general boundary condition for a changed atom

For any proposed join of projection jets with the value structure(7),
let p_R be the right initial vector, R_R its
norm, and w_L the nonzero left endpoint. For any left receiving residual
y with positive energy e_rec=<g,y>, decompose y along w_L:

 a_axis=<y,w_L>/||w_L||,
 b_perp=||y-a_axis w_L/||w_L||||.

For EVERY convex combination v of retained right gradients, write
v_perp=v-(<v,p_R>/R_R^2)p_R. Alignment(3) and Cauchy--Schwarz give the
necessary original-row condition

 alpha[-a_axis <p_R,v>/R_R-b_perp||v_perp||]<=e_rec,
                  alpha=r||w_L||/R_R.                (24)

Taking the supremum of the left side over the actual retained convex
hull gives the strongest bound furnished by this single-row Cauchy
argument. This is still a necessary condition, not simultaneous
sufficiency for all receiving rows and the full transverse contraction.

The present proof uses the retained source combination(17) in(24),
then prices the leaf's transverse allowance by(11). A successful new
atom must change this quantitative relationship, the retained source
geometry, the source-matched join rule, or the value/residual structure.
The quadratic example avoids the receiving obstruction because every
y is zero. It illustrates an escape in geometry, while leaving the
original all-schedule objective-profile problem unresolved.
