# Direct closure attempt R4: static pasting and its unresolved radius tradeoff

Date: 2026-10-08. Status: UNVERIFIED analytical draft; original target OPEN.

This invocation used two substantive lines: an actual static pasting with one auxiliary coordinate, then a shared-coordinate replacement. It produced no complete affirmative or negative proof. The first construction below is an admissible hard function, with its original radius and every cross-history row paid explicitly. Its quantified failure concerns that construction, not the original conjecture or a family augmented by other witnesses. The second line did not produce a uniform all-word pasting.

No mathematical program, numerical evaluation, array, solver, additional model, or formal submission was used. Text and primary-source reads and manual derivation were used. No truth store was changed.

## 1. Target and normalization

For a nonnegative predetermined word \(h=(h_0,\ldots,h_{n-1})\), GD is

\[
x_{i+1}=x_i-h_i\nabla f(x_i)
\]

after setting \(L=1\). The supremum defining \(W_n(h)\) is over all finite dimensions, convex differentiable functions with 1-Lipschitz gradient and an attained minimum, every selected minimizer, and every nonzero initial radius:

\[
W_n(h)=\sup\frac{2(f(x_n)-f_\star)}{\|x_0-x_\star\|^2}.
\]

Let \(C_0=B_0=1\), and, for every integer split \(k+l=n-1\), let

\[
\Omega(x,y)=\frac{x+y+\sqrt{x^2+6xy+y^2}}2,
\quad
G(x,y)=2x+\frac y2+\frac{\sqrt{y^2+8xy}}2,
\]

\[
C_n=\max_{k+l=n-1}\Omega(C_k,C_l),
\qquad B_n=\max_{k+l=n-1}G(C_k,B_l).
\]

The required affirmative result is

\[
\forall\epsilon>0\ \exists N_\epsilon\ \forall n\ge N_\epsilon\
\forall h\in[0,\infty)^n:
\quad W_n(h)\ge\frac{1-\epsilon}{B_n},
\]

with \(N_\epsilon\) independent of \(h\). A negative resolution would instead require a rigorous full-function upper certificate with a fixed asymptotic improvement over \(1/B_n\). Neither was obtained.

The source examined was Zhang--Jiang, [v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12395v2), especially Sections 2.2, 3.1 and 3.2, Lemma 15 and the retained square in the dominant composition. These prove sufficient compositions; their reversal was not assumed. Proposition 24, also checked in that source, gives \(C_n\le(n+1)^\rho\), where \(\rho=\log_2(1+\sqrt2)>1\).

## 2. Calibration checked independently

For a word split at \(\alpha>1\), write \(x,y\) for the masses before and after the bridge, \(C=1+x\), \(b=\alpha-1\). The single fixed function with derivative

\[
f'(z)=\operatorname{clip}(z,-r,1),\qquad f(0)=0,
\]

started at \(C\), reaches 1 before the bridge and \(-b\) after it. With \(r=b/(2y+1)\), its whole suffix remains on the negative affine ray and

\[
\frac{2f(z_n)}{C^2}=\frac{b^2}{C^2(2y+1)}.
\]

Balancing this with the whole-word Huber witness \(1/[2C+2b+(2y+1)]\) yields

\[
(b+C)\{2b^2+(2y+1)b-C(2y+1)\}=0.
\]

The positive root is the stated \(\psi\) bridge. With \(r=b/(y+1)\), the terminal is \(-r\), the rows \(Q_{i,\star}=f_i-f_\star-\langle g_i,x_i-x_\star\rangle+\|g_i\|^2/2\) are zero, and the primitive retained energy is

\[
\frac12\{C^2-(x+\alpha+y+1)^2r^2\}.
\]

Its zero is \(b(C+(y+1)+b)=C(y+1)\), the stated \(\varphi\) bridge. These are calibration, not a converse or a recursive construction.

## 3. Line 1: an actual static pasting of two complete histories

### 3.1 A concrete finite-dimensional function

For a finite set of gradients whose convex hull (K) contains 0, define

\[
F_K(X)=\max_{g\in K}\{\langle X,g\rangle-\tfrac12\|g\|^2\}.
\tag{1}
\]

The maximizing gradient is (P_KX). Its uniqueness gives differentiability, and the projection inequalities give

\[
\|P_KX-P_KY\|^2\le\langle X-Y,P_KX-P_KY\rangle.
\]

Thus (1) is convex and 1-smooth. Also (F_K\ge0=F_K(0)). A proposed jet ((X_i,g_i)) is realized, with value (\langle X_i,g_i\rangle-\|g_i\|^2/2), precisely when

\[
\langle X_i-g_i,g-g_i\rangle\le0\quad(g\in K).
\tag{2}
\]

It suffices to check (2) at all generating gradients, including 0. This gives one fixed function after the whole word has been selected; it is not an adaptive reselection.

### 3.2 Inputs and the pasted trajectory

Let the prefix word have length \(k\), mass \(\sigma\), and partial masses \(s_i\), \(0\le i\le k\). Let its complete projection-type history be \((u_i,a_i)\), including its terminal gradient. Let the suffix word have length \(l\), mass \(\tau\), partial masses \(t_j\), and complete history \((v_j,b_j)\), \(0\le j\le l\). Both histories satisfy GD and (2), with their own 0 included. Their values are denoted \(F_A(u_i)\), \(F_B(v_j)\).

Fix any bridge \(\alpha>1\), and put \(d=\alpha-1\). Choose

\[
r\ge0,\quad\theta\ge0,\quad
\theta+(\tau+1)r\le d,\quad p>0,
\qquad T=(\sigma+1+\theta)p.
\tag{3}
\]

In the orthogonal space of dimension (d_A+d_B+1), prescribe the prefix jets

\[
X_i=(u_i,v_0,T-s_ip),\qquad A_i=(a_i,0,p),
\tag{4}
\]

and suffix jets

\[
Y_j=(c,v_j,(\theta-d+t_jr)p),\qquad
D_j=(0,b_j,-rp),\qquad c=u_k-\alpha a_k.
\tag{5}
\]

Each block has exactly its prescribed GD recursion, and

\[
X_k-\alpha A_k=Y_0.
\]

The coordinates called inactive here really are frozen along the displayed trajectory: their gradients are 0 in the relevant block. This is enforced by the global interpolation below, not assumed from a direct sum.

### 3.3 Every old row, cross row, and minimizer row

Take (K=\operatorname{conv}(\{0\}\cup\{A_i\}\cup\{D_j\})). Within each block, its projection rows are exactly the original child rows; the added gradient coordinate is constant. The rows against 0 hold because the original rows hold and the clock products are nonnegative by (3).

For a prefix normal against a suffix gradient, (2) is exactly

\[
-\langle u_i-a_i,a_i\rangle+\langle v_0,b_j\rangle
-(\sigma-s_i+\theta)(1+r)p^2\le0.
\tag{6}
\]

For a suffix normal against a prefix gradient it is exactly

\[
\langle c,a_i\rangle-\langle v_j-b_j,b_j\rangle
-[d-\theta-(t_j+1)r](1+r)p^2\le0.
\tag{7}
\]

The original minimizer rows imply that both subtracted child inner products are nonnegative. Consequently define

\[
M_1=\max(0,\max_j\langle v_0,b_j\rangle),\qquad
M_2=\max(0,\max_i\langle c,a_i\rangle),\qquad M=\max(M_1,M_2).
\]

The explicit choice

\[
\theta=d/4,\qquad r=\frac d{2(\tau+1)},\qquad
p^2\ge\frac{4M}{d(1+r)},\quad p>0
\tag{8}
\]

satisfies all rows: each clock margin in (6)--(7) is at least (d(1+r)p^2/4). Thus (1), with this finite (K), realizes the full pasted history.

The child function values are not silently reset. The exact global values are

\[
F_K(X_i)=F_A(u_i)+(\sigma-s_i+\theta+\tfrac12)p^2,
\tag{9}
\]

\[
F_K(Y_j)=F_B(v_j)+[r(d-\theta)-(t_j+\tfrac12)r^2]p^2.
\tag{10}
\]

Thus the old child contributions and all their internal interpolation rows are retained, with an explicitly specified lift of the global values. The displayed global data are fixed before any step and remain unchanged afterwards. This is not a zero-cost pasting of the original unlifted jets.

### 3.4 The actual original-radius lower witness

Using the valid selected minimizer 0, the original radius and terminal value give

\[
W_{k+l+1}(h_A,\alpha,h_B)\ge
\frac{2F_B(v_l)+2[r(d-\theta)-(\tau+\tfrac12)r^2]p^2}
{\|u_0\|^2+\|v_0\|^2+(\sigma+1+\theta)^2p^2}.
\tag{11}
\]

Zero steps inside either child cause no difficulty in the formulas. Children may be empty. The construction accepts arbitrarily large finite bridges (\alpha>1). It does not prescribe an arbitrary-word decomposition, and (11) has not been shown to match the exact (B_n) recursion.

### 3.5 An all-depth audit of this particular pasting

The auxiliary radius is a real loss, not just an overly distant selected minimizer, on the following family.

Write (\lambda=1+\sqrt2), (q=\sqrt2-1=1/\lambda), and (c_m=\lambda^m). Define the Silver words

\[
s_0=(),\qquad s_{m+1}=(s_m,1+qc_m,s_m).
\]

Direct induction gives length (2^m-1), mass (c_m-1), and, for (m\ge1),

\[
\prod_{h\in s_m}(1-h)=-1/c_m.
\tag{12}
\]

Take both child histories to be nonzero one-dimensional unit-curvature quadratic trajectories on (s_m), use the bridge (1+qc_m), and paste by (3)--(5). Their terminal quadratic ratios are (1/c_m^2). For any parameters satisfying (3), the clock-only terminal ratio is

\[
\frac{2r(d-\theta)-(2\tau+1)r^2}{(c_m+\theta)^2}
\le\frac{(d-\theta)^2}{(2\tau+1)(c_m+\theta)^2}
\le\frac{q^2}{2c_m-1}.
\tag{13}
\]

The full ratio in (11) is a weighted average of this ratio, the suffix quadratic ratio (1/c_m^2), and a zero contribution from the frozen prefix coordinate. Therefore the actual normalized error (E_m) produced by every such feasible pasted instance satisfies

\[
E_m\le\max\{c_m^{-2},q^2/(2c_m-1)\}.
\tag{14}
\]

For \(r>0\), these pasted functions have a unique minimizer in their three-dimensional space. Indeed \(\arg\min F_K=K^\circ\), since \(\nabla F_K=P_K\). Each nonzero Silver quadratic child contains positive and negative scalar gradients. The prefix inequalities for \(z\in K^\circ\) force \(z_{\rm clock}\le0\); the suffix inequalities force \(z_{\rm clock}\ge0\). Then both scalar child components must vanish. Thus \(K^\circ=\{0\}\), and changing the selected minimizer cannot improve (14). If \(r=0\), the polar cone instead forces \(z_B=0\), \(z_{\rm clock}\le0\). The initial prefix gradient gives \(u_0z_A+pz_{\rm clock}\le0\), so \(\langle X_0,z\rangle\le(T-p)z_{\rm clock}\le0\). Hence 0 is still the closest minimizer and the denominator conclusion remains valid.

For comparison with the exact benchmark, (G(x,y)\le4x+y), as (\sqrt{y^2+8xy}\le y+4x). Combining this with the source bound (C_n\le(n+1)^\rho), induction over the exact integer-split recurrence gives

\[
B_n\le4(n+1)^\rho.
\tag{15}
\]

In that induction use (a^\rho+b^\rho\le(a+b)^\rho), with (a=k+1,b=l+1). At (n_m=2^{m+1}-1), ((n_m+1)^\rho=\lambda c_m); (14)--(15) imply

\[
\limsup_{m\to\infty} B_{n_m}E_m
\le 2\lambda q^2=2(\sqrt2-1)<1.
\tag{16}
\]

This is an all-depth failure of the displayed pasted witnesses. It is not an upper bound on (W_{n_m}(s_{m+1})), and it does not exclude a proof that chooses a global Huber or quadratic witness instead. In particular, those alternative witnesses must not be discarded when interpreting (16).

The responsible cross row can also be seen without the final ratio. Choose the first child's terminal (a>0), so its start is (-c_ma). Its post-bridge frozen coordinate is (-da). Because the second child has zero quadratic residual, (7), applied to the old start and the suffix terminal, requires

\[
[d-\theta-c_mr](1+r)p^2\ge dc_ma^2.
\]

Since (r\le d/c_m=q), necessarily (p^2\ge c_ma^2/(1+q)), and (T^2\ge c_m^3a^2/(1+q)). The old prefix radius was only (c_m^2a^2). This explains why making the auxiliary coordinate larger eventually replaces the child information by a single-cut clock witness.

## 4. Line 2: shared evolving coordinates

### 4.1 Exact pasting without any extra initial coordinate

Suppose a projection-type prefix ends at (u_k=a_k=a\ne0). A bridge (\alpha=1+d) sends it to (-da). Embed a suffix history by an isometry and a common scaling so that its start is exactly (-da), and take the convex hull of the two sets of gradients and 0 in their shared space. This produces an actual global function (1) if and only if its cross projection rows hold. If they hold, there is no added original radius, and the terminal ratio is the suffix ratio multiplied by (d^2\|a\|^2/\|u_0\|^2).

This was attacked at the actual histories rather than declared as a gluing lemma. A unit-curvature quadratic prefix exposes the obstruction. If its queried jets include opposite points on a line and satisfy \(g_i=x_i\), \(f_i=\|x_i\|^2/2\), then every convex 1-smooth extension agreeing with those jets is forced to be the same quadratic on the segment between them. To see this, \(H(x)=\|x\|^2/2-f(x)\) is convex for convex 1-smooth \(f\); at both endpoints \(H=0\) and \(\nabla H=0\). Convexity forces \(H=0\), hence \(\nabla f(x)=x\), on the entire segment.

For (m\ge2), a quadratic prefix on (s_m), started at (-c_ma), has queried points (-c_ma) and (qc_ma>0), and terminal (a). The next bridge (1+qc_m) lands at (-qc_ma), inside that already forced quadratic segment. A following unit step reaches the minimizer 0 exactly and all later steps stay there. Adding dimensions with zero initial components and keeping these old jets cannot change that fact. Thus a recursive rule cannot extend this already chosen near-extremal prefix history through an arbitrary suffix at no cost.

This is a failure of a universal history-preserving child-extension claim. The original adversary may choose a different prefix history after seeing the entire word, so it does not disprove the target.

### 4.2 A realizable transverse perturbation, with its exact budget

To test whether a small curvature loss repairs that obstruction, let (0<\kappa<1) and let (u_0,\ldots,u_k\) be the GD history of the quadratic (\kappa\|u\|^2/2) on the prefix. Embed it with a new coordinate initially 0. Retain all old jets for (i<k), and replace only the terminal gradient by

\[
x_k=(u_k,0),\qquad
g_k=(\kappa u_k,\eta),\qquad
f_k=\kappa\|u_k\|^2/2.
\tag{17}
\]

The prefix GD positions are unchanged, because this terminal gradient has not yet been used. For every earlier jet, its two directed interpolation slacks against (17) are both

\[
\frac{\kappa(1-\kappa)}2\|u_k-u_i\|^2-\frac{\eta^2}2.
\]

Both directed minimizer slacks are

\[
\frac{\kappa(1-\kappa)}2\|u_k\|^2-\frac{\eta^2}2.
\]

Thus all these rows hold exactly when

\[
\eta^2\le\kappa(1-\kappa)
\min\bigl(\{\|u_k\|^2\}\cup
\{\|u_k-u_i\|^2:0\le i<k\}\bigr).
\tag{18}
\]

A repeated query \(u_i=u_k\) forces \(\eta=0\), which matters for unrestricted words including zero steps.

For completeness, these consistent finite jets have a concrete static realization. Include the minimizer jet ((0,0,0)), and, over all prescribed jets, set

\[
H(g)=\frac12\|g\|^2+
\max_i\left\{\phi_i+\langle x_i-g_i,g-g_i\rangle\right\},
\qquad
\phi_i=\langle g_i,x_i\rangle-f_i-\tfrac12\|g_i\|^2,
\qquad F=H^*.
\tag{19}
\]

The interpolation inequalities say that the (i)-th affine term is active at (g_i); hence (x_i\in\partial H(g_i)). The quadratic term makes (H) 1-strongly convex, so (F) is finite, differentiable and 1-smooth with the specified jets. The included 0 jet and its inequalities give (H(0)=0), (0\in\partial H(0)); thus (F(0)=0) is an attained minimum. Formula (19) realizes (17) before the bridge, without a function change afterwards.

The scale in (18) is the terminal distance, not the original radius. If a proposed prefix has \(\|u_k\|\le K R/C\), its bridge obeys \(\alpha\le A C\), and \(R=\|u_0\|\), then

\[
\alpha|\eta|\le AK R\sqrt{\kappa(1-\kappa)}.
\tag{20}
\]

For (1-\kappa=O(C^{-2})), this is only (O(R/C)) transverse displacement. In particular, the absolute statement “gradient of size (1/\alpha) costs curvature (1/\alpha^2)” cannot be applied to a normalized near-extremal prefix whose terminal radius is itself of order (R/C). No conclusion about the full future objective is inferred from (20) alone.

### 4.3 Changing the whole shared history

A two-mode quadratic provides a literal shared-history replacement, not an endpoint-only injection. For orthogonal eigenvectors

\[
v_1=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta),\qquad
v_2=(\sin\theta,-\cos\theta),
\]

curvatures \(\lambda_1,\lambda_2\in(0,1]\), initial point \(e_1\), and

\[
P_t(z)=\prod_{i<t}(1-h_i z),
\]

the actual quadratic trajectory has transverse coordinate

\[
y_t=\sin\theta\cos\theta[P_t(\lambda_1)-P_t(\lambda_2)].
\]

If (P_k(\lambda_1)=P_k(\lambda_2)=P\ne0), then (y_k=0), but its transverse gradient is

\[
\sin\theta\cos\theta P(\lambda_1-\lambda_2).
\]

All past values, gradients and the initial radius now belong to a single actual quadratic; (18) does not apply because the whole history changed. Keeping this quadratic for the whole word, however, gives exactly

\[
2f(x_n)=\cos^2\theta\,\lambda_1P_n(\lambda_1)^2
+\sin^2\theta\,\lambda_2P_n(\lambda_2)^2,
\]

a convex combination of scalar quadratic errors. Rotation alone therefore supplies no stronger witness than the scalar quadratic family. A nonquadratic suffix is still necessary for this proposed repair to do additional work.

One direct attempted static extension is

\[
F(X)=\sup_{g\in K}\{\langle X,g\rangle-\tfrac12g^T A^{-1}g\},
\tag{21}
\]

where \(A\) has the two curvatures and \(K\) contains every old quadratic gradient and 0. All old quadratic jets are retained automatically, and \(0\prec A\preceq I\) makes (21) 1-smooth. This gives real freedom to cap future gradients without paying a new initial coordinate. It also exposes the exact sticking point: whenever \(AX\) lies in the old gradient hull, its unconstrained maximizer is still available, so (21) is forced to take the original quadratic gradient at \(X\). The future cap cannot simply be placed across that hull.

### 4.4 A shared-history cap that does paste exactly

There is a concrete special case of (21) where the cap can be placed and all cross rows close with no auxiliary initial radius.

Let the actual two-dimensional quadratic prefix have Hessian \(A\), \(0\prec A\preceq I\), initial point \(e_1\), and points \(x_i\) with

\[
e_2^Tx_i\ge0\quad(0\le i\le k),\qquad x_k=P e_1.
\tag{22}
\]

All these are statements about the actual prefix trajectory. Its old gradients are \(g_i=Ax_i\), its values are \(x_i^TAx_i/2\), and its original radius is 1.

Suppose the bridge \(\alpha\), suffix mass \(\tau\), and numbers \(r>0,s\ge0\) satisfy

\[
P(e_1-\alpha A e_1)
=-sA^{-1}e_2-rA^{-2}e_2,\qquad s\ge\tau r.
\tag{23}
\]

Set \(b=-rA^{-1}e_2\), and use the single fixed function (21) with

\[
K=\operatorname{conv}(\{0,b\}\cup\{Ax_i:0\le i\le k\}).
\tag{24}
\]

Each old gradient is its unconstrained quadratic maximizer in (21), so every old point, gradient and function value is unchanged. For a suffix partial mass \(t_j\), prescribe

\[
X_j=-sA^{-1}e_2-rA^{-2}e_2+t_jrA^{-1}e_2.
\]

This is exactly the GD recursion if the suffix gradient is \(b\). Its metric projection normal is

\[
q_j=X_j-A^{-1}b=-(s-t_jr)A^{-1}e_2.
\]

For every old gradient,

\[
\langle q_j,Ax_i-b\rangle
=-(s-t_jr)e_2^Tx_i
-r(s-t_jr)\|A^{-1}e_2\|^2\le0.
\tag{25}
\]

The row against 0 is also nonpositive, and the row against \(b\) is zero. Thus \(b\) is exactly the maximizing gradient throughout the suffix, including its terminal point. All old-to-new rows hold because the old metric projection normals are 0; (25) checks the reverse direction. All data come from the one function (21),(24).

It follows, with the same original start and selected minimizer 0, that this admissible full-word instance has

\[
2F(X_l)
=r^2 e_2^TA^{-3}e_2
+2r(s-\tau r)e_2^TA^{-2}e_2.
\tag{26}
\]

No child was restarted and no initial coordinate or radius was added. The steps after the bridge may be any nonnegative word with the prescribed mass \(\tau\); individual zero or large steps do not invalidate the monotonic partial-mass check.

The parameter constraints in (23) can be checked without an unproved geometric lemma. Write

\[
m_j=e_1^TA^je_1,\quad D=\det A,\quad a_{12}=e_1^TAe_2\ne0.
\]

Solving the two linear equations in (23), or using Cayley--Hamilton, gives

\[
s=\frac{P}{a_{12}}(\alpha m_3-m_2),
\qquad
r=\frac{PD}{a_{12}}(m_1-\alpha m_2).
\tag{27}
\]

When \(P/a_{12}>0\), a sufficient and, apart from endpoint degeneracies, necessary interval for \(r>0,s\ge\tau r\) is

\[
\frac{m_2+\tau Dm_1}{m_3+\tau Dm_2}
\le\alpha<\frac{m_1}{m_2}.
\tag{28}
\]

The interval is nonempty for a genuinely mixed pair of distinct positive eigenvalues because \(m_2^2<m_1m_3\). That inequality follows by Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the eigenvalue moments.

Condition (22) also has a concrete sufficient prefix test. In the rotated two-mode setup of Section 4.3, let \(0<\lambda_1<\lambda_2\le1\), \(0<\theta<\pi/2\), assume

\[
P_k(\lambda_1)=P_k(\lambda_2)=P<0,
\]

and suppose exactly one prefix step exceeds \(1/\lambda_2\). Equality and the negative product imply that this unique step exceeds \(1/\lambda_1\), and that no other factor vanishes. Before that step both products are positive and their ratio is at least 1. After it both products are negative; their ratio is nondecreasing and ends at 1, hence is at most 1. Therefore \(P_i(\lambda_1)-P_i(\lambda_2)\ge0\) for every earlier query, giving (22). Also \(a_{12}<0\), so \(P/a_{12}>0\) as required in (28).

This sufficient test has a substantial restriction: (28) forces \(\alpha< m_1/m_2\le1/\lambda_1\), whereas the unique earlier sign-changing step exceeds \(1/\lambda_1\). The selected bridge is therefore smaller than that earlier step. It cannot be applied automatically to the maximum step of an arbitrary word. A unit bridge is also excluded in the genuinely mixed case because \(m_2/m_3>1\) when the positive eigenvalues are at most 1 and are not both 1.

Equations (22)--(28) are an actual compatible-prefix/static-suffix construction. What is missing is not its interpolation: it is a proof that every word needing a stronger witness admits such a compatible prefix or a recursively compatible replacement, and that the payoff (26), with all previous history retained, yields the exact \(B_n\) bound. No such selection or uniform loss sum was obtained. This special pasting therefore does not close the original target and is not asserted to do so.

## 5. Closure audit and stopping status

The auxiliary-coordinate construction passes static realizability, finite dimension, attained minimum, GD chronology, original-radius accounting, and explicit cross-history interpolation. The all-depth ceiling (16) applies only to its stated witness family and includes a minimizer-set check. For any prescribed \(L>0\), replacing a constructed \(F\) by \(LF\) gives an \(L\)-smooth function, leaves normalized GD unchanged, and leaves \(2(f_n-f_\star)/(L\|x_0-x_\star\|^2)\) equal to the displayed ratio.

The endpoint-only replacement has an exact realizable prefix (17)--(19), but no all-word suffix construction. Changing the whole shared history by an actual two-mode quadratic leads to the exact static cap (22)--(28), preserving every old value and the original radius under its explicit conditions. Those conditions have not been proved available for arbitrary words or closed under repeated pasting.

The missing work is the mathematical content of the original problem: a choice of globally compatible histories for every word, or a different sharp universal lower argument, together with a schedule-independent (1-o(1)) loss against the exact integer-split profile. It is not a technical interpolation check and was not assumed as a lemma. No full-function asymptotic upper certificate beating (1/B_n) was produced either.

This invocation stops at its one-call/two-line allocation. The original target remains OPEN; none of these drafts is submitted or accepted as a project fact.
