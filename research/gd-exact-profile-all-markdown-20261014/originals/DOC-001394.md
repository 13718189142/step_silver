## 2. Explicit inverse and the actual two trajectory directions

The inverse of T requires no assumed nonsingular submatrix. Define R by

    R_ii=1, R_ij=0 for i<j,
    R_ij=-h_j product_(k=j+1)^(i-1)(1-h_k) for i>j.      (5)

and i+1 give z_(i+1)=(1-h_i)z_i for i>j. Thus the proposed column is
the unique solution. All columns prove TR=I; a square matrix with a
right inverse is invertible (its columns span R^n), so R=T^(-1).
Equivalently the forward recursion itself supplies its unique inverse.

For v=Re the same recursion gives

    v_0=1, v_(i+1)=(1-h_i)v_i,
    v_i=product_(t<i)(1-h_t).                            (6)

## 3. A fixed, explicit orthonormal frame, including zero steps

Assume r>=1. Define the n-by-r Helmert matrix E, with columns indexed
j=0,...,r-1 and rows i=0,...,r, by

    E_ij=1/sqrt((j+1)(j+2))        if 0<=i<=j,
         -(j+1)/sqrt((j+1)(j+2))  if i=j+1,
         0                        if i>j+1.             (9)

Every column sums to zero and has squared norm1. For columns j<k,
the entries of column k are constant on the support of column j, so
their inner product vanishes. There are r orthonormal columns in the
r-dimensional space e-perp, hence E^T E=I and EE^T=I-ee^T/n.

If h is nonzero, set

    mu=e^T v/n, u=v-mu e, nu=u^T u>0,
    q=u/sqrt(nu), w=E^T v/sqrt(nu), eta_frame=1+w_0.     (10)

    w_0=(v_0-v_1)/sqrt(2nu)=h_0/sqrt(2nu)>=0,
    1<=eta_frame<=2.                                   (11)

For j=1,...,r-1 define the n-by-(r-1) matrix F by

    F_j=E_j-w_j(q+E_0)/eta_frame.                       (12)

coordinate vector in R^r, t=w+f_0, and H=I-t t^T/eta_frame. Since
t^T t=2eta_frame, H^T H=I and H f_0=-w. Its other columns are
orthonormal and orthogonal to w. Equation(12) is exactly E times
these other columns. Consequently

## 8. Optional physical coordinates that enforce chi>=0 automatically

In contrast, h itself is not a basic primitive tree. In every nonempty
primitive tree built from empty leaves the first step is at least
sqrt2. This follows by induction: a nonempty left child preserves
its first step; with an empty left child, right capacity C_right>=1,
and bridge excess d_right solving
d_right(1+C_right+d_right)=C_right, substitution of rho gives

    C_right-rho(1+C_right+rho)

The left side of the bridge equation is strictly increasing in its

it is not a claim about all other possible composition formalisms.
