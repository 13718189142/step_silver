## Fixed mathematical object and direct derivation

For enclosing m in {2,6,10,14}, put lambda=1+sqrt(2), beta=1-1/m,
c=lambda(1+1/m), a=beta/c. Define H_0 empty and
H_(j+1)=(H_j,1+a*c^j,H_j), j=0,...,m-1, keeping this SAME m throughout.
Thus n=2^m-1. Equivalently physical h_i=1+a*c^v2(i+1), 0<=i<n,
where v2(k) is the exponent of 2 dividing the positive integer k.
The equivalence follows by splitting the sequence at its central odd bridge:
left and right halves repeat H_(m-1), and the central index has v2=m-1.

Put r_tree=2/c, Zgeom=sum_(j<m)r_tree^j, w_j=r_tree^j/Zgeom. The full
space has an orthonormal coordinate for every internal binary-tree node
v of depth less than m. For each leaf s in {0,1}^m, the feature a_s has
coordinate sqrt(w_j)*(-1)^s_(j+1) at its depth-j ancestor and zero
elsewhere. Define F(x)=log(2^-m sum_s exp(<a_s,x>)).

The feature mean is zero: at each coordinate the leaves in its left and
right halves cancel. Distinct coordinate rows are orthogonal, since they
have disjoint support or the descendant's signs cancel inside an ancestor's
constant-sign support. Each row has positive norm. Therefore the feature
matrix has full row rank. Jensen gives F>=0, with equality precisely when
all leaf logits coincide; their mean is zero, and full row rank then forces
x=0. Thus the full minimizer is uniquely 0 and F_star=0.

Differentiation of the finite sum gives gradient E_p a and Hessian
Cov_p(a). For a real random variable in [u,v], its variance is at most
(v-u)^2/4: its second moment about the midpoint is at most that number,
and variance is the minimum second moment about a constant. Applying this
to every unit projection bounds the Hessian by one quarter of the squared
feature diameter. Leaves first diverging at depth j have squared distance
4w_j+2 sum_(k>j)w_k. The corresponding quarter distances decrease with j,
because w_j-w_(j+1)/2>0. Their maximum is (1+w_0)/2, denoted Ltree.
This is a globally valid gradient Lipschitz constant. It is also sharp:
the full row rank maps onto all zero-mean leaf logits, allowing probability
to approach equal mass on any selected pair of root-diverging leaves.
The covariance in their difference direction approaches Ltree.

Initialize only the left-spine coordinates x_(0^j)=log(2)/sqrt(w_j);
all other coordinates are zero. The TRUE initial squared radius is
R2=(log 2)^2 sum_j 1/w_j=(log 2)^2 r_tree^(1-m) Zgeom^2.
The second equality is the finite geometric-sum reversal identity.

A first-right-turn group at depth k<m contains 2^(m-k-1) leaves;
the final all-left group k=m contains one leaf. Its uniform reference
mass is q_k=2^(-k-1) for k<m and q_m=2^-m. If y_j=sqrt(w_j)x_(0^j),
their logits are ell_k=sum_(j<k)y_j-y_k for k<m and ell_m=sum_j y_j.
All nodes off the spine lie inside one group. When their coordinates
vanish, its leaves have equal logits and equal probabilities; hence its
two descendant halves give zero gradient on that coordinate. Induction
proves these coordinates remain zero at EVERY physical query.

Consequently P_k=q_k exp(ell_k)/sum_l q_l exp(ell_l), and the actual
spine gradient has coordinate sqrt(w_j)d_j, where
d_j=sum_(k>j)P_k-P_j. The complete actual step is
y'_j=y_j-(h_i*w_j/Ltree)d_j. No child restart or transition labels occur.
Initially q_k exp(ell_k)=1/4 for k<m and 1 for k=m; thus
P_k=1/(m+4), P_m=4/(m+4), F(x0)=log((m+4)/4).
Also sum_k q_k ell_k=0, so F=sum_k q_k log(q_k/P_k).

## Stable evaluation of this identical finite function

Let G_m=0 be the log partition of the final empty subtree. Splitting the
depth-j subtree into its right zero-field group and left spine gives
G_j=log((exp(-y_j)+exp(y_j+G_(j+1)))/2)
   =G_(j+1)/2+logcosh(t_j), t_j=y_j+G_(j+1)/2.
Thus F=G_0, with every term nonnegative. Evaluate logcosh(t) as
log1p(2*sinh(t/2)^2) for |t|<=20, and as
|t|+log1p(exp(-2|t|))-log(2) otherwise. This avoids the cancellation
of subtracting log(2) or a shifted normalizer near the minimizer.

The conditional left probability at node j is sigmoid(2t_j), the right
probability sigmoid(-2t_j). Starting at reach mass U_0=1, set
P_j=U_j sigmoid(-2t_j), U_(j+1)=U_j sigmoid(2t_j), and P_m=U_m.
Both sigmoid signs use exp(-2|t_j|), never a positive large exponent.
The exact derivative difference is d_j=U_j*tanh(t_j), evaluated in that
form to avoid cancellation between almost equal probabilities.
This is an algebraic evaluation of the SAME P, not a new update rule.
