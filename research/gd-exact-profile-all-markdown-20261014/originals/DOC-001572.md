## Line 1. A fresh bank of positive ridges cannot borrow saturated old curvature

### 1.1 The actual global condition, including unvisited regions

For fixed finite vectors (v_j\in\mathbb R^d), thresholds (t_j\ge0), and a symmetric (A\succeq0), consider the SINGLE static function

\[
 F(x)=\tfrac12x^TAx+H(x),\qquad
 H(x)=\sum_{j=1}^m \tfrac12(\langle v_j,x\rangle-t_j)_+^2,
 \qquad M=\sum_jv_jv_j^T.
 \tag{1}
\]

Every coefficient is frozen before the run. Positive scalar ridge weights can be absorbed into (v_j,t_j). The sufficient global condition is

\[
 A+M\preceq I. \tag{2}
\]

Indeed, the gradient difference between any two points equals

\[
 \nabla F(x)-\nabla F(y)=(A+\sum_j\theta_jv_jv_j^T)(x-y),
 \qquad 0\le\theta_j\le1,
\]

using scalar divided differences of (z_+). The matrix is positive semidefinite and at most (I); this proves global convexity, differentiability and 1-Lipschitz gradient. Moreover (F\ge0=F(0)), and its complete minimum set is

\[
 \arg\min F=\ker A\cap\{x:\langle v_j,x\rangle\le t_j\ \text{for every }j\}.
 \tag{3}
\]

If the ridge normals are linearly independent, (2) is also NECESSARY for this representation to be 1-smooth. The map (x\mapsto(\langle v_j,x\rangle)_j) is onto, so some open region has every ridge strictly active. Its Hessian is exactly (A+M).

The same necessity holds for independent one-sided Huber ridges, with any strictly positive cap for each ridge: choose all residuals strictly within their quadratic intervals. Therefore disjoint curvature intervals ALONG THE RUN do not remove the full global curvature condition when the fresh normals are independent. This observation concerns this additive representation, not all smooth convex functions.

### 1.2 The whole old hull, not only the last query

Suppose the proposed new bank has (H(x_i)=0) at all earlier actual queries and at the selected original minimizer 0. Then it vanishes on

\[
 K=\operatorname{conv}\{0,x_0,\ldots,x_k\}. \tag{4}
\]

This follows directly from convexity and nonnegativity. In particular (\nabla H=0) on (K), since every point of (K) is a global minimizer of (H). An actual first activation can occur only at a point outside (K). A fresh orthogonal coordinate does not on its own establish such an escape.

For a unit normal (u), a squared hinge zero on (K) needs a threshold at least (\sup_{z\in K}\langle u,z\rangle). Its positive residual at (y) is therefore at most (\operatorname{dist}(y,K)). This is sharp over unit normals for (y\notin K): take the outward normal from the Euclidean projection of (y) onto (K). The projection inequality supplies the supporting threshold, and Cauchy--Schwarz supplies the reverse bound.

The aggregate bank has the stronger matrix bound

\[
 2H(y)\le\inf_{z\in K}(y-z)^TM(y-z).
 \tag{5}
\]

For each (z\in K), each positive residual is at most (\langle v_j,y-z\rangle_+); sum its square and then discard the positive-part restriction. This proves (5), with no assumptions on the dimension or number of hinges.

If (x=x_k\in K), (g=Ax) is the old gradient at the last query, and the next ACTUAL point is (y=x-hg), then

\[
 2H(y)\le h^2g^TMg\le h^2g^T(I-A)g,
 \qquad 2H(y)\le y^TMy.
 \tag{6}
\]

The second inequality uses (0\in K). Also

\[
 \|\nabla H(y)\|^2\le h^2g^T(I-A)g, \tag{7}
\]

because (\nabla H=V^Tr), (r_j=(\langle v_j,y\rangle-t_j)_+), (\|V\|^2=\|M\|\le1), and the same residual estimate bounds (\|r\|^2). These statements remain valid for one-sided Huber profiles, whose value is at most the squared hinge and whose derivative is between 0 and the positive residual.

### 1.3 A sharp transverse budget, including its actual attained example

Let (e) be a unit vector, (0<\delta<1), and let

\[
 A=(1-\delta)ee^T,\qquad D=I-A.
\]

Consider a possible first crossing to (y=-re), (r>0), from an earlier collinear history on which the new bank was zero. For every static bank of the preceding type satisfying (2),

\[
 \|P_{e^\perp}\nabla H(-re)\|\le \frac{r\sqrt\delta}{2}.
 \tag{8}
\]

This includes arbitrary nonnegative thresholds and arbitrary positive Huber caps. To derive it, for each ridge write its derivative at (-re) as (\theta_j(-r\langle v_j,e\rangle)), where (0\le\theta_j\le1); ridges with nonnegative (\langle v_j,e\rangle) have derivative zero. Set (N=\sum_j\theta_jv_jv_j^T). Then (0\preceq N\preceq M\preceq D), and (\nabla H(-re)=-rNe).

For (T=D^{-1/2}ND^{-1/2}), one has (0\preceq T\preceq I), hence (T^2\preceq T). Writing (a=\langle e,Te\rangle),

\[
 \|P_{e^\perp}Te\|^2\le a-a^2\le\tfrac14.
\]

Since (P_{e^\perp}Ne=\sqrt\delta P_{e^\perp}Te), this proves (8).

The constant is attained by the explicit TWO-dimensional function

\[
 F(x)=\frac{1-\delta}{2}\langle e,x\rangle^2
       +\frac12\left\langle-\sqrt{\delta/2}\,e+\frac{w}{\sqrt2},x\right\rangle_+^2,
 \qquad w\perp e,\quad\|w\|=1. \tag{9}
\]

Its global curvature condition follows from

\[
 v^TD^{-1}v=1,\quad v=-\sqrt{\delta/2}\,e+w/\sqrt2,
\]

which gives (vv^T\preceq D). Starting at (x_0=e), every positive collinear query has an inactive hinge. Any actual step (h>1/(1-\delta)) taken from (e) lands at (y=-re), where (r=h(1-\delta)-1). The new transverse gradient is (r\sqrt\delta,w/2). The minimum set is

\[
 \{x:\langle e,x\rangle=0,\ \langle w,x\rangle\le0\},
\]

with value 0, and 0 is its closest point to (x_0=e). Thus the original radius is exactly 1; there is no source reset or prepaid fresh initial coordinate in this attainment example.

Equation (8) rules out obtaining an order-one transverse gradient at a bounded crossing distance by adding arbitrarily many silent positive ridges to an old direction whose curvature tends to 1. It does NOT bound the eventual objective of the whole function: old energy remains, and an arbitrary future word may amplify the new component. The attempt to obtain a lossless recursive child simply by increasing the fresh bank's dimension therefore stops at this quantified cost. It does not exclude a globally chosen, substantially different prefix.

## Line 2. A static arbitrary-depth relay that pays every return guard

The second line avoids a near-unit source curvature. It gives a literal high-dimensional static function and complete chronology for a stated word, then tests its paid constant. It is not a freely selectable state recurrence.

### 2.1 Word, normals, and global function

Fix an integer (K\ge1), and partition the original chronological word as

\[
 h=(a^{(0)},b_0,a^{(1)},b_1,\ldots,b_{K-1},a^{(K)}),
 \qquad s_j=\sum a^{(j)}\ge0.
 \tag{10}
\]

Every (a^{(j)}) is the complete original intervening subword, including zero entries. No entries are removed. Choose (d,c>0) with

\[
 2c\cos\frac\pi{K+2}<d,\qquad
 d+2c\cos\frac\pi{K+2}\le1. \tag{11}
\]

There are explicit independent vectors (v_0,\ldots,v_K\in\mathbb R^{K+1}) with

\[
 \langle v_i,v_j\rangle=
 \begin{cases}d&i=j,\\-c&|i-j|=1,\\0&|i-j|>1.\end{cases} \tag{12}
\]

For completeness put (\vartheta=\pi/(K+2)), and for (1\le\ell\le K+1) define coordinates

\[
 (v_j)_\ell=\sqrt{d-2c\cos(\ell\vartheta)}\,
 \sqrt{\frac2{K+2}}\sin((j+1)\ell\vartheta).
 \tag{13}
\]

The finite sine basis diagonalizes the tridiagonal matrix in (12): the identity (\sin((j+2)t)+\sin(jt)=2\cos t\sin((j+1)t)), with the two zero boundary values, gives its eigenvalues (d-2c\cos(\ell\vartheta)). Orthogonality of distinct eigenvectors and the identity (\sum_{j=1}^{K+1}\sin^2(j\ell\vartheta)=(K+2)/2) give the normalization in (13). Thus every eigenvalue lies in ((0,1]), and

\[
 \sum_{j=0}^K v_jv_j^T\preceq I. \tag{14}
\]

These formulas define finite vectors analytically; no array or recurrence was executed.

For (\mu>0), use the explicit one-sided Huber function

\[
 \phi_\mu(z)=
 \begin{cases}
 0&z\le0,\\
 z^2/2&0\le z\le\mu,\\
 \mu z-\mu^2/2&z\ge\mu.
 \end{cases}
 \quad q_\mu(z)=\phi_\mu'(z)=\min\{\mu,\max\{z,0\}\}.
 \tag{15}
\]

Assume (b_j>1/d). Choose an arbitrary scale (\mu_0>0). Define

\[
 A_1=c(b_0-1/d)\mu_0,
 \qquad
 \mu_j=\frac{A_j}{1+ds_j}\quad(1\le j<K),
 \qquad A_{j+1}=cb_j\mu_j\quad(1\le j<K),
 \qquad\mu_K=\frac{A_K}{1+2ds_K}. \tag{16}
\]

All these quantities are positive, including when a block is empty. Set

\[
 t_0=t_1=0,\qquad t_j=c\mu_{j-1}s_{j-1}\quad(2\le j\le K),
 \quad x_0=\frac{\mu_0(1+ds_0)}d v_0,
 \quad F(x)=\sum_{j=0}^K\phi_{\mu_j}(\langle v_j,x\rangle-t_j).
 \tag{17}
\]

This is one function fixed from the entire word before the run. Equations (14)--(15) prove global convexity and 1-smoothness by the divided-difference argument used in Line 1. Its complete minimum set and minimum value are

\[
 \arg\min F=\{x:\langle v_j,x\rangle\le t_j\ \forall j\},
 \qquad F_\star=0. \tag{18}
\]

All thresholds are nonnegative, so 0 is a minimizer. The halfspace (\langle v_0,x\rangle\le0) alone forces every minimizer's distance from the positive multiple (x_0) of (v_0) to be at least (\|x_0\|). Equality is attained at 0. Consequently the TRUE original squared radius is

\[
 R^2=\operatorname{dist}(x_0,\arg\min F)^2
 =\frac{\mu_0^2(1+ds_0)^2}{d}. \tag{19}
\]

### 2.2 Exact guards, including backward reactivation

The required conditions are

\[
 c\mu_{i+1}(s_{i+1}+b_{i+1})\le\mu_i(db_i-1)
       \quad(0\le i\le K-2),
 \qquad
 c\mu_Ks_K\le\mu_{K-1}(db_{K-1}-1).
 \tag{20}
\]

For (K=1), the first set is empty. These are conditions on (10)--(17), not assumptions that future queries may be selected independently. Under (20), the COMPLETE ACTUAL chronology is:

* Throughout (a^{(j)}), and at its following bridge query if (j<K), only ridge (j) has positive derivative. Its derivative is exactly (\mu_j).
* Every earlier ridge has derivative zero and stays off; every later ridge has derivative zero until the corresponding bridge has physically crossed its threshold.
* The last ridge remains in its linear Huber region through the terminal point.

Here is the induction including all region boundaries. Write (r_j=\langle v_j,x\rangle-t_j). While only ridge (j) is active at cap (\mu_j), an actual step (h\ge0) changes the residuals by

\[
 r_j\longmapsto r_j-hd\mu_j,\qquad
 r_{j-1}\longmapsto r_{j-1}+hc\mu_j,\qquad
 r_{j+1}\longmapsto r_{j+1}+hc\mu_j,
 \tag{21}
\]

with every other residual unchanged. These are the inner products of the actual update (x\mapsto x-h\mu_jv_j).

Initially (r_0=\mu_0(1+ds_0)), (r_1=-c\mu_0(1/d+s_0)), and (r_j=-t_j\le0) for (j\ge2). During the first ordinary block, (r_0\ge\mu_0), while (r_1\le-c\mu_0/d<0). At the first bridge the old residual becomes (\mu_0(1-db_0)<0), and the next one becomes (A_1>0). This proves both the first crossing and the subtraction (1/d) in (16). Omitting that subtraction would fail to preserve the original minimum and radius in (17)--(19).

For (1\le j<K), the entry residual is (A_j=\mu_j(1+ds_j)). During its ordinary block it remains at least (\mu_j); the next ridge starts at (-c\mu_js_j) and reaches exactly 0 at the bridge query. At the bridge, ridge (j) becomes negative and ridge (j+1) obtains (cb_j\mu_j=A_{j+1}>0). Any still later ridge is unchanged and remains nonpositive.

The only possible return is to ridge (j-1). It entered this block with residual (\mu_{j-1}(1-db_{j-1})). Its total increase over the block AND bridge is at most (c\mu_j(s_j+b_j)). The appropriate condition in (20) keeps it nonpositive at every actual query, because the partial sums of nonnegative steps are at most the full block sum. After this stage it never changes again, by (12). This proves every old-return guard; no assumption of one-way activation is hidden in the construction.

At the final block, (r_K=A_K=\mu_K(1+2ds_K)), so after any partial sum (s\le s_K), it equals (\mu_K(1+2ds_K-ds)\ge\mu_K). The final condition in (20) keeps ridge (K-1) off. Boundary residual 0 has derivative 0, and residual (\mu_j) has derivative (\mu_j); hence equality guards, empty blocks and zero steps cause no ambiguity.

### 2.3 Exact normalized terminal objective

The terminal residual is (r_K=\mu_K(1+ds_K)). Every other ridge is zero in value as well as gradient. Therefore

\[
 2F(x_n)=\mu_K^2(1+2ds_K)=\frac{A_K^2}{1+2ds_K}.
\]

Combining this with (16) and the ORIGINAL radius (19) gives the complete witness payoff

\[
 \boxed{
 E(h)=\frac{2F(x_n)}{R^2}
 =\frac{d\,c^{2K}(b_0-1/d)^2\prod_{i=1}^{K-1}b_i^2}
 {\left[\prod_{i=0}^{K-1}(1+ds_i)^2\right](1+2ds_K)}.
 } \tag{22}
\]

This is a valid lower witness for the ORIGINAL (\mathcal W_n(h)) whenever the displayed hypotheses and guards hold. No interpolation oracle, origin replacement, new initial component, unproved active-set law or infinite-dimensional limit occurs in this calculation.

It does not establish (T). In particular, positivity and the global norm alone do not imply the guards. For example, for (K\ge2) the first return guard, after cancellation of the positive (b_0-1/d), is exactly

\[
 c^2(s_1+b_1)\le d(1+ds_1). \tag{23}
\]

If this is violated, the old ridge really reactivates before the claimed second bridge has finished. Its nonzero derivative changes the actual update; formula (22) is then inapplicable.

The globally paid coupling also satisfies

\[
 \frac cd<\frac1{2\cos(\pi/(K+2))}. \tag{24}
\]

After rewriting (1+ds_i=d(s_i+1/d)), (22) contains ((c/d)^{2K}). As (K\to\infty), the upper edge of this coefficient behaves as (4^{-K}) up to a factor tending to 1. The statement follows from (cos x=1-x^2/2+O(x^4)), so (-2K\log\cos(\pi/(K+2))\to0). This is a REAL paid factor, not an (o(1)) that can be discarded. It is not by itself a proof that every choice of checkpoints fails: their numerators can grow too. It does show that this static relay cannot be presented as a cost-free realization of the Zhang--Jiang split using the same source budgets.

using (c\le d). Formula (22) is valid for every (b>1/d), and the same-word standard Huber witness is (H_{\rm base}=1/(1+4s+2b)).

Put (z=ds>0), (r=b/s). Since (c\le d) and (db>1),

\[
 \frac{E}{H_{\rm base}}

 \tag{25}
\]

The last step uses (0<rz-1<r(1+z)) and (z/(1+2z)<1/2). Thus (25) is UNIFORM over all admissible (d,c), including choices depending on the enclosing horizon.
