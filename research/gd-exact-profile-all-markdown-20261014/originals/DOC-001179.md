## 3. Static functions, complete queries, minima, and every row

For u,v>0 let

    g(x)=clip(x,[-v,u]), f(x)=x*g(x)-g(x)^2/2.

and is -v*x-v^2/2 on x<=-v. Its derivative g is continuous,
nondecreasing and1-Lipschitz. Partitioning an interval at the two
thresholds proves the Lipschitz assertion directly. Consequently f
is finite, convex, differentiable, globally1-smooth and has the
complete minimum set{0}, with f(0)=0. For general L set F=L*f.
The original updates with steps h_i/L then coincide with

    x_0=s, x_(i+1)=x_i-h_i*g(x_i),
    g_i=g(x_i), f_i=f(x_i), 0<=i<=n.                  (3.1)

These specify ALL physical queries and the original terminal on one
fixed function. The initial radius about the complete minimum is
exactly |s|. All theorem statements cover this full literal trajectory,
not an auxiliary trajectory with freely chosen gradients.

Let y_i=x_i-g_i. Direct substitution of the displayed f gives every
ordered interpolation residual, including terminal and optimum,

    Q_ij=f_i-f_j+g_i*(x_j-x_i)+(g_i-g_j)^2/2
        =y_j*(g_i-g_j)<=0.                          (3.2)

g_i>=-v. If y_j=0 the residual is zero. This proves both orders,

The one-sided comparison function is

## 4. Mechanism1: a genuine signed-energy obstruction

One tempting ratio-free primitive envelope is

    |Psi_m(s)+S_m*g(Psi_m(s))| <= |s|.               (4.1)

For this clipped function its squared left side equals the signed
objective/gradient expression

    x_n^2+2*S_m*f_n+S_m*(S_m+1)*g_n^2,

because f_n=x_n*g_n-g_n^2/2. Thus(4.1) retains the actual clipped
objective weight and is stronger than a bare radius claim. It would
imply the desired objective bound: on either ray, maximizing
2f(x)/(x+S_m*g(x))^2 gives1/D_m by completing the saturated-ray
square, while the core ratio is1/A_m^2<=1/D_m.

Nevertheless(4.1) is false for every advertised m. Put

    A=A_(m-1), d=d_(m-1), S=A-1,
    choose u>0, v=d*u/A, s=A*u.                     (4.2)

Here0<v<u since d/A<1/2. Every query of the first child is
x=u*(A-P), where P is its actual prefix mass, and that child ends
at u. The root sends u to-d*u=-A*v. Every query of the second
child is x=-v*(A-Q), where Q is its actual prefix mass. The ACTUAL
terminal after its last leaf is -v. All queried saturated guards hold
because both children end at their threshold and all earlier prefix
masses are smaller. The function, rows, complete minimum and radius
are those of Section3, with own radius A*u.

To check the strict violation, first prove d/A>1/lambda. At level2,

    lambda*d_2-A_2
      =epsilon*(1+4/[lambda*(1+epsilon)]-lambda^2*epsilon)>0.

Here lambda^2*epsilon<6/6144<1. The ratios d_j/A_j increase by(2.2),
so the same strict comparison holds at m-1>=2. If t=d/A, then

    |x_n+S_m*g_n|/|s|=A_m*d/A^2=t*(2+t)>1,           (4.3)

because (1/lambda)*(2+1/lambda)=1. This includes the small-side
clipping weight exactly. It is not a claim that the objective itself
increases beyond the target: this trajectory has score d^2/A^4, and

    D_m*d^2/A^4 < (5*A-1)/(4*A^2)<1                 (4.4)

since d/A<1/2, D_m<5*A-1 and A>2. The latter inequality follows
from4*A^2-5*A+1=(4*A-1)*(A-1)>0 for A>2.

A second direct energy-only closure also loses necessary history.
If one replaces a child's actual image by the entire sublevel
2f<=R^2/D_j, then on a saturated ray with threshold1 the permissible
radius is (R^2/D_j+1)/2. Passing this enlarged symmetric interval
through a bridge and another child objective bound yields, for large
R, a bound with leading growth R^4/(4*D_j^3). The desired parent
bound grows only as R^2/D_(j+1). For sufficiently large R the former
cannot prove the latter. This is an obstruction to that enlargement,
not an actual trajectory counterexample: the omitted affine prefix
history is precisely the missing information.

Mechanism1 is therefore not promoted to a uniform invariant. The next
mechanism keeps threshold-conditioned affine exterior branches and
exact signed interval capture, rather than assuming(4.1).

## 7. Uniform whole-word bound for every single-crossing history

We will need an elementary bound for EVERY position of H_m. At index i
write P_i=sum_(t<i)h_t and T_i=sum_(t>i)h_t. If that step has level k,
so h_i=1+d_k, then

    d_k^2/[(1+P_i)^2*(1+2*T_i)] <=1/D_m.             (7.1)

This is an upper estimate of a scalar expression; it does not turn the
usual single-cut lower witness into an upper bound on unrestricted W.
Here is a direct proof with all occurrences covered.

Every level-k bridge has a complete H_k immediately before and after
it, so P_i,T_i>=S_k. Put A=A_k,d=d_k and
M=S_m-S_(k+1)>=0. There is some0<=x<=M for which
P_i=S_k+x and T_i=S_k+M-x. Their denominator is

    (A+x)^2*(2*A-1+2*M-2*x).

Its logarithm is concave on[0,M], because it is the sum of logarithms
of positive affine functions. Hence its minimum is at an endpoint.
The endpoint x=M minus the endpoint x=0 is

    M*[2*A*(A-1)+M*(2*A-1)]>=0.

The denominator is therefore at least A^2*(2*A-1+2*M). Also
D_m=4*A+2*d-1+2*M. It remains to prove

    d^2*(4*A+2*d-1) <= A^2*(2*A-1), d^2<=A^2.

The second follows from(2.1). At k=0 the first is
 a^2*(3+2*a)<1, since a<1/2. At k>=1, A=A_k>2 and d/A<1/2,
so

    (d/A)^2*(4*A+2*d-1)/(2*A-1)
      <(1/4)*(5*A-1)/(2*A-1) <=3/4<1,

where5*A-1<=3*(2*A-1) is equivalent to A>=2. Adding2*M*d^2
versus2*M*A^2 proves(7.1), including the first and last leaf.

Now take any finite u,v and a positive start s for which the physical
trajectory has a FIRST positive-to-negative crossing at step i and
NO subsequent return to positive. Zero states have zero final gap
and can be omitted from this discussion. Before step i every query
is positive saturated: an unsaturated positive query would cross at
its very next step because all h_t>1. Thus the incoming query is

    y=s-P_i*u>0,
    min(y,u)<=s/(1+P_i).

The latter inequality follows separately from y>=u, which implies
s>= (1+P_i)*u, and y<=u, which implies s=P_i*u+y>=(1+P_i)*y.
The crossing lands at-r, with

    0<r<=d_k*min(y,u)<=d_k*s/(1+P_i).                (7.2)

For y<=u the first inequality is equality r=d_k*y. For y>=u,
r=(1+d_k)*u-y<=d_k*u.

With no return, all subsequent negative queries must be saturated;
an unsaturated one would become positive at its next step. The
terminal magnitude is w=r-T_i*v>0. If w>=v, its energy is

    2*f(-w)=2*v*r-(2*T_i+1)*v^2<=r^2/(2*T_i+1).

If0<w<=v, then v>=r/(T_i+1), so w<=r/(T_i+1) and
2*f(-w)=w^2<=r^2/(T_i+1)^2<=r^2/(2*T_i+1). This includes T_i=0.
Together with(7.1)-(7.2) it gives

    2*f(x_n)/s^2 <=1/D_m.                            (7.3)

If a positive trajectory has no crossing at all, all nonterminal
queries are positive saturated and x_n=s-S_m*u>=0. A saturated
terminal has the usual squared-difference bound1/D_m. A core terminal
has score at most1/A_m^2<=1/D_m: writing y=x_n in[0,u], one has
s=S_m*u+y>=A_m*y. Thus ALL zero-crossing and single-crossing
histories are controlled, at every clipping ratio, without assuming
that such histories cover the whole family.
