# Exact cross-block moments and an endpoint-state collision

Status: unverified exact identities and an explicit counterexample to a proposed compression. No new optimization or verifier call. A competitive closed moment certificate is not yet available.

Work at smoothness1 with

    Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2<=0.

Define transformed sample coordinates and values

    z_i=x_i-g_i, F_i=f_i-||g_i||^2/2.

Then the actual interpolation slack is

    I_ij=-Q_ij=F_j-F_i-<g_i,z_j-z_i> >=0.           (1)

## 1. A finite exact moment tuple

For any nonnegative weights u on actual samples define

    D=sum u_i, U=sum u_i g_i, Z=sum u_i z_i,
    M=sum u_i F_i,
    N=sum u_i(<g_i,z_i>-F_i),
    C=M+N=sum u_i<g_i,z_i>.

The letters M,N here denote weighted function moments, not a PSD matrix or a horizon. N is the conjugate-value moment; this notation avoids reusing the primitive capacity K.

For a block beginning at x_start and ending at x_end with value f_end, its intrinsic objective energy satisfies exactly

    E=2<U,x_start>-||U||^2-2N-2D f_end.             (2)

For two weighted sets a,b of samples from the SAME function, rank-one sums of every directed cross edge satisfy

    T_ab=sum_(i in a,j in b) u_i v_j I_ij
        =D_a M_b+D_b N_a-<U_a,Z_b> >=0,
    T_ba=D_b M_a+D_a N_b-<U_b,Z_a> >=0.             (3)

These equations follow by expanding (1) and factoring the product weights. In particular the internal all-pairs sum is

    T_aa=D_a C_a-<U_a,Z_a> >=0.                    (4)

The moment tuple has an exact additive update. If the old head weights are scaled by alpha, the tail weights by beta, and extra weights eta,gamma are placed at the bridge point y and final point z, then

    D_new=alpha D_a+beta D_b+eta+gamma,
    U_new=alpha U_a+beta U_b+eta g_y+gamma g_z,
    Z_new=alpha Z_a+beta Z_b+eta z_y+gamma z_z,
    M_new=alpha M_a+beta M_b+eta F_y+gamma F_z,
    N_new=alpha N_a+beta N_b
             +eta(<g_y,z_y>-F_y)+gamma(<g_z,z_z>-F_z). (5)

Here z_y=y-g_y and z_z=z-g_z; the repeated z is a point name in the subscript and a transformed coordinate on the right. All positions and function values in (5) refer to their actual common global coordinates. There is no reset at the start of the tail. The actual final proof weights must be nonnegative. Equation(2) then computes the combined objective energy exactly.

This supplies a precise interface for the all-cross plan. It does not establish that the elementary inequalities(2)--(4) characterize all legal moments or provide a closed accelerated certificate.

## 2. Endpoint information, even including the dual slack, does not determine (3)

Consider the fixed physical schedule h=(1,1,1), start x_0=12/5, minimizer0 and proof weights u=(1,1,1,1). Construct two one-dimensional convex1-smooth functions A and B, each with f(0)=0. Their sample data are:

|i|x_i for A|g_i for A|f_i for A|x_i for B|g_i for B|f_i for B|
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|0|12/5|1|293/200|12/5|1|293/200|
|1|7/5|7/10|123/200|7/5|23/30|47/72|
|2|7/10|1/2|39/200|19/30|13/30|283/1800|
|3|1/5|1/5|1/50|1/5|1/5|1/50|

Both columns satisfy x_(i+1)=x_i-g_i exactly.

For function A, linearly interpolate the displayed gradients between successive points. The three slopes from the first interval to the last are3/10,2/7,3/5, all in[0,1]. Set g(x)=x for x<=1/5 and g(x)=1 for x>=12/5. Integrating this continuous monotone1-Lipschitz derivative gives precisely the displayed values: its three drops f_i-f_(i+1) are17/20,21/50,7/40.

For function B, on each interval[x_(i+1),x_i] let L_i=g_i be its length, let delta_i=g_i-g_(i+1), and place a slope1 ramp of length delta_i after a lower flat piece of length a_i. Before the ramp the derivative is g_(i+1); after it the derivative is g_i. Choose

    (a_0,a_1,a_2)=(289/420,11/100,1/10).

The corresponding (L_i,delta_i) are(1,7/30),(23/30,1/3),(13/30,7/30). Each a_i lies in[0,L_i-delta_i]. The area under the derivative on such an interval is

    g_i L_i-delta_i^2/2-delta_i a_i.

It is respectively731/900,223/450,247/1800 for the three intervals, yielding the displayed function values from f_3=1/50. Extend the derivative by x below1/5 and by1 above12/5. This again gives one globally defined continuous, nondecreasing1-Lipschitz derivative, and its integral is minimized at zero. Thus both constructions satisfy the full function assumptions, not just adjacent interpolation tests.

## 3. All proposed endpoint data agree, but a needed reverse sum differs

For both trajectories,

    D=4, d=x_0-x_3=11/5, U=12/5, e=U-d=1/5,
    p=g_0=1, q=g_3=1/5,
    f_0=293/200, f_3=1/50, f_0-f_3=289/200,
    E=443/100.

The physical primitive energy, for S=3 and capacity4, is V=E-12q^2=79/20 on both instances. Their primitive dual expression is also the same:

    H=2(f_0-f_3)-6q^2-|p-(d+q)/4|^2=249/100.

These equalities use the direct definitions; they do not assume any unverified primitive theorem for the schedule.

Nevertheless their weighted proximal-point sums and primal moments differ:

    Z_A=23/10, M_A=281/200,
    Z_B=67/30, M_B=2497/1800.

The conjugate moment N is the same, as equation(2) already implies from the shared endpoint data. At the terminal point z_3=x_3-g_3=0 and F_3=f_3-q^2/2=0. Consequently the reverse-anchor sum is

    sum_i u_i I_(3,i)=M-qZ.

It equals189/200=1701/1800 on A and1693/1800 on B, a difference of1/225. Thus no formula depending only on the common tuple(D,E,d,U,e,p,q,f_0,f_3,V,H) can recover this reverse interpolation sum for all legal trajectories.

This is a counterexample to exact endpoint compression, not to inequalities that bound that sum using the tuple, and not to carrying the larger moment tuple from part1.

## 4. The open quantitative step

Equation(3) makes the missing quantities explicit. Substituting M=C-N trades the missing primal value moment for the within-block mixed moment C=sum u_i<g_i,z_i>. The inequality(4) provides only a lower bound on C. In a proposed energy certificate the coefficient of C after subtracting a positive cross-edge sum can have the opposite sign, so (4) cannot silently be used as an upper bound.

A successful all-cross join must retain a valid bound with the correct sign for this moment, or preserve C and its function-value counterpart through the next join. Additivity(5) alone is not that bound. This is the precise remaining mechanism in the moment plan; no exact-profile improvement is claimed.
