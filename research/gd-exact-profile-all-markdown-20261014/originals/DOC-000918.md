## 2. Formal coefficient interface, including zero rows

Fix a finite nonnegative word `h=(h_0,...,h_(n-1))`, and set

\[
 T_i=\sum_{k<i}h_k,\qquad S=T_n,\qquad M=1+2S,\qquad
 t_i=1+T_i,\qquad t_p=M.
\tag{2.1}
\]

On one nonempty compact convex body `D` containing zero, let

\[
 x_i=x_0-\sum_{k<i}h_kg_k,\quad g_i=P_D(x_i),\quad
 p=P_D(x_0/M),\quad
 U_d(x)=\max_{g\in D}\{2\langle x,g\rangle-d\|g\|^2\}.
\]

The physical terminal gradient `g_n` has no associated physical step. The
formal labels are `I={0,...,n,p}` together with a zero label `*`.
Write `G_i=g_i`, `G_p=p`, and

\[
 B_iG=g_i+\sum_{k<i}h_kg_k,\quad B_pG=Mp,\quad
 a_i=1\ (i\le n),\quad a_p=1/M.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Thus `X_j-G_j=a_j(x_0-B_jG)` for nonzero labels, whereas
`X_*=G_*=0`. The original orientation is

\[
 Q_{ij}=\langle X_j-G_j,G_i-G_j\rangle\le0.
\tag{2.3}
\]

We consider only sufficient certificates of the following specific form:
nonnegative constants `lambda_ij`, depending on the word, such that

\[
 R=J+\sum_{i,j}\lambda_{ij}Q_{ij}\quad\hbox{is a globally PSD
 quadratic in all formal coordinates},\qquad
 J=U_M(x_0)-U_1(x_n).
\tag{2.4}
\]

In (2.4), the U values are replaced by their actual-maximizer expressions
before taking the formal quadratic:

\[
 J=2\langle x_0,p-g_n\rangle
   +2\Big\langle g_n,\sum_{k<n}h_kg_k\Big\rangle
   +\|g_n\|^2-M\|p\|^2.
\tag{2.5}
\]

On an actual projection history, (2.4) would give `J>=R>=0`.
No converse is asserted: this report does not prove conic dual attainment
or that every true U inequality has a certificate (2.4).

Rows with `j=*` and diagonal rows are identically zero and may be removed.
For every remaining row define

\[
 \mu_{ij}=a_j\lambda_{ij}.
\tag{2.6}
\]

The coefficient of `x_0` must vanish. Indeed, there is no `||x_0||^2` term;
a nonzero linear coefficient in `x_0` prevents global PSD. Set all nonstar
gradients equal in this coefficient equation. Contributions of nonstar
rows cancel pairwise and the objective coefficient is zero, leaving
`-sum_j mu_(*,j) G`. Consequently

\[
 \mu_{*,j}=0\quad\hbox{for every nonstar }j.
\tag{2.7}
\]

This conclusion uses nonnegativity and exact cancellation, not a presumed
constant-gradient legal history. All nontrivial star rows vanish. On `I`,
the remaining cancellation equations are exactly

\[
 b_i:=\operatorname{out}_i(\mu)-\operatorname{in}_i(\mu)
     =2(\mathbf1_{i=n}-\mathbf1_{i=p}).
\tag{2.8}
\]

Here the edge convention is `i -> j` for `mu_ij`; it follows the indices
of the original row, not the physical GD direction. After (2.8), the entire
remaining polynomial is

\[
 R=2\Big\langle g_n,\sum_{k<n}h_kg_k\Big\rangle+
       \|g_n\|^2-M\|p\|^2
       +\sum_{i,j\in I}\mu_{ij}\langle B_jG,G_j-G_i\rangle.
\tag{2.9}
\]

This verifies the sign in the root lead. Original coefficients are recovered
without ambiguity by `lambda_ij=mu_ij` for physical `j` and
`lambda_(i,p)=M mu_(i,p)`.

For a coefficient-level specification of every matrix entry, let `e_i`
be the coordinate columns, `H=sum_(k<n)h_k e_k`, `b_j` the column of
`B_j`, and `sym(u,v)=(uv^T+vu^T)/2`. Then the symmetric matrix of (2.9) is

\[
 2\operatorname{sym}(e_n,H)+e_ne_n^T-Me_pe_p^T
 +\sum_{i,j}\mu_{ij}\operatorname{sym}(b_j,e_j-e_i).
\tag{2.10}
\]

Every coordinate, including `p` and `g_n`, remains present.

## 3. Constant-gradient contact and absorption

At a formal assignment `G_i=v` for all `i in I`, (2.9) is zero because
`2S+1=M`. A globally PSD quadratic that is zero on this subspace must have
zero first variation there. This follows by substituting `G+u Z` and
allowing both signs of the scalar `u`.

Define the clock drift

\[
 d_i=\sum_j\mu_{ij}(t_j-t_i).
\]

The row term's first variation at coordinate `i` is
`-b_i t_i-d_i`. The first three terms of (2.9) contribute `2h_i`
for `i<n`, `2(S+1)` for `i=n`, and `-2M` for `i=p`.
Using (2.8), all contact equations are therefore

\[
 \boxed{d_i=2h_i\ (i<n),\qquad d_n=d_p=0.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

For `S>0`, `t_p=M>1+S>=t_i` for every physical node. Nonnegative weights
and `d_p=0` force

\[
 \mu_{p,j}=0\ (j\ne p),\qquad
 w_i:=\mu_{i,p}\ge0,\qquad \sum_{i=0}^n w_i=2.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus the maximal clock really is absorbing for every certificate in this
class. It was not imposed in advance. Equal physical clocks caused by zero
steps do not affect this argument. At `S=0`, strict clock maximality fails;
no absorption claim is made. The all-zero word's actual U inequality is
trivial because every physical point and the auxiliary input coincide.

For strictly positive words, (2.8) and (3.1) admit a literal absorbing
Markov interpretation. Give each physical node transition probabilities
`P_ij=mu_ij/out_i` and make `p` absorbing. Every preterminal node has
positive outgoing mass by its positive clock drift; `n` has positive
outgoing mass by its divergence two. No closed communicating class can
avoid `p`: a maximal-clock preterminal member would have nonpositive drift,
and if the maximal member is `n`, zero drift would force no edge to the
strictly earlier clocks, contradicting its outgoing mass. Hence absorption
occurs with probability one. Half the edge flow is the expected edge
occupation of this chain started at `n`, by the finite transient balance
equations. This interpretation alone gives no sign to (2.9).

To expose the unpaid term, put

\[
 F_j=\sum_{k<j}h_k(G_k-G_j)\quad(j\le n),\qquad F_p=0.
\]

Then `B_jG=t_jG_j+F_j`. The complete Dirichlet identity is

\[
 \boxed{R=\frac12\sum_{i,j}\mu_{ij}t_j\|G_i-G_j\|^2
 -\sum_{i<n}h_i\|g_i-g_n\|^2
 +\sum_{i,j}\mu_{ij}\langle F_j,G_j-G_i\rangle.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

For its derivation, expand each `t_j<G_j,G_j-G_i>` as a squared difference
plus a norm difference. The norm coefficient is
`-(b_i t_i+d_i)/2`; substitution of (2.8) and (3.1) gives (3.3).
The final term has no assigned sign. Replacing its sum by an expectation
does not pay it.

## 4. Exact absorption-mixture reduction

For any feasible interface (2.8), (3.1), define the fixed convex mixture

\[
 z=\frac12\sum_{i=0}^n w_i g_i.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Here “fixed” refers to the weights; `z` itself depends on the gradients.
Absorption (3.2) gives an exact square completion:

\[
 R=M\|p-z\|^2+K,
\quad
 K=J_z+\sum_{i,j\le n}\mu_{ij}Q_{ij},
\quad
 J_z=2\langle x_0,z\rangle-M\|z\|^2-U_1(x_n).
\tag{4.2}
\]

The `x_0` coefficient of `K` vanishes by (2.8); it is a quadratic only
in physical gradients. Thus (2.4) is PSD if and only if `K` is PSD,
because `p` is an unrestricted formal coordinate and can be set to `z`.
In particular every such certificate necessarily satisfies

\[
 J_z\ge0\quad\hbox{on every actual physical projection history.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is a precise restriction on these certificates: they must already
prove the U lower evaluation at one word-dependent convex combination of
the physical gradients. The auxiliary projection supplies the nonnegative
maximization remainder. No actual history is required to have `p=z`.

## 5. Law 1: explicit terminal fan

For every word with `S>0`, prescribe

\[
 w_i=\frac{2h_i}{M-t_i}\ (i<n),\quad
 w_n=2-\sum_{i<n}w_i,
\quad
 \mu_{n,i}=\mu_{i,p}=w_i\ (i<n),\quad
 \mu_{n,p}=w_n.
\tag{5.1}
\]

All unlisted entries are zero. Since `M-t_i=2S-T_i>S` whenever `h_i>0`,
`sum_(i<n) w_i<2`; hence every entry is nonnegative, including `w_n`.
Equations (2.8) hold directly. For `i<n`, clock drift is
`w_i(M-t_i)=2h_i`; at `n` it is
`2S-sum_(i<n)w_i(M-t_i)=0`. This verifies the full contact interface.

Contact makes (2.9) invariant under a common translation of all formal
gradients. Set `g_n=0`. The whole polynomial, including `p`, is

\[
 R=M\Big\|p-\frac12\sum_{i<n}w_i g_i\Big\|^2
 +\sum_{i<n}w_i\|g_i\|^2
 +\sum_{t<i<n}w_i h_t\langle g_t,g_i\rangle
 -\frac M4\Big\|\sum_{i<n}w_i g_i\Big\|^2.
\tag{5.2}
\]

For one fixed `i<n`, set `g_i=v`, every other physical gradient zero,
and `p=w_i v/2`. The entire value is

\[
 R=w_i\left(1-\frac{M w_i}{4}\right)\|v\|^2.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Thus a positive `h_i` requires

\[
 h_i\le 2\left(1-\frac{t_i}{M}\right)<2
\tag{5.4}
\]

for PSD of this law. Every `h_i>=2` violates (5.4). The H3 bridge
`1+k` has `k=beta c>1`, so this law is excluded for H3 and for every
larger hierarchy containing that bridge. This is a negative formal
quadratic assignment, not a legal U counterexample.

The one-step sign calibration is exact: `w_0=w_1=1`, and (5.2) is
`M||p-g_0/2||^2+(1-M/4)||g_0||^2` after `g_1=0`. Its threshold is
`h_0<=3/2`. No numerical evaluation was used.

## 6. Law 2: recursive binary-tree routing

This is the sole recursive family tried after Law 1. It is constructed
from clocks and subtree masses, without an inherited primitive multiplier
array.

First consider any rooted tree on physical nodes `0,...,n`, rooted at
`n`, in which every parent `j=pi(i)` has `j>i`. For a strictly positive
word, let `A_i` be the sum of the step weights `h_v` over the subtree
rooted at `i` (the root `n` carries no step weight). Put

\[
 L_i=t_{\pi(i)}-t_i>0,
\quad
 \mu_{i,\pi(i)}=\frac{2A_i t_i}{M L_i},\qquad
 \mu_{\pi(i),i}=\frac{2A_i t_{\pi(i)}}{M L_i}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The absorption edges are fixed as

\[
 w_i=\frac{2h_i}{M}\ (i<n),\qquad
 w_n=\frac{2(1+S)}{M}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

All unlisted entries are zero. Every coefficient is nonnegative. Across
a subtree edge the net outward physical flow is `-2A_i/M`; summing at a
node gives physical divergence `-2h_i/M`, and at the root `2S/M`.
Adding (6.2) proves (2.8).

For `Lambda_ij=M mu_ij` on physical edges, the weighted conductance
`nu_ij=Lambda_ij t_j` is symmetric. Therefore
`sum_j Lambda_ij t_j=t_i in_i(Lambda)`. Its clock drift is
`-t_i div_i(Lambda)`: this is `2h_i t_i` at preterminal nodes and
`-2S(1+S)` at `n`. Adding the absorption-edge drift proves (3.1).

For the literal H_m, the explicit recursive tree is

\[
 \ell(i)=v_2(i+1),\qquad
 \pi(i)=i+2^{\ell(i)},\qquad 0\le i<2^m-1,
\tag{6.3}
\]

with root `n=2^m-1`. Each subtree consists of the contiguous copy
`(H_(ell(i)),1+a c^(ell(i)))` ending at `i`. Consequently

\[
 A_i=S_{\ell(i)}+1+a c^{\ell(i)},\qquad L_i=A_i,
\tag{6.4}
\]

and (6.1) simplifies to `mu_(i,pi(i))=2t_i/M` and
`mu_(pi(i),i)=2t_(pi(i))/M`. This specifies every original coefficient
at every depth; both orientations and all absorption edges are retained.

The family has an exact first obstruction already at H2. Write

\[
 A=1+a,\quad B=1+\beta,\quad C=1+2A+B,\quad M_2=1+2(2A+B).
\]

The physical tree is `0--1--3` and `2--3`. Set the full formal assignment

\[
 g_0=g_1=g_3=0,\qquad g_2=v,\qquad p=(A/M_2)v.
\tag{6.5}
\]

The mixture square in (4.2) vanishes. Only the two rows on `2--3`
contribute a nonzero physical diagonal. Substitution in (2.9), including
the absorption square completion, gives the entire value

\[
 M_2R=\big[A^2+2C(1-A)\big]\|v\|^2<0.
\tag{6.6}
\]

For the strict sign, the same-enclosing parameters give
`1/3<a<sqrt(2)-1<1/2` and `B>19/10`. Hence `4/3<A<3/2`,
`C>11/2`, and

\[
 A^2-2C(A-1)<9/4-11/3=-17/12<0.
\]

At H1, the same tree law reduces instead to
`M_1 R=||M_1p-A g_0-(1+A)g_1||^2+(2-A^2)||g_0-g_1||^2`,
which is PSD because `A<sqrt(2)`. Thus H2 is the first exact obstruction
to this explicit recursive law. Assignment (6.5) is not asserted to be a
legal projection history. In particular it does not contradict the

## 7. A global obstruction to the entire absorption pattern of Law 2

The next exclusion is not a third candidate law. It allows **arbitrary**
nonnegative coefficients on every physical original row while retaining
only the absorption pattern (6.2). It therefore rules out repairing Law 2
by any topology change, extra physical circulation, or global physical
row redistribution that preserves that pattern.

For (6.2),

\[
 z=\frac{\sum_{i<n}h_i g_i+(1+S)g_n}{M}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Let the primitive energy of the whole word be

\[
 V=\|x_0\|^2-\|x_n+Sg_n\|^2.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Writing `A_vec=sum_(i<n)h_i(g_i-g_n)` gives
`V=2<x_0,A_vec>-||A_vec||^2`. Direct substitution in (4.2) yields

\[
 J_z=V/M,\qquad
 R=M\|p-z\|^2+
   \frac1M\left[V+\sum_{i,j\le n}\Lambda_{ij}Q_{ij}\right],
\quad \Lambda_{ij}=M\mu_{ij}\ge0.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Consequently a PSD certificate with (6.2) would imply `V>=0` on every
actual physical projection history, independently of the physical array.
This is stronger than the U inequality and is false for the actual H3.

To give an analytical H3 sign calibration, use one enclosing `m>=1536`
and put

\[
 C=1+S_2=4+2a+\beta,\quad k=\beta c,\quad
 S=S_3=2C+k-1,\quad C_3=1+S=2C+k,\quad M=1+2S,
\quad r=k/C.
\tag{7.4}
\]

The strict defect is

\[
 \delta=k(2C+k)-C^2>0.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Here is a direct parameter check, not an imported unaccepted claim.
Let `u=sqrt(2)-1=1/lambda`, `lambda=1+sqrt(2)`. Then

\[
 C=\lambda^2-\epsilon\left[1+\frac{4u}{1+\epsilon}\right],

so

\[
 k-uC=\epsilon\left[u+\frac{4u^2}{1+\epsilon}-\lambda\epsilon\right]>0.

The last bracket is positive already from `u>2/5`, `lambda<5/2`, and
`epsilon<1/10`. Finally
`delta=(k-uC)(k+lambda C)>0`.

Consider the interval body `D=[-r,1]` and start `x_0=C`. The entire

## 8. Quantitative absorption transfer forced at H3

Keep the H3 data (7.4). For an arbitrary certificate of the full original-row
class (2.4), define the left-block absorption mass

\[
 \alpha=\frac12\sum_{i=0}^3\mu_{i,p},\qquad 0\le\alpha\le1.
\tag{8.1}
\]

\[
 z=(1+r)\alpha-r.
\tag{8.2}

\[
 2Cz-Mz^2-r^2\ge0.
\tag{8.3}
\]

The canonical left mass and mixture from (6.2) are

\[
 \alpha_0=\frac{C+k}{M},\qquad z_0=\frac{C-Sr}{M},\qquad
 2Cz_0-Mz_0^2-r^2=V/M<0.
\tag{8.4}
\]

Set `d=z-z_0=(1+r)(alpha-alpha_0)`. Subtraction gives the exact scalar

\[
 0\le\frac VM+2Sr\,d-Md^2.
\tag{8.5}
\]

Because `V<0` and `Sr>0`, (8.5) forces `d>0`. Dropping only the
nonpositive final square gives the explicit necessary lower bound

\[
 d\ge\frac{-V}{2MSr}.
\tag{8.6}
\]

One can retain the square for a sharp interval. The discriminant is
positive: `C>5`, `k<C/2`, and `M=4C+2k-1<5C` imply
`Mr^2<5C/4<C^2`. Thus (8.3) is exactly

\[
 \frac{C-\sqrt{C^2-Mr^2}}{M}\le z\le
 \frac{C+\sqrt{C^2-Mr^2}}{M}.
\tag{8.7}
\]

In particular the sharp required shift above canonical is

\[
 \alpha-\alpha_0\ge
 \frac{Sr-\sqrt{C^2-Mr^2}}{M(1+r)}>0.
\tag{8.8}
\]

The square-root-free lower bound (8.6), using (7.6), is

\[
 \boxed{\alpha-\frac{C+k}{M}\ge

\tag{8.9}

This constraint holds for every certificate in the specified one-auxiliary
full original-row PSD class, allowing all physical pairs and all
nonnegative weights. It identifies a quantitatively necessary change in
the absorption distribution. It does not control the remaining matrix
