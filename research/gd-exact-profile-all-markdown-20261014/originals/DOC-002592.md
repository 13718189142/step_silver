# Whole optimal-certificate set: quantitative terminal rigidity and a common barrier

Status: UNVERIFIED, self-contained candidate calculations. No new verifier
allocation is assumed. This is the bounded face-level output requested by the
updated TASK.md. It neither proves Huber tightness nor resolves the exact
Zhang--Jiang profile comparison.

## 1. Full setting and a matching stationary instance

Normalize L=1 and x_star=g_star=f_star=0. Fix a positive length-n schedule h,
n>=1, and q>0. For distinct sample indices i,j in {star,0,...,n}, use

    Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2 <=0.

A full certificate has lambda_ij>=0, finite-node flow

    sum_j lambda_ij - sum_j lambda_ji = 2*1_(i=n),

and a PSD quadratic form after x_i=x_0-sum_(t<i)h_t g_t:

    M_lambda=q||x_0||^2-2f_n+sum lambda_ij Q_ij
            =q||x_0||^2-<x_0,sum_i mu_i g_i>
                         +sum_(i,j) A_ij <g_i,g_j>,

where mu_i=lambda_(i,star). Let C(h,q) denote ALL such certificates. Its
gradient Schur complement is Z_lambda=A-mu mu^T/(4q)>=0.

Assume there is an ACTUAL legal convex 1-smooth function, normalized by
||x_0||=1, whose GD trajectory attains 2f_n=q. Suppose one certificate
lambda^0 in C(h,q) satisfies every stepsize stationarity equation

    D_t(lambda^0;g)=0, 0<=t<n,

where D_t is the derivative, with all formal vectors fixed, of M_lambda
with respect to h_t. This is a full matching stationary tuple hypothesis.
It does not assert that arbitrary fixed schedules or arbitrary matching
primals are stationary. A globally minimizing schedule to which the usual
full stationary interface applies satisfies this hypothesis.

Every lambda in C(h,q) matches THIS SAME actual instance: its quadratic
value is sum lambda_ij Q_ij<=0, and PSD makes it nonnegative. Consequently

    lambda_ij Q_ij=0 for all i,j,
    sum_i mu_i g_i=2q x_0,                         (1)
    Y Z_lambda=0,                                 (2)

where Y is the linear map from R^(n+1) to the ambient Euclidean space
defined by Y r=sum_i r_i g_i. To prove (1)--(2), complete the square:

    M_lambda=q||x_0-(sum_i mu_i g_i)/(2q)||^2
                    +sum_(i,j)(Z_lambda)_ij <g_i,g_j>.

Both summands are nonnegative and have sum zero. The second gives
Y Z_lambda^(1/2)=0, hence (2). No strict-complementarity assumption is made.

Write p=g_(n-1), g=g_n and b=h_(n-1)>0. Last-step stationarity implies

    <p,g>=0.                                      (3)

Here is a proof retaining the full matrix. Change only the final step by
a small signed u on the SAME actual function. Its terminal point changes
by -u p; its terminal gradient changes by O(u), and
f_n(u)=f_n-u<p,g>+O(u^2). Let v_u be the full formal-vector realization.
PSD and matching give M_lambda(v_0)=0 and M_lambda v_0=0, so
M_lambda(v_u)=O(u^2). Since the matrix is affine in the final step,

    q-2f_n(u)+sum lambda_ij Q_ij(u)
       =u D_(n-1)(lambda;g)+O(u^2).

Every Q_ij(u)<=0. Taking both signs of u proves

    D_(n-1)(lambda;g)=2<p,g>                      (4)

for EVERY lambda in C(h,q). Applying stationarity for lambda^0 gives (3).
Thus last-cut stationarity, unlike unproved earlier-cut stationarity,
survives re-selection with this same matching instance.

Define B_i=product_(t<i)(1+h_t), with B_0=1. Gradient Lipschitzness and
g_star=0 give ||x_i||,||g_i||<=B_i. Convexity and 2f_n=q give

    ||g||>=q/(2 B_n)>0,
    ||p||>=||g||/(1+b)>=q/[2B_n(1+b)].           (5)

The second inequality uses ||g-p||<=b||p||.

## 2. A quantitative invariant of the ENTIRE certificate set

Choose any reference certificate lambda^r in C(h,q), with source mu^r, and put

    L_face=span( {range Z_lambda : lambda in C(h,q)}
                  union {mu-mu^r : lambda in C(h,q)} ).

This is an ordinary linear span in the finite-dimensional gradient
coefficient space R^(n+1). Equations (1)--(2) imply

    L_face subset ker Y.                          (6)

Let J={0,...,n-2}, and T={n-1,n}; J is empty if n=1.
Let H_J=(<g_i,g_j>)_(i,j in J), which is the actual prefix-gradient Gram
matrix, not an assumed replacement for the full primal.

For EVERY r in L_face, including every source difference, (3) and (6) give
the exact identity

    ||p||^2 r_(n-1)^2 + ||g||^2 r_n^2
            = r_J^T H_J r_J.                      (7)

Indeed r_(n-1)p+r_n g=-sum_(i in J)r_i g_i; square both sides and use
<p,g>=0. In particular,

    r_n^2 + r_(n-1)^2/(1+b)^2
       <= [4 B_n^2/q^2] (sum_(i in J) B_i |r_i|)^2. (8)

This explicit estimate says how much earlier source coefficients must
change to permit a specified change of the two terminal source coefficients.
It is simultaneous over every optimal certificate; it does not require
the reselected certificate to be stationary on all earlier cuts.

Consequences include

    L_face intersect span{e_(n-1),e_n}={0},
    dim L_face<=n-1,                              (9)

and injectivity of mu -> mu_J on the entire source-coefficient set.
For n=1 every source coefficient is fixed across the whole set, and every
Z_lambda is zero. For larger n the conclusion allows nonzero earlier
Schur slack and source variation. It does not imply Z_lambda=0.

Applying (7) to the columns of a PSD square root of any Z_lambda gives
the additional exact identity

    ||p||^2 (Z_lambda)_(n-1,n-1)
       +||g||^2 (Z_lambda)_(n,n)
           = trace(H_J (Z_lambda)_JJ).             (10)

Thus, for example,

    (Z_lambda)_(n,n)
      +(Z_lambda)_(n-1,n-1)/(1+b)^2
    <= [4 B_n^2/q^2] (sum_(i in J)B_i^2)
                                  trace((Z_lambda)_JJ). (11)

Equations (7), (8), (10), (11) are quantitative restrictions on the full
optimal set, not a claim that its common kernel equals the observed
primal range. Additional common kernel directions are permitted.

The fixed-coordinate graph formulation is also useful. Because projection
L_face -> R^J is injective, the terminal coordinates of r in L_face are
linear functions of r_J. These functions can be extended to R^J. Hence
there are fixed vectors u,v in R^J such that

    r_(n-1)=-u^T r_J,  r_n=-v^T r_J for all r in L_face.

The vectors k_p=(u,1,0), k_n=(v,0,1) annihilate every Z_lambda, and
mu^T k_p and mu^T k_n are constant over C(h,q). In particular

    Z_JT=-Z_JJ [u v],
    Z_TT=[u v]^T Z_JJ [u v]                       (12)

for every certificate in the set, with the SAME u,v. This uses the full
Schur matrix, including singular prefix blocks.

## 3. Certificate mixing gives an explicit descent test without stationarity

The exact covariance identity explains why different certificates cannot
be inspected in isolation. For finitely many certificates lambda^a at
the same h,q, positive weights theta_a summing to one, and their average,

    Z_bar=sum_a theta_a Z_a
          +(1/(4q))sum_a theta_a(mu^a-mu_bar)(mu^a-mu_bar)^T. (13)

This follows by expanding A_bar-mu_bar mu_bar^T/(4q). Every multiplier
of the average is nonnegative, its flow is correct, and its FULL matrix
is PSD, being the average of the full matrices.

By choosing finitely many certificates whose ranges and source differences
span L_face, one gets range Z_bar=L_face. One direct justification is
that the range of a sum of PSD matrices is the sum of their ranges;
the covariance range is the span of the included source differences.

For completeness, a terminal vector in this range yields an actual
same-length descent, without a stationary-instance assumption.
Suppose for some averaged certificate

    Z_bar >= kappa (a e_(n-1)+s e_n)(a e_(n-1)+s e_n)^T,

where kappa>0, a>0 and s in {+1,-1}. Put B=B_n, Bprev=B_(n-1), and

    C=q/(2B^2)+1/kappa.

Choose a positive d with

    d<=a/C,
    d<=q^2/(8 a B^2 Bprev^2),
    d<b if s=+1.

Replace b by b-s d, leaving every earlier step fixed. Then

    W_n(h_0,...,h_(n-2),b-s d)
        <=q-d q^2/(8 a B^2)<q.                    (14)

Here is the full uniform argument. For ANY legal instance normalized by
||x_0||=1, put E=2f_n and R=a p+s g. The averaged PSD certificate gives
q-E>=kappa||R||^2. Convexity gives ||g||^2>=E^2/(4B^2).
The identity for ||R||^2 gives

    -2s<p,g> >= (||g||^2-||R||^2)/a.

The new endpoint is x_n+s d p, so smoothness gives

    E_new<=E-(d/a)[E^2/(4B^2)-(q-E)/kappa]+d^2 Bprev^2.

Insert E^2>=q^2-2q(q-E), then the displayed d bounds. This proves (14)
uniformly over dimensions and functions. The smoothness inequality can
also be written as one additional interpolation inequality plus the
nonnegative square ||g_new-g-s d p||^2/2. Thus the argument retains a
full interpolation/PSD certificate with an auxiliary old endpoint.

A vector supported only on e_n in range Z_bar contradicts tightness:
q-E>=kappa||g||^2>=kappa E^2/(4B^2) uniformly forces E<=r<q.
A vector supported only on e_(n-1) gives the same contradiction with
kappa/(1+b)^2. Consequently a nonzero intersection of L_face with the
terminal two-coordinate plane either improves the tight bound at the
same schedule or gives the genuine final-step descent (14).

For exactly TWO certificates, if their source difference is
Delta mu=A e_(n-1)+B0 e_n with A*B0!=0, the equal-weight average has

    kappa=B0^2/(16q),
    a=|A/B0|, s=sign(A/B0)

after rescaling the direction. Formula (14) is therefore an explicit
quantitative way of exploiting re-selection. It cannot occur under
the matching stationary hypothesis of Sections1--2, by (8).

## 4. A common strict forward-edge barrier two steps from the end

Assume now n>=2, set k=n-2 and a=h_k, and retain the actual stationary
instance of Section1. Suppose SOME certificate in C(h,q) has

    nu_k=lambda_(star,k)>0.

Complementarity for that certificate on the common instance forces

    delta_k=f_k-||g_k||^2/2=0.                    (15)

Meanwhile Q_(n,n-1)<=0 and (3) imply

    delta_(n-1)=f_(n-1)-||p||^2/2
                  >=q/2+||g||^2/2>0.              (16)

First a>1. To see this, the unit-step descent inequality from (15) shows
that y=x_k-g_k is a minimizer. For 0<=a<=1, Lipschitz gradient equality
between x_k and y forces p=(1-a)g_k. A detailed justification: the two
Lipschitz bounds along the segment sum to ||g_k||, so equality in the
triangle inequality forces the stated vectors. At a=1, p=0 contradicts
f_n>0. At a<1, (3) implies <g_k,g>=0; since x_n=x_k-a g_k-b p, the
constraint Q_(n,k)<=0 becomes q/2+||g||^2/2<=0, again impossible.
Thus a>1.

Define sigma_ij=-Q_ij>=0 for the common actual instance. Direct expansion
of the exact GD relation x_(k+1)=x_k-a g_k gives

    Q_(star,k+1)+(1/a)Q_(k+1,k)+((1-a)/a)Q_(k,k+1)
           -Q_(star,k)
      =(1/a)||p-(1-a)g_k||^2.

Using (15) and (16),

    sigma_(k,k+1)
      =[a delta_(k+1)+sigma_(k+1,k)
                     +||p+(a-1)g_k||^2]/(a-1)
      >=[a/(a-1)](q/2+||g||^2/2)
      >=[a/(a-1)](q/2+q^2/(8B_n^2))>0.          (17)

Therefore EVERY certificate in C(h,q) has

    lambda_(k,k+1)=0,  nu_(k+1)=0.                (18)

The first is a common strict inactive edge, not a missing edge in one
bad extreme certificate. Re-selection at unchanged h,q cannot restore
that adjacent capacity. This obstruction is compatible with remote
forward edges and does not prove that a simultaneous full motion fails.

## 5. The inactive cost remains after re-selection

The precise finite joint-perturbation identity is recorded here to prevent
a representation change from hiding the cost in (17). Use the stationary
base lambda^0 and its common actual instance. For a new full certificate
(q',lambda',h'), let dh=h'-h and dlambda=lambda'-lambda^0. Define

    z_t(lambda)=sum_(j>t)[mu_j+sum_(l<=t)lambda_(j,l)]g_j
                   -sum_(i<=t)[sum_(j>t)lambda_(i,j)]g_i,
    B_mix=sum_(t<n)dh_t <g_t,z_t(dlambda)>.

Here z_t is linear in lambda, and D_t(lambda;g)=<g_t,z_t(lambda)>.
The full matrix is affine separately in h and lambda; pairing it with
the OLD Gram realization, using flow, old matching, and all old
stationarity equations, gives exactly

    <M(q',lambda',h'),G_old>
       =q'-q-sum_ij lambda'_ij sigma_ij+B_mix.

Full PSD therefore requires

    q'-q >= sum_ij lambda'_ij sigma_ij-B_mix.      (19)

In the setting of Section4 this includes the explicit charges

    sum_ij lambda'_ij sigma_ij
       >= lambda'_(k,k+1) [a/(a-1)](q/2+q^2/(8B_n^2))
                     +nu'_(k+1)(q/2+q^2/(8B_n^2)). (20)

The two edges are distinct and every omitted charge is nonnegative.
If one instead uses a reselected base certificate that is not stationary
on this same primal at earlier cuts, the linear term
sum dh_t D_t(lambda_reselected;g) must be retained. Formula (19) with
the original stationary base already includes it inside B_mix.
No unsupported stationarity transfer across all cuts has been made.

## 6. Scope

The identities and explicit bounds (7)--(14), together with the strict
common barrier (17)--(20), are new face-level quantitative constraints.
They survive arbitrary re-selection in the full nonnegative-flow/PSD set.
They allow extra kernel directions, all remote edges, and non-Huber
levels q>1/(2sum h+1).

They do not show that a global minimizer has a positive nu_(n-2), that
every other star-outgoing multiplier vanishes, that the full slack is
rank one, or that q equals the Huber value. Even Huber tightness would
still leave the exact/asymptotic DP mass restriction. The remaining
search must pay (20) by a genuine joint schedule/flow motion, or obtain
a different uniform comparison; convex-face terminology alone does
not supply that bridge.
