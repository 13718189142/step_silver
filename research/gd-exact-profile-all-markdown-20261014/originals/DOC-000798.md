## 1. Statement and exact computable bound
Partition a prescribed finite nonnegative schedule as
(block_0,b_0,...,block_(k-1),b_(k-1),block_k),
with ordinary sums s_i. Fix epsilon>0,gamma>0 and require
b_i>=1+epsilon^(-2), 0<=i<k.
Define c=sqrt(1+2gamma+epsilon^2), R=sqrt(epsilon^2+(1+gamma)^2).

For each ordinary block independently, the finite backward recursion in Section2 supplies horizontal and vertical displacements P_i^arc,Q_i^arc. They are exact deterministic functions of the ordered steps in that block. Define
A_i^arc=P_i^arc+epsilon+(1+Q_i^arc)/epsilon,
M_i^arc=P_i^arc+max{epsilon b_i,epsilon+(1+Q_i^arc)/epsilon},
rho_i^arc=1/M_i^arc.
Put
D_0=1, D_(i+1)=D_i rho_i^arc(b_i-1).
Here D_i is an activation length, not the Markov residual weight from earlier findings.

There is one finite max-affine nonnegative J on R^(k+1), and f=env_1 J, whose exact GD trajectory for the whole supplied schedule starts at e_0. Its closest minimizer x_star is explicitly identified, with
||e_0-x_star||^2
=(rho_0^arc)^2[(epsilon+P_0^arc)^2+(1+Q_0^arc)^2].
Its terminal value is
2 f(x_n)=D_k^2/(1+2s_k).
Consequently, for k>=1,
W_n(h)>=
 (b_0-1)^2 / {[(epsilon+P_0^arc)^2+(1+Q_0^arc)^2](1+2s_k)}
 times product_(i=1)^(k-1)[(b_i-1)/M_i^arc]^2.           (1)
For k=0 the construction is the ordinary one-sided Huber witness1/(1+2s_0).

There are explicit mass-only bounds. Set
T_arc=c(1+gamma)/(epsilon gamma),
H_arc=exp(T_arc)-1,
A_arc=epsilon+exp(T_arc)/epsilon.
Then P_i^arc<=c s_i, Q_i^arc<=H_arc, and
M_i^arc<=c s_i+max{epsilon b_i,A_arc}.
Thus (1) implies the uniform, still restricted-checkpoint bound
W_n(h)>=
 (b_0-1)^2 / {[(epsilon+c s_0)^2+exp(2T_arc)](1+2s_k)}
 times product_(i=1)^(k-1)[(b_i-1)/(c s_i+max{epsilon b_i,A_arc})]^2.  (2)
There is no factor1/2 multiplying the component gradients: all nonsmooth pieces are assembled first and one Moreau envelope gives global smoothness. The exact cost is the activation buffer, which changes each transfer numerator to b_i-1.

## 2. Local arc trajectory, proved from explicit normals
Let V(q)=(p(q),-q), where
p(q)=sqrt(R^2-(q+gamma)^2), 0<=q<=1.
Then V(0)=(c,0), V(1)=(epsilon,-1),
epsilon<=p(q)<=c, and 0<=q/p(q)<=1/epsilon.
Let K be the convex hull of0 and this compact arc. It lies in the disk centered at(0,gamma) with radius R, and in the half-plane with second coordinate<=0.

For a block with m ordinary steps alpha_1,...,alpha_m, set
r_m=(u_m,-y_m)=(epsilon,-1), v_m=V(1).
Work backward. Given r_j, set Delta_j=y_j+gamma-alpha_j gamma.
If Delta_j<=u_j gamma/c, set v_(j-1)=(c,0).
Otherwise set
E_j=sqrt(u_j^2+Delta_j^2),
p_(j-1)=R u_j/E_j,
q_(j-1)=R Delta_j/E_j-gamma,
v_(j-1)=(p_(j-1),-q_(j-1)).
Finally set r_(j-1)=r_j+alpha_j v_(j-1).
This is a finite textual recursion, not a numerical optimization or feasibility assumption.

We prove inductively u_j>=epsilon, y_j>=1, y_j/u_j<=1/epsilon, v_j in the arc, and v_j the Euclidean projection of r_j onto K. At j=m these statements hold. In the turning branch,
gamma/c<Delta_j/u_j<=(1+gamma)/epsilon.
The increasing function t -> Rt/sqrt(1+t^2)-gamma takes values0 and1 at the two displayed endpoints. Hence0<q_(j-1)<=1 and the formula indeed gives V(q_(j-1)). In the other branch it gives V(0). Adding alpha_j times a vector with p>=epsilon,q>=0 preserves u>=epsilon,y>=1; the new ratio y/u is a convex combination of the preceding y/u and q/p, so remains<=1/epsilon.

In the turning branch, direct substitution gives
r_(j-1)-v_(j-1)
=[E_j/R+alpha_j-1][v_(j-1)-(0,gamma)].
The coefficient is nonnegative: the reverse triangle inequality gives
E_j>=sqrt(u_j^2+(y_j+gamma)^2)-alpha_j gamma>=R-alpha_j gamma,
so the coefficient is>=alpha_j(1-gamma/R)>=0.
The vector in brackets is an outward disk normal and therefore a normal to K at v_(j-1). In the capped branch put mu=u_j/c+alpha_j-1. Its branch inequality gives y_j<=gamma mu, so mu>0, and
r_(j-1)-(c,0)=mu(c,-gamma)+(0,gamma mu-y_j).
Both summands are nonnegative multiples of normals to K at(c,0). Thus, in both branches,
<r_(j-1)-v_(j-1),w-v_(j-1)><=0 for every w in K.
Expanding squared distances proves the claimed projection property directly. These formulas additionally prove u_j>=p_j at every index.

Set
P^arc=sum_j alpha_j p_(j-1), Q^arc=sum_j alpha_j q_(j-1).
Then r_0=(epsilon+P^arc,-1-Q^arc), and P^arc<=c sum_j alpha_j.
Once the capped branch occurs, it persists toward the beginning: after a capped update y stays fixed, u increases by alpha_j c, and y+gamma<=gamma u_new/c; subtracting any subsequent nonnegative alpha gamma preserves the cap criterion. Thus every positive q contribution belongs to one contiguous turning portion.

Here is a self-contained, crude bound for that portion. Write sigma_j=(y_j+gamma)/u_j. At a turning update,
sigma_(j-1)=sigma_j-alpha_j gamma/u_j,
log(u_(j-1)/u_j)
=log(1+alpha_j p_(j-1)/u_j)
<= (c/gamma)(sigma_j-sigma_(j-1)).
Summing over the turning portion gives its total multiplicative increase of u at most exp(T_arc), because the terminal sigma is(1+gamma)/epsilon and the final sigma is nonnegative. Since each turning increase of u is at least epsilon alpha_j, the sum of its alpha_j is at most exp(T_arc)-1. The cap contributes q=0, so
Q^arc<=exp(T_arc)-1.
This proves all bounds stated in Section1 without the paper's more delicate turning estimate.

## 3. One local max-affine piece and its proximal margins
For one block suppress the index i and use its exact P^arc,Q^arc,b,M^arc,rho^arc. Put
z_circ=(1,0)-rho^arc r_0,
U=1-rho^arc(P^arc+epsilon b)>=0,
Y=rho^arc Q^arc.
Let V_fin consist of0,(c,0),v_0,...,v_m and define
phi(z)=rho^arc max_(v in V_fin)<v,z-z_circ>.
All data are fixed once the block and checkpoint are given.
The projection inequality from Section2 remains true after restricting K to conv(V_fin), since each projected vector v_j remains in that smaller set. Therefore v_j maximizes <v,r_j-v_j> over V_fin. At
x_j=z_circ+rho^arc r_j,
the point x_j-rho^arc v_j is a proximal point of phi, and grad env_1 phi(x_j)=rho^arc v_j. This follows from the subgradient optimality equation, not from an independent interpolation assertion.

The local GD path starts at(1,0), has first coordinate nonincreasing and second coordinate nondecreasing through the ordinary block, reaches
(U+rho^arc epsilon b,Y),
and the checkpoint reaches
(U,Y+rho^arc b).
The following exact zero regions and proximal margins are needed for global gluing:
(a) phi(x,y)=0 whenever x<=0,y>=0;
(b) phi(U,y)=0 whenever y>=Y;
(c) every ordinary or checkpoint-query proximal point x_j-rho^arc v_j has first coordinate>=U>=0 and second coordinate in[0,Y+rho^arc].

To prove(a), each nonzero v=(p,-q) has p>=0,q>=0,q<=p/epsilon. At(0,0),
<v,-z_circ>=-p+rho^arc[p(epsilon+P^arc)+q(1+Q^arc)]
<=-p+rho^arc p A^arc<=0.
Monotonicity in the two coordinates then proves the whole region.
At(U,Y), each affine argument is
rho^arc[(1-b)epsilon p+q]<=0
because b>=1+epsilon^(-2); increasing y preserves this, proving(b).
For(c), the proximal first coordinate minus U equals
rho^arc[epsilon(b-1)+u_j-p_j]>=0.
Its second coordinate is rho^arc(1+Q^arc-y_j+q_j), which lies between0 and rho^arc(1+Q^arc): y_j<=1+Q^arc, q_j>=0 give the lower bound, while y_j>=1>=q_j gives the upper bound.
All these checks concern the nonsmooth piece phi, as needed for the one-envelope construction.

## 4. Global maximum and the activation buffer
Set ell_0=0 and recursively
ell_(i+1)=D_i rho_i^arc(1+Q_i^arc)=D_i(Y_i+rho_i^arc).
On coordinates0,...,k define
Phi_i(z)=D_i^2 phi_i((z_i-ell_i)/D_i,z_(i+1)/D_i), i<k.
Let delta=D_k/(1+2s_k), and define the terminal affine hinge
Phi_k(z)=delta max{z_k-ell_k,0}.
Now take the ONE nonsmooth function
J(z)=max{Phi_0(z),...,Phi_k(z)}
and its ONE envelope
f(x)=min_z{J(z)+||x-z||^2/2}.
Each Phi_i is nonnegative finite max-affine; all its nonzero affine pieces have nonpositive value at0 by Section3(a), since ell_i>=0. Hence J>=0,J(0)=0. Its envelope is globally finite, convex, differentiable and1-smooth. To verify the last assertions directly, the unique proximal minimizer z(x) exists by the quadratic's coercivity and strict convexity. The subgradient equation x-z(x) in partial J(z(x)), the two minimizing inequalities, and subgradient monotonicity yield grad f(x)=x-z(x) and
||grad f(x)-grad f(y)||^2<=<grad f(x)-grad f(y),x-y>.
Thus every stated sample, including the minimum, belongs to one globally legal function.

Inductively, at the beginning of block i, coordinates j<i are frozen at ell_j+D_j U_j, coordinate i equals ell_i+D_i, and all coordinates j>i are0. During the current block use the local path of Section3 in coordinates i,i+1. At its candidate GLOBAL proximal point:
- the current piece Phi_i has the prescribed local subgradient;
- piece i-1, if present, has its first argument frozen at U_(i-1), and its second argument at least ell_i/D_(i-1)>=Y_(i-1), because the current proximal first coordinate is>=ell_i; Section3(b) makes it0;
- all earlier pieces stay in their post-checkpoint zero regions;
- piece i+1, if present, has first coordinate at most ell_(i+1) by Section3(c), and its second coordinate0; Section3(a) makes it0;
- every later piece is also0 by Section3(a), or by the terminal hinge's threshold.
Thus the current piece dominates all other pieces at the candidate proximal point, even when its value is0. Its subgradient is consequently a subgradient of J. This proves every actual gradient and GD update, with no old or future affine plane discarded.

After checkpoint i, the new coordinate equals
D_i(Y_i+rho_i^arc b_i)=ell_(i+1)+D_i rho_i^arc(b_i-1)
=ell_(i+1)+D_(i+1),
and coordinate i is frozen at ell_i+D_i U_i, proving the induction.
The buffer D_i rho_i^arc is exactly what prevents future activation at the previous proximal query.

In the terminal block the only moving coordinate has initial height D_k above ell_k. The constant gradient delta is legal at every query and at the terminal point, because for every cumulative partial sum0<=u<=s_k,
D_k-(u+1)delta>=0.
All earlier pieces remain0 by the same post-checkpoint check. Therefore
f(x_n)=delta D_k-(s_k+1/2)delta^2=D_k^2/[2(1+2s_k)].

## 5. The actual nearest minimizer
The minimizer set of f equals {J=0}: both terms in its defining minimum are nonnegative, and the minimum is attained.
Define x_star by its first two coordinates
(x_star)_0=1-rho_0^arc(epsilon+P_0^arc),
(x_star)_1=rho_0^arc(1+Q_0^arc)=ell_1,
and all later coordinates0.
This is the first component's translated center z_circ. Hence Phi_0(x_star)=0. The next component has first input0 and second input0, so is0 by Section3(a); all later components are0 as well. For k=1 the terminal coordinate is exactly its threshold ell_1. Thus x_star is a GLOBAL minimizer, regardless of later block choices.

It is the closest one. Every affine piece of Phi_0 is active with value0 there. Moreover
e_0-x_star=rho_0^arc(epsilon+P_0^arc,-1-Q_0^arc,0,...,0)
is the following nonnegative combination of two active normal vectors:
[(P_0^arc-epsilon Q_0^arc)/c] rho_0^arc(c,0,0,...)
 +(1+Q_0^arc) rho_0^arc(epsilon,-1,0,...).
The first coefficient is nonnegative since every p_j>=epsilon q_j. For any minimizer z, its inner product with z-x_star is therefore nonpositive. Squared-distance expansion proves
||e_0-z||^2>=||e_0-x_star||^2.
This establishes the EXACT initial distance in Section1. Dividing the terminal value by it cancels the first local scale, and gives(1). It does not assume the origin is closest.

## 6. An explicit unbounded-horizon test with all quadratic errors vanishing
Take epsilon=gamma=1/10. Then c=11/10,T_arc=121,
A_arc=1/10+10 exp(121).
Choose any fixed integer N>=max{10,A_arc}. For each m>=1 consider m repetitions of a block of N unit steps followed by checkpoint3N, with no terminal ordinary steps.
The checkpoint condition3N>=101 holds. For every block,
P_i^arc<=11N/10, A_i^arc<=11N/10+A_arc<=21N/10,
M_i^arc<=11N/10+max{3N/10,A_arc}<=21N/10.
The initial distance factor also obeys
sqrt((epsilon+P_0^arc)^2+(1+Q_0^arc)^2)
<=A_0^arc<=21N/10:
the first inequality follows from 1+Q>=0 and epsilon<=1, since the sum P+epsilon+(1+Q)/epsilon dominates the Euclidean norm.
Thus every normalized transfer factor is at least
(3N-1)/(21N/10)>=29/21.
The explicit high-dimensional nonlinear construction gives
W_(m(N+1))(h)>=(29/21)^(2m).                            (3)

For the same schedules, a scalar quadratic eigenvalue lambda in[0,1] has block multiplier
q_N(lambda)=(1-lambda)^N(1-3N lambda).
If0<=lambda<=1/(3N), its magnitude is at most exp(-N lambda).
If lambda>=1/(3N), it is at most
(3N lambda-1)exp(-N lambda)<=3exp(-4/3)<4/5.
The last strict inequality follows already by summing the degree5 Taylor lower bound for exp(4/3), which exceeds15/4. Therefore every finite-dimensional convex quadratic has normalized terminal objective at most
max{1/(2 e m N),(4/5)^(2m)} ->0.
The dimension of the nonlinear witness is m+1 and its sampled checkpoint gradients contain m independent directions proportional to epsilon e_i-e_(i+1), followed by the terminal e_m direction.

The function is nonseparable in every fixed orthonormal coordinate system. Each coupled endpoint normal epsilon e_i-e_(i+1) can be exposed alone against the zero plane: choose a recession direction with all coordinates negative, with t_(j+1)<epsilon t_j for j!=i and t_(i+1)=epsilon t_i for j=i, where the coordinates are -t_j. Every other local arc normal then has negative inner product with that direction, while the selected endpoint has zero inner product. Starting on the selected affine hyperplane and moving sufficiently far along this direction makes every other nonzero plane strictly negative. The terminal normal e_m is exposed similarly with final coordinate0 and sufficiently negative preceding coordinates. Near each resulting hinge, the envelope has the rank-one Hessian projector onto that normal. Adjacent coupled normals, or the last coupled normal and e_m, are nonorthogonal and nonparallel, so the corresponding projectors do not commute. A function separable in one orthonormal basis would have commuting Hessians. This proves the assertion.

## 7. What remains open
This mutation permits arbitrary nonnegative ordinary steps and varying within-block gradient directions, retains all planes, gives exact nonzero offsets, supplies a finite real PSD Gram realization, and computes the true closest-minimizer distance. It has a concrete exponential-error family whose quadratic channel converges.

It does NOT prove the exact profile. The constants c>1,A_arc and the checkpoint threshold remain; the norm/cost bounds do not yet imply an arbitrary-schedule comparison to(1-o(1))U_n^ZJ. Taking epsilon,gamma small makes c approach1 but makes A_arc very large. No claim is made that optimizing the partition and parameters eliminates that loss. Formula(1), not a postulated covariance-feasibility assertion, is the new exact candidate interface.
