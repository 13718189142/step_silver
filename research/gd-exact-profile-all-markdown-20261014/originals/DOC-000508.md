## 2. Independent check of the sufficient interface

Normalize `L=1`. Let `h_i>1`, `r_i=h_i-1`, and let an actual convex 1-smooth GD trajectory satisfy

\[
x_{i+1}=x_i-h_i g_i,\qquad g_i=\nabla f(x_i).
\]

For distinct physical or terminal indices use the positive interpolation orientation

\[
Q_{ij}=2(f_i-f_j)-2\langle g_j,x_i-x_j\rangle
-\|g_i-g_j\|^2\ge0.
\]

Take finite real sequences satisfying

\[
A_{-1}=0<A_0\le\cdots\le A_{n-1},\qquad
D_1\ge\cdots\ge D_n\ge D_{n+1}=0.
\]

The bookkeeping value `r_n=0` supplies no physical step. For `0<=u<v<=n`, set

\[
\lambda_{uv}=(A_u-A_{u-1})(D_v+r_vD_{v+1}),
\qquad
\lambda_{vu}=(A_{u-1}+r_uA_u)(D_v-D_{v+1}).
\]

All these multipliers are nonnegative. The cumulative cut from indices `0,...,k` to indices `k+1,...,n` is

\[
F_k=A_k\left[D_{k+1}+\sum_{b=k+2}^{n}h_{b-1}D_b\right]
-D_{k+1}\left[r_kA_k+\sum_{u<k}h_uA_u\right].
\tag{2.1}
\]

Writing `T_k=sum_{i<k}h_i`, the constraints `F_k=T_{k+1}` are exactly the required divergence equations: the divergence is `h_i` at every physical index and `-S` at the terminal index. Consecutive cuts give the individual physical divergences, including the first one; total divergence gives the terminal one.

### Mixed coefficients

For `u<v`, the coefficient of `2<g_u,g_v>` in `sum lambda_ij Q_ij` is

\[
\lambda_{uv}+\lambda_{vu}
-h_u\sum_{i\le u}\lambda_{iv}
+h_v\sum_{i>v}\lambda_{iu}.
\]

At `v=n` the final sum is empty and no `h_n` is used. The two sums that occur are

\[
\sum_{i\le u}\lambda_{iv}=A_u(D_v+r_vD_{v+1}),\qquad
\sum_{i>v}\lambda_{iu}=(A_{u-1}+r_uA_u)D_{v+1}.
\]

Substitution gives zero, using only `h_u=1+r_u` and `h_v=1+r_v` at physical indices. At the terminal index it gives the same cancellation with the empty last sum. Thus the mixed cancellation is valid for the variable steps.

### Diagonal coefficients

Define

\[
q_0=1,\qquad q_k=1+2A_{k-1}D_k\quad(1\le k\le n),
\qquad \delta_k=q_k-r_k^2q_{k+1}\quad(0\le k<n).
\]

At a physical index `k`, incoming earlier mass is

\[
A_{k-1}(D_k+r_kD_{k+1}),
\]

and incoming later mass is

\[
(A_{k-1}+r_kA_k)D_{k+1}.
\]

Using divergence `h_k`, the coefficient of `||g_k||^2` is consequently

\[
-h_k-2A_{k-1}(D_k+r_kD_{k+1})
+2r_k(A_{k-1}+r_kA_k)D_{k+1}
=-h_k+2r_k^2A_kD_{k+1}-2A_{k-1}D_k.
\]

The term involving `A_{-1}` is zero at `k=0`. At the terminal index the coefficient is `S+1-q_n`. Therefore the root identity is correct:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&2\sum_{k<n}h_k(f_k-f_n)
+\sum_{k<n}h_k(1-h_k)\|g_k\|^2-\Gamma\|g_n\|^2\\
&\quad=\sum_{i\ne j}\lambda_{ij}Q_{ij}
+\sum_{k<n}\delta_k\|g_k\|^2
+(q_n-1-S-\Gamma)\|g_n\|^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Thus all cut equations, monotonicity, `delta_k>=0`, and `q_n>=1+S+Gamma` are a sufficient interface. This audit identifies no sign or index correction to that interface.

### Terminal normalization and the `17/16` threshold

Let the left side of (2.2) be `H_Gamma`. Summing the distance identity and the valid minimizer interpolation inequality gives

\[
R^2\ge\|x_n-x_\star\|^2+2S(f_n-f_\star)
+\Gamma\|g_n\|^2+H_\Gamma,
\qquad R=\|x_0-x_\star\|.
\]

If `H_Gamma>=0`, set `Delta=f_n-f_star`. For `g_n!=0`, terminal interpolation and Cauchy-Schwarz yield

\[
R^2\ge(2S+1)\Delta
+\frac{\Delta^2}{\|g_n\|^2}
+\left(\Gamma+\frac14\right)\|g_n\|^2.
\]

For `Gamma+1/4>=0`, the last two terms are at least
`2 Delta sqrt(Gamma+1/4)`. If `g_n=0`, convexity gives `Delta=0`. With `W=2 Delta/R^2`, this is the full-class implication

\[
\mathcal W_n(h)\le
\frac1{S+\frac12+\sqrt{\Gamma+\frac14}}.
\]

The sufficient terminal threshold is therefore exactly

\[
\Gamma\ge\frac{225}{289}\left(S+\frac12\right)^2-\frac14
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal W_n(h)\le\frac{17}{16(1+2S)}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

This implication keeps the original chosen minimizer and its distance. It remains valid as a conditional statement despite the family obstruction below.

## 3. Exact prefix identity with arbitrary diagonal slack

For `0<=k<=n`, define the two nonnegative weighted sums

\[
B_k=\sum_{i<k}h_iA_i,\qquad
E_k=\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}h_iD_{i+1},\qquad
\Delta_k^{\rm slack}=\sum_{i<k}\delta_i.
\]

The empty sums are `B_0=0`, `E_n=0`, and `Delta_0^slack=0`. The symbol `Delta_k^slack` is a sum of certificate residuals, unrelated to the function gap in Section 2.

**Prefix identity.** Every solution of all the cut equations satisfies

\[
\boxed{q_k+\Delta_k^{\rm slack}=(T_k+1)^2-2B_kE_k,
\qquad 0\le k\le n.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

This identity does not assume any diagonal equality or prescribed product law.

To prove it, fix `k<n` and abbreviate `h=h_k`, `r=r_k`, `A=A_k`, and `D=D_{k+1}`. Equation (2.1) becomes

\[
AE_k-DB_k-2rAD=T_k+h.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The exact updates of the finite sums are

\[
B_{k+1}=B_k+hA,\qquad E_{k+1}=E_k-hD,
\qquad q_{k+1}=1+2AD.
\]

Using (3.2), the increment of the right side of (3.1) is

\[
\begin{aligned}
&2h(T_k+1)+h^2-2h(AE_k-DB_k)+2h^2AD\\
&\qquad=h(2-h)(1+2AD)=(1-r^2)q_{k+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The increment of the left side is also

\[
q_{k+1}-q_k+\delta_k=(1-r^2)q_{k+1}.
\]

Both sides equal one at `k=0`, proving (3.1) by symbolic induction.

If every `delta_i>=0`, (3.1) gives the quantitative prefix bound

\[
q_k\le(T_k+1)^2.
\tag{3.3}
\]

In fact the inequality is strict for `1<=k<n`. The last cut is

\[
S=D_n\left[(1-r_{n-1})A_{n-1}-B_{n-1}\right].
\]

Since `S>0`, it forces `D_n>0`; monotonicity then gives every `D_i>0`. Thus `B_k>0` and `E_k>0` at every such interior index. The strict version follows from (3.1), even if all preceding diagonal slacks vanish.

At the terminal index, (3.1) specializes to

\[
q_n+\sum_{k<n}\delta_k=(S+1)^2.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Consequently, if the largest compatible penalty `Gamma=q_n-1-S` were used, the threshold (2.3) would require and be equivalent to

\[
\sum_{k<n}\delta_k\le
\frac{64}{289}\left(S+\frac12\right)^2.
\]

The exclusion below uses the strictly stronger prefix information in (3.1), rather than treating this global slack budget as the only constraint.

## 4. A necessary central-bridge inequality for the entire family

Consider any palindrome with a nonempty left block of total mass `s`, central physical step `1+r` with `r>0`, and right block of the same total mass `s`. Let `k` be the central physical index. Thus

\[
T_k=s,\qquad T_{k+1}=s+1+r.
\]

Assume the full free `A/D` family is feasible, including every physical diagonal sign. This implies

\[
r^2q_{k+1}\le q_k<(s+1)^2.
\tag{4.1}
\]

In the central cut, `D_{i+1}<=D_{k+1}` for every right-block index `i>k`. Therefore

\[
E_{k+1}\le sD_{k+1}.
\]

Writing `z=A_kD_{k+1}>0`, equation (2.1) and `B_k>0` give

\[
\begin{aligned}
s+1+r
&=A_kE_{k+1}+(1-r)z-D_{k+1}B_k\\
&<z(s+1-r).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.2}
\]

It follows first that `s+1-r>0`; if this denominator were nonpositive, feasibility would already be impossible. Since `q_{k+1}=1+2z`, (4.2) then implies

\[
q_{k+1}>1+\frac{2(s+1+r)}{s+1-r}
=\frac{3(s+1)+r}{s+1-r}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Set `x=r/(s+1)`, so that `0<x<1`. Combining (4.1) and (4.3) gives

\[
x^2\frac{3+x}{1-x}<1.
\]

The numerator after bringing the right side to the left factors exactly:

\[
x^3+3x^2+x-1=(x+1)(x^2+2x-1).
\]

Thus every feasible member of this family necessarily satisfies

\[
\boxed{\frac r{s+1}<\sqrt2-1=\frac1\Lambda.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

The proof allows arbitrary dependence of `A` and `D` on the whole word, arbitrary nonnegative diagonal slack, and every terminal value. No child certificate, local saturation equality, deleted interpolation row, or fixed multiplier array was assumed.

## 5. The actual same-`m` hierarchy violates the necessary inequality

Let `m>=5` and `j=m-1`. At the central bridge of `H_m`,

\[
r=ac^j,\qquad s=\sum H_j.
\]

The exact finite geometric identity for the left mass is

\[
s=2^j-1+a\frac{c^j-2^j}{c-2}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

It follows either by counting the `2^(j-1-l)` copies of the level-`l` step or by symbolic induction on the defining concatenation. No recurrence was executed. Dividing (5.1) by `r` gives

\[
\frac{s+1}{r}
=\frac1{c-2}
+\left(\frac1a-\frac1{c-2}\right)\left(\frac2c\right)^j.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Use `Lambda^2=2 Lambda+1`. Direct symbolic expansion yields

\[
\Lambda(c-2)-1=\Lambda^2\varepsilon,
\]
\[
c-2-a=\frac{\varepsilon}{c}
\left(2\Lambda+3+\Lambda^2\varepsilon\right).
\]

Consequently (5.2) gives the exact gap formula

\[
\Lambda-\frac{s+1}{r}
=\frac{\varepsilon}{\beta(c-2)}
\left[
\beta\Lambda^2-
\left(2\Lambda+3+\Lambda^2\varepsilon\right)
\left(\frac2c\right)^j
\right].
\tag{5.3}
\]

Every prefactor in (5.3) is positive. The bracket is strictly positive uniformly for every `m>=5`, as shown by the following rational bounds:

\[
\varepsilon\le\frac1{20},\quad
\beta\ge\frac{19}{20}>\frac9{10},\quad
\frac{12}{5}<\Lambda<\frac52,\quad \Lambda^2>5.
\]

Thus

\[
\beta\Lambda^2>\frac92,
\qquad
2\Lambda+3+\Lambda^2\varepsilon
<8+\frac5{16}<9.
\]

Also `j>=4` and `c>Lambda>12/5`, so

\[
\left(\frac2c\right)^j
<\left(\frac56\right)^4
=\frac{625}{1296}<\frac12.
\]

The second term in the bracket of (5.3) is therefore smaller than `9/2`, whereas the first is larger than `9/2`. Equation (5.3) proves

\[
\frac{s+1}{r}<\Lambda,
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
\boxed{\frac r{s+1}>\frac1\Lambda.}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Equations (4.4) and (5.4) contradict one another. Hence no monotone nonnegative `A,D` can satisfy all the cut equations and all the nonnegative physical diagonal conditions on these words for any `m>=5`. In particular, no terminal `Gamma` in the specified family can reach the `17/16` target at any requested `m>=1536`.

## 6. Consequence and limits

The family-level exclusion is complete within this direct analytical draft. There is no remaining inequality for an explicit `A/D` law to solve on the requested hierarchy: the necessary root condition already fails. Reopening the same family with another scalar law or another allocation of nonnegative diagonal slack cannot evade (3.1)-(5.4).

This proof supplies neither an interpolation-feasible hard function nor a lower bound on `W_n(H_m)`. It does not assert that the history inequality `H_Gamma>=0` is false when certified by a different multiplier family. It does not exclude a certificate with a non-diagonal positive semidefinite residual, another dense kernel, or a different potential. Accordingly it cannot be combined with the task-supplied accepted benchmark comparison to conclude separation. The original objective upper bound and original minimax target remain OPEN.
