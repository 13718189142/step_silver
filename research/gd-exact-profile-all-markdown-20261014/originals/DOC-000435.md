## 3. Elementary growth and adjacent-value bounds

First, 1<rho<4/3: lambda>2 and lambda^3=7+5sqrt(2)<16. Thus s>3/2
and lambda^(3/2)<4.

For completeness, Omega(x,y)<=(x^(1/rho)+y^(1/rho))^rho for x,y>=0.
For positive x,y, put A=x^(1/rho), C=y^(1/rho), and T=(A+C)^rho.
Let D(t)=(1+t)^rho-1. For g(z)=log D(exp z), differentiation gives

    g''(z) = rho*t*(1+t)^(rho-2)
             *((1+t)^rho-1-rho*t)/D(t)^2 > 0,  t=exp z.

The last bracket has value zero at t=0 and strictly positive derivative for
t>0. Hence D(t)D(1/t)>=D(1)^2=(lambda-1)^2=2. It follows that
(T-x)(T-y)>=2xy, or T^2-(x+y)T-xy>=0. Since T>=x+y and Omega is the
positive root of that polynomial, T>=Omega(x,y). Zero arguments follow by
continuity. Induction in the full primitive recurrence gives U_N<=N^rho.
Balanced dyadic joins give U_(2^j)=lambda^j; monotonicity then yields

    N^rho/lambda <= U_N <= N^rho,  N>=1.                  (3.1)

Also G(a,d)>=Omega(a,d) for a,d>=0. Indeed, after multiplying their
difference by two, the required inequality is
3a+sqrt(d^2+8ad)>=sqrt(a^2+6ad+d^2); squaring the nonnegative sides
leaves 8a^2+2ad+6a sqrt(d^2+8ad)>=0. Induction gives V_N>=U_N.

Here is the adjacent primitive estimate derived from (2.1). Let
R_N=U_(N+1)/U_N, Delta(r)=sqrt(r^2+6r+1), and

    T0(r)=(1+r+Delta(r))/(2lambda),
    T1(r)=lambda*(Delta(r)-r-1)/2.

Then R_(2N)=T0(R_N), R_(2N+1)=T1(R_N). Both maps fix 1 and have positive
derivative at most 1/2 on [1,lambda]: their derivatives are
(1+(r+3)/Delta(r))/(2lambda) and lambda*((r+3)/Delta(r)-1)/2;
(r+3)/Delta(r) has derivative -8/Delta(r)^3. Starting at R_1=lambda,
binary induction gives

    0<R_N-1 <= (lambda-1)/2^floor(log_2 N) <= 2sqrt(2)/N.

Using (3.1), U_(N+1)-U_N<=2sqrt(2) N^(rho-1). With U_0=0 this implies
U_m-U_(m-1)<=2sqrt(2) m^(rho-1) for every m>=1.

The full dominant recurrence now gives

    0 < V_(N+1)-V_N <= 8sqrt(2) N^(rho-1).                (3.2)

For the upper bound, take a maximizing prefix m for V_(N+1). If m>=2,
compare it to the admissible split (m-1,N+1-m) for V_N. If m=1, use
G(U_0,V_N)=V_N. The derivative of G with respect to its first variable is
2+2d/sqrt(d^2+8ad)<=4. Since m<=N, the bound follows. Strict increase
follows by choosing prefix 1 in the recurrence.

Consequently, with Delta_n=rootB_(n+1)-rootB_n and barq_n=(rootB_n+rootB_(n+1))/2,

    Delta_n <= 4sqrt(2lambda) (n+1)^(rho/2-1),
    delta_n := Delta_n^2/(4barq_n)
             <= 8lambda^(3/2) (n+1)^(rho/2-2)
             < 32 n^(-4/3),  n>=1.                       (3.3)

The denominator bound used here is rootB_n>=sqrt(U_(n+1))
>=(n+1)^(rho/2)/sqrt(lambda). No concavity of rootB_n is asserted.

## 4. Exact merge inequality and contraction along maximizing trees

Let a,b,d>0, write Q=sqrt(d), u=T_b(Q), t=T_a(u), where

    T_a(v)=(v+sqrt(v^2+8a))/2,  T_a(v)^2=G(a,v^2).

If a<=d, then

    G(a,G(b,d)) < G(Omega(a,b),d).                        (4.1)

To prove this, u>Q and Q(u+Q)>2Q^2>=2a. Since t is the positive
solution of t^2-ut=2a, this implies t<u+Q. Set r=t/u and c=a+br.
The polynomial defining Omega gives

    c^2-(a+b)c-ab = ab*(t-u-Q)/u < 0.

Here r(r-1)=2a/u^2 and 2b/u^2=1-Q/u were substituted to obtain the
identity. Thus c<Omega(a,b). Finally,
t^2-Qt=2a+(u-Q)t=2(a+bt/u)=2c<2Omega(a,b), so T_Omega(a,b)(Q)>t.
Squaring proves (4.1).

Consider two successive right-continuation joins in ANY maximizing B tree.
Let their primitive leaf counts be m1,m2 and the remaining leaf count be R.
Their values are a=U_m1, b=U_m2, d=V_R. The same total leaf count permits
merging the two primitive blocks: Omega(a,b)<=U_(m1+m2). Therefore (4.1)
would strictly improve the maximizing tree if a<=d. Necessarily a>d.
By (3.1) and V_R>=U_R,

    m1^rho >= a > d >= R^rho/lambda,
    R < 2m1,
    R < (2/3)(m1+m2+R).                                  (4.2)

Thus the remaining leaf count decreases by a factor less than 2/3 every
TWO edges along a maximizing right spine. This is an optimality argument
for the exact full DP, not a restriction imposed on allowed splits.

## 5. A one-step rounding defect inequality

Extend C_-1=0. Let v_half(x) be linear interpolation of rootB_n on nonnegative
integers: v_half(n+theta)=(1-theta)rootB_n+theta rootB_(n+1), 0<=theta<=1. Define

    e_t = max(0, rootB_t/s - v_half(t/2)),  t>=0.                  (5.1)

For a maximizing split k+l=t-1, the claim is

    e_t <= e_l                              if t is even,
    e_t <= e_l+delta_floor(t/2)              if t is odd. (5.2)

Only t>4095 will require the odd formula, so delta_0 need not be bounded.

The primitive bounds from (2.1) are

    C_(2a+1)/lambda=C_a,
    C_(2a)/lambda<= (C_(a-1)+C_a)/2.                      (5.3)

For a>=1 the second follows from
Omega(x,y)<=lambda(x+y)/2, obtained using 4xy<=(x+y)^2. For a=0,
C_0/lambda<=1/2=(C_-1+C_0)/2 because lambda>2.

The exact dominant split inequality can be written without a radical:

    2C_i+rootB_j rootB_m <= rootB_m^2,  i+j=m-1.                    (5.4)

It is equivalent to T_(C_i)(rootB_j)<=rootB_m. We additionally permit the formal
boundary i=-1,j=m, where (5.4) is equality.

If t=2n and k=2a+1,l=2b, then a+b=n-1. Use (5.4) directly. If
k=2a,l=2b+1, then a+b=n-1; average (5.4) at the two splits
(a-1,b+1) and (a,b), both with parent n. Using (5.3) gives a root at
most rootB_n in either case before adding the continuation defect e_l.

For t=2n+1, set Qbar=(rootB_n+rootB_(n+1))/2. If k=2a+1,l=2b+1,
then a+b=n-1. Average the inequalities with parent/split
(n;a,b) and (n+1;a,b+1). For u=C_a and w=(rootB_b+rootB_(b+1))/2, this gives

    2u+Qbar*w-Qbar^2 <= (Delta_n^2-Delta_n*Delta_b)/4
                       <= Delta_n^2/4.

If k=2a,l=2b, then a+b=n. Average parent/split
(n;a-1,b) and (n+1;a,b). With u=(C_(a-1)+C_a)/2 and w=rootB_b,
the corresponding upper bound is Delta_n^2/4. Boundary a=0 uses the
formal equality in (5.4). In both cases w<=Qbar. The derivative of
y^2-wy-2u for y>=Qbar is at least Qbar. Its positive root is therefore
at most Qbar+Delta_n^2/(4Qbar)=Qbar+delta_n.

Finally, T_a is increasing and 1-Lipschitz in its nonnegative argument:
T_a'(v)=(1+v/sqrt(v^2+8a))/2<=1. Homogeneity and
rootB_l/s<=v_half(l/2)+e_l imply

    rootB_t/s = T_(C_k/lambda)(rootB_l/s)
           <= T_(C_k/lambda)(v_half(l/2))+e_l.

Combining this with the preceding parity cases proves (5.2).

    0 <= Z_b-lambda^(-j)B_(M-1) <= E(b,j).               (9.4)

    Z_b-lambda^(-j)B_(M-1)

## 10. Full original-space seed condition

For a nonnegative word h of length n, W_n(h) denotes the supremum of
2(f(x_n)-f(x_star))/(L||x_0-x_star||^2), over every finite-dimensional
Euclidean space, every convex differentiable f with L-Lipschitz gradient
and a minimizer x_star, and every x_0!=x_star, under
x_(i+1)=x_i-(h_i/L)grad f(x_i). Here L>0, and U_n^ZJ=1/B_n.

Normalize L=1, translate a selected minimizer to zero, and subtract f_star
so that its value is also zero; the optimum jet is x_star=g_star=f_star=0. For a finite
nonnegative word h of length r, let s=sum h_i, A=1+s, and retain every
formal gradient g_0,...,g_r, including gradients at zero steps. Put

    x_i=x_0-sum_(t<i)h_t g_t,
    Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2,
    V_h=2sum_(i<r)h_i(f_i-f_r)
          +sum_(i<r)h_i(1-h_i)||g_i||^2-s(s+1)||g_r||^2.

Every actual smooth convex trajectory has Q_ij<=0 by accepted fact1252b8823bb5a290, proof paragraph1, with exactly this residual orientation. We call the seed
primitive when V_h>=0 on every such trajectory in every finite dimension.
A concrete sufficient certificate retains ALL ordered finite multipliers
Lambda_ij>=0, i!=j, and Lambda_ii=0. Let e be the all-ones column, and set

    T_ii=1, T_it=h_t for t<i, T_it=0 for t>i,
    L_flow=diag(Lambda e)-Lambda,
    omega=(-2h_0,...,-2h_(r-1),2s)^T,
    D_prim=diag(h_0(2-h_0),...,h_(r-1)(2-h_(r-1)),-s(s+2)).

For a real matrix K, sym(K)=(K+K^T)/2. Gram(g_0,...,g_r) denotes the matrix of all pairwise Euclidean inner products of these formal gradients, and <M,Gram> means trace(M^T Gram).

The feasibility conditions are

    L_flow^T e=omega,
    M_prim=D_prim+(L_flow T+T^T L_flow^T)/2 >=0.            (4)

Indeed substituting the GD equations and collecting function coefficients
and gradient inner products gives the exact identity

    V_h+sum_(i!=j)Lambda_ij Q_ij=<M_prim,Gram(g_0,...,g_r)>.

To audit the matrix formula, the position terms of sum Lambda Q contribute

sym(L_flow)-diag(omega)/2. Adding V_h's diagonal yields (4).
Thus (4) proves the required actual primitive inequality. It has no support,
rank, symmetry, quotient, or terminal-edge restriction.

Adding optimum multipliers cannot supply a missing positive x_0 diagonal:
that diagonal is zero. Its mixed coefficient with g_i is
-Lambda_(i,star), so PSD forces each Lambda_(i,star)=0. Summing the finite
function-flow equations then forces sum_i Lambda_(star,i)=0, and their

No equality between formal gradients at duplicated query positions is
imposed. For the implication used here, sufficiency of (4) is enough;
no unproved dual attainment is invoked.

## 11. Complete primitive and dominant composition identities

Primitive join: let a,b be primitive, with capacities A=1+sum a and
C=1+sum b. Choose d>0 by d(A+C+d)=AC, put T_mass=A+C+d and
kappa=(T_mass/C)^2, and use the physical word h=(a,1+d,b).
Let p be its bridge query, z its terminal query, and j range over the
nonterminal queries of b. Direct expansion gives

    V_h=V_a+kappa V_b-(kappa-1) E_PP,
    E_PP=sum_j b_j Q_(j,p)+Q_(z,p)+d Q_(p,z)
                                      +sum_j b_j Q_(j,z).       (5)

For a coefficient audit, the value part of E_PP is
2sum b_j f_j+(d-C)f_p+(2-C-d)f_z. Its gradient part is

    sum_j b_j(1-b_j)||g_j||^2
       +[(C-d)/2-d^2]||g_p||^2+(C+d)||g_z||^2/2.

The remaining coefficient identities are
(kappa-1)(C-d)=2(A+d), kappa d^2=A^2, and kappa C^2=T_mass^2.
They follow from d T_mass=AC. Also 0<d<C and kappa>1, so all
added multipliers are nonnegative. If the child certificates have arbitrary
PSD remainders, the full parent remainder is their original-coordinate sum
M_a+kappa M_b, embedded on the actual child-gradient indices. The resulting
capacity is exactly Omega(A,C).
The physical bridge obeys 1+d=phi(A-1,C-1), as required by the recurrences in Section 1.

Dominant join: for a word h with terminal z, put D=2sum h+1. For
nonnegative weights u_i summing to D define

    J_(h,u)=||x_0||^2+2sum_i u_i Q_(i,star)-2D f_z
                                  -||x_0-sum_i u_i g_i||^2.    (6)

The empty word has u=1 and J=0. A primitive word is dominant with
u=(h,s+1), since direct expansion gives J=V_h. In general J>=0 implies
2f_z<=||x_0||^2/D on every actual trajectory.

Let a be a primitive left child of capacity A and b a dominant right child
with mass D and weights v. Define

    mu=2A/D,  zeta=(1+sqrt(1+8A/D))/2,
    gamma=A+1,  beta=1+A/zeta.

Use the physical parent word (a,beta,b), and weights u=(a,gamma,zeta v).
Their mass is G(A,D)=D zeta^2=2A+zeta D=2sum(parent)+1. With p the
bridge query, all right-child queries i>p, including its terminal, give

    J_parent=V_a+zeta^2 J_right
                         -2mu sum_(i>p)v_i Q_(i,p).             (7)

The physical bridge is exactly beta=psi(A-1,(D-1)/2); multiply
zeta^2-zeta=2A/D by D/zeta to check this against the radical in psi.

This is an equality on the full original formal space. Function
coefficients use mu D=2A. Expanding all squares and the GD bridge uses

    (A-1)A+mu D+gamma=gamma^2,
    zeta^2=zeta+mu,  zeta^2 beta=mu+gamma zeta;

these cancel the mixed terms and give (7). The child remainder is pulled
back from the actual right-child starting position x_p-beta g_p; hence the
original x_0 and all inherited gradients remain in the certificate.
For an additional coefficient check, put y=x_p-beta g_p,
xi_i=x_i-y on the right child, and R_v=sum_(i>p)v_i g_i. Expanding
J_parent-V_a-zeta^2 J_right gives precisely

    -2mu sum_i v_i(f_i-f_p)
    -mu sum_i v_i||g_i||^2-mu D||g_p||^2
    +2mu sum_i v_i<g_i,xi_i>-2mu(beta-1)<R_v,g_p>.

Indeed the left-prefix displacement cancels between the position terms
and the final square; its remaining diagonal is V_a's diagonal. The
bridge diagonal difference is -2A=-mu D. The right-child local quadratic
has coefficient zeta-zeta^2=-mu, and the remaining bridge cross coefficient
is 2zeta(beta-gamma)=-2mu(beta-1). As

    Q_(i,p)=f_i-f_p+(beta-1)<g_i,g_p>-<g_i,xi_i>
                         +(||g_i||^2+||g_p||^2)/2,

the displayed expansion is exactly the last term in (7). This also checks
the endpoint i=z and the empty-right-child case without deleting either.
All extra interpolation coefficients are nonnegative. Induction on the
actual tree supplies a full PSD objective certificate: divide (6), after
inserting its nonnegative interpolation representation, by D. Its PSD
