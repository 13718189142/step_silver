## 3. Integer-ray monotonicity and exact dyadic scaling

    B_(n+1)>=G(1,B_n)>B_n,

    B_(b*2^j-1)<=B_(c*2^j-1)

## 4. The finite product and its strict margin

Choose positive integers b_pc0,d,K and an integer ell>=0. Let C_pc be a
positive certified upper bound for Z_d/Z_(b_pc0). Suppose

    m^K b_pc0<=2^ell d.

Define, for 0<=i<=K,

    b_pc_i=b_pc0 m^i,
    P_pc=lambda^ell C_pc.

All quantities Z_b are positive. Exact telescoping, monotonicity, and
(6), in that order, prove

    product_(i=0)^(K-1) [Z_(m b_pc_i)/Z_(b_pc_i)]
      =Z_(m^K b_pc0)/Z_(b_pc0)
      <=Z_(2^ell d)/Z_(b_pc0)
      =lambda^ell Z_d/Z_(b_pc0)
      <=P_pc.                                             (7)

Every inequality has the direction required for an UPPER product bound.
In particular this argument never turns an upper ray enclosure into a
lower bound for an unknown ratio.

Also m^K b_pc0>=b_pc0, so monotonicity makes the telescoped ratio at least 1.
Thus P_pc>=1, and the strict capacity condition A^K>P_pc implies A>1.

It is convenient to allow a certified capacity floor a with 1<a<=A.
Assume a^K>P_pc and choose a positive gamma satisfying

    K gamma<=1-P_pc/a^K.                                  (8)

Necessarily gamma<1. Write

    q_pc_i(a)=Z_(m b_pc_i)/(a Z_(b_pc_i)).

The product of the q_pc_i(a) is at most t_pc=P_pc/a^K<1. Therefore some
index i_star in {0,...,K-1} satisfies q_pc_i_star(a)<=t_pc^(1/K).
Otherwise their product would exceed t_pc.

For completeness, for x in [0,1] the inequality
(1-x)^K>=1-Kx follows by induction: multiplying the induction bound by
1-x adds the nonnegative term K x^2. With x=gamma, (8) gives

    (1-gamma)^K>=1-K gamma>=t_pc.

Consequently there is a fixed index i_star with

    Z_(m b_pc_i_star)/(A Z_(b_pc_i_star))
      <=q_pc_i_star(a)<=1-gamma.                             (9)

This is an existence assertion about a finite list, not a selection of
an unproved optimal phase. Sections 5--6 make it a physical and certified
statement.

the fixed shift m-1; in (10) use b_pc_(K-1) and Z_(b_pc_(K-1)). These give
common errors tending to zero, while every denominator limit is at least
A because Z_b>=L_pc(b,0)=B_(b-1)>=1. Therefore (11) is uniform over
the finite list and over all maximizing-tree ties. No interchange of
an infinite phase minimum with a limit is involved.

For any one fixed selected i, the integers N_(i,j) strictly increase
and tend to infinity with j. The words at different horizons need not
be prefixes of one another; the original prescribed-horizon guarantee
does not require that property.

## 6. Certified phase selection and an explicit common horizon

At a search depth t>=0 compute, or supply certified enclosures for,
the K quantities

    F_pc_i(t)=U_pc(m b_pc_i,t)/(a L_pc(b_pc_i,t)).                   (12)

They are valid upper bounds on q_pc_i(a): the numerator is bounded from
above and the positive denominator from below. Both endpoints in (2)
converge, so F_pc_i(t)->q_pc_i(a). Choose the first t for which some

    F_pc_i(t)<1-gamma/2,                                    (13)

and freeze the smallest such i. This rule terminates: the index supplied
by (9) has limit at most 1-gamma, leaving the strict slack gamma/2.
It is unnecessary to decide which index minimizes the true ray ratio.

This is a certified convergent procedure, not a prescription to use
uncertified floating-point comparisons. For rational a and gamma, it has
the following finite-precision implementation in principle. At stage
t>=1, form outward rational enclosures of each L_pc(b_pc_i,t) and
U_pc(m b_pc_i,t) with absolute error at most 2^(-t), keeping every lower
denominator positive. Use the lower L endpoint and upper U endpoint
in (12), and stop only upon the strict rational inequality (13).
Each finite B value is defined by finitely many additions, products,
positive square roots, and maxima in the ORIGINAL recurrence. These
operations admit arbitrarily accurate rational outward enclosures:
rational square comparisons bracket each positive square root, and the
maximum of lower or upper endpoints bounds the corresponding maximum.
For a fixed stage only finitely many such operations are needed. The
recurrences have positive inputs, so arbitrary prescribed absolute
accuracy follows by refinement. The extra errors tend to zero with t,
and termination follows from the same strict slack. There is no claim
that this procedure is computationally economical, and it was not run.

More generally, an arbitrary real seed need not come with a computable
capacity oracle. The effective input is a certified rational floor
1<a<=A with a^K>P_pc, and a certified positive rational gamma satisfying
(8). If A^K>P_pc, such choices exist by strictness and rational density;
their certification uses the supplied seed data. This states the actual
input required for effective selection rather than presuming an oracle
for arbitrary real numbers. The exact-real formulation (12)--(13) is
also a valid mathematical selection rule.

It remains to turn the selected ratio into a bound for every sufficiently
large physical horizon. Put b_pc_max=b_pc_(K-1) and

    J=ceil(log(60 b_pc_max/gamma)/log(3/2)).                  (14)

This can equivalently be selected by the finite integer test
(3/2)^J>=60 b_pc_max/gamma, with rational arithmetic when gamma is rational.
Its finiteness follows from 3/2>1.

Here is a common error bound with no unproved rates. Since
sqrt(2)>5/4, lambda>9/4 and sqrt(lambda)>3/2. Also sqrt(2)<3/2,
and (4) gives rho-1<1 and rho/2<1. For b>=1,
j>=0, and q=(2/3)^j,

    E(b,j)+2lambda^(-j/2)
       <[12b+(3/25)(b+1)+9/10000+2]q
       <=[12+6/25+9/10000+2]b q
       <15b q.                                          (15)

Every inequality uses nonnegative terms; 2^(-j)<=q and
lambda^(-j)<=q as well. Thus for all i and all j>=J,

    E(b_pc_i,j)+2u_j<gamma/4,   u_j=lambda^(-j/2).            (16)

By (2) and Z_(b_pc_i)>=1,

    B_(n_(i,j))>=lambda^j Z_(b_pc_i)(1-E(b_pc_i,j)).

Also (4) implies B_(n_(i,j))>=lambda^j, so

    (1-A^(-1/2))/sqrt(B_(n_(i,j)))<=u_j.

Using (5), monotonicity, and the LOWER ray enclosure for the larger ray,

    B_(N_(i,j))<=B_(m b_pc_i 2^j-1)<=lambda^j Z_(m b_pc_i),

we get, whenever (16) holds,

    B_(N_(i,j))W_(N_(i,j))(H_(i,j))
       <=[Z_(m b_pc_i)/(A Z_(b_pc_i))]
          /[(1-E(b_pc_i,j))(1-u_j)^2].                      (17)

The factors in the denominator are positive by (16). For 0<=E,u<1,

    (1-E)(1-u)^2
       =1-E-2u+2Eu+(1-E)u^2>=1-E-2u.

Hence that denominator is greater than 1-gamma/4. For the fixed index
selected by (13), Z_(m b_pc_i)/(A Z_(b_pc_i))<=q_pc_i(a)<1-gamma/2, so

    B_(N_(i,j))W_(N_(i,j))(H_(i,j))
       <(1-gamma/2)/(1-gamma/4)
       <1-gamma/4,            EVERY j>=J.                (18)

The last strict inequality follows from
(1-gamma/4)^2-(1-gamma/2)=gamma^2/16>0. Thus the explicit positive
relative separation is gamma/4, and the chosen phase is fixed before
the unbounded horizon sequence is used. The family and selection depend
only on the seed and benchmark, never on an objective or its trajectory.

## 7. The exponent interface follows from a finite integer cover

Take b_pc0=d=1 and C_pc=1. Then (7) becomes precisely

    product_(i=0)^(K-1) Z_(m^(i+1))/Z_(m^i)<=lambda^ell
       whenever m^K<=2^ell.                              (19)

Thus A^K>lambda^ell is sufficient. This finite condition for some
integers K>=1,ell>=0 is equivalent to A>m^rho. In one direction,
lambda^ell=(2^ell)^rho>=m^(K rho), so the strict power inequality implies
A>m^rho. Conversely, if A>m^rho, choose an integer K with

    K log(A/m^rho)>log(lambda)

and set ell=ceil(K log_2(m)). Then m^K<=2^ell<2m^K, and

    lambda^ell<lambda m^(K rho)<A^K.                     (20)

Here rho>0 and lambda=2^rho justify raising inequalities to rho.
If desired first choose a rational a with m^rho<a<A and perform the
same argument with a; Sections 4--6 then apply with certified inputs.
This proves sufficiency of A>(r+1)^rho without a claim about an
exponent obstruction or capacity ceiling for arbitrary seeds.

For an accepted upper comparison Z_d/Z_(b_pc0)<=C_pc, the stronger finite
interface available from (7) is

    A>(lambda^ell C_pc)^(1/K),
    m^K b_pc0<=2^ell d.                                    (21)

The word "stronger" refers to using this additional certified comparison;
it is not an assertion that every choice in (21) beats (19), or that
its value is below m^rho. For instance, at K=1,ell=0, b_pc0=3 and d=3m,
the accepted constants in `4c92d3fc4eb6153e` recover its sufficient
capacity hypotheses for r=5 and r=6. Its more specialized horizon and
margin estimates remain valid in their stated scope.

## 9. Complete conditional theorem proved by the joint argument

Fix the original function class and ALL-split benchmark from Section 1.
Suppose an actual length-r seed, r>=1, satisfies EVERY condition (1),
with h>=0 and no coordinate identifications, and put m=r+1, A=1+sum h.
Let b_pc0,d,K be positive integers and ell>=0 an integer. Let C_pc>0 be
a proved upper bound for Z_d/Z_(b_pc0) and assume

    m^K b_pc0<=2^ell d,    A^K>lambda^ell C_pc.

Choose any certified floor 1<a<=A and gamma>0 satisfying

    a^K>lambda^ell C_pc,
    K gamma<=1-lambda^ell C_pc/a^K.

Such real choices exist, and rational choices exist under the same strict
inequality, as explained in Section 6. Define the K physical families
H_(i,j), the exact horizons N_(i,j)=m b_pc0 m^i 2^j-m, and J by (14).
Then the certified convergent rule (12)--(13), or its rational outward
enclosure version, selects a FIXED i in {0,...,K-1}, terminates after
finitely many stages, and for every integer j>=J gives

    W_(N_(i,j))(H_(i,j))<(1-gamma/4) U_(N_(i,j))^ZJ.

The horizons form an unbounded increasing integer sequence. The full
all-function upper certificates and Huber equalities are supplied by the
actual basic-tree construction (5); every limiting comparison and finite
bound uses these actual horizons. The family is finite in its phase
index, and the error estimates are uniform over that finite index and
all maximizing-tree ties.

In particular A>(r+1)^rho is a sufficient interface, witnessed by some
finite K and ell. The exact separate sufficient capacities A>=49/5 for
r=5 and A>=12 for r=6, with the phase lists, J values, and relative
margins (27) and (30), follow. A>=197/20 for r=5 has the shorter nine-phase
alternative in Section 8.3. None of these hypotheses supplies an actual
seed or its full multiplier array.

## 10. Scope of the conditional conclusion

The selected index is fixed before using the unbounded physical horizon
sequence. The construction proves prescribed-horizon full-function objective
guarantees. It makes no all-horizon or anytime claim and supplies no actual
seed satisfying the hypotheses. It asserts neither arbitrary-template
inflation nor any exponent-based finite capacity ceiling. The power A^K
arises only from a finite product of ray ratios; it is not the capacity
of a constructed seed. No real-base interpolation, phase optimality, or
unrestricted exact-profile resolution is asserted.

## intuition
A finite telescoping product of integer-ray ratios forces a good phase. Certified ray enclosures freeze one phase, and the accepted physical seed substitution turns its strict ratio margin into full-class objective guarantees at the true physical horizons.
