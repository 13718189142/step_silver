## 1. Physical words, parameters, and conventions

All norms and inner products are Euclidean. A subscripted e_i denotes
the standard coordinate vector in the dimension of its occurrence;
the explicitly defined planar e(theta) below is a separate notation.
For a finite nonnegative
word h=(h_0,...,h_(n-1)), the physical iteration at advertised smoothness
one is x_(t+1)=x_t-h_t grad f(x_t). Define its actual closest-distance
payoff by

    E(f,h)=2(f(x_n)-min f)/dist(x_0,argmin f)^2.

The construction always has positive initial distance. Its advertised
smoothness remains one; it is not retuned to a smaller Lipschitz constant.
Multiplying f by any L>0 and using h_t/L preserves all points and E.
W_n(h) is the supremum over the original full class of such instances;
an upper bound for the constructed family is not an upper bound on W_n.

Write a selection of k checkpoints in original physical order as

    h=(block_0,b_0,block_1,b_1,...,block_(k-1),b_(k-1),block_k),

where s_i=sum block_i. An ordinary block may be empty, consist of zeros,
or contain arbitrarily large steps. One pair of independent parameters
is fixed for the entire selection:

    kappa>0, 0<epsilon<=1/2,
    theta_sp=arctan(1/epsilon),
    C=chi=sqrt(1+epsilon^2) exp(kappa theta_sp),
    T=(exp((1-kappa^2)theta_sp/kappa)-1)/(1-kappa^2),  kappa<1,
    T=theta_sp/kappa,                                kappa>=1,
    H_sp=C[1+(1+kappa)T]-1,
    A=A_sp=epsilon+(1+H_sp)/epsilon.                     (1)

The two branches for T agree continuously at kappa=1. C is an arc
constant, not a benchmark denominator C_n; A is an activation allowance,
not a primitive-certificate capacity. In particular C>1 and A>=2C.

The source realization requires

    b_i>=2(1+epsilon^(-2)) for every selected i.          (2)

For the main realization define

    qmax=1,                                         kappa<=epsilon,
    qmax=sqrt((1+epsilon^2)/(1+kappa^2))

    B_sp=1+max(epsilon^(-2),N_sp/epsilon),

The physical hierarchy is fixed by an enclosing integer m>=1536 and

## 2. Actual backward blocks, including zeros and caps

For 0<=theta<=theta_sp put

    R(theta)=C exp(-kappa theta),
    e(theta)=(cos theta,-sin theta),
    t(theta)=(-sin theta,-cos theta),
    v(theta)=R(theta)e(theta)=(p(theta),-q(theta)),
    n(theta)=e(theta)+kappa t(theta),
    K=conv({0} union {v(theta):0<=theta<=theta_sp}).

The endpoints are (C,0) and (epsilon,-1). With Delta=phi-theta,

    <n(theta),v(phi)>/R(theta)
       =exp(-kappa Delta)(cos Delta+kappa sin Delta).

Its derivative in Delta is
-(1+kappa^2)exp(-kappa Delta)sin Delta. Since |Delta|<pi/2,
its maximum is one at zero. The value at the added point0 is zero.
Thus n(theta) supports K at v(theta); (0,1) also supports K at(C,0).
Differentiation gives

    p'=-R(kappa cos theta+sin theta)<0,
    q'=R(cos theta-kappa sin theta),
    p>=epsilon, q>=0, p>=epsilon q.                    (4)

The sign of q' proves the qmax formula in Section 1.

For an actual local chronological list alpha_1,...,alpha_r, alpha_j>=0,
set r_r=v_r=(epsilon,-1). Given r_j=(u_j,-y_j) and its projected arc
point v_j=v(theta_j), use alpha=alpha_j. If

    y_j<=kappa[u_j+(alpha-1)C],                          (5)

set v_(j-1)=(C,0), r_(j-1)=r_j+alpha(C,0). Otherwise choose the unique
theta in(0,theta_j] solving

    <r_j,e(theta)-kappa^(-1)t(theta)>/R(theta)=1-alpha,  (6)

and set v_(j-1)=v(theta), r_(j-1)=r_j+alpha v(theta).
These definitions specify real numbers; no root search is executed.

Here is the existence and projection proof. Inductively
u_j>=epsilon, y_j>=1, y_j/u_j<=1/epsilon. Before a cap the stronger
representation is

    r_j=R(theta_j)[(1+nu_j)e(theta_j)+kappa nu_j t(theta_j)],
    nu_j>=0.                                           (7)

It holds at the endpoint with nu_r=0. If(5) holds, put
mu=u_j+(alpha-1)C>=y_j/kappa>0. Then

    r_(j-1)-v(0)=mu n(0)+(0,kappa mu-y_j),

a nonnegative combination of supporting normals. Projection follows.
The same formula gives y_(j-1)<=kappa(u_(j-1)-C), so every earlier
backward step is capped, including zero steps.

If(5) fails, the current state is uncapped. The left side of(6) is one
at theta_j by(7), is less than1-alpha at zero, and has derivative
(kappa+kappa^(-1))(u_j cos theta+y_j sin theta)/R(theta)>0. There is
exactly one root, including theta=theta_j when alpha=0. Representation
(7) shows that the polar angle of r_j is at least theta_j. Consequently
<r_j,t(theta)>=0 at the root. Equation(6) makes
r_j+(alpha-1)v(theta) a nonnegative multiple of n(theta), proving the
preceding projection and(7). Adding alpha(p,-q), using(4), preserves
the stated bounds on u_j,y_j and their ratio.

Define the actual outputs

    P=sum_j alpha_j p(theta_(j-1)),
    Q=sum_j alpha_j q(theta_(j-1)), s_loc=sum_j alpha_j.

Then r_0=(epsilon+P,-1-Q), P>=epsilon Q, P<=Cs_loc.
For consecutive uncapped angles theta_old<=theta_new let
Delta=theta_new-theta_old. Resolving the backward equation along
t(theta_old) gives the exact scalar relation

    nu_old=B_D+A_D nu_new,
    A_D=exp(-kappa Delta)(cos Delta+sin Delta/kappa),
    B_D=exp(-kappa Delta)sin Delta/kappa.                (8)

Both coefficients are nonnegative. For d=1-kappa^2,
A_D'-(d/kappa)A_D=-(1+kappa^(-2))exp(-kappa Delta)sin Delta<=0.
If kappa<1, then A_D<=exp(d Delta/kappa), B_D<=Delta/kappa, and
1+dB_D<=exp(d Delta/kappa). Telescoping
1+d nu_old<=exp(d Delta/kappa)(1+d nu_new) proves nu_j<=T.
If kappa>=1, A_D<=1 and B_D<=Delta/kappa give nu_j<=T directly by
summing angle differences. Therefore every uncapped state has
y_j<=C[1+(1+kappa)T]. A cap leaves y unchanged. We have proved

    0<=Q<=H_sp, P>=epsilon Q, 0<=P<=Cs_loc.             (9)

Two lower bounds from the actual slopes will supply UPPER payoff bounds:

    p(theta)+q(theta)>=1, P+Q>=s_loc,
    (epsilon+P)^2+(1+Q)^2 >=(1+epsilon+s_loc)^2/2.       (10)

Indeed R(theta)>=sqrt(1+epsilon^2)>=1 and cos theta+sin theta>=1.
The quadratic inequality is (x+y)^2<=2(x^2+y^2). It is an inequality
for actual chronological outputs, not a claim they depend only on s_loc.

For the main assembly we also need y_j>=q_j and u_j-p_j>=-N_sp.
The first follows from(7), since
y_j-q_j=R nu_j(sin theta_j+kappa cos theta_j)>=0; at a cap q_j=0.

For clarity, the envelope properties used here have elementary proofs.
A nonnegative finite affine maximum J having a zero has a unique
proximal minimizer z at each x, by coercivity and strict convexity of
J(z)+||x-z||^2/2. Put g=x-z; its optimality condition is g in partial J(z).
For another pair z',g', subgradient monotonicity gives
||g-g'||^2<=<g-g',x-x'>. Using z as a trial point gives
env_1 J(x+w)<=env_1 J(x)+<g,w>+||w||^2/2; if z' is the new proximal
point, the subgradient at z gives
env_1 J(x+w)>=env_1 J(x)+<g,w>+||w-(z'-z)||^2/2. These prove convexity,
differentiability, gradient g, and 1-Lipschitz gradient. Attainment and
nonnegativity prove argmin(env_1 J)={J=0}, with minimum zero.

At x_j=z_circ+sigma r_j the point x_j-sigma v_j is proximal: the
projection inequality against all v in V makes sigma v_j an active
