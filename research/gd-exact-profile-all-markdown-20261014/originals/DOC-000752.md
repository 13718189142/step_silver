# A recursive family defeats the combined ray, quadratic, and single-cap channels

Status: complete symbolic candidate, not verifier-accepted. Worker: max3.
No extra verifier allocation is assumed. The result bounds specified lower-bound
functionals from above. It does not bound the true smooth-convex worst case from
above and does not decide the project's minimax target.

## Statement and definitions

Put c=13/5 and a=c^(-17). Define the ordered lists H_0=() and

    H_m=(H_(m-1), a c^(m-1), H_(m-1)),   m>=1.

Their lengths are N_m=2^m-1. Write b_u=a c^u for the step at level u and
s_m=sum H_m. Directly from the recursion,

    s_m=a(c^m-2^m)/(c-2),    s_(m+1)=2s_m+b_m.

F_ray is precisely the verified functional of fact 1fca3e5dd555cb4a, including
all its sign, remote-offset, old-slope, and retained-area constraints. K is the
functional of fact b98bb807d514e80d. Define the full quadratic functional by

    Q_n(h)=max_{0<=lambda<=1} lambda product_(t<n)(1-lambda h_t)^2.

For clarity, define also the algebraic single-cap functional F_cap_n. For each
lambda in (0,1], sigma in {-1,1}, and checkpoint j<n, put

    p_t=product_(v<t)(1-lambda h_v),
    R=max(0,sigma p_t:0<=t<=j), A=sigma p_(j+1), T=sum_(v>j)h_v.

Take the supremum of

    2 lambda r A - lambda(1+2 lambda T)r^2

over A>0 and radii R<=r<=A/(1+lambda T), and include zero. This is exactly
the candidate quadratic-core/terminal-cap family, but its upper bounds below
use only this displayed definition, not any unverified prior lemma. It is a
legal lower-bound family directly: integrate the derivative of lambda x^2/2
until sigma x=r and continue with constant derivative sigma lambda r beyond
that point. The record constraint preserves the quadratic prefix, while
A-lambda rT>=r preserves the affine suffix. The derivative is continuous,
nondecreasing, and lambda-Lipschitz; the minimum is zero at zero and x_0=1.

For every m>=18 the candidate conclusions are

    F_ray_(N_m)(H_m)=K_(N_m)(H_m)<=1/(1+s_(m-1))
                                      <=8/(5a c^m),
    Q_(N_m)(H_m)<=1/(a c^m),
    F_cap_(N_m)(H_m)<=729 c^13/(a c^m).

In addition the exact benchmark recursions satisfy

    S_circle(n)+1<=(n+1)^(4/3),
    B_n=2S_bullet(n)+1<=1+4n^(4/3).

Consequently, since c^3=2197/125>16,

    max(F_ray_(N_m), Q_(N_m), F_cap_(N_m))(H_m) / U_(N_m)^ZJ -> 0.

Thus even adding the one-terminal-cap extension to the verified ray functional
and all convex quadratic witnesses cannot give the desired uniform comparison.
The comparison fails along an explicit subsequence of horizons, which suffices
to refute a proposed comparison uniform over all horizons and all schedules.

## 1. Elementary scale estimates

Let d=2/c=10/13. Since d^3<1/2 and d^2<4/5,
d^17=d^15 d^2<1/40. Therefore for u>=17,

    s_u/b_u=(1-d^u)/(c-2)>=(39/40)/(3/5)=13/8.

Write eta=13/8. Then eta>1, eta^2=169/64>13/5=c.
Every step b_u>1 has u>=18, so this estimate applies to every eligible
checkpoint. At ineligible levels b_u<=1, the inequality s_u>=b_u-1 also
holds. Thus s_u>=b_u-1 for every level u, and s_u>=eta b_u whenever u>=17.

## 2. Every feasible ray chain has at most one checkpoint

The j-th step of H_m, indexed here by 1<=j<2^m, is b_u where u is the
largest integer for which 2^u divides j. Each such occurrence has an exact
copy of H_u immediately before it and an exact copy immediately after it.

Consider proposed first two checkpoints at indices i<j, with step levels
u and v. Both steps must exceed one for a feasible chain: otherwise the
verified first crossing or second return has nonpositive distance budget.
Let P be the sum before i, and M the sum strictly between i and j.
The verified recurrence gives the distance budget after the second crossing as

    [(b_u-1)(b_v-1)/(1+M)-P]/(1+P).

Any chain of at least two checkpoints needs this budget positive. If more
checkpoints follow, the next magnitude must be positive; if these are the
only two, the terminal old-slope constraint is at least m_0>0. It therefore
suffices to show P M >= b_u b_v, which is a stronger obstruction.

If u>=v, the copy of H_u before i yields P>=s_u. The copy of H_v before
j lies strictly after i, since j-i is a positive multiple of 2^v; hence
M>=s_v. Thus P M>=s_u s_v>=b_u b_v.

Suppose u<v. If i lies before the copy of H_v immediately preceding j,
then M>=s_v and P>=s_u, with the same conclusion. Otherwise i lies inside
that copy. Let P_loc be the sum in that copy strictly before i. The local
copies of H_u on each side of i give

    P_loc>=s_u, M>=s_u, P_loc+M=s_v-b_u, P>=P_loc.

For two nonnegative numbers with this fixed sum and each at least s_u,
their product is at least s_u(s_v-b_u-s_u). This follows by factoring
(P_loc-s_u)(M-s_u)>=0.

For fixed u, put D_v=s_u(s_v-b_u-s_u)-b_u b_v. At v=u+1,

    D_(u+1)=s_u^2-c b_u^2>=0.

For v>=u+1, using s_(v+1)-s_v=s_v+b_v and b_(v+1)=c b_v,

    D_(v+1)-D_v=s_u(s_v+b_v)-(c-1)b_u b_v
                  >=(3-c)b_u b_v>0,

because s_u>=b_u and s_v>=b_v. Thus D_v>=0 for all v>u. This proves the
pair obstruction and rules out every chain with two or more checkpoints.
No offset or retained-area term has been dropped from a feasible chain;
the exact first-return condition prevents such a chain from existing.

## 3. Bound on all remaining single cuts

At an occurrence of b_u, prefix and tail sums P,T satisfy

    P,T>=s_u, P+T=s_m-b_u.

Put V=s_m-b_u-s_u. Since b_u+s_u increases with u,

    V>=s_m-b_(m-1)-s_(m-1)=s_(m-1).

On P in [s_u,V], the logarithm of
(1+P)^2[1+2(s_m-b_u-P)] is concave. Its minimum is therefore at an
endpoint. If b_u>1, the two endpoint values for the cut ratio are bounded by

    (b_u-1)^2/[(1+s_u)^2(1+2V)] <=1/(1+2V),
    (b_u-1)^2/[(1+V)^2(1+2s_u)]
                               <=b_u/(1+V)^2.

Here s_u>=b_u-1. Also b_u<=b_(m-1)<=1+s_(m-1) and V>=s_(m-1),
so both ratios are at most 1/(1+s_(m-1)). Ineligible cuts give zero. The
global Huber term 1/(1+2s_m) obeys the same bound. Fact1fca3e5dd555cb4a
therefore gives F_ray=K<=1/(1+s_(m-1)). For m>=18 the scale estimate yields
1/(1+s_(m-1))<=c/(eta a c^m)=8/(5a c^m).

## 4. Exact quadratic-product damping

For lambda in (0,1], put z=a lambda. The whole-block polynomial is

    P_m(lambda)=product_(u=0)^(m-1)(1-b_u lambda)^(2^(m-u-1)).

Choose the integer k>=17 such that c^k z<=1<c^(k+1)z, and set
y=c^k z in (1/c,1]. If a factor is zero, the following whole-block bounds
are immediate. Otherwise, when m>k,

    log|P_m| / 2^(m-k-1)
       =sum_(j=-k)^(m-k-1) 2^(-j) log|1-c^j y|.

For j<0 use log(1-c^j y)<=-c^j y. For j>=1 use
log|1-c^j y|<=log(c^j y)=j log c+log y; these latter upper bounds are
nonnegative because c^j y>1. Extending only these nonnegative bounds to an
infinite series gives

    log|P_m| / 2^(m-k-1)
       <=-2y(1-d^k)/(c-2)+log[y(1-y)]+2log c
       <=-(13/4)y+log(1/4)+2log c
       <=-5/4+log(169/100)
       <=-5/4+69/100=-14/25<-1/2.

The endpoint y=1 gives a zero factor when m>k and was already handled.
We have proved

    |P_m(lambda)|<=exp(-2^(m-k-2))  if m>k.

If m<=k, every factor has b_u lambda<=1, so |P_m|<=1. In particular
|P_m(lambda)|<=1 for every m and lambda in [0,1].

If lambda<=1/(a c^m), the desired weighted bound follows immediately.
Otherwise m>k. Put r=m-k>=1. Then

    lambda |P_m|^2
       <=(1/(a c^m)) c^r exp(-2^(r-1))
       <=1/(a c^m).

The last inequality uses c<e, hence r log c<r<=2^(r-1). For example,
e>1+1+1/2+1/6=8/3>13/5, so no decimal estimate is required. Taking the
maximum over lambda proves the Q bound. This is also the full quadratic
class: diagonalizing a positive semidefinite Hessian with spectrum in [0,1]
makes normalized terminal error a weighted average of these scalar errors.

## 5. Uniform prefix bound and the entire one-cap extension

Define R_u(lambda)=P_u(lambda)(1-b_u lambda), a prefix block of length2^u.
Every prefix of H_m of length ell, 0<=ell<=2^m-1, has polynomial equal to
the product of R_u over the nonzero binary digits of ell. This follows by
successively consuming its largest complete block (H_u,b_u), of length2^u;
the remaining list begins another copy of the same lower-level pattern.

For u<=k, all factors of R_u have b_v lambda<=1, so |R_u|<=1. For
u=k+r with integer r>=1, the damping estimate gives

    |R_u|<=c^r exp(-2^(r-2)).

For r>=4, 2^(r-2)>=r and c<e, so this bound is at most1. For r=1,2,3
it is less than3: for r=1 use c<3; for r=2,3 use e>8/3 and respectively
c^2/(8/3)<3 and c^3/(8/3)^2<3. There are therefore at most three factors
exceeding one in any prefix, each less than3. Every prefix satisfies

    |p_t(lambda)|<=27.

We also need the weighted bound

    lambda |R_u(lambda)|^2 <= c^12/(a c^u).

If lambda<=1/(a c^u), it follows from |R_u|<=1. Otherwise u=k+r with
r>=1, and the left side is at most

    (1/(a c^u)) c^(3r) exp(-2^(r-1)).

For r<=4 this is at most c^12/(a c^u). For r>=5, 2^(r-1)>=3r and
c<e make its extra factor at most1. This proves the bound for every u.

Now consider any feasible single-cap checkpoint j in H_m. Maximizing over
all real r, rather than only its legal interval, gives the rigorous upper bound

    2 lambda r A-lambda(1+2lambda T)r^2
                           <=lambda A^2/(1+2lambda T).

Let u=m-1, and consider the central step b_u. If j precedes it, the tail
contains b_u, so T>=b_u, while |A|<=27. The cap payoff is at most
729/(2b_u). If j is central or follows it, the prefix contains the complete
block (H_u,b_u), followed by a prefix of H_u. Hence |A|<=27|R_u| and
the cap payoff is at most lambda A^2<=729 c^12/(a c^u).
Both cases are bounded by 729 c^13/(a c^m). All signed-record and radius
feasibility constraints were retained in defining the family; enlarging the
radius domain here only makes this upper bound more permissive.

## 6. A sufficient bound on the exact benchmark recurrence

This part uses the exact phi/psi recursions in fact b98bb807d514e80d, not an
asymptotic decimal constant. Put C_n=S_circle(n)+1. Their shifted joins are

    Omega(A,D)=(A+D+sqrt((A+D)^2+4AD))/2,
    G(A,D)=2A+D/2+sqrt(D^2+8AD)/2.

The first joins C_k,C_(n-k-1), and the second joins C_k,B_(n-k-1).
Both are increasing in their nonnegative arguments.

For 0<=t<=1,

    t^(4/3)+(1-t)^(4/3)+[t(1-t)]^(4/3)<=1.

To prove this, put x=t^(1/3), y=(1-t)^(1/3). Then x^3+y^3=1 and

    1-x^4-y^4-x^4y^4
      =x^3y^3[1/(1+x+x^2)+1/(1+y+y^2)-xy]>=0.

Each reciprocal is at least1/3, and xy<=2^(-2/3)<2/3; the latter strict
inequality follows by cubing, since1/4<8/27. Endpoints cause no division
problem. Since Omega is the positive root of X^2-(A+D)X-AD=0, this proves

    Omega(A,D)<=(A^(3/4)+D^(3/4))^(4/3).

Induction in the exact primitive recursion now gives C_n<=(n+1)^(4/3).
Also sqrt(D^2+8AD)<=D+4A, hence G(A,D)<=D+4A. Induction in the exact
dominant recursion gives

    B_n<=1+4[(n-k-1)^(4/3)+(k+1)^(4/3)]<=1+4n^(4/3)

for every split, so it holds for the maximum. The base values are C_0=B_0=1.

Combining Sections2--5 with this recurrence bound gives

    max(F_ray,Q,F_cap)(H_m)/U_(N_m)^ZJ
      <=[729 c^13/a] c^(-m)[1+4(2^m-1)^(4/3)] ->0,

because 2^(4/3)<c. This completes the candidate restricted-channel result.

## Structural consequence and next mechanism

This family has recursive large steps separated by substantial symmetric
blocks. Those blocks prevent the first outward return in the ray construction
and damp every quadratic scale; the prefix damping also defeats a lone terminal
cap. A next witness must permit nonlinear changes followed by additional
returns and further changes, or use a genuinely different multidimensional
interaction. This result does not establish that these schedules perform well
on that richer class. No conclusion about W_(N_m)(H_m) from above is made.

claim returned unrelated results, none used. Every new estimate above is
proved in the text. Correctness status remains unverified.

## 7. The same family controls every two-curvature homogeneous objective

This extension is part of the same unverified candidate proof. It does not
assume correctness of a separate unverified finding. For u,v in (0,1], define

    f_(u,v)(x)=u x^2/2 for x>=0, and v x^2/2 for x<=0.

Its derivative is continuous, monotone, and 1-Lipschitz, including across zero;
zero is a minimizer. Write F_two_n(h) for the supremum of normalized terminal
error on this family with either initial point +1 or -1. The claim is

    F_two_(N_m)(H_m)<=c/(a c^m),   m>=18.

Scaling or reflecting the initial point adds no larger case, by positive
homogeneity and by allowing both initial signs. Random mixtures and separable
direct sums of such objectives cannot exceed their supremum, since their
normalized errors are convex combinations of component errors.

It suffices first to consider u<=v, with neither reciprocal equal to a step
level. The reverse order follows by reflecting the objective. The exceptional
parameter pairs follow by continuity: each finite GD update and final objective
is continuous in (u,v), even at a sign change or zero iterate. All bounds are
uniform in the parameters. The cases with a zero curvature follow by the same
limit if they are included in the definition.

Let I be the first integer level with b_I v>1, and J the first with b_J u>1.
Then 18<=I<=J. A whole block H_l maps each starting sign to a deterministic
ending sign and nonnegative magnitude. Represent these two magnitudes by the
columns of a two-state map. Before level I, both signs are preserved, and the
whole-block magnitudes are P_l(u),P_l(v), both positive. If m<=I, the two
trajectories are ordinary quadratics, already bounded by Section4.

### 7a. Different first-crossing levels: I<J

The step b_I crosses from the negative side to the positive side but leaves
the positive side positive. Thus H_(I+1) sends both initial signs to the
positive side. Its amplitudes, from positive and negative starts respectively,
are

    A_(I+1)=(1-b_I u)P_I(u)^2,
    D_(I+1)=(b_I v-1)P_I(u)P_I(v).

Every larger whole block also ends positive from both starting signs, because
the last copy of the preceding block has this property. Put

    r_0=D_(I+1)/A_(I+1)>0.

The ratio D_l/A_l stays equal to r_0. Indeed composition with the central
step yields

    (A_(l+1),D_(l+1))=(1-b_l u)(A_l^2,A_l D_l),  l<J,
    (A_(l+1),D_(l+1))=(b_l u-1)(A_l D_l,D_l^2),  l>=J.

In particular, for I+1<=l<=J,

    A_l=P_l(u),
    D_l=(b_I v-1)P_I(v) P_l(u)/[(1-b_I u)P_I(u)].

All factors of P_l(u) in this range are positive. Dividing by
(1-b_I u)P_I(u) removes a submultiset of its factors with total step mass
b_I+s_I. Since 1-t<=exp(-t), it follows that

    A_l<=exp(-u s_l),
    D_l<=exp(-u s_l) exp(u(s_I+b_I)-v s_I+log(b_I v-1)).

Set S=s_I/b_I>=eta>1, t_u=b_I u<1 and t_v=b_I v>1. The exponent in
the latter extra factor is at most

    (S+1)-S t_v+log(t_v-1)
       =1-S(t_v-1)+log(t_v-1)<=-log S<=0.

Here the maximum of log w-Sw over w>0 is -1-log S, found by differentiating.
Consequently both whole-block amplitudes obey exp(-u s_l) up to l=J.
Their common terminal side has curvature u, so for I< m<=J the error is

    <=u exp(-2u s_m)<=1/(2e s_m)<=1/(a c^m),

using s_m>=eta a c^m.

For l>=J, put E_l=max(A_l,D_l). The exact recurrence gives

    E_(l+1)<=E_l^2(b_l u-1),    E_J<=exp(-u s_J).

Let t=b_J u in (1,c) and m=J+r with r>=1. Expanding this scalar recursion,

    log E_m / 2^(r-1)
      <=-2u s_J+sum_(j=0)^(r-1)2^(-j)log(c^j t-1)
      <=-2eta t+2log c+2log t
      <=-2eta+2<-1.

The series bound uses log(c^j t-1)<=j log c+log t and sums the nonnegative
upper bounds to infinity. The last inequality uses log c<1 and the fact that
-2eta t+2log t decreases on t>=1 because eta>1. Thus

    u E_m^2<=u exp(-2^r)
       <=[c/(a c^m)] c^r exp(-2^r)<=c/(a c^m),

since u<c/(a c^J), log c<1, and r<=2^r. This covers I<J completely.

### 7b. Equal first-crossing levels: I=J

At level I both signs cross, so H_(I+1) interchanges the two starting signs.
Every larger block also interchanges them: an interchange, the central
interchange, and another interchange compose to an interchange. Let A_l be
the amplitude from a positive start and D_l the amplitude from a negative
start. Then

    A_(I+1)=(b_I u-1)P_I(u)P_I(v),
    D_(I+1)=(b_I v-1)P_I(u)P_I(v),
    A_(l+1)=A_l^2(b_l v-1),
    D_(l+1)=D_l^2(b_l u-1),     l>=I+1.

Put S=s_I/b_I>=eta, t_u=b_I u>1, t_v=b_I v>1. Both t_u,t_v are less
than c. All earlier factors are positive, so P_I(w)<=exp(-w s_I).
For m=I+r, r>=1, the exact recursion therefore implies

    log A_m / 2^(r-1)
      <=log(t_u-1)-S(t_u+t_v)
                   +sum_(j=1)^(r-1)2^(-j)log(c^j t_v-1)
      <=[-S t_u+log(t_u-1)]+[-S t_v+log t_v]+2log c.

For any t>1, -St+log(t-1)<=-S-1-log S. Also -St+log t<=-S, because
S>1. The displayed bound is consequently

    <=-2S-1-log S+2log c<=-2eta+1=-9/4<-2.

The identical estimate for D_m follows after interchanging u,v. Thus both
amplitudes are at most exp(-2^r). Their terminal curvatures are at most
v<c/(a c^I), giving normalized error at most

    [c/(a c^m)] c^r exp(-2^(r+1))<=c/(a c^m).

This proves the asserted two-curvature bound. In conjunction with Sections1--6,
the maximum of F_ray, Q, F_cap, and F_two still has ratio tending to zero
relative to the exact ZJ benchmark along N_m=2^m-1.

### Scope of the stronger exclusion

The proof controls a continuous two-parameter nonlinear family, with repeated
sign changes and inner returns. It does not control a derivative whose slope
changes more than once along either ray, nor a genuinely coupled multidimensional
function. Such changes remain concrete mechanisms to investigate. Six fixed
curvature pairs were checked using only forty compositions of a two-state map
(about0.1 seconds, no long trajectory stored); those checks suggested the proof
but are not used in it. No numerical sweep or asymptotic computation is evidence
for the claimed bound.

## 8. Arbitrary monotone continuations after a quadratic-gradient prefix

One can enlarge the single-cap class substantially without changing this
obstruction. Consider any legal scalar convex1-smooth function with minimizer0,
subtract its minimum so that f(0)=0, and take initial point1. Choose a checkpoint
j such that its sampled gradients through j
satisfy g_t=lambda p_t, where p_t=product_(v<t)(1-lambda h_v) and
lambda in(0,1]. Function values on the prefix need not equal quadratic values.
After step j, allow an arbitrary nonlinear continuation, with the sole
restriction that all subsequent iterates stay on the same side of zero. A
zero iterate makes every later iterate zero and can be discarded as trivial.
The supremum of terminal error over this enlarged class, for H_m, is at most

    1458 c^13/(a c^m),   m>=18.

Here is a direct proof, not an appeal to a prior unverified claim about the
continuation. Let A=|p_(j+1)| and T=sum_(t>j)h_t. Reflect the current side so
that its coordinates are positive. Convexity implies every later gradient is
nonnegative; the no-crossing assumption and nonnegative steps make the
coordinates and gradients nonincreasing. Let x_f and g_f be the final radius
and gradient magnitude. Then

    x_f+T g_f<=A,    0<=g_f<=x_f.

For a monotone1-Lipschitz derivative with endpoint value g_f and value zero
at the origin, its integral from0 to x_f is at most
x_f g_f-g_f^2/2. Indeed its pointwise upper bound there is min(r,g_f), whose
integral has that value. Consequently

    2f(x_f)<=2A g_f-(2T+1)g_f^2<=A^2/(2T+1).       (8.1)

Set u=m-1, so the central step is b_u. If j is at or before the central
step, then T>=s_u, while Section5 gives A<=27. Thus (8.1) is at most
729/(1+2s_u), below the asserted bound.

If j follows the central step, both p_j and p_(j+1) contain the complete
factor R_u and a prefix factor of H_u. Hence each has magnitude at most
27|R_u|. There are two cases.

If lambda h_j<=1, this checkpoint does not cross zero. Since every later
point is closer to zero on the same side as p_j, monotonicity of the global
derivative gives g_f<=lambda |p_j|. The integral bound, or just convexity,
then gives

    2f(x_f)<=2x_f g_f<=1458 lambda |R_u|^2
                               <=1458 c^13/(a c^m).

If lambda h_j>1, the checkpoint is a crossing. Its level v obeys b_v>1,
and its following exact copy of H_v is included in the suffix. Therefore
T>=s_v>=eta b_v>eta/lambda. Combining this with (8.1) yields

    2f(x_f)<=729|R_u|^2/(1+2T)
             <=[729/(2eta)]lambda |R_u|^2
             <=[729/(2eta)]c^13/(a c^m).

This proves the stated bound. It applies to every monotone continuation
consistent with all old gradient data, including many changes of curvature and
interior interpolation gaps; neither a constant tail nor a preserved quadratic
prefix value was assumed. It makes no assertion about a continuation with
further sign crossings. Thus adding arbitrary curvature changes on one final
ray does not repair the earlier restricted constructions on this family. The
new scalar mechanism must permit nonlinear changes followed by additional
crossings of the minimizer.
