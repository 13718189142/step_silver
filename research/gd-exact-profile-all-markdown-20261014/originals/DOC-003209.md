Status: complete joint proof candidate, **UNVERIFIED**. Ninth max4 launch,
first round (cumulative20). No numerical evaluation, optimizer, installation,
or fact submission is used. This document gives all new arguments together;
earlier unverified common-prefix drafts are not correctness predecessors.
The accepted inputs are `1252b8823bb5a290` (original interpolation and maximal
terminal completion) and `b98bb807d514e80d` (exact benchmark definitions and
the bound B_n<=1+4 n^(log_2 3)).

The old 1/sqrt(2) boundary is not the boundary of the original-step argument.
Retaining the physical leaf preceding the level1 bridge changes it. Moreover,
the movement bound need not divide by its vanishing angular coefficient:
one-block look-ahead controls the missing mass at equality. These assertions

that construction is NOT a full-word construction for the positive mixture.

## 1. Word, measure, normalization, and quantitative statement

Let m>=1024 be an integer, and define

    sigma=1+sqrt(2), delta=1-1/m, c_chr=sigma(1+1/m),
    a_chr=delta/c_chr, N=2^m, n=N-1,
    epsilon=1/N, eta=1/N^2, l=1+a_chr, k=1+delta.

Use the ACTUAL word H_chr,0=() and

    H_chr,j+1=(H_chr,j,1+a_chr*c_chr^j,H_chr,j), 0<=j<m.

Every subword keeps these enclosing-m parameters. Equivalently the original
step at index i<n is h_i=1+a_chr*c_chr^(nu_2(i+1)), where nu_2 is the exponent
of2 in a positive integer. Set rho=1-epsilon/2 and gamma=9/10.

For a bit vector s=(s_0,...,s_(m-1)) define

    chi_s(i)=(-1)^(sum_j s_j i_j),
    B(s)=(-1)^s_0, C_j(s)=(-1)^(s_1+...+s_j), 1<=j<m,

where i_j are the standard binary digits of the original index i. The map
s -> (B,C_1,...,C_(m-1)) is bijective. Choose beta in(0,1), and set
mu_+=-beta, mu_-=gamma. Conditional on B=b, define

    A_b(c)=(1+mu_b)/2 * 1_(all c_j=1)
                +(1-mu_b)/2 * 1_(all c_j=-1),
    P_b(c)=product_(j=1)^(m-1) (1+mu_b c_j)/2,
    w_s=(1+(1-epsilon)B(s))/2
                 *[(1-eta)A_(B(s))(C(s))+eta P_(B(s))(C(s))].       (1)

These are positive weights summing to1. Define the following explicit
finite-m quantities BEFORE choosing beta:

    lambda=rho(1-eta), omega=1-lambda, e=omega/lambda,
    d_mass=(l+k)/(l+1+(1-l)e),
    beta_M=sqrt((1-e)/d_mass)-e,
    p=1-epsilon, d_eps=gamma epsilon/2,
    r_leaf=[(2-l)p+sqrt((2-l)^2 p^2+4(l-1))]/2,
    L_leaf=l/r_leaf,
    beta_R=[k d_eps+sqrt((k+L_leaf)(1+L_leaf p)
                                  +L_leaf^2 d_eps^2)]
                          /[(k+L_leaf)rho].                       (2)

The candidate theorem covers EVERY

    7/10 <= beta <= beta_R,                                      (3)

including the endpoint beta=beta_R. In particular4/5<beta_R<6/7 and
beta_R tends to2^(-1/4), strictly beyond1/sqrt(2). The auxiliary beta_M
is the zero of the simpler mass coefficient; the complete proof crosses
it and reaches beta_R by retaining the actual final level2 bridge.

Prescribe only QUERIED gradients

    g_i=(Gamma_i sqrt(w_s) chi_s(i))_s, 0<=i<n,                    (4)

with arbitrary positive amplitudes Gamma_i. Suppose a finite-dimensional
convex differentiable1-smooth function F realizes (4) along its actual GD
trajectory X_(i+1)=X_i-h_i g_i, and has a minimizer. The anchor, values,
amplitudes and unqueried terminal gradient are otherwise unrestricted.
All isometric realizations of the Gram matrix in(4) are included. Let

    X_star be a true closest minimizer to X_0,
    R=||X_0-X_star||, F_star=min F,
    T_path=sum_(i<n)h_i Gamma_i,
    V_chr=2(F(X_n)-F_star)/R^2.

Then the claimed bounds, uniform over(3), are

    R>0,  T_path<8192 R,
    ||X_i-X_star||<512 R for every0<=i<=n,                        (5)
    ||grad F(X_n)|| <= R[2(3/5)^(N/8)+3*2^17*m/N^2],              (6)
    V_chr <=2048(3/5)^(N/8)+2^29*m/N^2.                          (7)

The comparison uses the exact original benchmark and its normalized
objective2(F(X_n)-F_star)/R^2, preserving the project's factor of2:

    B_n V_chr <=(1+4n^(log_2 3))
                       [2048(3/5)^((n+1)/8)+2^29*m/(n+1)^2]
                   ->0,  n=2^m-1.                              (8)

For L-smooth f apply the theorem to F=(f-f_star)/L, so the normalized

## 2. Original inequalities and elementary finite bounds

A closest minimizer exists because the nonempty minimizer set is closed,
and a distance-minimizing sequence can be chosen bounded in finite dimension.
The positive initial gradient makes R>0. Translate X_star to0.
Accepted `1252b8823bb5a290` gives all actual original inequalities

    Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,X_j-X_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2<=0,
    f_i=F(X_i)-F_star.

The optimum sample has position, gradient and value0. Adding orientations
gives, for any two actual samples,

    ||g_i-g_j||^2 <= <g_i-g_j,X_i-X_j>.                           (9)

In particular <g_i,X_i>>=||g_i||^2, and smoothness gives
||g_i||<=||X_i||. A physical step0<h<=2 therefore satisfies

    ||X-hg||^2<=||X||^2-h(2-h)||g||^2,
    ||grad F(X-hg)||<=||g||.                                    (10)

For the second assertion put d_g=grad F(X-hg)-g in(9):
-h<d_g,g>>=||d_g||^2. Expanding ||g+d_g||^2 gives the assertion.
For h>=2 the same optimum inequality gives instead

    ||X-hg||^2<=||X||^2+h(h-2)||g||^2
                  <=(h-1)^2||X||^2.                            (11)

All applications below distinguish these two regimes.

The enclosing parameters satisfy

    7/5<l<3/2, 199/100<k<2,
    1<delta*c_chr<5/2,
    e<1/1000, 1/sqrt(lambda)<1001/1000,
    sqrt(omega)/lambda<1/255, 1-epsilon>999/1000.                 (12)

c_chr>2 gives l<3/2. Also delta*c_chr=sigma(1-1/m^2)<5/2 and is>1.
The powers of2 give omega<=epsilon/2+eta<1/65536, which implies the
displayed mixture bounds. These estimates require no numerical evaluation.

Because l>1 and e>=0,

    d_mass>1+(99/100)/(5/2)=349/250>11/8,
    d_mass<3/2.                                                (13)

For the second inequality, subtract l+2 from
(3/2)[l+1-(l-1)e]; the result is (l-1)(1/2-3e/2)>0.
Thus

    beta_M>sqrt(333/500)-1/1000>4/5,
    beta_M<sqrt(8/11)<6/7.                                    (14)

The first last comparison follows from (801/1000)^2<333/500; the second

## 3. Movement without division by a vanishing angular margin

Split the SAME probability measure into the component B=1 with all prefix
signs common, of mass lambda, and its remaining component of mass omega.
In the first component the unit directions repeat a_0,a_0,b_0,b_0 and
<a_0,b_0>=-beta. Hence the full gradients admit the direct-sum realization

    g_i=Gamma_i(sqrt(lambda)u_i,sqrt(omega)z_i),
    u_i in{a_0,b_0}, ||z_i||=1.                                (15)

An isometry on the gradient span, extended to the initial displacement,
preserves the Gram data and true radius; thus this representation loses
no actual realization. Write X_0=(x^c,x^r).

At a block start t=4r,0<=t<=N-4, let P_a and P_b be the total previous
amplitude-weighted physical step masses on a_0 and b_0, respectively.
Put T=P_a+P_b and

    A_x=<a_0,x^c>/sqrt(lambda)-P_a+beta P_b,
    B_x=<b_0,x^c>/sqrt(lambda)-P_b+beta P_a,
    r_0=sqrt(omega)R/lambda.

The remainder displacement before t has norm at most sqrt(omega)T.
Let A=Gamma_t,C=Gamma_(t+1). The first physical leaf gives A>=C by(10).
At the QUERIED sample t+1, its optimum inequality yields

    A_x >= l A+C/lambda-r_0-e(T+l A)
         = l(1-e)A+(1+e)C-r_0-eT.                            (16)

Indeed the remainder contribution to <g_(t+1),X_(t+1)>/C is at most
sqrt(omega)[R+sqrt(omega)(T+l A)]. Division by lambda proves(16).

The mass of the first two physical steps is D_a=l A+k C. For0<=C/A<=1,
the function (l+k r)/[l(1-e)+(1+e)r] is increasing: its derivative has
the sign of k(1-e)-(1+e)>0 by(12). Its value at1 is d_mass. Consequently

    D_a<=d_mass(A_x+r_0+eT).                                 (17)

At the QUERIED sample t+2, the coarse direction is b_0. Dropping its
positive norm term from the optimum inequality gives

    B_x+(beta+e)D_a+r_0+eT>=0.                               (18)

Substituting(17), then expanding A_x,B_x,T, gives EXACTLY

    c_a P_a+c_b P_b
       <=<b_0+d_mass(beta+e)a_0,x^c>/sqrt(lambda)
                         +[1+d_mass(beta+e)]r_0,
    c_a=(beta+e)[d_mass(1-e)-1],
    c_b=1-e-d_mass(beta+e)^2.                                (19)

Throughout(3),

    c_a>(7/10)[(11/8)(999/1000)-1]>1/4.

Since beta+e<1, the right side of(19) is<4R by(12),(13).
When c_b>=0 this already gives P_a<16R at every block start, including
equality c_b=0. To cover the entire interval(3), let
xi=max(0,-c_b). Equation(14b), epsilon<=2e, and the denominator
D_mass=l+1+(1-l)e>9/4 imply

    xi <=[9epsilon+10e+3e^2]/D_mass<13e<13/1000.              (19a)

For this calculation, the exact numerator of
d_mass(beta+e)^2-(1-e) is
(l+k)beta^2-(l+1)+[2(l+k)beta+2l]e+(k+1)e^2;
the last two coefficients are<10 and<3, respectively.

We do not divide by c_b. First rearrange(18):

    (1-e)P_b <=<b_0,x^c>/sqrt(lambda)
                            +(beta+e)(P_a+D_a)+r_0.           (21)

Write A_rad=(1/sqrt(lambda)+sqrt(omega)/lambda)/(1-e)<2 and
v_mass=(beta+e)/(1-e)<2. If the next block start exists, its a-mass is
exactly P_a+D_a. Thus(21) reads P_b<=A_rad R+v_mass P_a(next).

For the LAST block start t_last=N-4, use its preceding block t_prev=N-8.
The bridge between them has ORIGINAL level2, H_2=1+delta*c_chr<7/2.
Writing C=Gamma_(t_prev+1), the two following short steps k,l imply
Gamma_(t_prev+2),Gamma_(t_prev+3)<=C. The b-mass added between these
block starts is therefore at most(l+H_2)C. Since the a-mass added there
is D_a=l Gamma_(t_prev)+k C, the added b-mass is at most
[(l+H_2)/k]D_a<3D_a. It follows from(21) at t_prev that

    P_b(last)<=2R+5P_a(last).                               (20a)

If c_b<0, apply(19) only at this last start and substitute(20a):

    [c_a-5xi]P_a(last)<=(4+2xi)R<5R.

By(19a), c_a-5xi>1/4-65/1000>1/8. Hence P_a(last)<40R.
If c_b>=0, the earlier bound16R applies instead. In either case,
the nonnegative chronological a-mass is nondecreasing, so

    P_a<64R at EVERY block start.                            (20)

For each t<=N-8, (21) and the bound at its next block start now give
P_b<2R+2*64R=130R. Hence

    T<194R and ||X_t||<195R at every block start t<=N-8.       (22)

The three following steps are l,k,l<2, so their samples also obey195R.
This covers every sample before the final block. From t=N-8 the next

There are just seven physical steps left from N-8 to n. Their total

## 4. Exact finite-epsilon correlations and the physical-leaf threshold

For the return argument split(1) differently: the entire common-prefix
component has mass1-eta. Write its common prefix sign as V. Its four unit

    a_e=1,a_o=B,b_e=V,b_o=BV.

Set

    p=E B=1-epsilon,
    z=E V=-rho beta+gamma epsilon/2,
    w=E(BV)=-rho beta-gamma epsilon/2.                        (24)

The correlations are <a_e,a_o>=<b_e,b_o>=p,
<a_o,b_o>=<a_e,b_e>=z, and <a_e,b_o>=<a_o,b_e>=w.

three correlations whenever only B and one individual prefix sign occur.

At ANY original leaf with amplitude ratio r>0, (9) is exactly

    r^2+(l-2)p r-(l-1)<=0.

Let r_leaf be its unique positive root. Evaluation at p and1 gives

    p<=r_leaf<1,
    r_leaf=[(2-l)p+sqrt((2-l)^2 p^2+4(l-1))]/2.                (25)

The relevant original-step return coefficient is

    alpha_ret=1-k z^2+(l/r_leaf)(p-zw).                       (26)

To state its finite-m zero exactly, put L_leaf=l/r_leaf and
d_eps=gamma epsilon/2. Substitution in(26) gives

    alpha_ret=1+L_leaf p-(k+L_leaf)rho^2 beta^2
                   +2k rho d_eps beta+(L_leaf-k)d_eps^2.

Its positive zero is

    beta_R=[k d_eps+sqrt((k+L_leaf)(1+L_leaf p)
                                  +L_leaf^2 d_eps^2)]
                          /[(k+L_leaf)rho].                  (27)

In contrast, the old coefficient1-kz^2 has positive zero
(1/sqrt(k)+d_eps)/rho. Replacing either by1/sqrt2 at finite m is
incorrect. The limit of(27) is2^(-1/4), while the old zero tends1/sqrt2.

For(3), alpha_ret>=0 by its exact positive root(27). Also -z>=2/3, p>0, and
p-zw>=p-beta^2>0 by(12),(14). Also z^2<=beta^2, zw<=beta^2 and
L_leaf>=l. To compare the auxiliary mass boundary with this return
boundary, specialize temporarily to beta<=beta_M. Then

    alpha_ret >=(l+1)-(l+k)beta^2-l epsilon

The second inequality uses beta^2<=(1-e)/d_mass and the exact denominator

The two explicit finite thresholds need not coincide. The strengthened
mass proof in section3 covers their gap, including beta=beta_R. Neither
threshold is asserted to be the full-word feasibility boundary beyond

## 5. Original return pairs, with no inverse-alpha loss

For any original i,j the full direction correlation differs from its
common-prefix correlation by at most2eta. If endpoint amplitudes are A,B
and the intervening total physical mass is T_ij, the resulting difference
in the cocoercivity expression is at most

    2eta(A+B)T_ij+4eta AB <=4eta K_path(A+B).                  (29)

Here T_ij<=T_path<K_path and A,B<=K_path, which were proved first.
Let e_0=4eta K_path.

Take an original queried odd-b return j=4r+3 with j+4<n. Write its
physical steps and gradient amplitudes as

    j: H on b_o with B_0,
    j+1: l on a_e with A_0,
    j+2: k on a_o with C_0,
    j+3: l on b_e with E_0,
    j+4: b_o with D_0.

The original high step H is arbitrary here. Put tau=z, bbar=-z>0,
M=B_0+bbar C_0 and Delta=D_0-B_0. In the common component set

    Z=-H B_0 b_o-l A_0 a_e,
    X=<b_o,Z>, Y=<a_o-tau b_o,Z>=-l A_0(p-zw).

The ORIGINAL pair(j,j+2), with(29), gives

    C_0^2+B_0^2-2tau B_0 C_0
       <=(tau C_0-B_0)X-l A_0 C_0(p-zw)+e_0(C_0+B_0).       (30)

If Delta>0, the ORIGINAL pair(j,j+4) gives

    X>=Delta+k C_0 tau+l E_0 p-e_0(D_0+B_0)/Delta.            (31)

The coefficient tau C_0-B_0=-M is negative. Substitution in(30), followed
by A_0>=C_0/r_leaf from the ACTUAL leaf at j+1, gives

    alpha_ret C_0^2+bbar(2-k)B_0 C_0+B_0^2
                      +M Delta+M l E_0 p
       <=e_0(C_0+B_0)+M e_0(D_0+B_0)/Delta.                 (32)

This is where the old proof discarded the nonnegative term
l A_0 C_0(p-zw), losing the sharper coefficient.

Because bbar>=2/3, C_0+B_0<=(3/2)M. Suppose Delta>=2m e_0.
The right side of(32) is at most

    (5/2)e_0 M+(1/m)B_0 M
      <=(5/2)e_0 M+B_0^2+(bbar/m)B_0 C_0.

The left side is at least B_0^2+(bbar/m)B_0 C_0+M Delta, since
2-k=1/m and alpha_ret>=0. Cancellation forces Delta<=(5/2)e_0,
contradicting m>=1024. Thus EVERY such original return satisfies

    Gamma_(j+4)<=Gamma_j+d_amp,
    d_amp=2^17*m*eta*R,                                     (33)

by(23). This proof also works with alpha_ret=0. It retains the explicit
factor m=1/(2-k), rather than hiding it in a perturbation symbol.
There is no1/alpha_ret loss. For eta=0 the same calculation directly
forbids Delta>0 whenever alpha_ret>=0 and the stated signs hold.

## 8. Nonvacuity for these same positive weights

This calibration is not a competitive lower bound. For the same actual
word and weights, additionally prescribe the terminal character gradient.
Let H_max=max_i h_i, kappa=min(1,(min_i h_i-1)/4)>0 and

    q_decay=kappa/[8(H_max+kappa+1)], Gamma_i=q_decay^i,
    f_i=(kappa+1/2)Gamma_i^2, 0<=i<=n.

The full gradient Gram G is positive definite: the full character matrix
is invertible, as summing chi_s(i)chi_s(j) over s is N for i=j and0
otherwise, and every weight and amplitude is positive. Define

    b_i=(kappa+1)Gamma_i^2+sum_(t<i)h_t G_ti,
    X_0=sum_i(G^(-1)b)_i g_i,
    X_i=X_0-sum_(t<i)h_t g_t.

Then <X_i,g_i>=(kappa+1)Gamma_i^2. For i<j, direct substitution gives

## 9. Sharp local scope test beyond the limiting boundary
