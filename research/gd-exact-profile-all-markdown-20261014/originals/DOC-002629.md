# Arbitrarily many nested inward scalar returns remain too weak on H_m

Status: complete scoped candidate proofs, not verifier-accepted. No additional
verification call is allocated. The main result allows asymmetric functions,
arbitrary core curvature, nonconstant gradients between crossings, and any
number of crossings. Its essential restriction is the nested-return condition
defined below. It is not an upper bound on the full scalar or multidimensional
worst case.

## 1. A schedule-level obstruction for nested returns

Let f:R->R be differentiable and convex, with 1-Lipschitz derivative g,
f(0)=0=min f, and let x_(t+1)=x_t-h_t g(x_t) for a finite nonnegative schedule.
Write H=max_t h_t and S=sum_t h_t. Assume that some eta>=1 has the property

    sum_(t<j) h_t >= eta h_j and sum_(t>j) h_t >= eta h_j
    whenever h_j>1.                                             (1)

A nonzero terminal objective implies that the trajectory never hits a
minimizer. Reflect the line if needed so that x_0>0. List every strict crossing
of zero as checkpoint steps b_0,...,b_(k-1), and let block_i be the intervening
same-sign steps, with sum s_i. Let a_i be the absolute position at the start of
block_i, and r_i its absolute position at its end, before the next checkpoint;
set r_k=|x_n|. Thus a_0=x_0. Each block has nonincreasing absolute positions.

The nested-return hypothesis is exactly

    a_(i+2) <= r_i,                  0<=i<=k-2.                  (2)

Each re-entry to a ray therefore lands inside the interval traversed on its
previous visit. There is no assumption that the derivative is constant on a
block, that the two rays agree, or that their curvature is one.

For k>=1, conditions (1)-(2) imply

    2 f(x_n)/x_0^2 <= 2(1+1/eta)/H.                            (3)

For k=0, the sharper bound

    2 f(x_n)/x_0^2 <= 1/(1+2S)                                (4)

holds without (1). If the terminal objective is zero all these bounds hold
directly. Scaling f by L gives the same normalized statements for arbitrary
L>0 and steps h_t/L.

### Proof

On either ray, put G(r)=|g(plus-or-minus r)|. Convexity and smoothness give
0<=G(r)<=r and make G nondecreasing and 1-Lipschitz. A strict sign crossing
requires its step b>1. Let u be the positive gradient magnitude immediately
before the first crossing. The preceding block gives

    a_0 >= (1+s_0)u,
    a_1 = b_0 u-r_0 <= (b_0-1)u.

The first inequality follows since every earlier gradient magnitude is at
least u and r_0>=u. Since s_0 is the entire prefix before b_0, (1) gives

    a_1 <= [(b_0-1)/(1+s_0)]a_0 <= a_0/eta.                    (5)

By (2), every later point on the initial ray has radius <=a_0, and every
point on the opposite ray has radius <=a_1. This controls all iterates in the
fixed interval [-a_1,a_0], hence every single-step displacement by a_0+a_1.

Let w=|g(x_n)|>0 and let v be the gradient magnitude immediately before the
last crossing b=b_(k-1). All tail gradients are at least w, so the landing
radius on the final ray satisfies

    a_k >= (1+s_k)w.

Here r_k>=w accounts for the extra 1. The crossing itself gives

    a_k=bv-r_(k-1)<=(b-1)v.

The entire tail after this last crossing has sum s_k, and (1) therefore yields

    v >= [(1+s_k)/(b-1)]w >= eta w >= w.                       (6)

Nested returns and raywise monotonicity imply that every earlier gradient on
the final ray is at least w, while every earlier gradient on the other ray is
at least v. Thus every sampled gradient magnitude, including at the largest
step H, is at least w. At that step, (5) gives

    H w <= H |g(x_t)|=|x_(t+1)-x_t|
        <= a_0+a_1 <= (1+1/eta)a_0.                           (7)

Finally convexity gives f(x_n)<=|x_n|w<=a_0 w. Combining this with (7) proves
(3). Every value and gradient belongs to the same fixed function; no oracle
is chosen independently at different times.

For k=0, put r=|x_n| and w=|g(x_n)|. Then r+S w<=a_0. Since
G(u)<=min(u,w) for 0<=u<=r, integration gives f(x_n)<=rw-w^2/2. Consequently

    2f(x_n)<=2a_0 w-(2S+1)w^2<=a_0^2/(2S+1),

which proves (4). This also supplies a complete area calculation for that case.

## 2. Application to the recursive hostile schedules

Define c=13/5, a=c^(-17), b_u=a c^u, H_0=(), and

    H_(m+1)=(H_m,b_m,H_m),
    N_m=2^m-1,
    s_m=sum H_m=a(c^m-2^m)/(c-2).

Put eta=13/8. Since (2/c)^17<1/40, we have

    s_u/b_u=[1-(2/c)^u]/(c-2)>=eta,       u>=17.               (8)

Every step greater than 1 in H_m is b_u for u>=18. Its occurrence has a
complete H_u immediately before it and a complete H_u immediately after it,
by the defining recursion. Thus its entire prefix and tail satisfy (1).
The largest step is B=b_(m-1). For m>=19, (3)-(4) give the uniform bound

    sup_(nested scalar trajectories) 2f(x_(N_m))/|x_0|^2
       <= (42/13)/B = [42/(5a)]c^(-m).                       (9)

In the zero-crossing case use S>=B in (4), which is smaller than (9).
The supremum permits any asymmetric convex 1-smooth function and any initial
point whose full trajectory satisfies (2). The function and all its knots may
depend on the full schedule and on m; there is no fixed-function-across-horizons
restriction.

For completeness, the elementary exact-DP bound needed to compare (9) with
the target profile is proved here. With C_n=S_circle(n)+1 and
D_n=2S_bullet(n)+1, the shifted joins are

    Omega(A,B)=(A+B+sqrt((A+B)^2+4AB))/2,
    G(A,D)=2A+D/2+sqrt(D^2+8AD)/2.

One has Omega(A,B)<=(A^(3/4)+B^(3/4))^(4/3). After normalization and squaring
at the positive root, this is equivalent to

    t^(4/3)+(1-t)^(4/3)+[t(1-t)]^(4/3)<=1,  0<=t<=1.

Set x=t^(1/3), y=(1-t)^(1/3). The difference from 1 is

    x^3 y^3 [1/(1+x+x^2)+1/(1+y+y^2)-xy],

which is nonnegative because the reciprocal terms are each at least 1/3
and xy<=2^(-2/3)<2/3. Induction in the exact primitive DP gives
C_n<=(n+1)^(4/3). Also G(A,D)<=D+4A. The dominant recursion then gives
D_n<=1+4n^(4/3), using u^(4/3)+v^(4/3)<=(u+v)^(4/3).

Since c^3>16 and U_n^ZJ=1/D_n, (9) implies

    sup_(nested scalar trajectories) [2f(x_(N_m))/|x_0|^2]
             /U_(N_m)^ZJ ->0.                               (10)

This excludes arbitrarily many nested inward returns, not mixed returns
that revisit or extend a previously traversed radial interval. The shared
coupled-coordinate candidate d1d7cfb25e10bfc5 supplies a divergent lower
bound on the actual multidimensional W_(N_m)(H_m); there is no contradiction.
That separate candidate is not needed in the proof of (9)-(10).

## 3. An explicit fixed multi-knot family inside this obstruction

This construction records the proposed new inward mechanism and its complete
old-area terms. Split any nonnegative schedule into k checkpoints b_i>1 and
k+1 ordinary blocks of sums s_i. Choose e>=0, put d_i=s_i for i<k and
d_k=s_k+e, choose m_k>0, and define backwards

    m_i=[m_(i+1)+sum_(j=i+1)^k d_j m_j]/(b_i-1),
                                            i=k-1,...,0.       (11)

Require m_i>=m_(i+2) for 0<=i<=k-2. Put

    O_i=sum_(j>i, j congruent i modulo 2) d_j m_j,
    ell_i=m_i+O_i,     a_i=ell_i+d_i m_i.                     (12)

On the ray of parity i, prescribe a plateau of gradient magnitude m_i on
[ell_i,a_i]. Build each ray from its innermost plateau outward. Start with a
unit-curvature core up to its innermost m_i. Consecutive plateaus on this ray
are connected by a slope-one ramp, since

    ell_i-a_(i+2)=m_i-m_(i+2)>=0.                            (13)

Equal heights simply merge. Extend the outermost plateau to infinity; if
one ray has no prescribed plateau, a unit quadratic on that ray suffices.
These two profiles, with the negative ray assigned a negative derivative,
define one continuous, nondecreasing, globally 1-Lipschitz derivative.
Define f(x) by integrating it from zero. Then f is convex and 1-smooth,
with its fixed minimum f(0)=0.

Start at x_0=a_0. For i<k, the ordinary block moves from radius a_i to ell_i
with gradient magnitude m_i, regardless of its individual nonnegative steps.
The next checkpoint lands at radius a_(i+1), because (11)-(12) give

    b_i m_i-ell_i=a_(i+1).

The last ordinary block moves from (1+s_k+e)m_k to (1+e)m_k. Thus the exact
normalized terminal objective is

    J=(1+2e)m_k^2/a_0^2.                                    (14)

Equation (13) makes every return nested in the sense (2). Therefore all
choices of k, checkpoints, m_k, and feasible e in this construction on H_m
are already covered by the uniform bound (9). Increasing the knot count in
this particular construction cannot repair the missing profile comparison.

The retained old-area defect on plateau i is

    Q_i=f(x)-xg(x)+g(x)^2/2
       =-sum_(j>i, j congruent i modulo 2)
                      d_j m_j(m_i-m_j) =:-D_i.              (15)

To check (15), at the inner end ell_i=m_i+sum_j d_j m_j integrate the ray's
gradient magnitude over [0,ell_i]. All unit ramps together span gradient
magnitudes from 0 to m_i and contribute m_i^2/2 in gradient coordinates.
Every inner plateau j contributes its additional area d_j m_j^2. Thus
f(plus-or-minus ell_i)=m_i^2/2+sum_j d_j m_j^2. Subtract ell_i m_i and add
m_i^2/2 to obtain (15). Moving along plateau i leaves f-xg unchanged, so (15)
holds throughout.

Let S be the total sum of the actual schedule, including checkpoints but
excluding e. For the primitive energy used in the earlier two-cut test,

    V=2 sum_t h_t[f(x_t)-f(x_n)]
       +sum_t h_t(1-h_t)g(x_t)^2-S(S+1)g(x_n)^2,

the exact formula is

    V=a_0^2-(S+1+e)^2 m_k^2
          -2 sum_(i=0)^(k-1) (s_i+b_i)D_i.                 (16)

Indeed, substituting Q=f-xg+g^2/2 and telescoping
2h xg-h^2g^2=x^2-(x-hg)^2 gives
V=x_0^2-x_n^2-2S f(x_n)-S(S+1)m_k^2+2sum_t h_t Q_t.
The final plateau has Q_k=0, while |x_n|=(1+e)m_k and
f(x_n)=(1/2+e)m_k^2. These yield (16).

No old area is discarded. Formula (16) can still be useful for rejecting
primitive certificates, despite the objective limitation (9).

## 4. Next mechanism

The remaining scalar search must permit at least one genuine revisit or
outward re-entry, a_(i+2)>r_i. Merely adding nested inner plateaus, changing
their curvature, or allowing more ordinary gradient variation cannot avoid
(9). A mixed-return construction must preserve the derivative values and
integrals on the overlap with the already traversed interval; this is exactly
the compatibility condition absent from a timewise choice of gradients.
