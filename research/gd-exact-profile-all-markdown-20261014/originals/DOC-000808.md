    Phi(t)=max(0,1-t,1-2t),  Psi(s)=1,

The three proposed profiles, with the zero plane included in Phi, are

## 3. Exact zero-set and constrained-distance update

Write a general enlarged point as z=z_old+w e, with z_old in E, and put
t=<u,z_old-p>. The entire new zero set is exactly the set satisfying

    z_old in P_old,
    t <= theta-F/(d c),
    w >= Z(t):=max_(a,q),q>0
                       [d R_y+F/(d q)+(a/q)(t-theta)].    (7)

The cap gives the t bound, each positive-q normal gives its displayed lower
bound on w, and all old planes depend only on z_old. Thus (7) proves genuine
zero-set feasibility; J_new>=0 alone has not been used to infer a minimizer.
For example, existence is equivalent to some finite Dist(t) with
t<=theta-F/(d c); any such t admits a finite sufficiently large w.

At the new outgoing slice tau=<u_new,z-p_new>, one has

    w=w(t,tau):=lambda tau+d R_y+epsilon(t-theta).

Orthogonality to the complete old ambient gives exactly
||x_init-z||^2=||x_init-z_old||^2+w^2. Therefore

    Dist_new(tau)=
       inf {Dist(t)+w(t,tau)^2:
                t<=theta-F/(d c), w(t,tau)>=Z(t)}.        (8)

This is an EQUALITY, including +infinity for empty feasible slices. For
one direction, every new feasible z gives a t and a cost no smaller than
the expression. For the reverse, a finite old Dist(t) has an attaining
old minimizer; append exactly w(t,tau)e. It satisfies every inequality
in (7), and has the displayed distance. The whole new slice is closed,
so whenever its infimum is finite its squared-distance minimum is attained.

Consequently the three profiles, together with the actual boundary scalar
G and the explicit new cell data, are SUFFICIENT for this geometry. No
additional spatial coordinate or Gram entry is needed for future plane
tests, zero-set feasibility or nearest-minimizer normalization. The updated
boundary data are G_new=d lambda, p_new and u_new. This sufficiency is
restricted to fresh coordinates orthogonal to the entire old ambient.

If D_min=min_tau Dist_new(tau)<infinity, the exact normalized terminal
payoff of this particular instance is

    [2F+d^2(1+epsilon^2)]/D_min.                         (9)

All terms refer to the SAME function and actual initial point. When the

## 4. A finite mathematical update rule

Phi is a finite maximum of affine functions, Psi a finite minimum. Formula
(6) preserves this form exactly. Dist is a convex extended-real piecewise
quadratic function on a polyhedral interval. One direct reason is that
minimizing squared distance to a polyhedron with one affine slice has only
finitely many possible active faces; on each face the equality-constrained
minimum is quadratic in the slice parameter. The projection optimality
inequalities select polyhedral parameter pieces. No uniqueness of active
face is needed; the strictly convex distance has a unique minimizing point.

The initial arc cell also gives Dist explicitly, so no general projection
algorithm is needed to initialize. If its center is zero, gradient scale d,
level F>0, cap (c,0), endpoint (epsilon,-1), and initial local query
(R_x,-R_y), put lambda=sqrt(1+epsilon^2) and

    t_0=-d(epsilon R_x+R_y)/lambda,
    v_0=d(-R_x+epsilon R_y)/lambda.

With outgoing coordinate t and its orthogonal in-plane coordinate v, its
zero set is t>=F/(d lambda), v>=F lambda/(d c)-epsilon t. Thus

    Dist(t)=(t-t_0)^2+[F lambda/(d c)-epsilon t-v_0]_+^2
                for t>=F/(d lambda),                    (10)

and +infinity otherwise. For the concave arc, cap/endpoint constraints imply
all intermediate zero inequalities: write a plane as
A Cap+q End+(1-A-q)F with A=(a-epsilon q)/c>=0 and A+q>=1.
If Cap,End<=0 and F>=0, the intermediate plane is <=0. This proves the
initial zero-set reduction directly.

For chains of these concave-arc cells with positive concurrent levels and a
nonempty zero set, the distance update simplifies further. Inductively its
finite domain is exactly [F_old/G,+infinity), where F_old=J_old(p)>0 and
G is the boundary gradient magnitude. The base case is (10). On the cap
range t<=theta-F/(d c), the endpoint is the largest lower bound in (7).
Indeed for any other q>0,

    Z_(a,q)(t)-Z_end(t)
      =F/d(1/q-1)+(a/q-epsilon)(t-theta)
      <=F/(d q)[1-q-(a-epsilon q)/c] <=0.

Thus Z(t)=d R_y+F/d+epsilon(t-theta), and (8) becomes

    Dist_new(tau)=min_(F_old/G<=t<=theta-F/(d c))
        [Dist(t)+(lambda tau+d R_y+epsilon(t-theta))^2], (10a)

for tau>=F/(d lambda), and +infinity otherwise. If the displayed t interval
is empty, all new slices are empty. If it is nonempty, every such tau is
feasible, proving the domain induction. This leaves the nontrivial distance
cost intact while making zero-set feasibility a single explicit budget:

    d R_x+F/(d c) <= (b-1)G-F_old/G.                    (10b)

In particular a strictly positive next level requires b>1+F_old/G^2.
For a prescribed positive level F, SOME scale d>0 can satisfy this minimum
budget if and only if

    (b-1)G-F_old/G >= 2sqrt(R_x F/c),                    (10c)

by minimizing d R_x+F/(d c), whose minimum is attained at
d=sqrt(F/(c R_x)). Equivalently the maximum allowed F from zero-set
feasibility alone is c[(b-1)G-F_old/G]^2/(4R_x), when the bracket is positive.
This is necessary for a legal join; it is not sufficient for (5).

The old endpoint query gives a second mandatory budget, from the new
endpoint plane alone:

    F <= F_old+d epsilon(b-1)G-d^2(epsilon R_x+R_y).       (10d)

Equations (10b)--(10d) are explicit value/scale losses in the actual bridge
and local block data. They prevent the profile recurrence from being
mistaken for cost-free positive-value propagation. Both conditions still
leave every remote-plane comparison in (5) to be checked or bounded.

In (8), on each known quadratic piece of Dist, the constraints are affine
inequalities in (t,tau). For fixed tau they give an interval in t, with
endpoints finite maxima/minima of affine functions of tau. The objective
is a strictly convex quadratic in t; take its affine vertex and clip it to
that interval and to the old piece. The resulting finitely many quadratic
expressions and their validity intervals give Dist_new. Their lower envelope
is the required value, with empty cases assigned +infinity. This is an exact
finite symbolic rule; no enumeration job or solver sweep is being run.

## 6. Exact remaining conditions and limits

with the known scalar boundary gradient magnitude and complete fresh-space
orthogonality. To take another step one must actually choose d,F with (5),
theta>=0 and a nonempty zero set from (7). The new profiles then follow from

The increasing-old-plane cost has not vanished: if an inherited plane equals

slack this requires D>=(sigma/epsilon)(sqrt(R_x+R_y/epsilon)
+sqrt(R_y/epsilon))^2. This follows by comparing that plane at the new
center with the new endpoint plane at the old center. The profiles retain
the obstruction and any slack that repairs it; they do not assume it away.
