# Positive-value joining of two nonidentical cells

Status: UNVERIFIED joint construction and obstruction. No new verifier call is
allocated. Worker max, 2026-09-26. This is a concrete two-block interface, not the
uniform exact-profile theorem. All supporting calculations needed for this
candidate are included; earlier unverified findings are not used as facts.

## 1. Locally constructible data for arbitrary ordinary steps

A cell has a finite list of normals v=(p,-q) with p>0, 0<=q<=1, including
v_end=(epsilon,-1) and v_cap=(c,0), where epsilon,c>0. Its local queries
r_j=(u_j,-y_j) and selected normals v_j satisfy

    r_(j+1)=r_j-alpha_j v_j,   r_m=v_end,
    <v_j,r_j-v_j> >= <v,r_j-v_j> for every cell normal v,
    u_j>=p_j, y_j>=1>=q_j,
    <v_end,r_j-v_j> >=0.                                  (1)

The selected proximal height h_j=<v_j,r_j-v_j> is nonnegative. For the cells
used below, p>=epsilon q, with equality only at v_end. The endpoint has h_m=0.
These are explicit local data properties, not assumed global interpolation.

Here is one construction for EVERY finite nonnegative ordinary schedule.
Choose epsilon,gamma>0 and set

    c=sqrt(1+2gamma+epsilon^2),
    R=sqrt(epsilon^2+(1+gamma)^2),
    V(q)=(sqrt(R^2-(q+gamma)^2),-q), 0<=q<=1.

Start backward from r_m=V(1). Given r_j=(u,-y) and preceding step alpha>=0,
put Delta=y+gamma-alpha gamma. If Delta<=u gamma/c, select v_prev=(c,0).
Otherwise put E=sqrt(u^2+Delta^2) and select

    v_prev=(R u/E,-(R Delta/E-gamma)).

Set r_prev=r_j+alpha v_prev. Retain the selected normals, both endpoints,
and no zero normal. Thus the data require a deterministic finite recursion,
not a feasibility search. Write r_0=(R_x,-R_y), so

    R_x=epsilon+sum alpha_j p_j,
    R_y=1+sum alpha_j q_j.                                 (2)

For completeness, the recursion has the stated properties. Inductively
u>=epsilon, y>=1 and y/u<=1/epsilon. In the turning case
gamma/c<Delta/u<=(1+gamma)/epsilon, so the displayed normal belongs to the
arc. Adding alpha times a normal preserves those three inequalities because
q/p<=1/epsilon. In the turning case

    r_prev-v_prev=(E/R+alpha-1)(v_prev-(0,gamma)).

The coefficient is nonnegative: E>=sqrt(u^2+(y+gamma)^2)-alpha gamma
>=R-alpha gamma. The displayed direction is an outward normal to the disk
centered at (0,gamma), of radius R, containing the whole arc. In the cap case
put M=u/c+alpha-1. The branch inequality gives y<=gamma M and M>0, and

    r_prev-(c,0)=M(c,-gamma)+(0,gamma M-y).

Both terms are normals to the convex hull of the arc and zero at its cap.
Consequently <r_prev-v_prev,v-v_prev><=0 for every arc point and zero.
This proves the maximizing inequality and h_prev>=0. These formulas also
give u_prev>=p_prev. For the endpoint-normal inequality in (1), the turning
formula has inner product equal to a nonnegative coefficient times
epsilon p+q+gamma; in the cap case it equals epsilon(u_prev-c)+y_prev>=0.
All assertions hold at the terminal point too. Restricting the arc to the
finite retained normals preserves every maximizing inequality.

No external theorem is needed for this local proof. Its circular-arc design
was prompted by Ye--Liu, arXiv:2609.09152v2, Lemma 3.1 and Appendix A.1,
whose proof was previously downloaded and read. It is not imported as a
black box or promoted to a fact here.

## 2. Geometry and all cross-block plane inequalities

Construct two cells A and B, independently, for their actual prescribed
ordinary steps. They may use different epsilon, gamma and normal lists.
Embed A in a Euclidean plane and give it gradient scale d_A>0. Translate its
center to zero. Give every A plane the SAME positive value F_A at that center:

    L_v^A(z)=F_A+d_A <v,z>.

The initial point is x_0=d_A r_0^A; all A queries and gradients are
x_j=d_A r_j^A and g_j=d_A v_j^A. Their proposed proximal points are
z_j=d_A(r_j^A-v_j^A), with current plane value F_A+d_A^2 h_j^A.

Execute a prescribed checkpoint b>1 at the final A query. Define

    lambda_A=sqrt(1+epsilon_A^2),
    u=-v_end^A/lambda_A,
    D=(b-1)d_A lambda_A,
    Y=D u.

The checkpoint indeed sends d_A v_end^A to Y. Choose a unit vector e
orthogonal to the A plane. Let r_0^B=(R_x,-R_y). Choose a scale d>0 and put

    theta=D-d R_x >=0,
    p_B=theta u+d R_y e.

Embed the B normal (p,-q) as p u-q e. Its affine plane is

    L_(p,q)^B(z)=F_B+d <p u-q e,z-p_B>,

where F_B>0. The B initial point p_B+d(R_x u-R_y e) equals Y exactly.
Its queries and gradients are p_B+d(u_j u-y_j e) and d(p_j u-q_j e).

Define the positive directional slopes

    a_v=d_A(epsilon_A p+q)/lambda_A,
    mu=min_(v in A) a_v,
    nu=d_A lambda_A.

For the arc cells mu>0 and mu<=nu. Define

    I(d)=max_(p,q in B) [-d p theta+d^2 R_y q].

ALL cross-block plane inequalities hold if and only if

    I(d) <= F_A-F_B <= mu theta.                           (3)

Proof, including nonadjacent queries and normals: at any A proximal point,
its u coordinate is -d_A<v_end^A,r_j^A-v_j^A>/lambda_A<=0 and its e
coordinate is zero. Since every B normal has p>0, its affine value there
is <= its value at zero, namely F_B-d p theta+d^2 R_y q. The selected A
value is >=F_A. The left inequality of (3) is therefore sufficient at
EVERY A query for EVERY future B plane; it is necessary at the terminal
A proximal point zero, where the current value is exactly F_A.

At every B proximal point, its A-plane projection is
[theta+d(u_j-p_j)]u, whose coefficient is >=theta. Every old A plane
there has value <=F_A-a_v theta<=F_A-mu theta. The selected B value is
>=F_B. The right inequality is sufficient at EVERY B query for EVERY old
A plane; it is necessary at the terminal B proximal point p_B, where
the A-plane coefficient is exactly theta and the B value is F_B.

Take ONE nonsmooth function and ONE Moreau envelope:

    J(z)=max(0, all L_v^A(z), all L_w^B(z)),
    f(x)=min_z [J(z)+||x-z||^2/2].

By (1) and (3), each prescribed normal is an active-plane subgradient at
the proposed proximal point, so x-z belongs to partial J(z). This is the
optimality condition for that proximal point and proves every gradient and
the full GD recursion for (ordinary A, b, ordinary B). No future or remote
plane is discarded. The proximal values are F_A+d_A^2 h_j^A and
F_B+d^2 h_j^B; the function values add one half of the respective squared
gradient norms. In particular the terminal objective satisfies

    2 f_end = 2F_B+d^2(1+epsilon_B^2),                     (4)

provided the zero set discussed below is nonempty, so min f=0.

The smoothness claim can also be checked without an external import. The
strictly convex coercive proximal objective has a unique minimizer z(x).
Its optimality equation is x-z(x) in partial J(z(x)). Monotonicity of the
subgradient gives ||g(x)-g(y)||^2<=<g(x)-g(y),x-y>, with g=x-z(x).
The two minimizing inequalities and this continuity imply grad f=g.
Thus f is convex, differentiable, and 1-smooth on the full Euclidean space.

The Gram data are explicit: A--A products are d_A^2< v,v'>, B--B products
are d^2(pp'+qq'), and every A--B product is

    <d_A v,d(p'u-q'e)> = -d p' a_v.                       (5)

In particular this is one coupled nonseparable function in three dimensions,
not a direct sum of independent local smooth functions.

## 3. Exact scale loss and a constructive value choice

For arc B, the function q/(mu+d p(q)) is strictly increasing, because p is
decreasing. Thus I(d)<=mu theta is equivalent to the SINGLE endpoint test

    d^2 R_y <= (D-d R_x)(mu+d epsilon_B).                  (6)

The cap test is automatic. For arbitrary finite B normals the same equivalence
holds when p(q) is decreasing and the endpoint is included, including the
two-plane cells in Section 5. Solving the quadratic gives

    d <= d_max,
    d_max = [D epsilon_B-mu R_x
             +sqrt((mu R_x-D epsilon_B)^2
                    +4D mu(R_y+epsilon_B R_x))]
             /[2(R_y+epsilon_B R_x)].                    (7)

The positive root lies strictly between zero and D/R_x. When mu=0 this
reduces to the minimum-reset bound

    d_reset = D/(R_x+R_y/epsilon_B).

Positive inherited values can improve this scale when mu>0, but do not remove
its geometric cost. Setting theta=0 forces the endpoint B plane at the old
center to equal F_B+d^2 R_y>F_B, whereas (3) requires F_A<=F_B. This is a
contradiction. Thus a pure new-coordinate join with no advance theta is
impossible even with freely chosen positive values. The substantive changed
state in this construction is the positive advance theta and the retained
level difference, rather than a reset at a common minimum.

Here is a completely prescribed feasible choice, without solving global
interpolation. For ANY two ordinary schedules and ANY bridge b>1, choose

    d=D/[2(R_x+R_y/epsilon_B)],
    theta=D-d R_x,
    F_A=F_B= [d c_B nu theta]/[2(nu+d c_B)].               (8)

Then each B plane at the old center has negative increment, since
q/p<=1/epsilon_B and d(R_x+R_y/epsilon_B)=D/2. Thus I(d)<=0. The other
inequality in (3) holds because theta>0. The minimizer feasibility condition
in Section 4 holds with slack. This proves a NONPERIODIC sufficient criterion
in the actual prescribed steps: construct each ordinary block by the explicit
backward recursion and use (8). It does not say that the resulting normalized
payoff has the target size, or that the joined object can be iterated after
discarding its old planes.

More generally take delta=max(0,I(d)), F_A=F_B+delta. Whenever
delta<nu theta, a legal positive choice is any

    0<F_B<= d c_B(nu theta-delta)/(nu+d c_B).              (9)

If mu<nu, this includes d=d_max. The extra restriction is essential: global
minimum normalization must not be inferred merely from the nonnegativity of J.

## 4. Exact global minimum and distance

Take coordinates t=<u,z>, w=<w_A,z>, z_e=<e,z>, where
w_A=(-1,-epsilon_A)/lambda_A in the A plane. For an A normal put

    b_v=d_A(p-epsilon_A q)/lambda_A.

The endpoint has a_v=nu,b_v=0; every other retained arc normal has b_v>0.
The full zero polyhedron {J=0} is exactly

    F_A/nu <= t <= theta-F_B/(d c_B),
    w >= W(t):=max_(v in A, v!=v_end) (F_A-a_v t)/b_v,
    z_e >= Z(t):=max_(p,q in B, q>0)
                         [d R_y+F_B/(d q)+(p/q)(t-theta)]. (10)

Consequently it is nonempty exactly when

    theta >= F_A/nu+F_B/(d c_B).                          (11)

There is no omitted plane in (10): the endpoint A plane and cap B plane give
the t interval, and all other A and B planes give the two displayed maxima.
The minima of f are exactly this zero polyhedron, since both terms in the
envelope definition are nonnegative and its minimum is attained.

The initial point has coordinates

    t_0=-d_A(epsilon_A R_x^A+R_y^A)/lambda_A,
    w_0=d_A(-R_x^A+epsilon_A R_y^A)/lambda_A,
    z_0=0.

Thus its squared distance to the ACTUAL nearest minimizer is exactly

    dist_0^2 = min over t in the interval (10) of
       (t-t_0)^2 + (max(W(t),w_0)-w_0)^2 + max(Z(t),0)^2. (12)

This is an explicit scalar piecewise-quadratic formula determined by the
normal lists: the breakpoints of the displayed finite maxima and the vertex
of each resulting quadratic give a finite closed rule for the minimum. It
is a normalization calculation, not a hidden hypothesis about interpolation
feasibility. Together (4) and (12) give the exact normalized payoff of this
particular instance, [2F_B+d^2(1+epsilon_B^2)]/dist_0^2. Section 5 evaluates
the actual nearest minimizer in rational closed form.

## 5. Rational nonidentical example with all values and normalization

Cell A has normals (1,0) and (3/4,-1), one ordinary step 5, and d_A=1.
Its local queries are (23/4,-1) and (3/4,-1). At the first proximal point
(19/4,-1), the cap inner product is 76/16 and the endpoint inner product
is 73/16; at the final proximal point zero they agree. Hence this two-plane
cell is legal directly, independently of the arc recursion.

Take bridge b=3. Then u=(-3/5,4/5,0), D=5/2, mu=3/5, nu=5/4.
Cell B has normals (1,0),(1/2,-1) and ordinary step 3, with local queries
(7/2,-1),(1/2,-1). Its first proximal cap and endpoint inner products
are 5/2 and 9/4, respectively. Set

    d=3/5, theta=2/5, F_A=7/25, F_B=1/25,
    p_B=(-6/25,8/25,3/5).

Here I(d)=6/25=mu theta=F_A-F_B. Formula (7) gives d_max=3/5, whereas
d_reset=5/11. This improves squared transmitted scale by 1089/625 before
normalization; the actual normalized comparison is evaluated below.

In Cartesian coordinates (x,y,z), the ENTIRE nonsmooth function is

    J=max(0,L_0,L_1,L_2,L_3),
    L_0=7/25+x,
    L_1=7/25+(3/4)x-y,
    L_2=-(9/25)x+(12/25)y-1/5,
    L_3=7/25-(9/50)x+(6/25)y-(3/5)z.

Its envelope realizes schedule (5,3,3), with exact queries and gradients

    x_0=(23/4,-1,0),          g_0=(1,0,0),
    x_1=(3/4,-1,0),           g_1=(3/4,-1,0),
    x_2=(-3/2,2,0),           g_2=(-9/25,12/25,0),
    x_3=(-21/50,14/25,0),     g_3=(-9/50,6/25,-3/5).

The complete plane-value check at the four proximal points x_i-g_i is

    i    L_0       L_1       L_2       L_3       chosen

    1    7/25      7/25     -1/5       7/25     L_1

Every chosen value is nonnegative and dominates ALL four planes. The

The zero polyhedron, in t=<u,(x,y)> and w=<(-4/5,-3/5),(x,y)>, is

    28/125<=t<=1/3,

One half of its derivative is (29/16)t+293/240>0 throughout the feasible

    x_star=(-7/25,7/100,217/375),
    ||x_0-x_star||^2=42570737/1125000,
    2 f(x_3)/||x_0-x_star||^2=596250/42570737.             (13)

This is a lower bound on W_3(5,3,3), not its exact value.

## 6. Hostile schedules and the exact failure of memoryless iteration

The minimum-reset obstruction survives in a precise form: theta=0 is
impossible, and the nonzero allowed theta is constrained by both (6) and
the actual-minimum condition (11). Formula (7), rather than the cost-free
scale D/R_x, is the quantitative transfer law.

For the recursive hostile schedules H_0=(),
H_(r+1)=(H_r,b_r,H_r), b_r=(13/5)^(r-17), the two ordinary blocks H_r are
legitimate inputs to the arc recursion. When r>=18, b_r>1, so the explicit
choice (8) gives a legal positive-value join for the actual schedule H_(r+1).
It does not require b_r>=sum H_r+2. More generally ANY two nonidentical
ordinary lists with any bridge b>1 satisfy this constructive criterion.
No comparison of (12) with the full exact benchmark is inferred. In
particular the known Huber calibration of this recursive family is not a
new solution of the arbitrary-schedule problem.

There is a concrete closure failure if the joined object is compressed to
the B cell alone. Its outgoing unit direction is

    u_B=-(epsilon_B u-e)/sqrt(1+epsilon_B^2).

Every OLD A normal has POSITIVE slope in that direction:

    <d_A v,u_B>=epsilon_B a_v/sqrt(1+epsilon_B^2)>0.        (14)

The B normals have negative outgoing slopes. Thus the entire inherited
plane family cannot be replaced by a decreasing envelope based only on
F_B and the B normals. In the rational example L_0(p_B)=F_B and
<g_0,u_B>=3sqrt(5)/25>0. Immediately along p_B+s u_B, s>0, this remote
plane rises strictly above F_B while all B planes decrease. This is an
exact remote-plane counterexample to that proposed state compression,
not a prohibition on three-block joins retaining the old data or choosing
the function with knowledge of the complete schedule.

The next substantive variable is therefore the whole signed directional
profile of inherited planes at the new center, or a proved sufficient
envelope retaining its increasing branches. A further periodic example
would not resolve this closure issue. No extra verifier call was made.
