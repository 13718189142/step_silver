# Exact retained storage from a two-step primitive block

Status: unverified research identities. No new verifier or numerical PEP call has been used. These formulas do not yet certify an asymptotic improvement.

All functions below are convex and differentiable with 1-Lipschitz gradient. Write

    Q_ij = f_i-f_j + <g_i,x_j-x_i> + ||g_i-g_j||^2/2 <= 0.

## 1. Primitive dual residual, with the actual interpolation coefficients

For a GD block of nonnegative physical steps h, let S=sum h_i, A=S+1, d=x_0-x_n, p=g_0 and q=g_n. Define its saturated primitive energy

    V = 2 sum_(i<n) h_i(f_i-f_n)
        + sum_(i<n) h_i(1-h_i)||g_i||^2 - S A ||q||^2.

The identity below is algebraic for every block; the primitive assumption is V>=0. Put

    H = 2(f_0-f_n)-2S||q||^2-||p-(d+q)/A||^2,
    A_start = -2[sum_(i<n) h_i Q_(i,0)+Q_(n,0)],
    A_end   = -2 sum_(i<n) h_i Q_(i,n).

Then

    H = (2A-1)/A^2 V + A_start/A + (A-1)/A^2 A_end.       (1)

In particular H>=0 for an actual primitive block. This follows without importing a quadratic-product condition or identifying the ambient primitive and s-composable classes.

For a direct check, put Z=d+q and use proof weights (h,1). Their intrinsic energy is V+A(A-1)||q||^2. Expanding the two interpolation sums gives

    A_start = 2A(f_0-f_n)-V-A(A-1)||q||^2
              -||Z||^2+2<Z,p>-A||p||^2,
    A_end = -V+||Z||^2-A^2||q||^2.

Substituting proves (1). This is the exact identity obtained before the optimum interpolation is added in Grimmer--Shu--Wang, arXiv:2410.16249, Appendix A.1, Lemma17. That proof was read, including its definitions and Proposition6 input; the calculation here checks applicability to the present V definition.

## 2. The pair (3/2,3/2) has a sharp nonzero boundary remainder

For this specific two-step block let p=g_0, m=g_1, q=g_2. Its displacement is d=(3/2)(p+m), and S=3, A=4. Define the retained displacement error

    t=d-3q=(3/2)(p+m-2q).

The exact certificate is

    V = 4(-Q_10)+9(-Q_12)+16(-Q_21)+(-Q_02)
        + ||t||^2/9.                                (2)

All coefficients are nonnegative. Expanding each Q verifies the function coefficients (3,3,-6), gradient diagonal coefficients (-3/4,-3/4,-12), and zero off-diagonal coefficients after adding the displayed square. Thus

    V >= ||d-3q||^2/9.                               (3)

The coefficient 1/9 is sharp over all legal functions. Take the one-dimensional function with derivative g(x)=x for x<=1 and g(x)=1 for x>=1, normalized by f(0)=0. It is convex, 1-smooth, and minimized at zero. Starting at x_0=5/2 gives x_1=1, x_2=-1/2, gradients (1,1,-1/2), and values (2,1/2,1/8). Therefore V=9/4 and t=9/2, giving V=||t||^2/9.

This is a new retained term, not merely a second naming of the objective residual. It vanishes on both the flat-gradient and full-curvature equality trajectories, but need not vanish on a general trajectory.

## 3. Objective and dual slacks are correlated

For the same pair, set r=p-(d+q)/4 and H=2(f_0-f_2)-6||q||^2-||r||^2. Equation (1) becomes

    H = (7/16)V + (3/4)(-Q_10)+(1/2)(-Q_20)
                    +(9/16)(-Q_02)+(9/16)(-Q_12).   (4)

There is also the exact reverse-side combination

    V-(4/5)H
      = (2/5)(-Q_01)+(9/5)(-Q_10)+(26/5)(-Q_12)
          +(48/5)(-Q_21)+||t||^2/36.                (5)

Thus this actual block supplies both H>=0 and

    V >= (4/5)H + ||t||^2/36.                        (6)

Unlike separate assumed squared penalties, (5) preserves a concrete correlation between the objective energy and the dual slack. Every actual interpolation multiplier is displayed; no negative interpolation inequality is used.

Equivalently, with Delta=f_0-f_2 and boundary vector (p,d,q),

    V-(8/5)Delta >= (p,d,q)^T J (p,d,q),

    J = [ -4/5    1/5     1/5  ]
        [  1/5   -1/45   -2/15 ]
        [  1/5   -2/15   -23/5 ].                   (7)

Here the matrix is an inherited storage term and is not asserted PSD. Discarding it or resetting its displacement coordinate at a later join would be invalid. The same clipped-quadratic example in part2 attains (5) at equality: H=135/64, V=9/4 and t^2/36=9/16.

For checking (2) and (5), the following one-parameter coefficient identity is useful. For any real kappa,

    V = (1-4kappa)(-Q_01)+(1/2+2kappa)(-Q_02)
        +(7/2+2kappa)(-Q_10)+(17/2+2kappa)(-Q_12)
        +(1-4kappa)(-Q_20)+(14+8kappa)(-Q_21)
        + kappa ||p+m-2q||^2.                       (8)

Equation (2) uses kappa=1/4. Combining (8) with (4), using kappa=5/52 in the factor (13/20)V, proves (5). This was checked by a fixed nine-equation rational coefficient calculation, not by an optimization or numerical search.

## 4. Calibration also recovers the full dominant recursion

The existing exact join in max4_two_sided_residual_join.md recovers ConPP. Its ordinary ConPD specialization does not require an independent tail residual penalty.

Let the prefix be genuinely primitive with capacity K, energy V_a and weights (a,K), so D_a=2K-1 and e_a=K g. Let the tail have arbitrary nonnegative intrinsic proof weights v, mass D_b>0 and energy E_b>=0. Define

    beta=(1+sqrt(1+8K/D_b))/2,
    c=2K/D_b=beta^2-beta,
    tau=1+K/beta.

Keep the prefix physical weights, change its terminal weight from K to K+1, and scale every tail weight by beta. Its new mass is

    D_new=2K+beta D_b=beta^2 D_b=G(K,D_b).

For A_y=-2 sum_(i in tail) v_i Q_(i,y), where y is the bridge start, direct expansion gives

    E_new=V_a+beta^2 E_b+c A_y.                       (9)

Indeed the general join has eta=1, gamma=0, m=t_y=t_z=0; its quadratic remainder has coefficients -2K+cD_b=0 on g^2, beta(1-beta)+c=0 on U_b^2, and beta(tau-1-K)+c(tau-1)=0 on <g,U_b>. All actual weights and interpolation coefficients are nonnegative. The standard squared-distance identity therefore gives W<=1/D_new.

Consequently the exact boundary interface recovers both scalar F and G operations and hence every finite-horizon DP denominator B_n, not merely a dyadic exponent or a rate with a fixed asymptotic loss. Equations (2)--(7) are extra block information; their profitable propagation is the outstanding construction task.

## 5. Terminal-storage literature: a precise finite reduction

Scherer, arXiv:2207.01363, Theorem5.1 in the HTML numbering (Theorem11 in the main-agent PDF pointer), equations30--35 and AppendixA.5, reduces its finite terminal-cost expression to a doubly hyperdominant matrix M: off-diagonal entries are nonpositive and all row and column sums are nonnegative. Its filter is initialized at zero. The proof and the conjecture in Section7 were read; no completeness conjecture or nonzero-initial-state extension is used here.

The connection to the project's actual interpolation cone can be checked directly. For samples including a minimizer, translate x_star=0 and f_star=0 and set

    z_i=x_i-g_i, F_i=f_i-||g_i||^2/2.

Then Q_ij=F_i-F_j+<g_i,z_j-z_i>. For a doubly hyperdominant M on the non-optimum samples define weights

    a_ij=-M_ij (i!=j),
    a_i,star=sum_j M_ij,
    a_star,i=sum_j M_ji.

All weights are nonnegative. Their incoming and outgoing masses agree at every vertex, including the optimum. Thus the F coefficients cancel, and exactly

    sum_(i,j) a_ij(-Q_ij)=sum_(i,j) M_ij<g_i,z_j>.   (10)

The finite hyperdominant terminal form therefore gives an actual combination of the existing smooth-convex interpolation inequalities. It is a potentially useful parametrization of retained correlations, not an additional nonlinear premise. Equation (10) says nothing about completeness of the five-variable join or of any finite memory compression.

At a split, a one-step memory example already shows what must be retained:

    sum_(i=m)^n <g_i,z_i-z_(i-1)> -(F_n-F_(m-1))
      =sum_(i=m)^n (-Q_(i,i-1)) >=0.                (11)

Both z_(m-1) and F_(m-1) are inherited. Replacing them by zero at an internal join changes the identity. For this project, the concrete new candidate storage is (7), with the displacement and both endpoint gradients retained, rather than a bare appeal to a stability theorem.
