## Exact finite object and every original normal

Fix n+1 linearly independent nonzero vectors v_0,...,v_n in R^(n+1),
nonnegative h_0,...,h_(n-1), and V with those vectors as columns. Let

    D0=conv{0,v_0,...,v_n}, Kpolar={z:V^T z<=0},
    Phi(x)=max_(v in D0) [<x,v>-||v||^2/2],
    P_i=sum_(k<i)h_k v_k, A_ij=<P_i,v_j>, G_ij=<v_i,v_j>,
    C_ij=A_ij-A_ii+G_ij-G_ii,
    K_i=A_ii+G_ii, c_n=A_nn+G_nn/2.

Completing the square identifies the unique maximizer as the Euclidean
projection p(x) of x onto D0. Its normal condition is
<x-p(x),u-p(x)><=0 for every u in D0. Necessity follows by taking a
one-sided derivative along each segment; sufficiency follows by expanding
||x-u||^2. Checking the generating vertices, including zero, suffices.
The two projection normals imply
||p(x)-p(y)||^2<=<x-y,p(x)-p(y)> and hence p is 1-Lipschitz.
The maximizing formula gives the lower affine support with gradient p(x);
using p(x+e) for the upper comparison gives an error of at most ||e||^2.
Thus Phi is convex, differentiable, has gradient p, and is 1-smooth.
Since zero is a candidate, Phi>=0. Phi(x)=0 iff V^T x<=0: the forward
implication follows by considering arbitrarily small positive multiples of
each v_i. Thus its whole minimizer set is Kpolar.

For an initializer x0 let f=V^T x0 (initializer potentials, not function
values) and x_i=x0-P_i. The normal to v_i against v_j is exactly

    <x_i-v_i,v_j-v_i>=f_j-f_i-C_ij <=0.

The normal against zero is K_i-f_i<=0. Consequently every ordered pair
f_j-f_i<=C_ij and every zero row f_i>=K_i are jointly necessary and
sufficient for all prescribed vertices, terminal included, to be the actual
gradients along this GD transcript. There is no h_n. In that case

    Phi(x_i)=f_i-A_ii-G_ii/2,
    q(x0):=2 Phi(x_n)=2(f_n-c_n)>=G_nn>0.

Let r(x0)=dist(x0,Kpolar)^2. A closest point exists: Kpolar is nonempty
and closed, and a minimizing sequence can be restricted to a bounded ball.
The zero row at i=0 gives <x0,v0>>=||v0||^2, while every polar point has
nonpositive pairing; therefore r(x0)>=||v0||^2>0 for a legal initializer.
The closest-minimum normalized score is Q(x0)=q(x0)/r(x0), in W=2R
normalization. All initializer potentials are represented because V^T is
invertible. The numerical implementation never forms an inverse Gram.

## Exact perspective and both directions

Variables b,zstar in R^(n+1) and tau>=0 define F=V^T b. Consider

    maximize O=2 F_n-2 tau c_n,
    F_j-F_i<=tau C_ij for EVERY i,j,
    F_i>=tau K_i, V^T zstar<=0, ||b-zstar||^2<=tau.

The last inequality is represented exactly by the ordinary SOC

    ||(2(b-zstar),tau-1)|| <= tau+1,

with the explicit tau>=0 constraint retained. Squaring both sides yields
4||b-zstar||^2<=4tau, and the nonnegative right side makes this reversible.

Given any legal x0, choose its actual closest polar point y and set
tau=1/r(x0), b=tau x0, zstar=tau y. Homogeneity of the polar cone and
of the normal inequalities makes this a feasible perspective point.
The cone is tight and its objective equals Q(x0). Thus the perspective
supremum is at least the supremum over every legal initializer.

Conversely, any exactly feasible point with tau>0 gives x0=b/tau and
y=zstar/tau in Kpolar. Dividing the original rows proves full legality.
Its selected radius Rsel=||x0-y||^2<=1/tau, while r(x0)<=Rsel. Its
positive terminal numerator is q=O/tau. Hence

    Q(x0)=q/r(x0) >= Qsel=q/Rsel >= O.

This proves equality of the two suprema when the legal set is nonempty;
it does not assert that an arbitrary feasible zstar is a closest point.
At an attained positive optimum, failure of either distance equality would
allow mapping the same initializer back using its closest point to produce
a strictly larger objective. This analytical observation is not a license
to declare floating output exactly optimal or its returned zstar closest.

At tau=0 the cone forces b=zstar. Zero rows force F>=0, while polar rows
force F<=0, so F=0; independence gives b=zstar=0 and O=0. The zero point
is always feasible. Therefore the program value is precisely the supremum
of {0} union all legal initializer scores, even if the legal set is empty.
The conventional supremum of an empty legal set is not this zero value.

For floating output, O, Qsel, and a claim about Q must stay separate.
Positive numerical residuals invalidate an exact feasibility assertion;
no returned ratio becomes a rigorous lower bound without repairing every
normal and polar defect by an independently authorized exact argument.

## Two exact calibrations, derived before execution

1. Empty word, v0=1: P=A=C=0, G=K=1 and c=1/2. Here F=b,
   b>=tau, zstar<=0 and (b-zstar)^2<=tau. Thus b>=0, tau>=b^2 and
   O=2b-tau<=2b-b^2<=1. At tau=b=1,zstar=0 every original row and
   cone hold and O=1. This is the exact optimum, also Q=1.
2. h=(1), v0=(1,0), v1=(1/2,1/2). The determinant of V is 1/2,
   so this is a full-rank two-dimensional fixture. Direct inner products give

       G=[[1,1/2],[1/2,1/2]], P0=0, P1=(1,0),
       A=[[0,0],[1,1/2]], C=[[0,-1/2],[1/2,0]],
       K=(1,1), c_1=3/4.

   Let p=V^T x0. The rows 0->1 and 1->0 respectively give
   p1-p0<=-1/2 and p0-p1<=1/2. Thus p1-p0=-1/2 exactly.
   Write p1=u, p0=u+1/2. The two zero rows are u+1/2>=1 and u>=1,
   which together are equivalent to u>=1. Diagonal ordered rows are zero.
   Solving these two inner-product equations directly, without an inverse
   Gram, gives every legal initializer and no others:

       x0=(u+1/2,u-1/2), u>=1.

   In particular x0=v0+(2u-1)v1, whose two coefficients are positive.
   For every z in Kpolar, <z,v0><=0 and <z,v1><=0, hence <x0,z><=0.
   The exact norm comparison

       ||x0-z||^2=||x0||^2+||z||^2-2<x0,z> >= ||x0||^2

   has equality at z=0; for any z!=0 its ||z||^2 term makes it strict.
   Consequently zero is the unique closest polar point for every legal x0,
   and the exact squared distance is 2u^2+1/2, strictly positive on u>=1.
   The exact doubled terminal gap is 2(u-c_1)=2u-3/2>=1/2>0.
   The score in the W=2R normalization therefore is

       Q(u)=(2u-3/2)/(2u^2+1/2), u>=1.

   Its derivative is

       Q'(u)=(-4u^2+6u+1)/(2u^2+1/2)^2.

   The numerator has roots u_-=(3-sqrt(13))/4<0 and
   u_+=(3+sqrt(13))/4>1. The denominator never vanishes. The derivative
   is positive on [1,u_+) and negative on (u_+,infinity), so u_+ is the
   unique maximum on the entire legal domain. The endpoint is Q(1)=1/5,
   and Q(u) tends to zero as u tends to infinity. At the positive root,
   4u_+^2-6u_+-1=0 gives

       Q(u_+)=1/(2u_+)=2/(3+sqrt(13))=(sqrt(13)-3)/2.

   To check the perspective directly, any tau>0 feasible point divides
   to this legal x0 and a polar y. Its cone imposes
   tau<=1/||x0-y||^2<=1/(2u^2+1/2), so its positive objective is at most
   Q(u). Conversely, at every u>=1 set tau=1/(2u^2+1/2), b=tau*x0,
   zstar=0. Every original row holds and the cone is tight, attaining Q(u).
   At tau=0 the general zero-scale argument gives objective zero, smaller
   than Q(u_+). The perspective optimum is exactly the displayed radical.

## Frozen finite Gray construction

M=1536 in every production case. Define eps=1/(4M), beta=1-eps,
c=(1+sqrt(2))(1+eps), a=beta/c, H_l=1+a c^l,
h_k=H_(nu2(k+1)). Let Gray(k)=k xor floor(k/2). The N rows of w_k
consist of characters chi_t(Gray(k)) with all nonzero t=1,...,N-1,
followed by the constant coordinate 1/sqrt(3). The j distinguished
characters t_l=N-2^l have squared amplitude 2^l/(N-1); every other
nonzero character has squared amplitude eta=1/[N^2(N-1-j)]. Thus
Tframe=4/3+1/N^2 and direct character summation gives

    B_kl=Tframe-2(k xor l)/(N-1)+2eta popcount(k xor l)
          -eta N+eta N 1_(k=l).

The implementation constructs characters first and independently exports
every raw signed discrepancy between their inner products and this kernel.
Walsh orthogonality with all positive amplitudes, including the vertical
row, gives independence; multiplying columns by positive sigma preserves it.

Badj_l=Tframe-2E_l. Let rho_l be the positive root of
rho^2+(H_l-2)(Badj_l/Tframe)rho+1-H_l=0. The constant term is negative

All selected N exceed delta. Sigma is constructed chronologically from
sigma0=1 and sigma_(k+1)=sigma_k r_(nu2(k+1)), then v_k=sigma_k w_k.

Among integers 1,...,k, there are ceil(k/2) odd integers and
floor((k+2)/4) integers of valuation one (2,6,10,...). Hence, writing
