# Quantitative final-step descent from a terminal pair in the PSD Schur range

Status: unverified candidate. It extends the concrete descent search to certificates with no star-outgoing multipliers. It is not a general q>tau descent theorem.

## 1. Full-certificate hypothesis

Normalize L=1, x_*=f_*=0, and ||x_0||=1. Let a length-n schedule h, n>=1, have final step b=h_{n-1}>0 and a full feasible upper certificate (q,lambda), using all smooth-convex inequalities Q_ij<=0 and a PSD slack matrix

    M=[[q,-mu^T/2],[-mu/2,A]],   mu_i=lambda_{i,*}, q>0.

Let Z=A-mu mu^T/(4q)>=0 be its complete gradient Schur complement. This is the actual matrix from the full certificate, not a surrogate formed from a few minors.

Suppose there exist kappa>0, a>0 and s in {+1,-1} such that

    Z >= kappa v v^T,
    v = a e_{n-1}+s e_n.                                   (H)

The indices e_i refer to gradient coordinates. The sign s=+1 will give a decrease of the final step; s=-1 will give an increase.

Define the finite radius bounds

    B_prev=prod_{t<n-1}(1+h_t),    B=(1+b)B_prev,
    C=q/(2B^2)+1/kappa.

Choose any d>0 satisfying

    d<=a/C,
    d<=q^2/(8a B^2 B_prev^2),                               (d)
    d<b                  if s=+1.

The changed schedule, with all preceding steps fixed, is

    b_new=b-s d.

Then the actual worst-case value obeys the explicit strict improvement

    W_n(h_0,...,h_{n-2},b_new)
       <= q-d q^2/(8a B^2) < q.                            (D)

No Huber-tightness, vanishing star-outgoing flow, stationarity, or rank assumption on the old primal is needed. The sufficient condition is the concrete full-matrix domination (H).

## 2. Uniform proof, including nearly worst-case instances

Fix any legal function. Put E=2f(x_n), p=g_{n-1}, g=g_n, and r=a p+s g. The complete certificate identity and every interpolation sign give

    q-E >= M evaluated on the actual vectors
          >= g_all^T Z g_all
          >= kappa ||r||^2.                               (1)

Here the second inequality follows by completing the square in x_0 in the full matrix. Thus ||r||^2<=(q-E)/kappa; the formula remains valid for every instance, not only for a maximizing one.

The elementary bounds ||p||<=B_prev, ||x_n||<=B, and convexity yield

    ||g||^2 >= E^2/(4B^2).                                 (2)

The identity ||r||^2=a^2||p||^2+||g||^2+2s a<p,g> implies

    -2s <p,g> >= (||g||^2-||r||^2)/a.                     (3)

The new endpoint is x_n+s d p. Smoothness gives

    E_new <= E+2s d<p,g>+d^2||p||^2
           <= E-(d/a)[E^2/(4B^2)-(q-E)/kappa]
                    +d^2 B_prev^2.                        (4)

For every E, the scalar inequality E^2>=q^2-2q(q-E) is simply (E-q)^2>=0. Inserting it into (4) gives

    E_new <= q-d q^2/(4a B^2)
               -[1-(d/a)C](q-E)+d^2 B_prev^2.

Since E<=q and d<=a/C, the middle term is nonpositive. The other restriction in (d) then gives (D). Taking the supremum over all legal functions and dimensions proves the uniform bound.

Thus this is an actual stepsize perturbation with an explicit certified new value. It is not a first-order assertion evaluated only on a guessed active function. The elementary smoothness upper inequality in (4) also follows from Q_{new,old}<=0 by completing the square
||g_new-g-s d p||^2/2, so the argument is consistent with the full interpolation cone.

The proof provides a uniform analytic certificate for the new schedule. It does not claim an explicit closed formula for its minimal-format interpolation multipliers. Such a formula is unnecessary to conclude that a purported globally optimal schedule has been strictly improved.

## 3. A range exclusion at global minimizers

Let T=span{e_{n-1},e_n}. For a PSD matrix Z and a nonzero v in range(Z), one has

    Z >= kappa v v^T,   kappa=1/(v^T Z^dagger v)>0.

Indeed write v=Z^(1/2)w with w=Z^(dagger/2)v, and use (w^T Z^(1/2)u)^2<=||w||^2 u^T Z u. Thus any vector in range(Z) supported on the last two coordinates, with both entries nonzero, can be rescaled to condition (H). Either sign of their ratio is allowed.

A vector with only the last coordinate nonzero is incompatible with a tight positive certificate: its square would force g_n=0 on a matching worst-case instance, hence f_n=0. A vector with only coordinate n-1 nonzero likewise forces g_{n-1}=0, making x_n=x_{n-1} a minimizer. Matching worst cases exist in the finite full interpolation formulation; alternatively, the quantitative argument below rules these cases out without assuming attainment.

For the last coordinate alone, q-E>=kappa||g_n||^2>=kappa E^2/(4B^2). This uniform inequality implies E<=r<q, where r is the positive root of r+kappa r^2/(4B^2)=q. It improves the allegedly tight upper bound. For the penultimate coordinate alone, use ||g_n||<=(1+b)||g_{n-1}|| to get the same conclusion with kappa replaced by kappa/(1+b)^2.

Consequently every tight full certificate at a globally minimizing positive schedule satisfies

    range(Z) intersect T = {0}.                             (R)

In particular

    rank(Z)<=n-1.

For n=1 this forces Z=0. For larger n, it does not force the whole Schur complement to vanish: its range may involve earlier gradients in every nonzero vector.

An equivalent exact matrix formulation uses the earlier-coordinate set J={0,...,n-2}. The generalized Schur complement of Z with respect to its J block must vanish:

    Z_TT-Z_TJ Z_JJ^dagger Z_JT=0.                           (SC)

For a PSD block matrix, completing squares gives the factorization into a block supported on the J rows and a residual supported only on T. The latter is PSD and its nonzero range lies in range(Z) intersect T. Conversely, if that residual is zero, the factorized range has no nonzero vector with zero J coordinates. This proves the equivalence, including singular Z_JJ. For n=1, J is empty and (SC) means Z=0.

This range restriction retains all history-dependent entries of Z; it does not replace Z by a two-by-two principal minor.

## 4. Exact one-step checks, including nu=0 above the Huber level

For b=1+a with 1/2<a<1, there is a tight certificate with

    q=a^2,
    lambda_{0,*}=2a, lambda_{1,*}=2(1-a), lambda_{1,0}=2a,

and all other multipliers zero. Its Schur complement is

    Z=(2a-1)[[1,1/a],[1/a,1/a^2]]
      =[(2a-1)/a^2] (a,1)^T(a,1).

Thus (H) holds with s=+1, kappa=(2a-1)/a^2, even though every star-outgoing multiplier vanishes. Formula (D) gives an explicit strict descent by decreasing b. The matching legal quadratic has g_1=-a g_0, exactly the relation exposed by the PSD square.

For 0<b<3/2, the tight Huber certificate with lambda_{0,*}=lambda_{1,*}=lambda_{1,0}=1 has

    q=1/(2b+1), Z=(3-2b)[[1,-1],[-1,1]]/4.

Here (H) holds with a=1, s=-1. The final step increases and the uniform bound strictly improves. This is consistent with the exact one-step optimum b=3/2, where Z=0 and this descent disappears.

These checks also show why q>tau alone is neither the hypothesis nor the conclusion of this result: the range mechanism can detect a suboptimal Huber-tight schedule, and it detects a non-Huber certificate with nu=0.

## 5. Remaining obstruction

Together with the penultimate-flow descent in research/max2_penultimate_flow_descent.md, the new constraints rule out two concrete ways for a globally minimizing tight certificate to carry slack: lambda_{*,n-1}>0 and any Schur-range vector supported on the final two gradients.

They leave earlier star-outgoing multipliers, the final star-outgoing multiplier, and a Schur range intertwined with earlier gradients. There is no proved implication from (R) to q=tau, no proved capacity bound by S_bullet(n), and no exact-profile conclusion. The next mechanism would have to control that earlier-coordinate dependence without treating the final two-coordinate Schur complement as the full matrix.
