    ||g_i-g_j||^2<=<g_i-g_j,X_i-X_j>.                        (10)

    D^2+(h-2)<g',g><=(h-1)A^2.

    ||g'||<=max(1,h-1)||g||.                                (11)

## 6. A ruler-product estimate and bounded accumulated forcing

This section is an explicit scalar calculation, proved here as part of
the joint candidate. It is not imported from an unverified finding.
Put t0=tau_ang=2^-12 and define gains

    G_2=theta=3/5, G_q=1+t0(5/2)^q forq>=3.

Let W_s be the finite scalar level word of length2^s-1 with entries
2+nu_2(r),1<=r<=2^s-1. Thus W_1=(2) and

    W_s=(W_(s-1),s+1,W_(s-1)), s>=2.

At level2 apply the scalar map y->theta y. At any higher levelq apply
y->G_q y+d, where d>=0 is fixed. Composing in chronological order over
W_s gives a map

    y -> P_s y+E_s d,
    P_1=theta, E_1=0,
    P_s=G_(s+1)P_(s-1)^2,
    E_s=(1+G_(s+1)P_(s-1))E_(s-1)+P_(s-1).                (36)

We claim, for every integer s>=1,

    P_s<=(3/4)^(2^(s-1)), E_s<256.                         (37)

First expand the multiplicative recurrence:

    log P_s=2^(s-1)[log theta
                         +sum_(q=3)^(s+1)2^(2-q)log G_q].  (38)

For every x>=0, log(1+x)<=sqrt(x): both sides vanish at0, and forx>0
the derivative of their difference is
(sqrt(x)-1)^2/[2sqrt(x)(1+x)]>=0. Put r_geo=sqrt(5/8)<4/5. Hence
the entire infinite positive sum in(38) obeys the exact bound

    sum_(q=3)^infinity 2^(2-q)log G_q
       <=4sqrt(t0) sum_(q=3)^infinity r_geo^q
       =4sqrt(t0) r_geo^3/(1-r_geo)
       <(4/64)(4/5)^3/(1/5)=4/25<1/5.

Also
log(5/4)>1/5, by integrating1/x over[1,5/4]. Therefore the bracket
in(38) is less than log(3/5)+log(5/4)=log(3/4), proving the first
bound in(37).

For the additive coefficient put T_s=G_(s+1)P_(s-1), s>=2, and
r_prod=3/4. Since2^(j-1)>=j forj>=1,

    sum_(j>=1)P_j<=sum_(j>=1)r_prod^j=3.                   (39)

Also

    sum_(s>=2)T_s
       <=3+t0 sum_(s>=2)(5/2)^(s+1)r_prod^(2^(s-2))<4.     (40)

Here is an explicit bound for the last series. Its four terms s=2,3,4,5
are each<256. Write v_s=(5/2)^(s+1)r_prod^(2^(s-2)). For s>=6,

    v_(s+1)/v_s=(5/2)r_prod^(2^(s-2))<1/2,

because r_prod^8<1/9 and hence r_prod^16<1/81. Since v_6<(5/2)^7<1024,
the tail froms=6 is<2048. The whole series is<3072, whose product
witht0 is3/4<1. This proves(40).

Unrolling the additive recurrence in(36) now gives

    E_s=sum_(j=2)^s P_(j-1) product_(t=j+1)^s(1+T_t)
       <=[sum_(j>=1)P_j] exp(sum_(t>=2)T_t)
       <3 exp(4)<243<256.                                 (41)

For completeness exp(1)<3 follows by comparing the exponential series
with a geometric series using j!>=2^(j-1) forj>=1, with strictness for
j>=3.
The scalar estimate controls ALL accumulated forcing; a negative mean
log-gain alone would not have proved(41).
