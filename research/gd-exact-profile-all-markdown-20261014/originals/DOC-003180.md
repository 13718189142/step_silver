# All quadratic primitive tests and all single cuts still miss the capacity scale

Status: complete self-contained candidate, NOT verifier-accepted. No new
solver or fact_submit. Earlier ruler estimates are re-proved here, not used
as unverified premises. This concerns a necessary-test relaxation of
primitive validity; it does NOT prove that the constructed schedules are
primitive for general smooth convex functions.

## Statement

Put c_sc=13/5, a_sc=c_sc^(-17), bsc_j=a_sc c_sc^j, and

    Hsc_0=(),  Hsc_(j+1)=(Hsc_j,bsc_j,Hsc_j),
    ssc_m=sum Hsc_m=a_sc(c_sc^m-2^m)/(c_sc-2).

Consider TWO consecutive copies

    Gsc_m=(Hsc_m,Hsc_m),
    Lsc_m=2(2^m-1),   Dsc_m=1+2ssc_m.                (1)

For any finite nonnegative schedule h of sum S define the intrinsic
primitive energy on an actual smoothness-one GD trajectory by

    V_h=2sum_(i<n)h_i(f_i-f_n)
       +sum_(i<n)h_i(1-h_i)||g_i||^2
       -S(S+1)||g_n||^2.                             (2)

The following assertions hold for every m:

(a) EVERY single-cut primitive test holds strictly for Gsc_m:

    Dsc_m(h_j-1)_+
      <(1+sum_(i<j)h_i)(1+sum_(i>j)h_i)              (3)

    for each actual step j.

(b) V_(Gsc_m)>=0 on EVERY finite-dimensional convex quadratic with a
    minimizer and gradient Lipschitz constant at most1, from every initial
    point. This tests every curvature, and the actual chronological
    primitive energy, not only the final polynomial or curvature1.

(c) With rho_sil=log_2(1+sqrt(2)),

    Dsc_m/(Lsc_m+1)^(rho_sil) -> infinity.             (4)

Consequently the conjunction of ALL quadratic primitive-energy tests and
ALL single-cut primitive tests cannot imply the proposed silver-power
capacity bound, or a constant multiple of that bound. The remaining
simplex/projection geometry is essential to any proof by such testing.
No claim is made here that V_(Gsc_m)>=0 for arbitrary nonlinear functions.

## 1. Meaning and necessity of the cut tests

For a selected step b>1 put A=1+sum before it and B=1+sum after it;
the total capacity is D=A+B+b-1. Projection onto C=[-v,u], with
u>0, v=(b-1)u/B and initial point Au, has gradient u on the entire
prefix, lands at -Bv after the selected step, then has gradient -v
on the entire suffix and ends at -v. Its fixed legal function is

    F_C(x)=max_(z in C)(xz-z^2/2),  F_C'=P_C.

Every sample has f_i=x_i g_i-g_i^2/2. Expanding the squared-distance
telescope in (2) gives

    V_h=x_0^2-(x_n+Sg_n)^2
       =A^2u^2-D^2(b-1)^2u^2/B^2.

Hence primitive validity requires D(b-1)<=AB. For b<=1 its positive-
part version is automatic. This proves necessity of the tests in (3),
including all function values, independently of earlier findings.

## 2. All cuts in the two-copy ruler pass

Abbreviate c=c_sc, a=a_sc, s_j=ssc_j, b=bsc_j. For j>=17,

    s_j/b=[1-(2/c)^j]/(c-2)>=eta,  eta=13/8.          (5)

Indeed (2/c)^3<1/2 and (2/c)^2<4/5 imply (2/c)^17<1/40.
Every step b>1 has j>=18. A step b<=1 satisfies (3) strictly because
the right side is positive.

For a fixed level j<m, the first occurrence of b in Gsc_m is preceded
by exactly Hsc_j, and the last occurrence is followed by exactly Hsc_j.
The two-copy word is palindromic. Thus the prefix sum at any occurrence
lies between s_j and 2s_m-s_j-b. For fixed total step sum, the product
of the two cut masses is concave in the prefix sum, and the two endpoint
values agree. Its minimum is therefore

    (1+s_j)(1+2s_m-s_j-b).

Put s=s_j and L=2s_m. The margin over the cut left side is

    (1+s)(1+L-s-b)-(1+L)(b-1).

Its derivative in L is s+2-b>0 by (5). Since m>=j+1,
L>=2s_(j+1)=4s+2b. At this minimum L the margin is

    3s^2-3sb-2b^2+8s+2b+2.

The quadratic part is increasing for s>=eta b and is at least

    (3eta^2-3eta-2)b^2=(67/64)b^2>0.

Every margin is therefore strictly positive. This proves (a).

## 3. The exact quadratic energy and its chronological moment

Fix scalar curvature lambda in(0,1], initial point1, and
f(x)=lambda x^2/2. For ANY physical schedule h define

    P_i=product_(t<i)(1-lambda h_t),
    Q=|P_n|, tau=lambda sum h_i,
    R=lambda sum_(i<n) h_i P_i^2.

The quantity R includes every actual prefix product; order has not been
discarded. Direct substitution into (2), or the identity
1-Q^2=sum_i(2lambda h_i-lambda^2 h_i^2)P_i^2, gives

    V_h=1-[1+(1+lambda)tau+tau^2]Q^2-(1-lambda)R.     (6)

For the single word Hsc_j write Q_j,R_j,tau_j, and put
z_j=lambda bsc_j. The exact concatenation recurrences are

    Q_(j+1)=|1-z_j| Q_j^2,
    R_(j+1)=R_j+Q_j^2[(1-z_j)^2 R_j+z_j],
    tau_(j+1)=2tau_j+z_j.                             (7)

These formulas include the middle step and both copies in their actual
order. For two copies of Hsc_m the corresponding quantities are

    Q_double=Q_m^2,
    R_double=R_m(1+Q_m^2),
    tau_double=2tau_m.                                (8)

## 4. Any normalized-small-step word has nonnegative quadratic energy

Suppose every z_i=lambda h_i lies in[0,1]. During a chronological
prefix let tau,R,Q have their analogous partial meanings and set

    B_pot=R+(1+tau+tau^2)Q^2.

On adding a step z in[0,1], use R'=R+zQ^2, tau'=tau+z,
and (Q')^2=(1-z)^2Q^2. Exact expansion yields

    B_pot'-B_pot
      =-Q^2[tau^2 z(2-z)+tau z^2(3-2z)+z^3(1-z)]<=0.

Initially B_pot=1, so it remains at most1. Each partial product has
magnitude at least the final product, hence R>=tau Q^2. Equation (6)
can now be written

    V_h=1-B_pot+lambda(R-tau Q^2)>=0.                 (9)

This argument applies to any order and any physical h with lambda h<=1;
it does not require physical h<=1.

## 5. Controlling the moment before and at first activation

Let I be the first level for which t=z_I>1, and put J=I+1. Then
I>=18 and 1<t<=c. For all j>=17, (5) gives tau_j>=eta z_j.

First bound R_I. Put z=t/c in(1/c,1], so z is the central step of
Hsc_I. All normalized steps inside Hsc_(I-1) are at most z/c. If
q=Q_(I-1)^2, the squared-distance telescope there gives

    R_(I-1)<=(1-q)/(2-z/c).

Also q<=exp(-2tau_(I-1))<=exp(-2eta z), since those factors are in[0,1].
Set C=1/(2-z/c). Substituting into (7) and dropping a nonpositive term,

    R_I<=C+C z^2(1-1/c)q
        <=[c/(2c-1)] [1+(1-1/c)/(eta^2 exp(2))]
        <(13/21)[1+512/15379]<2/3.                   (10)

Here z^2 exp(-2eta z)<=1/(eta^2 exp(2)) follows by differentiating,
and exp(2)>7 follows from its power series. The final rational comparison
uses 13*512<15379.

The complete product on Hsc_I satisfies Q_I<=exp(-eta t).
Its first activated extension therefore has

    z_J Q_J<=c t(t-1)exp(-2eta t)
             <=3c/(16eta^3)=1248/10985<k_0:=1/8.     (11)

Use exp(2eta t)>(2eta t)^3/6 and (t-1)/t^2<=1/4 for this bound.
Meanwhile (7) and (10) give

    R_J<2/3+exp(-2eta t)[(2/3)(t-1)^2+t]<35/48.      (12)

To check the second inequality, put B(t)=(2/3)(t-1)^2+t. The polynomial
2B-B'=(4t^2-6t+5)/3 is positive, so B(t)exp(-2eta t) decreases
for t>=1, as 2eta>2. Its value at1 is exp(-13/4)<1/16.
The last bound follows from exp(3)>1+3+9/2+27/6+81/24>16.

## 6. Uniform moment control at every later level

For j>=J put w_j=z_j Q_j. Here z_j>=c>2. Equation (7) implies

    w_(j+1)=c(1-1/z_j)w_j^2<=c w_j^2.

With theta_0=c k_0=13/40, (11) therefore gives

    w_j<=k_0 theta_0^(j-J),
    sum_(j>=J) w_j^2<=k_0^2/(1-theta_0^2)=25/1431.  (13)

The moment recurrence also gives

    R_(j+1)-R_j<=w_j^2(R_j+1/z_j)
                         <=w_j^2(R_j+1/c).

Thus U_j=R_j+1/c obeys U_(j+1)<=(1+w_j^2)U_j. By (12)--(13),

    R_j+1/c
      <[35/48+5/13]/[1-25/1431]
      <3/4+5/13,

so R_j<3/4. We used product(1+w_j^2)<=exp(sum w_j^2)
<=1/[1-sum w_j^2]. The final exact comparison is
695*1431=994545<995448=59*12*1406.

Also tau_j<=z_j/(c-2)=5z_j/3 and Q_j^2<=k_0^2/z_j^2. Consequently

    [1+(1+lambda)tau_j+tau_j^2]Q_j^2
      <=(1/64)[1/c^2+10/(3c)+25/9]<1/8.             (14)

For the last simple inequality, the bracket is less than
1+4/3+3=16/3<8. This bound is uniform in lambda, its activation
phase t, and the horizon.

## 7. Every quadratic passes for the two-copy schedule

If m<=I, every normalized step in Gsc_m is at most1, so (9) applies.
If m>=J, (13)--(14) give Q_m^2<=1/(64c^2)<1/256 and R_m<3/4.
Using (8),

    R_double < (3/4)(1+1/256)=771/1024.

Further,

    [1+(1+lambda)tau_double+tau_double^2]Q_double^2
      <=4[1+(1+lambda)tau_m+tau_m^2]Q_m^4
      <(1/2)Q_m^2<1/512.

Equation (6) therefore yields the explicit positive margin

    V_(Gsc_m)>1-771/1024-1/512=251/1024>0             (15)

for a unit scalar initial point once m>=J. Curvature lambda=0 gives
energy0 and is harmless. Before activation (9) supplies nonnegativity.

For an arbitrary finite-dimensional convex quadratic with a minimizer,
translate that minimizer to zero, subtract the minimum value, and
orthogonally diagonalize its positive semidefinite Hessian. Smoothness
at most1 places every eigenvalue in[0,1]. The GD trajectory and (2)
separate across these eigendirections, with each scalar energy multiplied
by the squared initial coordinate. The proved scalar nonnegativity
therefore proves (b), including singular Hessians and arbitrary starts.

## 8. Capacity comparison and what remains open

From (1),

    Dsc_m ~ [2a_sc/(c_sc-2)] c_sc^m,
    Lsc_m+1=2^(m+1)-1.

Since c_sc=13/5>1+sqrt(2), dividing by
(Lsc_m+1)^(rho_sil) proves (4). Thus this family passes the ENTIRE
quadratic primitive-energy class and every one-cut projection test while
its capacity exceeds any fixed multiple of the proposed silver-power
bound for all sufficiently large m.

This is a concrete failure of that necessary-test closure, not a
counterexample to the primitive-capacity conjecture. No universal
primitive certificate for Gsc_m is supplied. If these schedules fail
primitivity, their failure must use geometry beyond all quadratics and
the displayed single-cut functions. The strict simplex norm game is an
appropriate remaining interface because it retains that full geometry;
an equivalent formulation alone does not settle its capacity bound.
