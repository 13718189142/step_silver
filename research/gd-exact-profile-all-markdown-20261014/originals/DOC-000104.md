## 2. Line 1: a static two-curvature function with genuine repeated returns

### 2.1 Explicit function and complete trajectory

For \(0<\mu\le1\), take the single fixed function on \(\mathbb R\)

\[
 f_\mu(x)=
 \begin{cases}
 x^2/2,&x\ge0,\\
 \mu x^2/2,&x\le0.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{5}
\]

Its derivative is continuous and nondecreasing, with slopes \(1\) and \(\mu\). Thus the function is convex and 1-smooth, with unique minimizer 0. At a positive query the next point is \((1-h)x\); at a negative query it is \((1-\mu h)x\). Every cross, old, future, and terminal interpolation condition is therefore realized by the displayed function. No function, curvature, or minimizer is changed at a checkpoint.

Define the original-radius family value on the entire physical word by

\[
 E_\mu(H_m)=\sup_{x_0\ne0}\frac{2f_\mu(x_{n_m})}{x_0^2}.
\]

The supremum over starts is included. Positive and negative starts need not have the same itinerary.

### 2.2 Small negative-side curvature: comparison with an actual quadratic

Set

\[
 \mu_0=\frac{1-a}{1+a}.
\]

Suppose \(0<\mu\le\mu_0\). Then \(\mu b_0\le1-a<1\). A negative-side query at a level-0 step cannot switch to the positive side. Any switch from negative to positive must occur at a nonzero-level bridge; its next physical step is a level-0 step. A positive start also encounters a level-0 step first. Consequently every positive-side query occurs at level 0, and it returns immediately to the negative side with magnitude multiplier \(a\).

At such a query,

\[
 a\le1-\mu b_0.
\]

At every negative-side query the magnitude multiplier is exactly \(|1-\mu h_t|\). Comparing the multipliers at every physical time gives

\[
 |x_{n_m}|\le |x_0|\,|p_m(\mu)|.
\]

The last step has level 0, so the terminal point is nonpositive; a zero reached earlier stays zero. Hence

\[
 \boxed{E_\mu(H_m)\le\mu p_m(\mu)^2
 \quad(0<\mu\le\mu_0).}
 \tag{6}
\]

This compares the returning nonlinear trajectory with the actual quadratic \(x\mapsto\mu x^2/2\) on the same word and original radius. It is not a comparison of unconstrained Gram data.

### 2.3 Larger negative-side curvature: all multipliers are paid

Suppose \(\mu_0\le\mu\le1\). Since \(d_j\ge a\), the decreasing function \((1-d)/(1+d)\) gives

\[
 1-\mu b_j\le d_j,
 \qquad \mu b_j-1\le b_j-1=d_j.
\]

Thus \(|1-\mu b_j|\le d_j\) on the negative side. On the positive side the magnitude multiplier is exactly \(d_j\). Every trajectory, irrespective of its sign switches, satisfies

\[
 |x_{n_m}|\le |x_0| P_m,
 \qquad
 P_m=\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}d_j^{2^{m-j-1}}
     =\beta^{2^m-1}c^{-m}.
\]

The last identity also follows from \(P_0=1\), \(P_{j+1}=d_jP_j^2\). Because \(2f_\mu(x)\le x^2\),

\[
 \boxed{E_\mu(H_m)\le P_m^2\le c^{-2m}
 \quad(\mu_0\le\mu\le1).}
 \tag{7}
\]

Here \(P_m^2=p_m(1)^2\) is the actual curvature-1 quadratic payoff. Combining (6) and (7) therefore gives the comparison proved in this line:

\[
 \sup_{0<\mu\le1}E_\mu(H_m)
 \le\sup_{0\le\lambda\le1}\lambda p_m(\lambda)^2.
 \tag{8}
\]

Using the auxiliary draft (3) yields

\[
 \boxed{\sup_{0<\mu\le1}E_\mu(H_m)\le\frac9{16D_m}.}
 \tag{9}
\]

Conditional on the reported accepted comparison (4), this is less than \((423/800)/B_{n_m}\). The standard noncrossing Huber witness remains larger, at \(1/D_m\).

### 2.4 The returning geometry is actually exercised

For a concrete member valid at every \(m\ge4\), take \(\mu=1/2\) and any \(x_0>0\). Level-0 and level-1 negative-side multipliers are positive because
\(b_0<2\), \(b_1=1+\beta<2\). Every level-\(j\) bridge with \(j\ge2\) has \(b_j>2\), so it switches the negative state to the positive side. The following level-0 step switches it back.

There are exactly \(2^{m-2}-1\) such negative-to-positive switches, each followed by a positive-to-negative return, in addition to the initial positive-to-negative step. None of their factors is zero. Thus this is an actual nonlinear, repeatedly returning history on the complete infinite-depth family. Its failure to provide the required scale follows from the original-radius bound (7), rather than from a failure of a proposed interpolation certificate.

This result covers the explicit family (5), including reflection of the coordinate axis, and arbitrary starts. It does not cover all one-dimensional smooth convex functions, all pairs of curvatures strictly below 1, or the full smooth-convex class.

## 3. Line 2: multiple Huber returns and the complete affine-terminal sector

### 3.1 One static function and an exact reachable interval

Use

\[
 F(x)=\max_{g\ge-1}(xg-g^2/2)
 =\begin{cases}
 x^2/2,&x\ge-1,\\
 -x-1/2,&x\le-1.
 \end{cases}
 \qquad \nabla F(x)=\max(x,-1).
 \tag{10}
\]

This is convex and 1-smooth, with unique minimizer 0. Its actual GD map is

\[
 T_h(x)=
 \begin{cases}(1-h)x,&x\ge-1,\\x+h,&x\le-1.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{11}
\]

Writing \(u=x+1\) gives \(u'=u+h-h\max(u,0)\). It allows repeated passages between the affine and quadratic pieces. The envelope in (10) is unbounded in gradient space, but this is not an admissibility issue for the original function class. Moreover, any finite trajectory is exactly reproduced by a compact interval hull \([-1,M]\) once \(M\) exceeds all visited gradients. The cap is chosen once before running that trajectory. The same statement holds simultaneously over a compact initial interval and a fixed finite word, because all its finite-time images are compact.

The parent supplied the following exact interval observation during this line. Its verification is included here. Start with every \(x_0\in[-R,R]\), \(R>0\). Every map is continuous and fixes 0, so the image at each time is a compact interval \([-A_t,B_t]\) containing 0. For \(h_t>1\), \(\delta_t=h_t-1\), its exact endpoints obey

\[
 A_{t+1}=\max\{A_t-h_t,\delta_tB_t\},\qquad
 B_{t+1}=\delta_t\min\{A_t,1\},
 \qquad A_0=B_0=R.
 \tag{12}
\]

Indeed the minimum of (11) on the interval is the smaller of the negative outer endpoint and \(-\delta_tB_t\). Its maximum is at \(-1\) if that point is present, and otherwise at \(-A_t\). Connectedness gives every point between those endpoints, so no independence or reachability correlation has been discarded.

Put

\[
 J(A)=\begin{cases}A^2,&0\le A\le1,\\2A-1,&A\ge1.\end{cases}
\]

Convexity of \(F\) implies that its maximum on the final interval is at an endpoint. The exact worst value for this fixed function is

\[
 E_F(H_m):=\sup_{x_0\ne0}\frac{2F(x_{n_m})}{x_0^2}
 =\sup_{R>0}\frac{\max\{J(A_{n_m}(R)),B_{n_m}(R)^2\}}{R^2}.
 \tag{13}
\]

For one direction, choose \(R=|x_0|\). For the other, an endpoint is attained by an actual start with \(|x_0|\le R\), and using its original radius only increases the ratio. If its endpoint value is positive, that start is nonzero. Formula (13) therefore does not replace the initial radius by a favorable new one.

Equations (12)--(13) alone are not a closure result. The quantitative part of this line is the full-word bound below, which handles every number and placement of returns in its stated sector.

### 3.2 Every returned excursion pays its physical suffix

Abbreviate \(n=n_m\), \(S=S_m\), \(b_0=1+a\). Unrolling the first equation in (12) gives the exact identity

\[
 A_n=\max\left\{R-S,
 \max_{0\le t<n}\left[
 \delta_tB_t-\sum_{u=t+1}^{n-1}h_u\right]\right\}.
 \tag{14}
\]

For \(t\ge1\), the second equation in (12) gives \(B_t\le\delta_{t-1}\). The adjacent-level structure of the complete word now pays every possible reflected contribution in (14).

First, for every \(j\ge2\),

\[
 S_j-b_0\ge a d_j.
 \tag{15}
\]

At \(j=2\), the left side is \(b_0+b_1=2+a+\beta\), while \(a d_2=\beta^2<1\). For the induction, let \(Q_j=S_j-b_0-a d_j\). Using \(a c=\beta\) gives

\[
 Q_{j+1}=2Q_j+b_0+1+(2a+1-\beta)d_j>0
 \quad\hbox{if }Q_j\ge0.
\]

If the current step has level \(j\ge1\), its preceding step has level 0. Thus \(\delta_tB_t\le a d_j\), and the physical suffix after this bridge contains a whole \(H_j\), of mass \(S_j\). The associated contribution in (14) is negative: for \(j=1\), \(a d_1=a\beta<b_0=S_1\); for \(j\ge2\), use (15).

If the current step has level 0 and \(t\ge1\), its preceding step is a level-\(j\) bridge for some \(j\ge1\). Again \(\delta_tB_t\le a d_j\). The remaining physical suffix contains that bridge's right child \(H_j\), except its first level-0 step, so its mass is at least \(S_j-b_0\). Equation (15) makes the contribution nonpositive when \(j\ge2\). When \(j=1\), it is at most \(a\beta\). These statements include occurrences inside every subtree; no restriction to a selected spine is made.

Finally, at \(t=0\), the term in (14) is \(aR-(S-b_0)\). Thus

\[
 \boxed{A_n(R)\le
 \max\{R-S,\ aR-(S-b_0),\ a\beta\},
 \qquad B_n(R)\le a.}
 \tag{16}
\]

This is a whole-history bound on actual interval images. It includes interior quadratic queries and arbitrary repetitions of earlier directions, without resetting the point, gradient hull, or radius.

### 3.3 Exact original-radius payoff for every affine-terminal trajectory

Let \(V=S+1\). For \(m\ge2\), \(V>4\), \(a<1/2\), and \(b_0<3/2\), so

\[
 (1-a)V>b_0.
\]

When \(R<V\), all three terms in the maximum in (16) are less than 1: the second one is less than
\(aV-S+b_0=1-[(1-a)V-b_0]\). When \(R\ge V\), the first term dominates the second and is at least 1, hence also dominates \(a\beta\). Starting at \(x_0=-R\) in this range stays on the affine ray throughout the whole word and ends at \(-R+S\). This realizes the bound. Consequently

\[
 \boxed{A_n(R)\ge1\ \Longleftrightarrow\ R\ge S+1,
 \qquad A_n(R)=R-S\quad(R\ge S+1).}
 \tag{17}
\]

Every actual trajectory with terminal point \(x_n\le-1\), regardless of prior returns, therefore satisfies, with its original \(R=|x_0|\),

\[
 \frac{2F(x_n)}{R^2}
 \le\frac{2(R-S)-1}{R^2}
 =\frac{2R-D_m}{R^2}
 \le\frac1{D_m}.
 \tag{18}
\]

The last inequality is exactly \((R-D_m)^2\ge0\). Equality is attained by the standard noncrossing start \(x_0=-D_m\). Thus the entire affine-terminal sector, including all possible returning itineraries, has exact best value \(1/D_m\). This is a bound within the fixed one-sided family, not an upper bound for \(W(H_m)\).

More generally, every radius \(R\ge\sqrt{D_m}\) in (13) gives at most \(1/D_m\). Between \(\sqrt{D_m}\) and \(V\), (17) and \(B_n\le a\) make the terminal numerator at most 1. Above \(V\), (17)--(18) apply, and the positive endpoint has still smaller value.

### 3.4 The small-radius quadratic region and the decisive unresolved inequality

Every prefix of \(H_j\) has scalar-quadratic multiplier of absolute value at most 1 at curvature 1. To prove this, use induction on \(j\). A prefix in the first child has the induction bound. The complete first child has magnitude \(P_j\), and multiplying by its bridge gives

\[
 P_jd_j=a\beta^{2^j-1}\le a<1.
\]

Multiplication by any prefix of the second child preserves this upper bound. The empty prefix is 1. Therefore, when \(0<R\le1\), every trajectory starting in \([-R,R]\) stays in the quadratic part of (10). In that region

\[
 A_n(R)=B_n(R)=P_mR,
 \qquad E_R=P_m^2<1/D_m\quad(m\ge4).
 \tag{19}
\]

The strict inequality uses only (2): \(D_mP_m^2\le D_mc^{-2m}<2c^{-m}\le2(5/12)^4<1\).

The remaining sector is precisely

\[
 1<R<\sqrt{D_m},\qquad -1<x_n<1.
\]

In particular, with (12) used for the complete inherited-parameter word, define

\[
 K_m=\sup_{1<R<\sqrt{D_m}}
 \frac{\max\{A_{n_m}(R)^2,B_{n_m}(R)^2\}}{R^2}.
\]

The preceding bounds give the exact decomposition for this fixed function:

\[
 E_F(H_m)=\max\{1/D_m,K_m\}\qquad(m\ge4).
 \tag{20}
\]

No estimate reaching the required scale for \(K_m\) was proved. A successful witness in this particular family would first have to establish

\[
 \liminf_{m\to\infty}B_{n_m}K_m\ge1,
 \tag{21}
\]

or a comparably strong bound after combining with another actual nonlinear family. In view of (4), reaching the original scale through this family requires an improvement over \(1/D_m\) in the unresolved quadratic-terminal sector; affine-terminal returns cannot supply it. Even (21) would only pass the inherited-family viability test. It would not prove the uniform arbitrary-word assertion (1).

The recurrence (12) and the restatement (21) are not claimed as progress toward a solved minimax theorem. The new quantitative information is (16)--(19), which closes the large-radius and affine-terminal parts of this actual returning family. The central intermediate-radius inequality remains open. No scalar cap, restart, or purported routine selection lemma is substituted for it.
