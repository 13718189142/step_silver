## 1. The unchanged target and the full certificate hypothesis

For a finite nonnegative schedule h=(h_0,...,h_(n-1)), W_n(h) is the
supremum of 2(f(x_n)-f(x_star))/(L||x_0-x_star||^2) over every finite
dimension, convex differentiable f with L-Lipschitz gradient and a minimizer
x_star, and x_0 != x_star, using x_(i+1)=x_i-(h_i/L) grad f(x_i).
The exact benchmark is U_n^ZJ=1/B_n, B_n=2S_bullet(n)+1, with the original
phi/psi recursions in accepted fact `b98bb807d514e80d`. The affirmative
target remains: for every epsilon>0, all sufficiently large n and EVERY
nonnegative length-n schedule have W_n(h)>=(1-epsilon)/B_n. A negative
answer requires a fixed positive relative improvement along unbounded
horizons by actual full-objective upper bounds. Neither outcome is claimed.

Work at smoothness one and let x_0,g_0,...,g_n be independent formal vectors.
Set x_i=x_0-sum_(t<i)h_t g_t and x_star=g_star=f_star=0. All finite f_i
are independent formal scalars. Put I={star,0,...,n}, lambda_ii=0, and

 Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2.

A supplied full certificate consists of a finite real q_cert and ACTUAL
lambda_ij>=0 for which

 M=q_cert||x_0||^2-2f_n+sum_(i!=j)lambda_ij Q_ij                 (1)

has every value coefficient zero and is PSD on the full original formal
space. In particular the finite-node flows obey

 out_i-in_i=2*1_(i=n).                                         (2)

No optimum-source edge is forbidden. No support, symmetrizability,
positive-definiteness, h_i<2, or Huber equality assumption is made.
Zero-step gradients remain independent.

Necessity of Q_ij<=0 on legal instances (accepted1252b8823bb5a290) gives
W_n(h)<=q_cert for any such certificate. Conversely, that SAME accepted
fact supplies a full certificate at q_cert=W_n(h) for every nonnegative
h, including empty/all-zero schedules, by positive-step attainment and
explicit zero-step lifting. Thus any universal necessary inequality proved
below can be applied to at least one attained certificate at the actual W.
An inequality involving the multipliers of a particular supplied certificate
is not by itself a multiplier-free lower bound on W.

Every feasible q_cert is positive. Setting all gradients to zero first
gives q_cert>=0. Summing (2) yields
sum_i lambda_(i,star)=2+sum_i lambda_(star,i)>=2. If q_cert=0, set all
finite gradients to the same scalar1. The coefficient of scalar x_0 in
M is then -sum_i lambda_(i,star)<0, so M is unbounded below. This excludes0.

## 2. A nontrivial queried-cut capacity inequality

Fix a queried index j<n, write b_cut=h_j, and let T_cut={j+1,...,n}.
Its complement in I includes the optimum and indices0,...,j. Define

 s_tail=sum_(j<t<n)h_t,
 ell_tail(k)=sum_(j<t<k)h_t for k in T_cut,
 C_in=sum_(i outside T_cut,k in T_cut)lambda_ik,
 C_out=sum_(i in T_cut,k outside T_cut)lambda_ik,
 F_cut=sum_(k in T_cut)lambda_(j,k),
 B_cut=sum_(k in T_cut)lambda_(k,j),
 I_cut=sum_(i!=j)lambda_(i,j),
 J_forward=sum_(k in T_cut)ell_tail(k) lambda_(j,k),
 J_in=sum_(i outside T_cut,k in T_cut)ell_tail(k) lambda_ik,
 mu_query=lambda_(j,star),
 mu_tail=sum_(i in T_cut)lambda_(i,star).

These are actual array sums, including every remote edge and optimum edge.
Summing (2) over T_cut gives C_out-C_in=2. Set

 A_cut=I_cut-b_cut F_cut,
 D_cut=1+2s_tail+C_in+J_in,
 K_cut=b_cut C_out-F_cut-B_cut-J_forward.                       (3)

The following matrix is necessarily PSD:

              [ q_cert       -mu_query/2       -mu_tail/2 ]
 T_query   =  [ -mu_query/2    A_cut             K_cut/2   ].    (4)
              [ -mu_tail/2    K_cut/2            D_cut     ]

Proof. Evaluate (1) on the formal subspace

 x_0=z,  g_i=0 (i<j),  g_j=u,  g_i=v (i>j).

All value coefficients have already canceled, so they may be set to0.
Only finite-source edges to star contribute z; their total is
-<z,mu_query*u+mu_tail*v>. The coefficient of ||u||^2 in the norm
terms is (out_j+in_j)/2=I_cut, since j<n. The only additional diagonal
terms are -h_j lambda_(j,k)||u||^2 for k>j. This gives A_cut.

For the ||v||^2 coefficient, set u=z=0. A tail point k then has
x_k=-ell_tail(k)v, and a non-tail finite point has position0. Within-tail
position terms sum to

 sum_(k in T_cut)ell_tail(k)(out_k^internal-in_k^internal).

Use (2) to replace the internal divergence by
2*1_(k=n)-out_k^cross+in_k^cross. The outgoing cross position terms
add back sum_k ell_tail(k)out_k^cross. This leaves
2s_tail+J_in. The cross norm terms are
(C_out+C_in)/2=1+C_in; internal norm terms are zero. Hence D_cut.

For the coefficient of <u,v>, norm terms give -(F_cut+B_cut).
An edge j->k with k in T_cut contributes -ell_tail(k)<u,v>, totaling
-J_forward. An edge sourced in T_cut and targeting its complement
has the position shift +b_cut*u, totaling b_cut C_out<u,v>.
All other position terms have either zero source gradient or cancel
the common tail shift. These are exactly K_cut. Thus the restriction is

 q_cert||z||^2-<z,mu_query*u+mu_tail*v>
                   +A_cut||u||^2+K_cut<u,v>+D_cut||v||^2,

whose scalar coefficient matrix is (4). Since the original polynomial
is PSD on every formal vector, its restriction is PSD. This restriction
is a necessary test, not an assertion that its formal vectors are legal
trajectories, and it deletes no edge from the original certificate.

Complete the independent z square, using q_cert>0. Put

 a_cut=A_cut-mu_query^2/(4q_cert),
 d_cut=D_cut-mu_tail^2/(4q_cert),
 k_cut=K_cut-mu_query*mu_tail/(2q_cert).

Then (4) is equivalent to

 a_cut>=0, d_cut>=0, k_cut^2<=4a_cut*d_cut.                     (5)

In particular, when d_cut>0 the quantitative incidence cost is

 I_cut >= b_cut F_cut + mu_query^2/(4q_cert)
              + [b_cut C_out-F_cut-B_cut-J_forward
                       -mu_query*mu_tail/(2q_cert)]^2/(4d_cut). (6)

When d_cut=0 the square numerator in (6) must be zero and the remaining
condition is I_cut>=b_cut F_cut+mu_query^2/(4q_cert). There is no division
by a vanished residual and no strict-PSD assumption. Equivalently,

 |b_cut C_out-F_cut-B_cut-J_forward
                     -mu_query*mu_tail/(2q_cert)|
       <=2 sqrt((I_cut-b_cut F_cut-mu_query^2/(4q_cert))
                 *(1+2s_tail+C_in+J_in-mu_tail^2/(4q_cert))).   (7)

If every edge incident to j vanishes, I_cut=F_cut=B_cut=J_forward=
mu_query=0. Then (5) forces b_cut C_out=0. Since C_out>=2, b_cut=0.
This recovers the qualitative incidence obstruction, now as the zero-cost
case of an exact quantitative condition at every finite certificate level.
If b_cut=0 there is no contradiction; the zero-step case is included.

For an alternative exact q cost define

 Z_cut=[ A_cut K_cut/2 ; K_cut/2 D_cut ],
 m_cut=(mu_query,mu_tail)^T.

Condition (4) is equivalent to Z_cut>=0, m_cut in Range(Z_cut), and

 q_cert >= (1/4) m_cut^T Z_cut^+ m_cut.                         (8)

Here Z_cut^+ inverts its positive eigenvalues and vanishes on its kernel.
For proof, diagonalize the real symmetric Z_cut. A component of m_cut
on a zero eigenvector would give an unbounded linear polynomial in that
free direction. Once these components vanish, complete one square for
each positive eigenvalue. This proves necessity and sufficiency, including
all singular cases. If 4A_cut D_cut-K_cut^2>0, the right side is explicitly

 (D_cut*mu_query^2-K_cut*mu_query*mu_tail+A_cut*mu_tail^2)
                         /(4A_cut D_cut-K_cut^2).             (9)

## 3. Why total cut flow cannot replace its position moment

Here is ONE exact supplied certificate. Take n=2, h=(1,1), q_cert=1/5,
with the following nonzero multipliers and every other multiplier zero:

 lambda_02=1, lambda_10=1, lambda_21=2,
 lambda_(1,star)=1, lambda_(2,star)=1.                          (10)

All coefficients are nonnegative. The finite flows are respectively0,0,2.
With x_1=x_0-g_0 and x_2=x_0-g_0-g_1, direct expansion of (1) gives

 M=(1/5)||x_0-(5/2)(g_1+g_2)||^2
                                      +(3/4)||g_1-g_2||^2.   (11)

For an explicit coefficient check, the sum of the position terms in (10)
is -<x_0,g_1+g_2>-||g_0||^2+<g_0,g_1>+<g_0,g_2>
+3<g_1,g_2>. The norm terms add ||g_0||^2+2||g_1||^2+2||g_2||^2
-<g_0,g_1>-<g_0,g_2>-2<g_1,g_2>. Their sum is exactly the expansion
of (11) after adding q_cert||x_0||^2. This is full PSD with the
original independent g_0 present as a kernel direction.

For j=0, T_cut={1,2}, the exact data are

 b_cut=1, s_tail=1, C_in=1, C_out=3,
 F_cut=B_cut=I_cut=1, J_forward=J_in=1,
 mu_query=0, mu_tail=2,
 A_cut=0, D_cut=5, K_cut=0.

Thus (4) is the singular rank-one square (z-5v)^2/5, and (8) gives
the exact cost1/5. Dropping the target-position moment J_in would assert

 mu_tail^2 <= 4q_cert(1+2s_tail+C_in),                          (12)

which here reads4<=16/5, false. Equivalently, that proposed scalar cost
would falsely require q_cert>=1/4. Dropping J_forward from K_cut likewise
produces K_cut=1 while A_cut=0, falsely rejecting the same full certificate.
Therefore unweighted cross-cut flow cannot be substituted in these exact
capacity tests. The actual remote edge0->2, whose target lies one unit
of step mass into the tail, supplies the missing moment. This refutes
(12), not every conceivable scalar invariant or an existential re-selection.


## 7. The correct mass-only relaxation, with its explicit loss

One CAN remove the actual first moments at a single cut if their loss is
paid explicitly. Nonnegative h and lambda imply

 0<=J_forward<=s_tail F_cut,
 0<=J_in-J_forward<=s_tail(C_in-F_cut).

In particular the valid scalar tail bound is

 q_cert>=mu_tail^2/[4(1+2s_tail+(1+s_tail)C_in)].               (24)

Proof: in(4), the z,v principal minor gives mu_tail^2<=4q_cert D_cut,
and D_cut<=1+2s_tail+(1+s_tail)C_in. The supplied certificate(10) has
equality in(24). Thus(24), which pays the full possible elapsed mass,
survives that example whereas the unweighted replacement(12) does not.

There is also a closed elimination of the TWO moment bounds from the
coupled query test. Write a=a_cut from(5) only within this paragraph, and
define the scalar data

 R_mom=b_cut C_out-F_cut-B_cut-mu_query*mu_tail/(2q_cert),
 E_mom=1+2s_tail+C_in+s_tail(C_in-F_cut)-mu_tail^2/(4q_cert),
 U_mom=s_tail F_cut.

Every true certificate necessarily satisfies

 a>=0, E_mom+R_mom+a>=0,
 dist(R_mom+2a,[0,U_mom])^2<=4a(E_mom+R_mom+a).              (25)

Here dist denotes the ordinary distance of a real number from the stated
closed interval. For proof put t=J_forward. Then0<=t<=U_mom and
d_cut<=E_mom+t. Equation(5) gives

 (R_mom-t)^2<=4a(E_mom+t), E_mom+t>=0.

Completing the scalar square gives
(t-(R_mom+2a))^2<=4a(E_mom+R_mom+a). For a>0, this is possible
for t in the interval exactly when the last two conditions in(25) hold;
the square inequality itself forces E_mom+t>=0. For a=0 the same
conditions require t=R_mom in[0,U_mom] and E_mom+R_mom>=0, which is
the correct zero-residual rule. This proves(25) including degeneracy.

Conversely(25) is sufficient for the ABSTRACT moment relaxation of(5):
choose such a t and set J_forward=t, J_in=t+s_tail(C_in-F_cut).
These values satisfy the two displayed interval bounds and yield(5).
No claim is made that those abstract moments arise from actual target
indices or a common array across several cuts. It is precisely that
cross-cut realizability/correlation information that(25) relaxes.
The scalar relaxation is therefore a genuine necessary inequality,
not a constant-preserving F/G separator or an exact iterative closure.
