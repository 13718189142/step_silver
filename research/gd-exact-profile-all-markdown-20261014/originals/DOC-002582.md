# General polynomial Gram data and the failure of spectral moments

Status: exploratory algebraic formulation, not a verified fact and not a target proof. Normalize L=1, x_*=g_*=0 and ||x_0||=1.

## Lossless formal-polynomial representation

First remove zero steps from a nonnegative schedule: they repeat the same point, value and gradient and do not change the final iterate. Let m be the number of remaining positive steps. Write them as h_0,...,h_{m-1}; define

    P_0(z)=1, P_i(z)=product_{t<i}(1-h_t z), 1<=i<=m.

Let V=R[z]_{<=m+1}. The list

    1, z P_0(z), z P_1(z), ..., z P_m(z)

is a basis of V: its degrees are 0,1,...,m+1 and its leading coefficients are nonzero because each retained h_t is positive.

For any actual legal GD transcript, the unique linear map T from V into its ambient Euclidean space satisfying

    T(1)=x_0, T(z P_i)=g_i

also satisfies T(P_i)=x_i. Indeed the polynomial telescoping identity is

    P_i=1-sum_{t<i} h_t z P_t,

and its image under T is precisely the GD recursion. Thus the PSD bilinear form

    K(U,V)=<T(U),T(V)>

contains all transcript Gram data, with K(1,1)=1.

Conversely, for any PSD symmetric bilinear form K on V, its quotient by the null space is a Euclidean space of dimension at most m+2. Denote polynomial equivalence classes by brackets and take x_i=[P_i], g_i=[z P_i]. The same polynomial identity gives the exact GD recursion. The optimum sample is x_*=g_*=0. Define

    Q_ij=K(z P_j,P_i-P_j)
          +(1/2)K(z P_i-z P_j,z P_i-z P_j),

with P_*=0 and z P_*=0. These samples admit convex 1-smooth interpolating values if and only if every directed cycle on {*,0,...,m} has nonpositive total Q weight, with Q_ij attached to j->i.

The sufficiency can be seen explicitly. Let ell_i be the maximum Q weight of a path i->*, with the empty path allowed at *. Every such maximum is over finitely many simple paths, since all cycles are nonpositive. Put F_i=-ell_i. Then F_i-F_j>=Q_ij, and these are the smooth-convex interpolation inequalities. The conjugate of

    H(g)=max_i { <g_i,x_i>-F_i+<x_i,g-g_i>+(1/2)||g-g_i||^2 }

is a globally finite convex 1-smooth interpolant. Its gradient is zero at the optimum sample. All candidate function values are at most F_i, by summing their inequalities along paths to *.

Hence the original W for this fixed schedule equals

    2 sup_K (-ell_m),

where K ranges over the PSD forms with K(1,1)=1 and all the displayed cycle inequalities. Zero steps can now be restored exactly, without a density argument or any restriction on real steps. This is an algebraic rewriting of the full interpolation problem, not a simplification of its minimax strength.

The matrix of K in the monomial basis has entries M_rs=K(z^r,z^s), 0<=r,s<=m+1. These form a two-index PSD array. There is no requirement that they depend only on r+s.

## A legal Huber transcript already needs non-Hankel entries

Take one step h_0=1 and the usual Huber function with threshold 1/3:

    f(x)=x^2/2                           for |x|<=1/3,
         |x|/3-1/18                     for |x|>=1/3.

From x_0=1, the transcript is x_1=2/3 and g_0=g_1=1/3. The function is convex, differentiable and 1-smooth, with minimizer 0. Its terminal value is f(x_1)=1/6, so normalized error is 1/3.

The representing map satisfies T(1)=1, T(z)=1/3 and
T(z-z^2)=1/3; hence T(z^2)=0. In the basis (1,z,z^2),

    M=[[1,1/3,0],[1/3,1/9,0],[0,0,0]].

It is a rank-one PSD matrix. It is not Hankel:
M_02=0 while M_11=1/9.

For any scalar spectral measure mu on R, a form of the type
K(U,V)=integral U(lambda)V(lambda) dmu(lambda) would instead have
M_02=integral lambda^2 dmu=M_11. Thus even this ordinary one-dimensional legal Huber transcript cannot be represented by scalar quadratic spectral moments.

There is a further issue beyond self-adjointness. Multiplication by z does not descend to the Euclidean quotient for this example. The polynomial z-1/3 is null because T(z-1/3)=0, but

    T(z(z-1/3))=T(z^2-z/3)=-1/9,

which is not zero. Thus the null space of K is not stable under multiplication by z. No single fixed linear operator, normal or nonnormal, represents this exact transcript on its one-dimensional realized space.

A scalar moment or fixed-matrix ansatz can therefore silently exclude legal smooth-convex behavior in two different ways: imposing Hankel symmetry, and requiring shift-invariance of null relations. The general bilinear polynomial form avoids both restrictions, at the price of retaining the full interpolation problem.

## What remains unresolved

The representation identifies the extra algebraic data exactly, but no lower-bound selection of K has been proved that matches the exact S_bullet profile uniformly over schedules. Choosing K by optimizing the full displayed interpolation problem is equivalent to the original fixed-schedule performance problem, so it is not an independent mechanism for the target comparison.
