## 2. One global function and its full minimum set

Let e=(1,0), K={g: ||g-e||<=1}, and

\[
 F(x)=\max_{g\in K}\{\langle x,g\rangle-\tfrac12\|g\|^2\}.
\]

Completing the square gives

\[
 F(x)=\tfrac12\|x\|^2-\tfrac12\operatorname{dist}(x,K)^2,
 \qquad g(x)=P_Kx.
\]

The maximum is attained uniquely because K is compact and the objective is strictly concave in g. A maximum of affine functions of x is convex. To check differentiability directly, let p=P_Kx and p_z=P_K(x+z). Comparing each maximizer against the other gives

\[
 \langle p,z\rangle\le F(x+z)-F(x)
 \le\langle p_z,z\rangle.
\]

Projection optimality gives <x-p,q-p><=0 for q in K. Using this for two projections and adding yields

\[
 \|P_Kx-P_Ky\|^2\le
 \langle P_Kx-P_Ky,x-y\rangle.
\]

Thus P_K is 1-Lipschitz. The preceding difference inequalities show F is differentiable with gradient P_K and that its gradient is globally 1-Lipschitz. In particular, the piecewise formula used below defines one admissible function on all of R^2.

Since 0 belongs to K, F>=0 and F(0)=0. Moreover P_Kx=0 exactly when <x,g><=0 for every g in K. The support function of K is x_1+||x||. Therefore

\[
 \arg\min F=\{(z,0):z\le0\},\qquad F_\star=0.
\]

This is the complete minimum set. The closest minimizer to (x_1,x_2) is (x_1,0) when x_1<=0, and is 0 when x_1>=0. A different minimizer only increases the denominator for this fixed start, so it cannot improve this particular function's normalized payoff.

For r=||x-e|| the exact global formula is

\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 r\le1:& g=x,\quad F(x)=\|x\|^2/2,\\[2mm]
 r\ge1:& g=e+(x-e)/r,\quad F(x)=r+x_1-1.
 \end{array}
\]

The formulas agree on the boundary. They follow either from the projection formula for a ball or directly from the completed square above.

## 3. Exact left-side dynamics and the original radius

Write

\[
 x=(1-u,v),\quad u\ge1,\quad
 r=\sqrt{u^2+v^2},\quad \delta=r-u,\quad q=r+u.
\]

This point is outside the open disk; it is strictly outside when v is nonzero. The preceding global formula gives

\[
 F(x)=\delta,\qquad
 g(x)=(\delta/r,v/r),\qquad v^2=\delta q.
\]

Every gradient of F has a nonnegative first component, because K is contained in {g_1>=0}. Thus every nonnegative physical step preserves u>=u_0>=1. Substituting the gradient, without changing h, gives

\[
 u'=u+h\delta/r,\qquad v'=(1-h/r)v.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

Expanding the two squares gives

\[
 r'^2-r^2
 =2h\left(\frac{u\delta-v^2}{r}\right)
   +h^2\frac{\delta^2+v^2}{r^2}
 =2h\delta(h/r-1),
 \tag{3.2}
\]

where u delta-v^2=-r delta and delta^2+v^2=2r delta.

The claimed monotonicity of q needs a separate check. Set A=r-h delta/r. Then

\[
 r'^2-A^2=\frac{h^2\delta q}{r^2}
             =\frac{h^2v^2}{r^2}\ge0.
\]

If A>=0, this implies r'>=A; if A<0, that inequality follows from r'>=0. Hence

\[
 q'-q=r'-r+h\delta/r\ge0.
 \tag{3.3}
\]

It is strict if h>0 and v is nonzero. In contrast, r need not be monotone: (3.2) gives its direction of change exactly. The varying curvature is therefore coupled to this same trajectory.

For v_0 nonzero the closest original minimizer is

\[
 x_\star=(1-u_0,0),\qquad R_0^2=\|x_0-x_\star\|^2=v_0^2.
\]

Using (3.1) through the true final time N gives the exact normalized payoff

\[
 E(h;u_0,v_0):=\frac{2F(x_N)}{v_0^2}
 =\frac{2}{q_N}\prod_{t=0}^{N-1}(1-h_t/r_t)^2.
 \tag{3.4}
\]

If a factor vanishes, the trajectory reaches the minimum ray and remains there, and (3.4) still gives zero. No post-step radius is used as the denominator.

Two further identities make the original-radius accounting explicit:

\[
 \|x_t-x_\star\|^2
 =(u_t-u_0)^2+v_t^2
 =(r_t-u_0)^2+2u_0\delta_t,
 \tag{3.5}
\]
\[
 \langle g_t,x_t-x_\star\rangle
 =\delta_t+\frac{u_0}{2}\|g_t\|^2,
 \qquad \|g_t\|^2=2\delta_t/r_t.
 \tag{3.6}
\]

For (3.6), its numerator is (u_0-u_t)delta_t+v_t^2=delta_t(u_0+r_t). Thus the horizontal drift remains a real distance cost relative to the originally selected minimizer.

For completeness, the global normal geometry also follows directly. Put s_t=(-u_t,v_t)/r_t. Then g_t=e+s_t, x_t-g_t=(r_t-1)s_t, and ||s_t||=1. For two trajectory samples,

\[
 \langle x_i-g_i,g_i-g_j\rangle
 =(r_i-1)(1-\langle s_i,s_j\rangle)
 =\tfrac12(r_i-1)\|g_i-g_j\|^2\ge0.
 \tag{3.7}
\]

This checks all disk supporting normals at once; no interpolation point is freely activated. The same formula includes the original minimizer as g_star=0, r_star=u_0, s_star=-e. On the exterior, differentiation gives the Hessian (I-s s^T)/r, whose nonzero tangential eigenvalue is 1/r.

## 4. A complete-word ceiling when q_0>=2 max h

**Draft sector theorem.** Let h be any finite nonnegative word with S=sum h>0 and b=max h. For the fixed disk and a left-side start with u_0>=1, v_0 nonzero, and q_0>=2b,

\[
 E(h;u_0,v_0)\le\frac1{eS}.
 \tag{4.1}
\]

Indeed r_t=(q_t+delta_t)/2>=q_t/2>=q_0/2>=b. Therefore every multiplier 1-h_t/r_t lies in [0,1]. Equation (3.2) also yields r_(t+1)<=r_t, so r_t<=r_0 throughout the complete word. By 1-z<=exp(-z) for z>=0,

\[
 \prod_t(1-h_t/r_t)^2
 \le\exp(-2\sum_t h_t/r_t)
 \le\exp(-2S/r_0).
\]

Equations (3.3)--(3.4) and q_0>=r_0 now give

\[
 E\le\frac2{q_0}e^{-2S/r_0}
 \le\frac2{r_0}e^{-2S/r_0}
 =\frac1S z e^{-z}\le\frac1{eS},
 \qquad z=2S/r_0.
\]

The last scalar maximum follows by differentiating z exp(-z), whose derivative is (1-z)exp(-z). A zero multiplier is already covered. Zero entries of h cause no difficulty.

For H_m, S_1=1+a and S_2=3+2a+beta>3, so S_m>3 for m>=2. Consequently

\[
 D_m E\le\frac{2+1/S_m}{e}
 <\frac7{3e}<\frac78.
 \tag{4.2}
\]

Here e>8/3 follows from the positive exponential series, whose terms through degree three sum to 8/3. Combining (4.2) with the accepted input B_(n_m)/D_m<47/50 gives

\[
 B_{n_m}E<\frac{329}{400}<1.
 \tag{4.3}
\]

This is an original-scale failure on every complete inherited word, uniformly over this whole start sector. It is not an upper bound for starts outside the sector.

The sector is substantial relative to the largest physical step. The exact mass, obtained by unrolling S_(j+1)=2S_j+1+a c^j and summing the geometric progression, is

\[
 S_m=2^m-1+a\frac{c^m-2^m}{c-2},\qquad
 b_m=1+a c^{m-1}.
\]

As the enclosing m tends to infinity with its prescribed parameters, c tends to 1+sqrt(2), a tends to its reciprocal, and (2/c)^m tends to zero. Thus

\[
 b_m/S_m\longrightarrow(c_0-2)/c_0=3-2\sqrt2.
\]

This asymptotic observation is not used in (4.2)--(4.3); those bounds hold for the literal finite words and parameters.

## 5. An explicit full-word curved trajectory, with its deficient payoff

The ceiling is not based on forcing a constant curvature. For every H_m with S=S_m, choose the explicit original start and minimizer

\[
 u_0=v_0=3S,\qquad
 x_0=(1-3S,3S),\qquad x_\star=(1-3S,0).
\]

The original radius is R_0=3S. Every physical h_t is at most S, while r_t>=u_t>=u_0=3S. Hence every multiplier lies in (0,1), v_t stays nonzero, and, since each H_m entry is positive, (3.2) gives r_(t+1)<r_t at every step. The tangential curvature 1/r_t strictly increases at each of the n_m physical steps. Equation (3.3) also gives a strictly increasing q_t.

At the same time,

\[
 \frac{v_N}{v_0}
 =\prod_t(1-h_t/r_t)
 \ge\prod_t(1-h_t/(3S))\ge1-\sum_t h_t/(3S)=2/3.
\]

The product inequality follows by induction from (1-a)(1-b)>=1-a-b for nonnegative a,b with sum at most one. Also q_N<=2r_N<=2r_0=6sqrt(2)S. Substitution in (3.4) yields the actual terminal bound

\[
 E(H_m;3S_m,3S_m)\ge\frac4{27\sqrt2\,S_m},
 \qquad
 D_m E>\frac8{27\sqrt2}.
 \tag{5.1}

This construction is finite-dimensional, uses one global function and the full word, and has genuine curvature drift. Its payoff is only a fixed subtarget constant. ## 6. The small transverse limit and its precise scope

For any fixed complete word and fixed u_0>=1, the exterior formulas are smooth in (u,v) near the invariant trajectory (u_0,0). Induction in the finite number of physical steps gives, as v_0 tends to zero,

\[
 u_t=u_0+O(v_0^2),\quad r_t=u_0+O(v_0^2),\quad
 v_t=v_0\prod_{s<t}(1-h_s/u_0)+O(v_0^3).
\]

The constants can depend on the entire word. Using (3.4),

\[
 \lim_{v_0\to0} E(h;u_0,v_0)
 =\frac1{u_0}\prod_t(1-h_t/u_0)^2.
 \tag{6.1}
\]

For u_0>=1, lambda=1/u_0 lies in (0,1]. Thus this limit is exactly the admissible scalar-quadratic payoff, with no rescaled physical steps. Taking u_0 large covers the endpoint limit lambda=0 as well.

## 7. The same disk's exact horizontal-axis baseline

Other starts require their actual minimum distance. On the horizontal axis the global function in section 2 reduces to

\[
 F(z,0)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&z\le0,\\
 z^2/2,&0\le z\le2,\\
 2z-2,&z\ge2.
 \end{cases}
\]

For any nonnegative word of mass S, put D=1+2S and x_0=2D e. The closest minimizer is 0, so R_0^2=4D^2. Every physical iterate is x_t=2(D-prefix_mass_t)e and lies in the affine region. The true terminal point is 2(S+1)e and F(x_N)=2D. Hence

\[
 \frac{2F(x_N)}{R_0^2}=\frac1D.
 \tag{7.1}
\]

For H_m, every physical step is greater than one. This permits an exact upper bound on the entire horizontal-axis family, including crossings. A nonpositive iterate is already a minimum and remains there. An iterate z in (0,2] is mapped by the next step h>1 to (1-h)z<0. Thus a nonzero terminal objective requires all earlier iterates to remain in the affine region, except that the final point itself may be in [0,2]. In that event z_0=z_N+2S_m and the original minimizer is 0.

If z_N>=2, the payoff is

\[
 \frac{4z_N-4}{(z_N+2S_m)^2}\le\frac1{1+2S_m},
\]

as differentiation gives the maximum at z_N=2(S_m+1). If 0<=z_N<=2, it is

\[
 \frac{z_N^2}{(z_N+2S_m)^2}
 \le\frac1{(S_m+1)^2}\le\frac1{1+2S_m}.
\]

An earlier quadratic visit gives zero, as already explained. Therefore, for every complete H_m,

\[
 \sup_{\text{horizontal-axis starts, minimizers}}
 \frac{2F(x_N)}{\|x_0-x_\star\|^2}=\frac1{D_m}.
 \tag{7.2}
\]

The supremum can restrict to closest minimizers because F>=0. The accepted comparison gives B_(n_m)/D_m<47/50, so this exact baseline still misses the original target.
