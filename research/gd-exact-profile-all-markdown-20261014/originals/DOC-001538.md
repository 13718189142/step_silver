## statement
Exact alternating-ray multi-cut hard functions with retained remote offsets and function-value areas.

Fix any integer n>=0, L>0, and any predetermined finite nonnegative schedule h of length n for gradient descent x_{t+1}=x_t-(h_t/L)grad f(x_t). Write W_n(h) for the supremum of 2(f(x_n)-f_star)/(L||x_0-x_star||^2) over all finite-dimensional convex differentiable functions with L-Lipschitz gradient, a minimizer x_star, and x_0!=x_star.

Choose k checkpoint steps, where 0<=k<=n, and write h in its exact chronological order as (block_0,b_0,block_1,b_1,...,b_{k-1},block_k). Each block_i is a possibly empty list of nonnegative steps; s_i is its sum. Each b_i>=0 is the intervening checkpoint.

For k>=1 set m_0=1/(1+s_0) and O_0=0. For i=0,...,k-2 define
m_{i+1}=((b_i-1)m_i-O_i)/(1+s_{i+1}), O_{i+1}=O_i+s_i m_i.
Require m_i>0 for 0<=i<k and m_i>=m_{i-2} for 2<=i<k.
Define z_term=(b_{k-1}-1)m_{k-1}-O_{k-1}, and define m_old=0 if k=1 and m_old=m_{k-2} if k>=2. Require z_term>=(1+s_k)m_old.
Define e_term=sum_{0<=i<k, i congruent k mod 2}s_i m_i^2.
Choose any q in the closed interval [m_old,z_term/(1+s_k)].

Then an explicitly constructed one-dimensional convex differentiable function f with L-Lipschitz derivative, f(0)=0=min f, and initial point x_0=1 realizes the full supplied GD schedule and has exactly
2 f(x_n)/L = 2e_term+2q z_term-(2s_k+1)q^2.
Consequently this expression is a lower bound for W_n(h). The largest value within the stated q interval is attained at
q_opt=max{m_old,z_term/(2s_k+1)}.

For k=0, use z_term=1, e_term=0, m_old=0, s_k=s_0 and q in [0,1/(1+s_0)], without defining any m_i or O_i. The same construction and payoff formula apply and their maximum is 1/(1+2s_0). The empty horizon n=0 is included.
For k=1 the feasibility condition is exactly b_0>=1, and the optimized payoff is (b_0-1)^2/[(1+s_0)^2(1+2s_1)].

Define F_ray_n(h) as the maximum of the optimized displayed payoff over all feasible checkpoint choices, including k=0. This is a finite maximum because h is finite. Then
K_n(h)<=F_ray_n(h)<=W_n(h),
where K_n(h)=max{1/(1+2sum_t h_t), max_{0<=j<n}(h_j-1)_+^2/[(1+sum_{t<j}h_t)^2(1+2sum_{t>j}h_t)]} is the lower-bound functional established in predecessor b98bb807d514e80d, with its inner maximum omitted at n=0 and (u)_+=max(u,0).
For h=(1,5,1,9,1), the checkpoint choice (5,9) has exact optimized payoff77/4, whereas K_5(h)=1/3.
No uniform comparison between F_ray_n and the Zhang–Jiang profile is asserted.

## proof
## Explicit construction on the two rays

Write a signed point as x=(-1)^i r at stage i, where r>=0 is its distance
from0. On each ray construct a radial derivative magnitude Q_+(r) or Q_-(r),
starting at Q_+(0)=Q_-(0)=0. Let A_+ and A_- denote the largest distances
previously visited on those rays, and let w_+,w_- be their derivative
magnitudes. Initially all four numbers are0.

At a nonterminal stage i the starting distance is a_i. On its active ray
write A_old,w_old for the previous record and slope and d_i=A_old-w_old.
Choose the magnitude m_i and extend that ray's derivative by a slope-1 ramp
from (A_old,w_old) to

\[
(r_i,m_i),\qquad r_i=A_{\rm old}+m_i-w_{\rm old}=d_i+m_i,
\]

followed by a constant segment of value m_i up to a_i. The same-ray magnitude assumptions
give m_i>=w_old; for the first visit to a ray its old slope is0. The
recurrence will give

\[
a_i=d_i+(1+s_i)m_i=r_i+s_i m_i,
\tag{7}
\]

so a_i>=r_i>=A_old. This extension does not change any previously visited
data. During block_i every iterate remains on this flat segment, since its
partial step sums lie between0 and s_i. Thus the stage ends at signed
distance r_i with gradient sign (-1)^i and magnitude m_i.

At the checkpoint b_i the next signed point has opposite sign and distance

\[
a_{i+1}=b_i m_i-r_i=(b_i-1)m_i-d_i.
\tag{8}
\]

The previously unvisited amount will be nonnegative by the next-stage
feasibility condition. The active ray's new outer record is a_i and its
slope there is m_i. Hence its offset on its next visit is

\[
d_{i+2}=a_i-m_i=d_i+s_i m_i.
\tag{9}
\]

Initially d_0=d_1=0. Write O_i=d_i+d_{i+1}. Equations (8)--(9) imply
O_{i+1}=O_i+s_i m_i and
a_{i+1}-d_{i+1}=(b_i-1)m_i-O_i. Equating this last quantity with
(1+s_{i+1})m_{i+1} gives exactly the recurrence in the statement. Also a_0=1 and d_0=0 imply the
stated m_0, completing the induction that verifies (7) for each nonterminal
stage. Positive m_i means nonterminal stages do not reach0. The terminal
case z_term=0 is permitted only with zero old slope and causes no difficulty.

At terminal stage k, the starting distance satisfies a_k-d_k=z_term. Extend
its ray by a slope-1 ramp from its old record to slope q and then by a flat
segment to a_k. The assumed feasible interval for q makes the ramp endpoint d_k+q no smaller
than the old record and no larger than a_k-s_k q. Thus every final-block
iterate stays on its flat segment. The final distance is a_k-s_k q.

For k=0 the same construction begins directly from the initial origin datum,
with a_0=1 and q in its stated feasible interval.

After the last stage extend each radial derivative constantly to infinity.
Define the global derivative by f'(x)=Q_+(x) for x>=0 and
f'(x)=-Q_-(-x) for x<=0, and define f(x)=integral_0^x f'(t)dt.
Each radial derivative is continuous, nondecreasing and 1-Lipschitz, with
Q_+(0)=Q_-(0)=0. Across0, for x<=0<=y,

\[
0\leq f'(y)-f'(x)=Q_+(y)+Q_-(-x)\leq y-x.
\]

Consequently f' is globally continuous, nondecreasing and 1-Lipschitz.
This proves differentiability, convexity, 1-smoothness, and a minimizer at0.
The earlier stage calculations prove the exact original GD recursion.
In particular no interpolation or dimensional-realizability gap remains
within the candidate construction.

## Retained function values give the exact objective

On one ray, a ramp from magnitude w to v adds area (v^2-w^2)/2. Its
following nonterminal flat segment has length s_i m_i and adds s_i m_i^2.
Therefore at the outer record after any nonterminal stage i, the radial
function value equals

\[
m_i^2/2+\sum_{j\leq i,\ j\equiv i\ ({\rm mod}\ 2)}s_jv_j^2.
\tag{10}
\]

This identity follows by induction on successive visits to that ray: the
squared-gradient contributions telescope and the flat-segment areas remain.
At the final stage the new ramp endpoint has value e_term+q^2/2. The distance
from that endpoint to the final point is z_term-(s_k+1)q, on which the derivative
equals q. The final value is consequently

\[
e_term+q^2/2+q[z_term-(s_k+1)q]
=e_term+qZ-(s_k+1/2)q^2,
\]

proving the stated exact payoff. The initial distance is exactly1, so the normalization is exact.

## Exact two-cut illustration

For h=(1,5,1,9,1) select checkpoint steps5 and9. Then s_0=s_1=s_2=1,
m_0=1/2, O_0=0, m_1=1, O_1=1/2, z_term=15/2, m_old=1/2,
e_term=1/4, and q_opt=5/2. All feasibility conditions hold. The exact payoff formula gives

\[
W_5(1,5,1,9,1)\geq2(1/4)+(15/2)^2/3=77/4.
\]

The actual points are 1,1/2,-2,-1,8,11/2. On the positive ray the
derivative is r on [0,1/2], 1/2 on [1/2,1], r-1/2 on [1,3], and5/2
on [3,infinity). On the negative ray its magnitude is min(r,1).
Direct integration gives f(11/2)=77/8, consistent with the formula.
By contrast the exact K_5 value is max{1/35,4/23,1/3}=1/3.



Optimization, base cases, and lower-bound conclusion.
For any s_k>=0 the function q -> 2e_term+2q z_term-(2s_k+1)q^2 is a strictly concave quadratic. Its unconstrained maximum is z_term/(2s_k+1). Feasibility implies z_term>=0, and 2s_k+1>=s_k+1, so that maximum does not exceed the interval's upper endpoint. Clamping only at its lower endpoint gives the stated q_opt. When k=0, the construction is a ramp from0 to q followed by a plateau to the initial point1. Each ordinary step stays in that plateau because q<=1/(1+s_0). The objective formula is 2q-(2s_0+1)q^2 and its optimum is1/(2s_0+1), including s_0=0 and n=0. When k=1, z_term=(b_0-1)/(1+s_0), m_old=0 and e_term=0. Feasibility is b_0>=1, and optimizing gives z_term^2/(2s_1+1). The case b_0=1, q=0 reaches0 at the checkpoint and remains there; the negative ray can have identically zero derivative, so this is a legal degenerate case. Checkpoints with b_0<1 contribute only a zero term to K_n and need not be selected. Thus k=0 and k=1 recover all terms of K_n as defined in predecessor fact b98bb807d514e80d. There are at most2^n selected checkpoint sets, so the maximum defining F_ray_n is finite and nonempty. Each feasible choice is realized by a legal function and has the exact computed payoff, hence their maximum is at most W_n(h) and is at least K_n(h). This finite counting observation is not a numerical enumeration or a computational ingredient in the proof.

Every construction above was at L=1. For general L>0 replace the constructed function f_1 by f_L=L f_1. Its derivative is L-Lipschitz and its GD update with step h_t/L is exactly the same as f_1's update with step h_t. Its minimizer0, initial point1, and dimension one stay the same. The normalized objective 2f_L(x_n)/L equals2f_1(x_n), proving the stated normalization for all L. Zero ordinary steps repeat a point and cause no change in the proof. All step and derived parameter values are finite real numbers; every denominator s_i+1 or2s_k+1 is positive. Arbitrarily large isolated or clustered checkpoint steps are allowed whenever the explicitly stated feasibility conditions hold. No claim is made that an arbitrary checkpoint selection satisfies those conditions.

## intuition
The function is built only beyond previously visited distances on alternating rays. Exact offsets prevent illegal overwriting of old interpolation data, and the accumulated flat-segment areas preserve old function values. This yields a genuine multi-cut lower bound extending the single-cut Huber construction, without asserting the missing uniform profile comparison.
