# One shared array for all chronological three-block tests

Status: self-contained candidate, **not verifier accepted**. This continuation
uses no optimization, new launch, installation, or fact_submit. The accepted
dependency is `1252b8823bb5a290`, for smooth-convex interpolation and the
original finite full-objective formulation. Every new statement below is
proved within this document. In particular an earlier unverified cut identity
or recursive identity is not a correctness premise.

The requested decision at q=1/M_m is NOT obtained: no feasible common array,
and no infeasibility proof for the entire collection, is asserted. What is
obtained is an exact attained legal-primal interpretation of the collection,
its common two-jump matrix, a quantitative optimum-source budget, and a
quantitative rejection of one explicitly constructed scalable common array
family by two cuts already in the collection.

## 1. Exact collection and notation

Let n>=2, h=(h_0,...,h_(n-1)) have nonnegative finite entries, and put
S=sum h_i, M=1+S, P_i=sum_(t<i)h_t, including P_n=S. Normalize L=1 and
x_star=g_star=f_star=0. The original independent formal vectors are
(x_0,g_0,...,g_n); x_i=x_0-sum_(t<i)h_t g_t. For distinct sample indices
in I={star,0,...,n}, write

 Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2.

Choose ONE finite nonnegative array lambda_ij with finite-node flow

 sum_j lambda_ij-sum_j lambda_ji=2*1_(i=n).                 (1)

Its value-free quadratic polynomial is

 C_lambda=q||x_0||^2-2f_n+sum_(i!=j)lambda_ij Q_ij.        (2)

For every 1<=k<l<=n, require (2) to be nonnegative whenever

 g_i=u for i<k, g_i=v for k<=i<l, g_i=w for i>=l,         (3)

with x_0,u,v,w independent arbitrary formal vectors. The same array is used
for every (k,l). This is the collection A_2. One-block and two-block tests
are included by equality of block values. All the earlier queried-j/tail
tests are included: for j>0 use k=j,l=j+1,u=0; for j=0 use a two-block
specialization. No position moment, optimum edge, or initial coordinate is
discarded in (2).

## 2. One common matrix, with all singular cases

Introduce chronological jumps d_k=g_k-g_(k-1), 1<=k<=n, and b=g_0. Then
g_i=b+sum_(k<=i)d_k. This is an invertible linear coordinate change. Write
the matrix of (2) in coordinates (x_0,b,d_1,...,d_n) as

              [ C       R ]
 A_lambda =   [ R^T     T ],                              (4)

where C is 2 by 2, r_k is column k of R, and T is n by n. A restriction
(3) is EXACTLY the principal block of (4) on x_0,b,d_k,d_l. Thus the entire
collection is equivalent to

 C>=0;
 r_k in Range(C) for every k;
 Z_kk>=0 and Z_kl^2<=Z_kk Z_ll for every k<l,              (5)
 Z_kl=T_kl-r_k^T C^+ r_l.

Here C^+ inverts positive eigenvalues and vanishes on Ker(C). To prove the
singular statement, diagonalize C. A nonzero component of any r_k in its
kernel makes the corresponding quadratic unbounded below in a free base
variable. Once every such component vanishes, complete the squares in the
positive base coordinates. The remaining two-variable quadratic has matrix
[[Z_kk,Z_kl],[Z_kl,Z_ll]], whose PSD criterion is exactly (5). This proves
both implications, including zero diagonal entries and C=0.

There are n(n-1)/2 coupled pair tests of ONE residual matrix Z. This is not
independent per-partition feasibility. It is also not full PSD of Z.

Here is an explicit matrix-only distinction. In any three jump coordinates
take diagonal entries 1 and all off-diagonal entries -3/4, with every other
jump diagonal 1 and other off-diagonals zero; take C=I_2 and R=0. Every
two-jump principal matrix is positive definite, but the vector with these
three jumps equal to 1 has quadratic value 3-9/2=-3/2. This exhibits the
three-jump correlation absent from A_2. It does NOT assert that this matrix
is realized by a nonnegative interpolation array for a particular h.

## 3. Quantitative budget for actual optimum-source edges

Set mu_i=lambda_(i,star), nu_i=lambda_(star,i), and nu=sum_i nu_i.
Summing (1) gives sum_i mu_i=2+nu. On the constant-gradient subspace
g_i=b, direct expansion gives

 C = [ q                -(2+nu)/2 ]
     [ -(2+nu)/2        1+2S+sum_i(1+P_i)nu_i ].          (6)

For the lower-right coefficient, finite edges contribute
sum_i P_i(out_i^finite-in_i^finite)
=2S-sum_i P_i mu_i+sum_i P_i nu_i. The finite-to-star position terms
add back sum_i P_i mu_i; the optimum-edge norm terms add 1+nu. The x_0
coupling is -(sum_i mu_i)<x_0,b>. These are exactly (6).

At the proposed q=1/M, positivity of C is equivalent to

 sum_i (S-P_i)nu_i + (M/4)nu^2 <= S.                     (7)

Indeed its determinant is
1-1/M-sum_i(S-P_i)nu_i/M-nu^2/4, and its first diagonal is positive.
In particular nu<=2sqrt(S/M), with a stricter penalty on early
optimum-source mass. This uses the actual optimum edges, not a choice to
delete them. The budget alone does not prove infeasibility, since nu=0
remains allowed.

## 4. The legal primal cone represented by the collection

Let V_(k,l) be the subspace of real coordinate lists
(X,G_0,...,G_n) in which G is constant on the three blocks (3), while X is
free. Define the cone

 K_2=sum_(1<=k<l<=n) { positive semidefinite matrices with range in V_(k,l) }.
                                                                    (8)

Equivalently, K_2 consists of finite sums vv^T where each scalar gradient
list in v has at most two chronological jumps. This follows by diagonalizing
each positive semidefinite summand. Conversely each such vv^T lies in one
of the summands, after inserting an unused cut if necessary.

Thus G belongs to K_2 if and only if it has a Gram realization in a finite
Euclidean space with an orthonormal coordinate system in which every
coordinate of (g_0,...,g_n) changes at most twice. Different coordinates may
use different cuts. The x_0 coordinate in each of these directions is free.
This is substantially more flexible than a whole vector gradient with only
two jumps: many cut patterns coexist in a single trajectory.

The cone is closed. If G_r converges and G_r=sum_a G_(r,a) is a decomposition
over the finitely many summands of (8), each summand is PSD and has trace at
most trace(G_r). In fixed finite dimension every PSD matrix has norm bounded
by its trace. Pass to a common subsequence on which all summands converge;
their ranges remain in their closed subspaces and their limits are PSD.
Their sum is the limit of G_r and belongs to K_2.

The cone is full dimensional in the space of symmetric (n+2)-matrices. In
jump coordinates every pair of coordinates occurs in one allowed block
{x_0,b,d_k,d_l}. Therefore the linear map from the direct sum of the
symmetric block spaces onto the ambient symmetric space is surjective.
It has a linear right inverse. Starting from identity matrices on all these
blocks, sufficiently small ambient perturbations can be lifted by this
right inverse while every block remains positive definite. The sum of the
embedded identities is consequently an interior point of K_2.

For n>=3 this is a PROPER subcone of the full PSD cone. Choose v with three
nonzero jumps. If vv^T=sum_a G_a with all G_a PSD, then every vector orthogonal
to v has zero quadratic value in each G_a, so Range(G_a) is contained in
span(v). Every nonzero summand is therefore a positive multiple of vv^T.
Such a summand cannot be supported on an allowed two-jump subspace. Hence
vv^T is not in K_2. This is a cone statement, not a claim that the particular
v is a legal GD transcript.

Finally, by (8), a matrix has nonnegative pairing with every G in K_2 if and
only if every restriction (3) is PSD. Thus (5) is exactly the dual-cone
condition K_2^*, with the original array flows imposed separately.

## 5. Exact attained equality on the actual positive-step family

Assume now h_i>1 for every i<n, as holds for every critical H_m below. Let
w_2(h) be the maximum of 2f_n over G in K_2, G_(x0,x0)<=1, and every original
ordered constraint Q_ij<=0 including the optimum. Then

 w_2(h) = min { q : some common lambda>=0 satisfies (1) and A_2 at q }.
                                                                    (9)

Both extrema are attained. The left side is an actual legal smooth-convex
GD performance over the coordinate class described above, not an aggregate
of independently chosen functions. In particular w_2(h)<=W_n(h).

Here is a proof including strict feasibility rather than an assumed generic
strong-duality theorem.

First construct a strict interpolation point within K_2. Put

 kappa=min(1,min_i(h_i-1))/4 >0,
 r=kappa/[2(kappa+1)], a_i=r^i,
 g_i=sqrt(a_i)e_i, f_i=(kappa+1/2)a_i,
 x_0=(kappa+2)sum_(i=0)^n sqrt(a_i)e_i+e_extra,             (10)

where the displayed coordinate vectors are orthonormal. For i<j, write
delta_i=f_i-a_i/2=kappa a_i. Direct substitution of orthogonality into the
two finite interpolation constraints shows they are strict exactly when

                   a_j < delta_i-delta_j < (h_i-1)a_i.   (11)

For the lower inequality use a_j/a_i<=r and
kappa(1-r)-r=kappa/2>0. For the upper use
delta_i-delta_j<kappa a_i<(h_i-1)a_i. The optimum residuals are
Q_(i,star)=-a_i and Q_(star,i)=-kappa a_i. Thus ALL ordered residuals are
strict. The extra coordinate makes the full Gram matrix positive definite.
Each gradient coordinate in (10) is supported on one query, hence has at
most two jumps; the extra coordinate has zero gradients. The Gram matrix
lies in K_2. Scale all vectors and the square roots of all values by one
small positive number to make the radius inequality strict as well.

This point may be on the boundary of K_2. Add a sufficiently small positive
multiple of the interior point constructed in Section 4 to its Gram matrix,
holding the values fixed. Finitely many strict linear inequalities remain
strict for small enough perturbations, including the radius. A point of a
convex cone plus an interior point lies in its interior: translate a small
ball in the cone by the cone point. This gives full conic strict feasibility.

The primal feasible set is compact. Every feasible Gram matrix is a genuine
finite set of Euclidean vectors. Adding Q_(i,star) and Q_(star,i) gives
||g_i||^2<=<g_i,x_i>, hence ||g_i||<=||x_i||. Inductively
||x_i||<=product_(t<i)(1+h_t), and all Gram entries are bounded by
Cauchy--Schwarz. Also ||g_i||^2/2<=f_i<=||x_i||^2/2. The cone is closed by
Section 4; all constraints are closed. Compactness proves primal attainment.
The interpolation assertion in accepted fact1252b8823bb5a290 supplies a legal
finite-dimensional smooth convex function for each feasible full transcript.
The maximum is positive: take x_0=1, every g_i=t=1/(2S+1), and
f_i=t-(P_i+1/2)t^2. Finite-pair and finite-to-star residuals vanish, while
Q_(star,i)=-t+(P_i+1)t^2<0. This constant-gradient Gram matrix is in K_2 and
2f_n=t>0. Homogeneous scaling consequently forces an optimal transcript to
have ||x_0||=1, so the maximum agrees with the normalized objective ratio.

For explicit separation, form the open convex set of tuples (s,t,c) for
which some G in K_2 and real f obey s_ij>Q_ij, t>G_(x0,x0)-1, c<2f_n.
The point (0,0,w_2) is outside. Separate it, obtaining nonnegative
coefficients (lambda,rho,eta) on (s,t,-c). If eta=0, substitution of the
strict feasible point makes the limiting separation inequality strictly
negative, contrary to separation. Hence eta>0; normalize eta=1. Because
the values f_i are unrestricted in the separation inequality, their
coefficients vanish and give exactly (1). Scaling G in K_2 implies that
the matrix of C_lambda with q=rho lies in K_2^*. Setting G=f=0 gives
rho<=w_2. The matrix condition and lambda>=0 give the reverse weak-duality
inequality w_2<=rho. Thus equality holds and the common array is attained.
This is the same elementary separation mechanism as the accepted full-cone
proof, applied here only after proving its needed hypotheses for K_2.

Consequently, on H_m, failure of the entire common-array relaxation at
q=1/M_m would have an actual legal witness in this coordinate class with
2f_n>1/M_m. This conclusion relies on (9), not on regarding any formal
negative vector of a particular certificate as a legal trajectory.

## 6. An explicit scalable common array that fails a retained two-cut test

This section screens one concrete construction; it does NOT disprove common
array feasibility in (9).

For a schedule a of sum A-1, define the intrinsic polynomial

 V_a=2sum_i a_i(f_i-f_end)+sum_i a_i(1-a_i)||g_i||^2
                                      -(A-1)A||g_end||^2.

Take two identical step lists of capacity A, separated by a physical bridge
1+r, and put t=r/A, T=(2+t)A. Let p be the bridge gradient, z the final
gradient, and H the weighted sum of gradients at the right-child steps.
Set W=H+z, and use right-child indices j in

 G=sum_j a_j Q_(j,p)+Q_(z,p),
 J=sum_j a_j Q_(j,z),
 k=(2+t)^2, u=2(2+t), v=2A,
 jcoef=2+6t+2t^2, delta=2-(1+t)^2.

The following identity holds for EVERY r>=0, including supercritical r:

 V_join=V_left+k V_right-u G-v Q_(p,z)-jcoef J
                                      +delta||W-Ap||^2.  (12)

For a direct coefficient derivation, put C_r=G+rQ_(p,z)+J. Expanding the
actual positions gives

 C_r,value=2sum a_j f_j+(r-A)f_p+(2-A-r)f_z,
 C_r,gradient=sum a_j(1-a_j)||g_j||^2
                  +[(A-r)/2-r^2]||p||^2+(A+r)||z||^2/2,
 V_right-2J=||W||^2-A^2||z||^2,
 Q_(p,z)=f_p-f_z-(r+1/2)||p||^2+||z||^2/2-<p,W>.

The cross terms in the second line cancel using
2sum_j a_j<g_j,sum_(i<j)a_i g_i>=||H||^2-sum_j a_j^2||g_j||^2.
These four displayed expansions verify (12) in all values and gradient
coefficients, by substituting the definitions of k,u,v,jcoef,delta. No
positivity of delta is used in this polynomial identity.

Start with the empty array at a leaf. Recursively retain the left array,
multiply the right array by k, and add uG+vQ_(p,z)+jcoef J. All actual
coefficients are nonnegative for r>=0. If R_left and R_right are the two
value-free intrinsic remainders, the joined remainder is exactly

 R_join=R_left+kR_right+delta||W-Ap||^2.                  (13)

Now fix m>=128 and, for this WHOLE horizon, set

 sigma=1+sqrt(2), c=sigma(1+1/m), a=(1-1/m)/c,
 H_0=(), H_(j+1)=(H_j,1+a c^j,H_j),
 r_j=a c^j, M_j=1+sum H_j,
 t_j=r_j/M_j, delta_j=2-(1+t_j)^2.                       (14)

The parameters are not recomputed inside the recursion. Counting gives

 M_j=A_mass c^j+(1-A_mass)2^j,
 A_mass=a/(c-2),
 t_j=(c-2)/[1+B(2/c)^j],
 B=(1-A_mass)/A_mass
   =[(sigma^2+2)m+sigma^2]/[m(m-1)].                    (15)

For m>=128, 0<B<8/m, 2/c<5/6 and 0<c-2<1/2. The first estimate follows
from sigma^2=3+2sqrt(2)<35/6 and m>83; the others follow from
12/5<sigma<17/7 and m>=128. For j=m-2 or m-1, we have j>=12 and
(2/c)^j<(5/6)^12<1/8. Using 1/(1+x)>=1-x in (15) gives

 t_j>(c-2)-(c-2)/m
     =(sigma-2)+[sigma-(c-2)]/m
     >(sigma-2)+1/m.

Therefore delta_j<-2/m for these two levels.

Use the actual three-block cuts

                   k=2^(m-1), l=3*2^(m-2).             (16)

Write U=M_(m-1), V=M_(m-2), r=r_(m-1), s=r_(m-2).
Set all gradients on the three blocks to respectively

        u0=-(V+s)/(U+r) v0,       v0,       0,           (17)

where v0 is any nonzero scalar or vector. The full step displacement is
D=sum h_i g_i=(U+r)u0+(V+s)v0=0, and g_n=0. All node squares in (13)
vanish except those at the root and its right child: every smaller node
has constant gradients. Thus

 R_Hm=M_m^2 { delta_(m-1)(V+s)^2/(U+r)^2
                             +delta_(m-2)V^2/U^2 }||v0||^2
      < -8 M_m^2/(25m) ||v0||^2.                       (18)

For the last inequality use both negative deltas and U/V=2+t_(m-2)<5/2.

The obstruction survives the whole following objective conversion family.
For any 0<alpha<=1/S with S=M_m-1, multiply the intrinsic array by alpha,
add lambda_(i,star)=2alpha h_i for i<n, add
lambda_(n,star)=2beta with beta=1-alpha S>=0, and take ANY q. Every
coefficient is nonnegative and (1) holds. Direct expansion gives

 C_lambda=q||x_0||^2-2<x_0,alpha D+beta g_n>
          +alpha||D||^2+2beta<D,g_n>
          +(alpha S M_m+beta)||g_n||^2+alpha R_Hm.       (19)

To check (19), expand 2sum h_i Q_(i,star)-V_h as
||D||^2-2<x_0,D>+2S f_n+S M_m||g_n||^2, and then add 2beta Q_(n,star).
The values cancel because alpha S+beta=1. On (17), set x_0=0; then
(19)=alpha R_Hm<0 independently of q. In particular this array does NOT
pass A_2 at q=1/M_m. Its failure is quantitative and occurs in the already
requested collection, with actual moments and full original flows.

The vector (17) is not a legal adversary: it is a negative test direction
for this particular coefficient family. Only a proof excluding EVERY
nonnegative common array, together with (9), would produce the claimed
legal lower obstruction. No such exclusion has been established here.

## 7. Remaining decision and next interface

The task's central dichotomy remains open. Section 5 converts it exactly to
an attained legal performance problem on a specified proper Gram cone;
Section 6 rejects an explicit scalable candidate at every q. Neither gives
the common feasible-array formula or the collection-wide lower bound at
1/M_m that the assignment requests.

Adding all four-interval partitions would test every three-jump principal
block in the common matrix (4). It adds the determinant/correlation excluded
by the explicit example after (5), without yet reverting to arbitrary
singleton PEP. This names the new constraint precisely; it is not a claim
that those extra partitions are sufficient for the quantitative target.
The exact all-schedule F/G comparison and the original target are unchanged.
