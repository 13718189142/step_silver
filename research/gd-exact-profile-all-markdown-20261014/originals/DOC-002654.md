# Candidate: finite terminal seed washout

Status: verifier accepted; fact_id 0be8b54e0a33381c. This is not the project target.
Shared finding: 65e3a18284ee82c4.

## Statement

Finite terminal-block improvements wash out under the Zhang–Jiang ConPD rule.

Define phi(x,y)=(-x-y+sqrt((x+y+2)^2+4(x+1)(y+1)))/2 and psi(x,y)=(3-2y+sqrt((2y+1)(2y+8x+9)))/4 for x,y>=0. Define S_circle(0)=S_bullet(0)=0 and, for n>=1,
S_circle(n)=max_{0<=k<n}[S_circle(k)+phi(S_circle(k),S_circle(n-k-1))+S_circle(n-k-1)],
S_bullet(n)=max_{0<=k<n}[S_circle(k)+psi(S_circle(k),S_bullet(n-k-1))+S_bullet(n-k-1)].
Write B_n=2 S_bullet(n)+1 and U_n^ZJ=1/B_n.

For a nonnegative schedule h=(h_0,...,h_{n-1}), let W_n(h) be the supremum of 2(f(x_n)-f_star)/(L||x_0-x_star||^2), over all finite-dimensional convex differentiable f with L-Lipschitz gradient, a minimizer x_star, and x_0!=x_star, with x_{t+1}=x_t-h_t grad f(x_t)/L.

Let E be any nonempty finite set of finite-length nonnegative schedules, allowing the empty schedule. For b in E, write m_b for its length and s(b) for the sum of its entries. Define
C_E=max_{b in E} max{0,sqrt(2s(b)+1)-sqrt(B_{m_b})}.
Construct a schedule by choosing b in E and an integer r>=0, setting H_0=b, and, for j=1,...,r, choosing any nonnegative schedule a_j of length k_j>=0 with s(a_j)<=S_circle(k_j) and setting
H_j=[a_j, psi(s(a_j),s(H_{j-1})), H_{j-1}].
If N=m_b+sum_{j=1}^r(k_j+1), then every such schedule satisfies
W_N(H_r)>=1/(sqrt(B_N)+C_E)^2
=U_N^ZJ (1+C_E/sqrt(B_N))^(-2).
Moreover B_N>=2N+1. Consequently the lower-bound ratio tends to 1 uniformly over all schedules in this construction as N tends to infinity. In particular no fixed positive relative asymptotic improvement below U_N^ZJ can be obtained solely by using a fixed finite library of improved terminal blocks while retaining the displayed ConPD rule and the primitive-block sum caps. This is a statement about that construction class, not the unrestricted minimax problem.

## Proof

All arguments below are elementary and self-contained; no previously verified or external theorem is invoked.

1. A lower bound valid for every nonnegative schedule.
Fix any length n>=0 and any h in [0,infinity)^n. Put s=s(h)>=0 and tau=1/(2s+1). In dimension one, for any fixed L>0 define
f(z)=L z^2/2 when |z|<=tau, and f(z)=L(tau|z|-tau^2/2) when |z|>=tau.
The two expressions and their first derivatives agree at z=+tau and z=-tau. The derivative is L times z clipped to [-tau,tau]. This derivative is nondecreasing, so f is convex. Clipping is 1-Lipschitz: if u<=v, its increment lies between 0 and v-u, as can be checked from the three intervals separated by -tau and tau. Thus f has L-Lipschitz derivative. It is differentiable everywhere, has minimizer x_star=0, and f_star=0. Take x_0=1.
Nonnegativity of h implies every partial sum lies in [0,s]. An induction on i yields x_i=1-tau sum_{t=0}^{i-1}h_t: indeed this formula implies
x_i>=1-tau s=(s+1)/(2s+1)>=tau,
so f'(x_i)=L tau and the next gradient update gives the same formula at i+1. This also handles s=0 and equality at the boundary. Thus
x_n=(s+1)/(2s+1), f(x_n)=L tau x_n-L tau^2/2=L/(2(2s+1)).
Since |x_0-x_star|^2=1, this legal instance proves W_n(h)>=1/(2s(h)+1), including n=0.

2. Scalar form of one join.
For x,y>=0 put A=x+1>=1 and D=2y+1>=1. Direct substitution into the definition of psi gives
2[x+psi(x,y)+y]+1
=2A+D/2+sqrt(D^2+8AD)/2
=((sqrt(D)+sqrt(D+8A))/2)^2.
Denote the middle expression by G(A,D) and define T_A(q)=(q+sqrt(q^2+8A))/2 for q>=0. Then sqrt(G(A,D))=T_A(sqrt(D)). Both G and T are increasing in each of their displayed arguments.
For q>=p>=0, rationalizing the difference of square roots gives
0<=T_A(q)-T_A(p)
=(q-p)/2 [1+(q+p)/(sqrt(q^2+8A)+sqrt(p^2+8A))]
<=q-p,
because the denominator in the fraction is at least q+p; when q=p=0 the difference is zero without division. It follows also by interchanging q and p that T_A is 1-Lipschitz. In particular if q<=p+C for C>=0, monotonicity and this inequality imply T_A(q)<=T_A(p)+C.
All bridge steps are nonnegative: since x,y>=0, sqrt((2y+1)(2y+8x+9))>=2y+1, the formula gives psi(x,y)>=1.

3. A reference recursion dominated by the exact benchmark.
Fix a construction from the statement. Write N_0=m_b, N_j=N_{j-1}+k_j+1 and A_j=1+s(a_j). Its actual masses D_j=2s(H_j)+1 satisfy
D_0=2s(b)+1 and D_j=G(A_j,D_{j-1}).
Set reference masses E_0=B_{m_b} and E_j=G(A_j,E_{j-1}).
We prove E_j<=B_{N_j} by induction. The assertion is equality at j=0. If it holds at j-1, then the primitive-block sum cap and monotonicity of G give
E_j<=G(1+S_circle(k_j), B_{N_{j-1}}).
In the defining maximum for S_bullet(N_j), choose the valid index k=k_j; the remaining index is N_j-k_j-1=N_{j-1}. By the identity in part 2 this choice yields
B_{N_j}>=G(1+S_circle(k_j), B_{N_{j-1}}).
This establishes the induction without any assumed structure for the a_j schedules beyond their length and sum cap.

4. Uniform comparison with a finite seed library.
By definition of C_E, sqrt(D_0)<=sqrt(E_0)+C_E. Applying the last assertion of part 2 inductively with A=A_j shows sqrt(D_j)<=sqrt(E_j)+C_E for every j. Part 3 gives
sqrt(2s(H_r)+1)=sqrt(D_r)<=sqrt(B_N)+C_E.
Apply the legal Huber lower bound in part 1:
W_N(H_r)>=1/D_r>=1/(sqrt(B_N)+C_E)^2.
This proves the claimed finite-horizon inequality.

5. Divergence and uniformity.
The maximum defining B_n includes k=0, so B_n>=G(1,B_{n-1}). Since sqrt(D^2+8D)>=D for D>=0, G(1,D)>=D+2. Starting with B_0=1 gives B_n>=2n+1 by induction.
Because E is finite, C_E is a finite constant independent of the depth r, of the chosen seed, and of all the a_j schedules. Thus
(1+C_E/sqrt(B_N))^(-2)>=(1+C_E/sqrt(2N+1))^(-2)->1.
Explicitly, for epsilon in (0,1), when C_E>0 choose any integer N_epsilon>=0 for which
sqrt(2N_epsilon+1)>=C_E/((1-epsilon)^(-1/2)-1);
if C_E=0 take N_epsilon=0. Then for every constructed H_r of any length N>=N_epsilon,
W_N(H_r)>=(1-epsilon)U_N^ZJ.
This threshold is independent of the constructed schedule. Were a fixed delta in (0,1) and an unbounded sequence of constructed schedules to satisfy W_N(H_r)<=(1-delta)U_N^ZJ, choose epsilon=delta/2 and N>=N_epsilon, obtaining a contradiction. The conclusion is restricted to the displayed construction; it imposes no assertion on arbitrary schedules outside that class.
