# A quantitative final-stage guard for every nonnegative prefix

Status: complete candidate proof, NOT verifier-accepted. No new submission is
authorized. This proof constructs and analyzes the function directly. It does
not use the unverified minimax finding 40601f4c6f496ab8 as a premise. The final
maximal-extension identification uses only accepted fact 1252b8823bb5a290.

## 1. Statement in the original normalization

Fix any integer k>=0, any L>0, and any finite nonnegative predetermined prefix
h_pre=(h_0,...,h_(k-1)). The empty prefix is allowed. Write S=sum_t h_t. Define

    t_S=(sqrt(8S+9)-1)/4,
    b_S=1+t_S=(3+sqrt(8S+9))/4,
    c_S=1/(S+1),
    q_S=1/(2t_S+1)^2=1/[1+2(S+b_S)],
    omega_S=1/(1+q_S t_S),
    kappa_S=q_S^2/[t_S(1+q_S t_S)].                      (1)

All these quantities are positive, 0<omega_S<1, and t_S>=1/2. The identities

    t_S(2t_S+1)=S+1,
    c_S^2 t_S^2=q_S                                   (2)

will be used repeatedly.

There is an explicit SINGLE convex differentiable L-smooth function f on R^2,
with a minimizer x_star=0 and an initial point x_0 of norm one, such that for
EVERY nonnegative final stepsize b, the GD trajectory for (h_pre,b) obeys

    2[f(x_(k+1)(b))-f(x_star)]/L
             >= q_S+kappa_S(b-b_S)^2.                    (3)

Equality with q_S holds at b=b_S, which is therefore the unique exact-line-
search minimizer for this one function after the supplied prefix. In particular,

    inf_(b>=0) W_(k+1)(h_pre,b) >= q_S.                   (4)

The complete fixed-prefix maximal-interpolation game for the particular jets
constructed below has EXACT optimized normalized value q_S. Thus (4) is backed
by one fixed realizable prefix and a tight terminal completion, not merely by
choosing a different scalar adversary after observing b.

For the Zhang--Jiang bridge notation in the project, b_S=psi(S,0). This is
a final-stage bound depending on the actual prefix sum S; it asserts no bound
on that sum in terms of k and does not prove the all-schedule exact profile.

## 2. The fixed two-dimensional function

It suffices first to set L=1. Define the even scalar Huber function A_S and
the one-sided capped quadratic C_S by

    A_S(u)=u^2/2                         if |u|<=q_S,
           q_S|u|-q_S^2/2               if |u|>=q_S;

    C_S(u)=u^2/2                         if u<=c_S,
           c_S u-c_S^2/2                if u>=c_S.

Their derivatives are respectively the clamp of u to [-q_S,q_S] and min(u,c_S).
They are continuous, nondecreasing, and 1-Lipschitz. The functions are convex
and differentiable, nonnegative, and minimized at zero.

Use the fixed product function and initial point

    F(u,v)=omega_S A_S(u/sqrt(omega_S))
              +(1-omega_S) C_S(v/sqrt(1-omega_S)),

    x_0=(sqrt(omega_S),sqrt(1-omega_S)).                   (5)

Its gradient is 1-Lipschitz: each coordinate gradient has Lipschitz constant
at most one in that coordinate, so the sum of squared gradient differences
is at most the squared Euclidean displacement. It is convex, differentiable,
and has minimum zero at the origin. The initial squared distance is exactly
one. The two scalar coordinates evolve independently under the SAME supplied
stepsizes, starting at scalar value one in both coordinates.

## 3. Every supplied prefix step is legal, in its actual order

Let P_i=sum_(t<i)h_t for 0<=i<=k. Nonnegativity gives 0<=P_i<=S. In the two
scalar coordinates the entire prefix trajectory is

    u_i=1-q_S P_i,    grad A_S(u_i)=q_S,
    v_i=1-c_S P_i,    grad C_S(v_i)=c_S.                  (6)

Indeed c_S=1/(S+1) gives v_i>=c_S, including equality at i=k. Also

    q_S(S+1)=t_S/(2t_S+1)<1,

so u_i>=1-q_S S>q_S. Thus the derivative formulas justify (6) inductively
under every step, regardless of its magnitude or its position in the prefix.
Zero steps merely repeat the same legal data; no strictness assumption occurs.

After the prefix the two scalar coordinates are

    u_k=1-q_S S,    v_k=c_S.

For a final step b>=0 they become

    u_(k+1)(b)=1-q_S(S+b),
    v_(k+1)(b)=c_S(1-b).                                (7)

The second coordinate always belongs to the quadratic region u<=c_S, including
arbitrarily negative values. Consequently its normalized error curve is exactly

    B(b):=2C_S(v_(k+1)(b))=c_S^2(1-b)^2.                 (8)

Let A(b):=2A_S(u_(k+1)(b)). This is a convex differentiable function of b,
being a convex differentiable scalar function composed with an affine map.

## 4. A uniform bound against every final step

At b=b_S the identities (1)-(2) give

    u_(k+1)(b_S)=(1+q_S)/2>q_S,
    A(b_S)=q_S,
    A'(b_S)=-2q_S^2,

    v_(k+1)(b_S)=-c_S t_S,
    B(b_S)=q_S,
    B'(b_S)=2c_S^2 t_S.                                (9)

Convexity supplies a GLOBAL tangent lower bound

    A(b)>=q_S-2q_S^2(b-b_S)                             (10)

for every b>=0, even when (7) has left the Huber linear region or crossed zero.
The exact quadratic (8) expands as

    B(b)=q_S+2c_S^2 t_S(b-b_S)+c_S^2(b-b_S)^2.          (11)

The choice of omega_S in (1) is precisely

    omega_S q_S^2=(1-omega_S)c_S^2 t_S.                 (12)

Multiplying (10) by omega_S and (11) by 1-omega_S cancels the linear terms.
For the ACTUAL single product function (5), its normalized error is
omega_S A(b)+(1-omega_S)B(b). We obtain

    2F(x_(k+1)(b))
       >=q_S+(1-omega_S)c_S^2(b-b_S)^2
        =q_S+kappa_S(b-b_S)^2.

At b=b_S both channel values equal q_S, so the error is exactly q_S.
Since kappa_S>0, this minimizer is unique. This proves (3) and (4) for L=1.
For arbitrary L take f=L F; the iteration x_next=x-(h/L)grad f(x) is the same
normalized iteration, and the division by L in (3) cancels the rescaling.

The terminal gradients also expose why the construction resists line search:

    g_k=(sqrt(omega_S)q_S, sqrt(1-omega_S)c_S),
    g_(k+1)=(sqrt(omega_S)q_S,
                         -sqrt(1-omega_S)c_S t_S).

Equation (12) makes their inner product zero, while the objective stays
q_S/2>0. Both coordinates belong to one fixed legal function throughout.

## 5. The maximal completion of this full prefix is also tight

Apply fact1252b8823bb5a290 to ALL the queried prefix jets of (5), including
their actual values, and let F_max be the pointwise maximal interpolant.
It is legal and agrees with F on every queried point. Since its gradient
at x_k is the same, its possible final points are still (7). Pointwise
maximality and (3) imply

    2F_max(x_(k+1)(b))>=q_S+kappa_S(b-b_S)^2.             (13)

We prove equality at b_S directly by its conjugate construction. Let w be
the terminal gradient of F at b_S, and use the usual prefix y_j=x_j-g_j
and delta_j=f_j-||g_j||^2/2. The two outgoing terminal residuals are

    Q_(k+1,star)=Q_(k+1,k)=0.                           (14)

For the Huber coordinate, both the last query and the terminal point lie
on the same positive linear region, giving equality in both inequalities.
For the capped coordinate, the last query is c_S and the terminal point
is negative, both lying in its quadratic region, again giving equality.
Weighted addition of the two scalar residuals yields (14).

All other prefix residuals with this terminal sample are nonpositive
because F is an actual smooth-convex function. Thus in

    H(v)=||v||^2/2+max_j(<y_j,v>-delta_j),

the optimum and last-query planes are both active at v=w, and no other
prefix plane has a larger value. Directly from (6),

    y_k=(sqrt(omega_S)[1-q_S(S+1)],0).

Set alpha_k=2t_S/(2t_S+1) and alpha_star=1/(2t_S+1). The identity

    x_(k+1)(b_S)=w+alpha_k y_k                          (15)

holds in both coordinates. In the second coordinate it states equality
of the position and gradient in the quadratic region. In the first it
reduces to

    1-q_S(S+b_S+1)
        =[2t_S/(2t_S+1)] [1-q_S(S+1)],

which follows by substituting (1)-(2).

Since (15) is a convex combination of gradients of the two active
quadratics at w, it makes x_(k+1)(b_S) a subgradient of H at w. This can
also be checked by taking the weighted sum of their quadratic supporting
inequalities, with the displayed nonnegative weights. Therefore w is
the conjugate maximizer at this point. Equality (14) identifies its value
as F(x_(k+1)(b_S))=q_S/2. Hence F_max has the same value there.

Together with (13), this proves the exact optimized fixed-prefix maximal
value q_S, using the accepted fact rather than an unverified minimax exchange.
All earlier queries remain in H, even though the two stated planes suffice
to certify the terminal equality for this PARTICULAR construction.

## 6. Quantitative use and precise limit

This gives a dimension-two function depending only on the fixed prefix sum S
whose error is bounded below uniformly over an arbitrary final step. Its
quadratic excess also gives the necessary condition

    W_(k+1)(h_pre,b)<=q_S+epsilon
       => |b-b_S|<=sqrt(epsilon/kappa_S)

for every epsilon>=0. No randomization or adaptive algorithmic choice is
being substituted for the predetermined schedule; (5) is one deterministic
function and works for all b at once.

For S=0 the formulas give t_S=1/2, b_S=3/2, q_S=1/4,
omega_S=8/9, and kappa_S=1/9, recovering an explicit one-step line-search
obstruction. Leading, interior, and trailing zero steps in the prefix do
not alter this conclusion.

For larger prefixes q_S~1/(2S). Consequently this construction alone does
not control an unusually large prefix sum, does not rule out arbitrary
remote forward edges, and does not yield the required exact-profile bound
as a function of k. Its role is a quantitative final-stage boundary
condition for a recurrence that must still constrain the COMPLETE earlier
trajectory. It complements the general minimax candidate and the three-anchor
obstruction without assuming either unverified result in its proof.
