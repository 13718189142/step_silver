## 1. A lossless parameterization at the Huber value

Fix n>=1 and any nonnegative schedule h=(h0,...,h_(n-1)) with sum S>0.
Set P_i=sum_(t<i)h_t for 0<=i<=n, h_n=0, and tau=1/(2S+1).
Use the independent formal vectors (x0,g0,...,gn), the GD identities
xi=x0-sum_(t<i)h_t gt, and all ordered interpolation residuals

    Qij=fi-fj+<gi,xj-xi>+||gi-gj||^2/2,

with the optimum jet equal to zero. Every legal trajectory satisfies Qij<=0
by fact1252b8823bb5a290.

Choose an arbitrary array r_ij>=0 for 0<=i<n and 0<=j<=n, j!=i, and set
r_ii=0. These are all finite multipliers whose source is a queried prefix
sample; there is no support restriction among them. Define

    mu_i = [2h_i+sum_(j=0)^n r_ij(P_i-P_j)]/(2S-P_i),  i<n,             (1)
    mu_n = 2-sum_(i<n)mu_i,
    beta_i=mu_i+sum_(j=0)^n r_ij-sum_(k<n)r_ki,          i<n.            (2)

The denominators in (1) are positive, since 2S-P_i>=S>0. Define the
complete multiplier array by

    lambda_(star,i)=0,
    lambda_(i,star)=mu_i,                 0<=i<=n,
    lambda_ij=r_ij,                      0<=i<n, j finite, j!=i,
    lambda_(n,i)=beta_i,                 0<=i<n.                       (3)

For the moment mu and beta may have either sign. Their eventual
nonnegativity will be checked explicitly.

Let e_i be the standard basis of the real space of dimension n+1, indexed 0,...,n. Put

    p_i=sum_(t<i)h_t e_t,
    v_ij=p_i-p_j,
    A_i*= e_i e_i^T/2+(e_i p_i^T+p_i e_i^T)/2,
    A_ij=(e_i v_ij^T+v_ij e_i^T)/2
                           +(e_i-e_j)(e_i-e_j)^T/2,
    A=sum_i mu_i A_i*+sum_(i!=j finite)lambda_ij A_ij,
    M=[[tau,-mu^T/2],[-mu/2,A]],
    Z_hub=A-mu mu^T/(4tau),
    K_hub=Z_hub restricted to rows/columns 0,...,n-1.                          (4)

All matrices in (4) are full formal coefficient matrices, not restrictions
to a recovered primal trajectory. The exact identity is

    tau||x0||^2-2fn+sum_(i!=j)lambda_ij Qij
                       =(x0,g0,...,gn)^T M (x0,g0,...,gn).             (5)

Here vector-valued quadratic notation means the corresponding sum of
inner products in any finite-dimensional real Euclidean space.

The sufficient and necessary certificate tests in these coordinates are

         r_ij>=0,  mu_i>=0,  beta_i>=0,  K_hub>=0.                        (6)

More precisely, (6) is equivalent to existence of this full certificate,
and every full certificate at level tau is represented by some r.

### Proof of the identity and the nullspace condition

At a prefix finite node i<n, equation (2) makes outgoing minus incoming
flow zero. At n it is

    mu_n+sum_i beta_i-sum_i r_(i,n)
      =sum_i mu_i=2.

Thus every fi coefficient in (5) cancels. Expanding each Q gives exactly
the matrices in (4), including the initial-vector cross coefficient
-mu_i/2. This proves (5).

Let 1 denote the all-ones vector of length n+1, and define the finite drift
d_i=sum_(j finite)lambda_ij(P_i-P_j). Directly from (4) and the value flow,

              2(A1)_i=2h_i+(1+P_i)mu_i+d_i.                          (7)

For a detailed derivation, the term e_i v_ij^T+v_ij e_i^T contributes
lambda_ij(P_i-P_j) to coordinate i and
h_k lambda_ij(1_(k<i)-1_(k<j)) to coordinate k. The difference-square
term annihilates 1. For k<n, summing the latter cut term over all finite
edges gives h_k[2-sum_(i>k)mu_i] by finite value flow. The star terms
contribute (1+P_k)mu_k+h_k sum_(i>k)mu_i. The two tail sums cancel.
For k=n both cut terms vanish and h_n=0. This establishes (7) for all k.

For i<n, equations (1) and (7) imply

              (A1)_i=mu_i/(2tau).                                    (8)

Also sum_i mu_i=2, so the x0 row of M annihilates
v_H=(1,tau,...,tau).

To establish the last gradient row without assuming it, use the exact
Huber jets

    x0=1, xi=1-tau P_i, gi=tau,
    fi=tau xi-tau^2/2, f_star=g_star=x_star=0.                         (9)

They are the GD trajectory of the fixed scalar function that is x^2/2
on |x|<=tau and tau|x|-tau^2/2 elsewhere. Since P_i<=S, every finite
xi>=tau. Every finite-source Qij in (3) is zero, and 2fn=tau.
Evaluate the polynomial identity (5) at (9): v_H^T M v_H=0, without any
positivity assumption on the coefficients. All rows except possibly the
last already annihilate v_H, and its last coordinate tau is positive.
Consequently the last row also annihilates it. We have

              M v_H=0,  A1=mu/(2tau),  Z_hub 1=0.                    (10)

Completing the x0 square in (5) gives a positive initial square plus the
gradient form with matrix Z_hub. Since Z_hub 1=0, for every scalar coordinate vector
z=(z0,...,zn),

    z^T Z_hub z=(z0-zn,...,z_(n-1)-zn)^T K_hub
                                      (z0-zn,...,z_(n-1)-zn).         (11)

Hence M>=0 iff K_hub>=0. If the signs in (6) hold, (5) proves
2fn<=tau||x0||^2 on every legal trajectory in every dimension. The exact
Huber function (9) attains equality. Normalization from the predecessor
then gives W_n(h)=tau.

### Why these coordinates cover every tight full certificate

Let any nonnegative full certificate at level tau be given. Evaluate it
on (9). The only strict interpolation residuals are those leaving star:

    Q_(star,i)=-tau[1-tau(P_i+1)]<0,

because P_i<=S and 1-tau(S+1)=S/(2S+1)>0. The objective gap is zero.
Full PSD and nonnegative multipliers therefore force lambda_(star,i)=0
and Mv_H=0. The value flow implies sum mu_i=2. Applying (7) to the
prefix rows gives (1), and the flow at each prefix node gives (2).
Take r to be the actual finite prefix-source multipliers of the original
certificate. This reconstructs every coefficient, proving the asserted
losslessness. This argument does not delete incoming terminal edges.

## 4. Consequences and limits

Together (3), (5), (6), (12) and (14) constitute a full exact candidate
certificate with objective level 9/(139+52sqrt(2)). The fixed Huber
function (9) attains it with initial distance exactly one. Every ordered
interpolation inequality and every formal gradient, including terminal,
belongs to the model. Returning to arbitrary L and initial distance uses
the normalization in accepted fact1252b8823bb5a290.

The strict sign and pivot margins imply, by continuity of the explicit
formulas, that the SAME fixed rational r gives a Huber-tight certificate
for every h in some open neighborhood of this seven-step word. This is
a local finite-horizon statement, not a claim that Huber tightness or the
pivot margins persist as m grows.

The numerical benchmark comparison is tau/U_7^ZJ approximately1.296454,
with the exact benchmark still defined by the full two-sequence DP.
Consequently this finite word is not evidence of improvement over ZJ.
The operationally useful output for the growing-horizon upper attempt is
an explicit lossless coordinate system in which the exact remaining tasks

choice of r, or uniform positive pivot bound, has been proved here.
