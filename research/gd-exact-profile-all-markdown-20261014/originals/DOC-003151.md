# C56: a legal two-dimensional repair and its unavoidable normalization cost

Status: **complete scoped joint proof candidate, not verifier-accepted**.
No optimization or fact_submit is allocated. The only mathematical
predecessor is accepted fact `1252b8823bb5a290`, for smooth-convex
interpolation, its explicit conjugate construction, and exact unqueried
terminal completion. The scalar template is defined and analyzed here;
no unverified C53/C55 claim is used as a premise.

This is a primal construction for the actual critical schedule. It produces
one globally defined convex smooth function, with all queried data jointly
legal, in dimension two. Its limitation is quantitative: the specific
repair mechanism cannot retain the very large formal objective ratio.
Neither this construction nor its obstruction decides the actual worst-case
performance of the schedule or the unrestricted minimax problem.

## 1. Schedule and a completely specified scalar formal trajectory

Fix m>=128 and put

    epsilon=1/m, lambda=1+sqrt(2),
    c=lambda(1+epsilon), a=(1-epsilon)/c,
    d_j=a c^j,
    H_0=(), H_(j+1)=(H_j,1+d_j,H_j),
    n_j=2^j-1, S_j=sum H_j, M_j=1+S_j.

Write h=H_m, n=n_m, S=S_m, M_m=1+S, and

    b0=1+a, b1=1+d_1=2-epsilon,
    h_max=1+d_(m-1).

Every step is greater than one; the largest step is the central bridge.
All choices depend only on the fixed horizon, before an oracle is queried.

Define signed template moments

    k_2=M_2+6epsilon,
    k_(j+1)=-k_j^2/d_j for j>=2.                       (1)

For an H2 block whose terminal gradient is the scalar v, prescribe

    g_0=(2-c+6epsilon/b0)v,
    g_1=v, g_2=c v, g_3=v.                           (2)

Start this block at x_0=k_2 v and use its actual GD steps. Directly,
b0 g_0+b1 g_1+b0 g_2+g_3=k_2 v, so x_3=v.
At a higher H_(j+1) block with terminal gradient v, use the level-j
template with terminal v on the right and with terminal

    v_left=-k_j v/d_j

on the left. Start the parent at x_start=k_(j+1)v. Since its left queried
displacement is (k_j-1)v_left, its left endpoint is v_left. Its bridge
then ends at -d_j v_left=k_j v, exactly the prescribed right start.
The right endpoint is v. This proves the actual chronological GD recursion
of the formal positions and gradients at every level.

Use terminal gradient 1 at the root. Thus

    x_0=k_m, x_n=g_n=1,
    x_(i+1)=x_i-h_i g_i.

For each scalar sample put y_i=x_i-g_i. Every H2 seed endpoint has y=0.
Inside a seed with terminal v, define G0=2-c+6epsilon/b0. Its gradients
and proximal coordinates, in order, are

    gradients:   (G0, 1, c, 1) v,
    proximal:    (k_2-G0, 1+d_1+b0 c, 1+a c, 0) v.     (3)

The template is not legal. In the rightmost seed the two queried indices
i=n-2 and j=n-1 have gradients 1,c and displacement x_i-x_j=b1.
Their sum of smooth-convex residuals, independently of function values, is

    Q_ij+Q_ji=(c-1)(d_1+c)>0,                        (4)

where Q is defined below. Thus no scalar function can realize these jets.

For the size estimates needed in the repair, the critical ratio obeys

    R0=k_2/(c d_2)<1-epsilon/4,
    |k_j|=(1-epsilon)c^j R0^(2^(j-2)).                (5)

Here is a direct verification of the strict gap. Since M_2=5-epsilon+2a,

    (c d_2-M_2)/epsilon
      =[6sqrt(2)+epsilon-2lambda^2 epsilon^2
                                  -lambda^2 epsilon^3]/(1+epsilon)>8.

For the last inequality subtract 8 after multiplying by 1+epsilon and use

    6sqrt(2)-8-7epsilon-2lambda^2 epsilon^2-lambda^2 epsilon^3
      >2/5-7/128-12/128^2-6/128^3>0.

It follows that c d_2-k_2>2epsilon, while c d_2<25/4. This proves the
first inequality in (5); the second follows by squaring the ratio at each
step of (1). Every absolute left-child scale |k_j|/d_j is less than c.
Consequently every seed's absolute terminal amplitude is at most c^(m-2).

The elementary bounds c<5/2, k_2<6, |G0|<1, and b0<sqrt(2)<3/2 give
from (3)

    |g_i|<3c^m, |y_i|<7c^m                           (6)

for every sample. For clarity, k_2<6 follows from
M_2<5+6/7 and 6epsilon<=3/64; and
G0 lies between -1/2 and 2-12/5+3/64<0. These bounds include the terminal
sample, with the optimum sample g_star=y_star=0 added separately.

Equation (5) also gives

    |k_m|<c^m exp[-2^(m-4)/m] ->0.

Thus the formal choice f_n=1/2 would have an enormous ratio 1/k_m^2. It
is precisely this initially tiny normalization that the repair must pay for.

## 2. One explicit extra coordinate repairs ALL interpolation inequalities

Choose any curvature

    0<kappa<1/h_max,

and define the monotone quadratic trajectory

    xi_0=1, xi_(i+1)=(1-kappa h_i)xi_i,
    xi_star=0.

All xi_i are positive and strictly decreasing. This is a single extra
coordinate; no incompatible instances are averaged. For a scalar T>0
set the proposed two-dimensional jets to

    X_i=(x_i,T xi_i),
    G_i=(g_i,T kappa xi_i),
    f_i=[g_i^2+T^2 kappa xi_i^2]/2,
    X_star=G_star=0, f_star=0.                        (7)

The entire vector GD recursion is exact:
X_(i+1)=X_i-h_i G_i. The scalar first coordinate by itself is still not a
legal function; the final interpolating function will be jointly convex
in both coordinates and is not asserted to be a separable sum.

Use the accepted interpolation convention

    Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<G_i,X_j-X_i>+||G_i-G_j||^2/2.

Direct expansion gives the FULL all-pair law, including every star pair:

    Q_ij=g_i(y_j-y_i)
                   -T^2 kappa(1-kappa)(xi_i-xi_j)^2/2. (8)

The first term follows by substituting f_i^formal=g_i^2/2 and x_i=g_i+y_i;
the second is the residual for the quadratic t -> kappa t^2/2. Thus the
same function values and the same two coordinates are tested against all
ordered pairs, not merely against one aggregated dual form.

From (6), g_i(y_j-y_i)<=42c^(2m). For any two distinct finite indices,
their xi displacement is at least one adjacent displacement, and

    xi_i-xi_(i+1)=kappa h_i xi_i>kappa xi_n.

For an optimum pair xi_i>=xi_n>kappa xi_n, since kappa<1. Therefore
the completely explicit choice

    T^2=84c^(2m)/[kappa^3(1-kappa)xi_n^2]+k_m^2        (9)

makes every Q_ij<=0. There is no optimization or nearest-feasible-point
problem hidden in (9): every quantity is an explicit scalar or product
of the predetermined steps.

To produce one actual function, let J={star,0,...,n}, and define on R^2

    H(v)=max_(i in J) {
       <G_i,X_i>-f_i+<X_i,v-G_i>+||v-G_i||^2/2 },
    F(X)=sup_v {<X,v>-H(v)}.                         (10)

By accepted fact `1252b8823bb5a290`, F is finite everywhere, convex,
differentiable with gradient Lipschitz constant one, and interpolates every
jet in (7). Formally this use of the predecessor is obtained by appending
one zero step to h, so all indices 0,...,n are queried prefix samples.
The predecessor explicitly permits that zero step. All the required
prefix interpolation inequalities were proved in (8)-(9).

The minimizer of F is UNIQUE and equals zero. Indeed write

    H(v)=||v||^2/2+
           max_(i in J){<X_i-G_i,v>+||G_i||^2/2-f_i}.

At v=0, the star term is zero, whereas every other intercept is

    ||G_i||^2/2-f_i=-T^2 kappa(1-kappa)xi_i^2/2<0.

There are finitely many terms. In a neighborhood of zero only the star
term is active, so H(v)=||v||^2/2 there. The set of minimizers of its
conjugate is the subgradient set of H at zero, which here is {0}.
This last assertion can also be checked from the defining supremum:
F(X)=0 requires <X,v><=H(v) for all v, hence X=0 by taking arbitrarily
small positive and negative v; and H(v)>=||v||^2/2 gives F(0)=0.

Consequently the TRUE squared distance to the closest minimizer is

    dist(X_0,argmin F)^2=k_m^2+T^2.                  (11)

There is no artificial enlargement from choosing a remote minimizer.
The exact normalized terminal payoff of the constructed function is

    P_assigned=[1+T^2 kappa xi_n^2]/[k_m^2+T^2].       (12)

Multiplying F by any L>0 gives a convex function with L-Lipschitz gradient,
the same GD trajectory for steps h_i/L, and the same project-normalized
payoff 2(F(X_n)-F_star)/dist(X_0,argmin F)^2.

## 3. This is a nonzero legal witness, with a fully quantified scale

For the explicit choice kappa=1/(2S), we have kappa h_i<=1/2, so it is in
the permitted range. For 0<=z<=1/2,

    log(1-z)>=-2z,

because the derivative of log(1-z)+2z is (1-2z)/(1-z)>=0 and its value
at zero is zero. It follows that xi_n>=exp(-2kappa S)=exp(-1).
Since (9) gives T^2>=k_m^2, (12) yields the explicit lower bound

    P_assigned >= kappa xi_n^2/2
                       >=exp(-2)/(4S).               (13)

This is an actual legal two-dimensional witness with order 1/S payoff.
It does not claim a new worst-case lower bound beyond what simpler
quadratic or Huber examples already provide. Its purpose is to make the
proposed deformation of the formal template fully concrete and legal.

## 4. One retained QUERY pair forces a cubic normalization cost

The cost is not an artifact of the conservative sufficient amplitude (9).
Take ANY T>0 and ANY function values for which the queried positions and
gradients (7) are jointly legal with minimizer zero. Keep only the queried
pair i=n-2, j=n-1; no terminal sample is used in this argument.

In the first coordinate the rightmost H2 seed has

    g_i=1, g_j=c, x_i-x_j=b1,
    Q_ij^formal+Q_ji^formal=(c-1)(d_1+c)=:D_bad.

Function values cancel in this sum, so these equalities remain valid for
arbitrary proposed values. In the second coordinate

    xi_i-xi_j=kappa b1 xi_i.

The necessary sum of the TWO original interpolation inequalities is
therefore exactly

    D_bad-T^2 kappa^3(1-kappa)b1^2 xi_(n-2)^2<=0.

Thus every feasible repair in this stated geometry must obey

    T^2 >= D_bad/[kappa^3(1-kappa)b1^2 xi_(n-2)^2].    (14)

We have D_bad>4: c-1>4/3 and d_1+c>3. Also b1<=2 and xi_(n-2)<=1.
Consequently

    1/T^2<kappa^3, and T^2>kappa^(-3)>h_max^3.        (15)

This is a necessary inequality for the original queried geometry. Changing
function values or applying maximal completion at the last unqueried point
cannot remove it. It explains the cubic price: the quadratic coordinate's
adjacent displacement is proportional to kappa, and its interpolation
slack is proportional to another kappa times the squared displacement.

For the assigned terminal values, combining (12),(15) gives

    P_assigned < kappa xi_n^2+kappa^3
               <=1/[2 exp(1) S]+h_max^(-3).           (16)

Here xi_n<=exp(-kappa S), from 1-z<=exp(-z), and
max_(t>=0) t exp(-2St)=1/[2 exp(1)S], as follows by differentiating.

## 5. Maximal terminal completion also pays the cost

Keep only the queried jets i<n with the assigned values (7), together
with the optimum. Use the accepted maximal prefix interpolant from
`1252b8823bb5a290` to complete the last unqueried point. This is a legal
single global function, and its value at X_n is maximal among all
interpolants of those prefix jets. The strict negative intercepts in
Section 2 still apply to every retained non-star jet, so its minimizer
is again uniquely zero. Thus (11) is still the actual closest-minimizer
distance. No terminal gradient or terminal value is imposed in this part.

There is a uniform upper bound on this maximal terminal payoff. More
generally it applies to any legal prefix with the displayed X_i,G_i and
minimizer zero, for its ratio normalized by ||X_0||^2. Let i=n-1 be the
last queried point. Interpolation with the optimum gives

    f_i<=<G_i,X_i>-||G_i||^2/2.

The smoothness inequality on the actual final step X_n=X_i-b0 G_i gives

    F(X_n)<=f_i-b0(1-b0/2)||G_i||^2.

These two inequalities imply

    2F(X_n)<=2<G_i,X_i>-(1+2b0-b0^2)||G_i||^2.        (17)

Here g_i=c, x_i=1+b0 c, and the second coordinate is (T xi_i,T kappa xi_i).
Since 1+2b0-b0^2>0, the right side is at most

    2c+(b0^2-1)c^2+2T^2 kappa xi_i^2
                     <12+2T^2 kappa xi_i^2.

Divide by k_m^2+T^2, apply (15), and use
xi_i<=exp[-kappa(S-b0)]. The normalized completed payoff satisfies

    P_completed < 12kappa^3+2kappa exp[-2kappa(S-b0)]
                 <=12h_max^(-3)+1/[exp(1)(S-b0)].     (18)

The denominator S-b0 is positive. This bound holds uniformly in the
curvature 0<kappa<1/h_max and in every feasible amplitude T. In particular
it covers the explicit repair (9) and exact maximal unqueried-terminal
completion. Every queried-prefix constraint, especially (14), is retained.

As m tends to infinity, S tends to infinity and b0 stays bounded. Also

    M_m<=1+n h_max<2^m h_max,
    h_max>(127/128)lambda^(m-2),
    M_m/h_max^3 < 2^m/h_max^2 ->0.

The last limit follows from 2/lambda^2<1. Equations (16),(18) therefore
give the explicit leading-scale bounds

    limsup M_m P_assigned <=1/[2 exp(1)],
    limsup M_m P_completed <=1/exp(1)<1.              (19)

Hence this whole monotone single-quadratic-coordinate repair mechanism
cannot supply a witness separating the schedule's actual worst case from
the proposed objective level 1/M_m. Its canonical repaired functions have
a genuine unique minimizer, so this failure is not caused by a loose choice
of normalization point. It is caused by the retained adjacent queried
pair (4), which forces the normalization cost (14).

## 6. Exact scope and the remaining repair requirement

The constructive statement covers the original C55 formal trajectory
with one monotone quadratic auxiliary coordinate and the explicit
conjugate function (10). The necessary cost (14) does not depend on the
values, while the unique-minimizer assertion is proved for the two
canonical functions with assigned prefix values: the full-jet interpolant
and its maximal unqueried-terminal completion. For arbitrary different
prefix values or different interpolants, (18) concerns the specified
minimizer zero; it does not silently assert that zero is their closest
minimizer.

No assertion is made about oscillating auxiliary coordinates, multiple
independent repair directions, a changed first-coordinate trajectory,
different knot/value rules, or a completely different adversary. The
result also does not claim that every fixed-dimensional witness has this
cost. The curvature restriction 0<kappa<1/h_max is essential to this
particular monotone repair and is displayed throughout.

The original target quantifies over arbitrary nonnegative schedules,
including zeros and unbounded positive steps. The present construction
uses a strictly positive schedule with growing bridges; it does not
replace those quantifiers. The appended zero step is only a precise way
to apply the already accepted full-prefix interpolation theorem.

For the next primal repair, a concrete requirement is now visible: the
auxiliary coordinates must supply order-one adjacent co-coercivity at
the rightmost H2 defect without paying order kappa^(-3) initial energy,
or the scalar gradients at that pair must be changed. This is a test of
a proposed new construction, not a theorem-equivalent claim that such a
repair exists or has negligible cost.

All new claims here remain draft evidence. There is no new accepted fact,
no unrestricted upper bound for W_n(H_m), and no resolution of the exact
Zhang--Jiang profile problem.
