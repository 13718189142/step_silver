## 1. Quantifiers and the complete prefix

Fix an integer n >= 1 and any fixed nonnegative prefix

    h_pre = (h_0,...,h_(n-2)).

The prefix is empty when n=1. Set k=n-1. The last step b_last ranges over
[0,infinity), and the full schedule is (h_pre,b_last). Use the normalized
setting L=1, x_star=g_star=f_star=0 and initial squared distance at most one.
The quantity W_n is exactly the normalized, arbitrary-finite-dimensional
worst-case objective in fact 1252b8823bb5a290.

Let C_pre be the set of pairs C=(G_pre,(f_i)_(0<=i<=k)) satisfying:

* G_pre is the PSD Gram matrix of all formal vectors (x_0,g_0,...,g_k);
* its x_0 diagonal is at most one;
* x_i=x_0-sum_(t<i)h_t g_t for 0<=i<=k;
* EVERY ordered inequality Q_ij<=0 is retained on J_pre={star,0,...,k},
  where Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2.

These conditions are imposed on Gram/value coordinates, so C_pre is a
finite-dimensional convex closed set. It is compact: the interpolation
construction in the cited fact realizes every feasible set of prefix jets
as a legal convex 1-smooth function. With D_i=product_(t<i)(1+h_t),
one has ||x_i||,||g_i||<=D_i and 0<=f_i<=D_i^2/2. Cauchy--Schwarz bounds
every Gram entry. The set contains the zero data and is nonempty.
This argument allows all zero steps and imposes no strictness condition.

Write Delta_pre for the probability simplex on J_pre. For every C and
alpha in Delta_pre set

    y_j=x_j-g_j,    delta_j=f_j-||g_j||^2/2,
    y_star=0,      delta_star=0,
    X_alpha=x_k-sum_j alpha_j y_j,
    z_pre=g_k,
    Phi_term(C,b_last,alpha)
      =2 sum_j alpha_j delta_j+||X_alpha-b_last z_pre||^2.       (1)

Norms and inner products in this display are Gram expressions, independent
of the choice of a vector realization. For fixed (b_last,alpha), (1) is
linear in C. For fixed C, it is convex jointly in (b_last,alpha), because
its vector inside the squared norm is affine and its other term is linear.
The function is continuous in all finite coordinates. Moreover delta_j>=0
follows from Q_(star,j)=-delta_j<=0.

## 2. The exact hull formula, with its proof

The maximal prefix interpolant in the cited fact is F_C=H_C^*, where

    H_C(v)=||v||^2/2+max_(j in J_pre)(<y_j,v>-delta_j).

For every x in the realizing Euclidean space,

    2F_C(x)=min_(alpha in Delta_pre)
                  [2 sum_j alpha_j delta_j
                    +||x-sum_j alpha_j y_j||^2].              (2)

Here is a direct proof, including attainment. Let v=grad F_C(x), the unique
maximizer of <x,v>-H_C(v) supplied by the predecessor. Let A be the nonempty
set of indices maximizing <y_j,v>-delta_j. One has

    x-v in conv{y_j:j in A}.                                  (3)

To prove this without a subgradient formula, suppose (3) fails. Separation
from the finite convex hull gives a vector d such that
<x-v,d> > max_(j in A)<y_j,d>. For small positive epsilon, the right
derivative of the maximum of the affine functions at v in direction d is
max_(j in A)<y_j,d>: the finitely many strictly inactive indices remain
inactive for all sufficiently small epsilon. The right derivative of
<x,v+epsilon d>-H_C(v+epsilon d) is therefore positive. This contradicts
maximality of v. Thus choose alpha supported on A with
x-v=sum alpha_j y_j. Its value in the right side of (2) equals 2F_C(x),
because the weighted active affine values equal their maximum and

    F_C(x)=<x,v>-||v||^2/2
              -sum alpha_j(<y_j,v>-delta_j)
          =||v||^2/2+sum alpha_j delta_j.

Conversely, for any alpha, H_C(u)>=||u||^2/2+
<sum alpha_j y_j,u>-sum alpha_j delta_j for every u. Maximizing
<x,u>-H_C(u) gives the upper bound in (2). The constructed active alpha
attains it. For ANY minimizing alpha in (2), completing the square gives

    grad F_C(x)=x-sum_j alpha_j y_j.                           (4)

Indeed equality in the upper bound at the unique conjugate maximizer
requires precisely that equality of vectors; alternatively strict
convexity in the barycenter proves that all minimizing barycenters agree.

The predecessor says F_C interpolates the entire prefix, is convex and
1-smooth, and is pointwise maximal among all such interpolants. Therefore

    W_n(h_pre,b_last)
       =max_(C in C_pre) min_(alpha in Delta_pre)
                           Phi_term(C,b_last,alpha).          (5)

For the <= direction, any original function produces a prefix in C_pre
and is bounded above at the resulting terminal point by F_C. For the >=
direction, F_C is itself legal and has the prescribed prefix and terminal
position. If ||x_0||<1, its unnormalized value is bounded by W times
||x_0||^2, and scaling every vector and every value gives the converse
normalization at any positive maximizer. A zero initial vector gives zero
data by the recursion. Maxima exist by compactness and (2).

Each such maximizing function lives in dimension at most n+1, the number
of prefix formal vectors. All information at the unqueried terminal is
constructed in this same space. No queried-prefix inequality is removed.

## 3. An elementary compact minimax lemma

The following finite-dimensional argument will be used here, rather than
an unexplained imported minimax theorem. Let C and A be nonempty compact
convex sets and let P(c,a) be continuous, affine in c, and convex in a.
Then

    max_c min_a P(c,a)=min_a max_c P(c,a),                      (6)

and both extrema are attained. To prove this, put m=max_c min_a P(c,a).
For each c let K_c={a in A:P(c,a)<=m}. Suppose some finite collection
c_1,...,c_s has empty intersection of these sets. The set

    U={u in R^s: some a in A has u_i>=P(c_i,a)-m for all i}

is convex, by convexity in a, and closed, by compactness of A. It does not
contain zero. It has a closest point u_* to zero, with ||u_*||>0. Since
U is invariant under addition of any vector with nonnegative coordinates,
the projection inequality <u_*,u-u_*>>=0 for u in U implies every coordinate
of u_* is nonnegative. Let p_i=(u_*)_i/sum_j(u_*)_j. These are nonnegative
and sum to one. Evaluating the same projection inequality at
u=(P(c_i,a)-m)_i gives, for every a,

    sum_i p_i P(c_i,a)
      >=m+||u_*||^2/sum_i(u_*)_i > m.

But c_bar=sum_i p_i c_i belongs to C, and the left side is P(c_bar,a).
This contradicts the definition of m. Thus the closed sets K_c have the
finite intersection property in compact A and have a common point a_*.
It satisfies max_c P(c,a_*)<=m. The reverse inequality is the direct
min-max inequality. Continuity and compactness give all stated extrema.

Applying (6) to (5) gives the exact terminal-weight minimax formula

    W_n(h_pre,b_last)
      =min_(alpha in Delta_pre) max_(C in C_pre)
                           Phi_term(C,b_last,alpha).          (7)

This retains the full prefix Gram matrix. It does not identify that state
with any scalar statistic of the prefix schedule.

## 4. Unbounded final steps, attainment, and one legal saddle function

Define

    rho_last(h_pre)=inf_(b_last>=0) W_n(h_pre,b_last).

The value function in b_last is continuous. This follows from (5), since
Phi_term is uniformly continuous on C_pre times Delta_pre times any
bounded closed stepsize interval. It is convex: in (7), the maximum over
C of the jointly convex functions in (1) is convex, and partial
minimization over the convex simplex preserves convexity. Equivalently,
(5) is a maximum over C of the convex functions 2F_C(x_k-b_last g_k).

The outer infimum is attained at a finite b_last. For a direct coercivity
check choose

    theta=1/[2(1+sum_(t<k)h_t)],
    P_theta=product_(t<k)(1-theta h_t)>0.

The scalar legal function f(x)=theta x^2/2 starting at one yields

    W_n(h_pre,b_last)>=theta P_theta^2(1-theta b_last)^2,

which tends to infinity. Continuity now gives an attained minimum.
Its value is positive: for each finite full schedule the cited fact proves
W_n>0 (or choose a sufficiently small positive curvature avoiding all
finitely many roots). Positivity at an attained minimizer gives rho_last>0.

The exact unbounded minimax relation is

    rho_last(h_pre)
      =max_(C in C_pre) min_(b_last>=0, alpha in Delta_pre)
                              Phi_term(C,b_last,alpha).       (8)

Here are all compactness details. For each positive integer B, apply (6)
to C_pre and [0,B] times Delta_pre. Let rho_B be the common value and
let C_B maximize the inner minimum. The values rho_B decrease to
rho_last by (7). Along a subsequence with B tending to infinity,
C_B converges to C_* in compact C_pre. For every fixed finite b_last
and every fixed alpha, the defining inequality
Phi_term(C_B,b_last,alpha)>=rho_B holds for all sufficiently large B.
Passing to the limit proves Phi_term(C_*,b_last,alpha)>=rho_last for
every pair. This and the direct min-max inequality prove (8).

Choose ANY optimal finite b_* and any alpha_* attaining (7) there.
Then

    Phi_term(C,b_*,alpha_*)<=rho_last
      =Phi_term(C_*,b_*,alpha_*)
      <=Phi_term(C_*,b_last,alpha)                             (9)

for every feasible C,b_last,alpha. The equality follows by applying the
two inequalities to each other at the middle point. Thus an actual saddle
pair exists without a strictly feasible Gram hypothesis and without any
compactness assumption on the last-step ray.

By (2), the SINGLE maximal prefix interpolant F_(C_*) satisfies

    min_(b_last>=0) 2F_(C_*)(x_k-b_last g_k)
      =2F_(C_*)(x_k-b_* g_k)=rho_last.                        (10)

Its initial norm is one: if it were smaller and nonzero, scaling its
prefix to norm one would scale its positive inner minimum upward,
contradicting (8). If the initial vector were zero, the inner value would
be zero. The constructed function needs dimension at most n+1.

Consequently, in the ORIGINAL normalization and arbitrary finite
dimensions, with the first n-1 GD steps fixed in advance,

    inf_(b>=0) W_n(h_pre,b)
      =sup_(legal f,x_0,x_star)
          inf_(b>=0) 2[f(x_k-(b/L)grad f(x_k))-f(x_star)]
                           /(L||x_0-x_star||^2).             (11)

The supremum on the right is attained by the function in (10), after
normalization. Its inner infimum is attained at b_*. For an arbitrary
legal f, maximal extension bounds its values at EVERY point of this
ray by F_C for the same prefix, so its inner infimum is bounded by the
right side of (8). Conversely F_(C_*) attains (8). This proves (11)
directly; it does not exchange a stepsize with the changing earlier GD
queries. Thus optimizing the final fixed step has exactly the unrestricted
worst-case value of allowing exact line search for that final step.

## 5. Explicit geometry and a consequence for the complete dual set

The inner minimum in (8) has the closed form

    min_(alpha in Delta_pre) [2sum alpha_j delta_j+||X_alpha||^2
                      -(<X_alpha,z_pre>_+)^2/||z_pre||^2]    (12)

when z_pre!=0; the fraction is replaced by zero when z_pre=0. This is
obtained by minimizing a scalar quadratic in b_last, whose minimizing
step is <X_alpha,z_pre>_+/||z_pre||^2. Here t_+=max(t,0).
For z_pre=0, the actual point x_k is a minimizer of F_C and the inner
value is zero (also choose alpha_k=1). Thus z_pre!=0 at C_*.

Let v_*=grad F_(C_*)(x_k-b_* z_pre). Since the value in (10) is positive,
v_*!=0. The minimizing last step is interior: at b=0 the derivative of
F_(C_*)(x_k-b z_pre) is -||z_pre||^2<0. Differentiability and (10) give

    <v_*,z_pre>=0.                                           (13)

Adding the two interpolation inequalities between x_k and its terminal
point gives
<z_pre-v_*,b_* z_pre>>=||z_pre-v_*||^2. Therefore

    b_* >= 1+||v_*||^2/||z_pre||^2 > 1.                     (14)

This holds for EVERY minimizing last step, since any one of them can be
paired with C_* in (9). The outgoing terminal-to-k interpolation inequality
also gives

    delta_k >= rho_last/2+||v_*||^2/2 > 0.                 (15)

Indeed its gradient-position term is b_*<v_*,z_pre>=0, and
||v_*-z_pre||^2=||v_*||^2+||z_pre||^2.

Now take ANY tight full nonnegative-flow PSD certificate at the schedule
(h_pre,b_*), not necessarily one with the terminal support selected in
the predecessor. Evaluate its full quadratic identity on the actual
normalized trajectory of F_(C_*). The objective gap equals its certificate
level rho_last, every Q_ij is nonpositive, and the quadratic is PSD.
Thus every nonnegative multiplier satisfies complementarity. Since
Q_(star,k)=-delta_k is strictly negative by (15), EVERY such certificate
has

    lambda_(star,k)=0.                                       (16)

For certificates with no incoming terminal multipliers, the accepted
square identity gives v_*=V_alpha. Their formal last-step matrix derivative
evaluated on this same transcript is 2<z_pre,v_*>=0. Thus one common
actual primal supplies last-step stationarity for every selected optimal
certificate. No corresponding earlier-cut stationarity is claimed.

## 6. A complete two-dimensional test and the role of dimension

Take n=1 and in R^2 choose

    x_0=(1,0),
    z_pre=(1/3,sqrt(2)/6),
    y_0=x_0-z_pre=(2/3,-sqrt(2)/6),
    delta_0=1/6,    f_0=1/4.

Then ||z_pre||^2=1/6, <x_0,z_pre>=1/3, and the two prefix inequalities
are Q_(0,star)=0 and Q_(star,0)=-1/6. Define the fixed legal function by

    F(x)=[v -> ||v||^2/2+max(0,<y_0,v>-1/6)]^*(x).

The cited fact proves that it is convex and 1-smooth, interpolates the
stated initial jet, and has minimum zero at the origin. Formula (2) is

    2F(x)=min_(0<=alpha<=1) [alpha/3+||x-alpha y_0||^2].

At b=3/2 and alpha=1/2, the residual is

    v_*=(1/6,-sqrt(2)/6),
    ||v_*||^2=1/12,
    <z_pre,v_*>=0,
    <y_0,v_*>=1/6.

Both derivatives of the jointly convex quadratic
alpha/3+||x_0-b z_pre-alpha y_0||^2 vanish at this pair. It is feasible,
so its global minimum on b>=0, alpha in [0,1] is 1/6+1/12=1/4.
This is an explicit function whose normalized error after exact line
search is 1/4.

For the matching universal upper bound at b=3/2, all unlisted multipliers
zero, set lambda_(0,star)=lambda_(1,star)=lambda_(1,0)=1. They have the
correct nonnegative flow, and on the FULL formal space

    (1/4)||x_0||^2-2f_1+Q_(0,star)+Q_(1,star)+Q_(1,0)
           =||(1/2)x_0-g_0-g_1||^2.

This proves the exact value rho_last(empty)=1/4 without an external
one-step performance theorem.

In dimension ONE, by contrast, exact line search from any nonoptimal
point reaches a minimizer: the derivative has the same sign as its
displacement from any chosen minimizer, so the nonnegative negative-
gradient ray contains that minimizer. If the derivative is zero, the
point is already a minimizer. This applies after ANY fixed prefix.
Hence the scalar supremum of the exact-line-search error is zero.
The positive value (11) therefore depends essentially on permitting
higher finite dimensions; its minimax interchange must not be applied
to a rank-one-restricted Gram set, which is not convex.

## 7. Scope, literature audit, and unresolved bridge

This candidate derives (2), (7), and (11) together with the explicit
attained saddle and all boundary details in a single proof. Its only
verified building block is 1252b8823bb5a290. All additional arguments are
included here; none is being silently promoted from an earlier finding.

For background the Matlas convex-conjugacy/minimax query returned generic
duality results, none needed as a proof premise. Maurice Sion's original
"On general minimax theorems", Pacific J. Math. 8 (1958), pp.171-176,
especially Section3, was downloaded and its definitions and proof read
through the browser's PDF extraction. Its broader one-compact-set theorem
is consistent with this finite convex setting, but Section3 above supplies
the particular elementary separation proof used here, and Section4 supplies
the unbounded-ray limit and attainment separately. No paper theorem is
used as a black box.

The full queried-prefix state remains in C_pre. Earlier steps change this
feasible set, so (11) cannot be iterated backward as a scalar recurrence.
Neither (12) nor (16) establishes Huber tightness, eliminates remote forward
prefix edges, or proves the Zhang--Jiang exact-profile lower bound. The next
target-facing question is whether the geometric inner value (12) admits
a useful lower bound under a realizable, explicitly constructed family
of full prefixes. Its dimension-two one-step witness shows why restricting
that question to scalar exact-line-search adversaries would lose everything.
