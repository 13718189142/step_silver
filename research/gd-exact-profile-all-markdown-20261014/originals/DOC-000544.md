This is one bounded direct analytical invocation. It supplies one explicit
schedule-derived mechanism: a single maximum of coupled affine facets whose
gradients have a shared component and a different fresh coordinate at each
query. The zero-shared-component case is a subfamily of the same mechanism,
not a second candidate. No second mechanism is pursued.

The construction does not start from an unknown adverse trajectory, use a
feasible-interpolation oracle, or assume the direct representation draft.
All facet slopes, heights along the run, the initial point, the minimum set,
the true normalization, and a fixed-extra-coordinate realization are given

This obstruction includes the actual short final step and all tree levels.

## 2. One explicit law on words with all h_i>1

This section includes n=0, with the condition on entries then vacuous.
Fix the whole word before constructing any function. Set

    S=sum_(i<n) h_i, A=S+1,
    r_n=1, T_n=1,
    r_i^2 = [product_(i<t<n) h_t/(h_t-1)]/(h_i-1), i<n,
    T_i = product_(i<=t<n) h_t/(h_t-1), 0<=i<=n,
    T_(n+1)=0.

All square roots are the positive real ones. These finite closed formulas
give

    T_i=r_i^2+T_(i+1),
    (h_i-1)r_i^2=T_(i+1), i<n.

Write w_i=h_i for i<n and w_n=1, and define

    J=sum_(i=0)^n 1/r_i^2,
    D=sum_(i=0)^n w_i^2 r_i^2.

In particular J>=1, D>=1, and w_i r_i^2=T_i for all i<=n.
These formulas are mathematical definitions, not executed recurrences.

For the whole-law statement allow any two real parameters z>=0 and b>=0.
They are fixed before the run. A completely deterministic selection from
the word, with no remaining choice, is

    z=D, b=S/J.                                             (2.1)

The larger two-parameter family is useful because its obstruction rules
out repairing this same law by changing either of these parameters.

Use R^(n+2) with its ordinary Euclidean metric and orthonormal basis
e_0,...,e_(n+1). Define, for 0<=i<=n,

    g_i=sqrt(z)e_0+r_i e_(i+1),
    v=sqrt(z)J e_0+sum_(i=0)^n e_(i+1)/r_i,
    p(y)=max{0, <g_0,y>, ..., <g_n,y>},
    x_0=sum_(i=0)^n w_i g_i+b v,
    f(x)=min_y {p(y)+||x-y||^2/2}.                          (2.2)

There are n+1 nonzero affine facets, all with intercept zero and weight
one. They belong to ONE coupled maximum, not a sum of independent
objectives. Their Gram matrix is explicitly

    <g_i,g_j>=z+1_(i=j)r_i^2.

This is an actual real Euclidean Gram matrix. In particular its positive
diagonal part is not a formal PSD variable to be chosen later.

The proposed original points and proximal heights are

    P_i=sum_(t<i) h_t, s_i=sum_(i<=t<n) h_t,
    x_i=x_0-sum_(t<i) h_t g_t, y_i=x_i-g_i,
    delta_i=z s_i+T_(i+1)+b(1+zJ), 0<=i<=n.                (2.3)

Here s_n=0 and T_(n+1)=0. In particular

    x_n=g_n+b v,
    y_n=b v,
    delta_n=b(1+zJ).                                      (2.4)

The positive middle heights in (2.3) are consequences of the same fixed
planes and coordinates. They are not independent value assignments.

## 3. All original queries and all remote support guards

Fix any original index i, including i=n. The common coordinate of y_i
is sqrt(z)(s_i+bJ). For its other coordinates, subtracting the actual
prefix updates in (2.3) gives

    (y_i)_(j+1)=b/r_j                         if j<i,
    (y_i)_(i+1)=(w_i-1)r_i+b/r_i,
    (y_i)_(j+1)=w_j r_j+b/r_j                if j>i.

Consequently the current plane has value exactly delta_i. Every earlier
plane j<i has value

    z s_i+b(1+zJ)=delta_i-T_(i+1),

and every later plane j>i has value

    z s_i+b(1+zJ)+T_j=delta_i-T_(i+1)+T_j.

The tails T_j decrease with j, so T_j<=T_(i+1) whenever j>i. Also
delta_i>=0. Thus ALL old, current, future, and zero planes satisfy

    p(y_i)=delta_i, g_i in partial p(y_i).                  (3.1)

There is no nearest-neighbor restriction. Adjacent facets may tie;
that does not prevent the stated vector from being a subgradient.

For completeness, any finite convex p>=0 has a unique minimizer y(x)
in the defining expression for f in (2.2), by coercivity and strict
convexity. Its optimality condition is x-y(x) in partial p(y(x)).
The two subgradient inequalities at x and x' give, for
u(x)=x-y(x),

    <u(x)-u(x'),x-x'> >= ||u(x)-u(x')||^2.                 (3.2)

For d=x'-x, evaluation at the two minimizers gives

    f(x')-f(x)<=<u(x),d>+||d||^2/2,
    f(x')-f(x)>=<u(x'),d>-||d||^2/2.

Together with (3.2), these bound the remainder after subtracting
<u(x),d> in absolute value by ||d||^2/2. Thus grad f(x)=u(x).
Equation (3.2) gives a 1-Lipschitz gradient. Joint convexity of
p(y)+||x-y||^2/2 proves convexity of f. It is finite everywhere.

Equation (3.1) now proves that y_i is the UNIQUE minimizer defining f
at x_i, so

    grad f(x_i)=g_i,
    f(x_i)=delta_i+||g_i||^2/2.                            (3.3)

Induction on the ORIGINAL physical indices proves every proposed update
in (2.3). In particular the final physical step is included before the
value in (2.4) is evaluated. No additional terminal step is taken.

All ordered interpolation rows follow directly as well. For ANY i,j,
including the terminal, put

    Q_ij=f(x_i)-f(x_j)+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2.

Exact cancellation gives

    Q_ij=delta_i-delta_j+<g_i,y_j-y_i> <=0,                 (3.4)

by (3.1). This includes both orientations and every remote pair.
The full Gram of (x_0,g_0,...,g_n) is the Gram of the real coordinates
in (2.2), with no omitted vector or adjusted radius.

## 4. Complete minima, true radius, and exact terminal payoff

The complete minimum set is the polyhedral cone

    C={x:<g_i,x><=0 for every 0<=i<=n}, min f=0.            (4.1)

Indeed p>=0, and a zero of its envelope requires simultaneously y=x
and p(y)=0; conversely those conditions make the value zero.

Since v=sum_i r_i^(-2)g_i, the initial point is

    x_0=sum_i (w_i+b/r_i^2)g_i.

All coefficients are positive. For any x in C, <x_0,x><=0, and hence

    ||x_0-x||^2 >= ||x_0||^2,

with equality only at x=0. Thus zero is the UNIQUE closest minimizer.
The true squared radius is

    N=||x_0||^2
     =z(A+bJ)^2+D+2Ab+b^2J >0.                            (4.2)

This proof is for the displayed complete set, not an inherited radius
from a different function. At any minimum x, the two optimum rows in
(3.4) are explicitly Q_(i,star)=<g_i,x><=0 and
Q_(star,i)=-delta_i<=0.

The ACTUAL terminal performance of the exact envelope is therefore

    V_z,b(h)=[z+1+2b(1+zJ)]/
                    [z(A+bJ)^2+D+2Ab+b^2J].              (4.3)

This is a completely evaluated value on a static globally admissible
function, without an unspecified terminal maximization.

## 5. Why the innovation product is genuinely forced

This observation explains the mechanism's obstruction; it is not needed
to assume correctness of its construction. Consider zero common component
and an arbitrary proposed sequence of distinct orthogonal gradients
g_i=r_i e_(i+1), with arbitrary affine intercepts and proximal heights
delta_i. Suppose each advertised plane is really active at y_i=x_i-g_i,
all original GD updates use these gradients, and the zero plane is
retained. For i<j, the two actual plane comparisons necessarily give

    r_j^2 <= delta_i-delta_j <= (h_i-1)r_i^2.              (5.1)

The left inequality uses the later direction, which has never been
updated at the earlier query. The right uses the earlier direction,
which is updated exactly once before any later query. Summing the
adjacent left inequalities and using the right inequality with j=n
gives

    sum_(j>i) r_j^2 <= (h_i-1)r_i^2.                     (5.2)

Thus, for positive gradients, h_i>1 is necessary and the backward
innovation product in Section 2 is the equality case of a collection
of NECESSARY remote guards. Adding affine offsets does not remove
those guards. This is a constraint on distinct fresh directions, not
on arbitrary nonlinear adversaries.

For the shared-component law actually constructed, the same guards
remain after subtracting z times the intervening physical mass from
the height differences. Its exact payoff still depends on D and J.
It is not an independent orthogonal mixture whose normalized values
could simply be averaged.

## 6. One deterministic selector on every nonnegative word

On words with every h_i>1, use (2.1)-(2.3). On all other words use the
following explicit one-facet fallback, retaining all physical indices:

    S=sum h_i, d=1/(1+2S), p(y)=max(0,d y), x_0=1,
    f(x)=min_y {p(y)+(x-y)^2/2},
    x_i=1-d P_i, g_i=d,
    y_i=1-d(P_i+1), delta_i=d-d^2(P_i+1).                (6.1)

Because 0<=P_i<=S, y_i>=0. Therefore p(y_i)=delta_i and
g_i is a subgradient there, including all endpoints and zero steps.
The proof in Section 3 establishes every update and ordered row.
The minimum set is (-infinity,0], its true initial radius is one,
and its terminal payoff is exactly d. Empty or all-zero words present
no singularity. Arbitrarily large or unordered entries present none
in either branch. A single entry <=1 triggers this fallback; that
is a stated limitation of the selector, not a restriction of W.

Both branches now have explicit p, true initial squared radius N,
every envelope delta_i, and terminal gradient g_n. These are the
inputs to the fixed-coordinate realization next. This creates a
selector in the requested polyhedral class, rather than stopping at
an envelope representation.

## 7. A fixed extra coordinate with error paid against the original U_n

For either branch, and R>0, define the explicit polyhedron and objective

    K_R={(y,s): s>=p(y)/R},
    F_R(x)=dist((x,-R),K_R)^2/2-R^2/2.                    (7.1)

All plane slopes and R are fixed before the run. The extra coordinate
is ALWAYS -R. The algorithm updates only x. Its normalization is the
true original-coordinate distance N from Section 4 or (6.1); it is
never the ambient distance to (0,0).

Since p>=0, eliminating s gives the exact identity

    F_R(x)=min_y {||x-y||^2/2+p(y)+p(y)^2/(2R^2)}.        (7.2)

The function p+p^2/(2R^2) is finite and convex, since t+t^2/(2R^2)
is convex and increasing for t>=0. The proof in Section 3 therefore
proves that F_R is finite, convex, differentiable, and 1-smooth.
Equation (7.2) proves that its COMPLETE minimum set is still exactly
{p=0}, with value zero. Thus its true radius remains precisely N.

At every explicit envelope point x_i, strong convexity in the
minimized variable, and evaluation at y_i, give

    0<=F_R(x_i)-f(x_i)<=delta_i^2/(2R^2),
    ||grad F_R(x_i)-g_i||<=delta_i/R.                      (7.3)

For the second inequality, the original minimized expression exceeds
its minimum by at least ||y-y_i||^2/2, whereas the extra cost at y_i
is delta_i^2/(2R^2). The new minimizing proximal point is consequently
within delta_i/R of y_i.

For a convex 1-smooth function, I-h grad F is

    M(h)=max(1,|1-h|)

Lipschitz for every h>=0. Indeed, writing u=x-x' and
v=grad F(x)-grad F(x'), (3.2) gives <u,v>>=||v||^2 and
||v||<=||u||. Expanding ||u-hv||^2 gives the claimed bound separately
for 0<=h<=2 and h>=2. No upper bound on h is assumed.

Set the explicit finite expression

    E=sum_(t=0)^(n-1) h_t delta_t
                        product_(j=t+1)^(n-1) M(h_j).      (7.4)

It is zero for an empty word. Starting the F_R-run at the SAME x_0,
induction using (7.3) gives

    ||x_n^(R)-x_n||<=E/R.

Smoothness at x_n and (7.3) then give the complete terminal estimate

    |2F_R(x_n^(R))/N - V_z,b(h)|
       <= 2||g_n||E/(RN)+(delta_n+E)^2/(R^2N).             (7.5)

For the fallback replace V_z,b by its explicit payoff d. In particular
this estimate concerns the actual terminal after EVERY original step,
not the value at an unperturbed point alone.

The following completely explicit height rule pays the error relative
to the original all-split benchmark without executing that benchmark:

    eta_n=1/[1000(1+4n^2)],
    R=max{1, 4||g_n||E/(eta_n N),
                 sqrt(2)(delta_n+E)/sqrt(eta_n N)}.         (7.6)

All quantities on the right were selected from the given finite word.
They are finite even for huge steps. Equation (7.5) is at most eta_n.
The accepted crude bound B_n<=1+4n^p<=1+4n^2, with B_0=1, gives

    eta_n <= U_n^ZJ/1000.                                 (7.7)

Thus the approximation error is uniformly paid in the original units.
R itself need not be uniform in h, because the adversary is allowed
to depend on h. There is no hidden interchange of a schedule supremum
and a large-R limit.

### 7.1 Exact original queries of F_R, without a feasible-set oracle

The bound above already proves its actual trajectory is controlled.
For additional explicitness, every F_R query has this finite exact
evaluation rule on the KNOWN polyhedron (7.1).

For nonzero facet slopes a_i of p, form row normals in the ambient
space

    n_*=(0,-1), n_i=(a_i,-R).

At q=(x,-R), enumerate all linearly independent subsets I of these
known rows, including the empty subset. For nonempty I put

    lambda_I=(N_I N_I^T)^(-1) N_I q,
    u_I=q-N_I^T lambda_I.

For the empty subset put u_I=q. Retain I exactly when lambda_I>=0
and EVERY original row satisfies <n_j,u_I><=0. Take the first
retained subset in a fixed lexicographic order. Then

    grad F_R(x)=x-(u_I)_horizontal,
    F_R(x)=(||q-u_I||^2-R^2)/2.                            (7.8)

This is finite enumeration over explicitly known row subsets, not
optimization of unknown interpolation data. No such enumeration or
trajectory has been executed in this invocation.

Here is why the rule exists and checks every support. Projection onto
the nonempty closed cone K_R has a unique minimizer u. Its residual
q-u lies in the cone of active row normals. To see this directly, a
separating direction if it did not would have nonpositive product
with every active normal and positive product with q-u. A sufficiently
small step in that direction retains every inactive strict inequality
as well and decreases the squared distance, a contradiction. The
finitely generated cone is closed: remove linear dependences from
nonnegative representations until the positive generators are
independent, and then use one of finitely many independent subsets
to take limits. The same elimination supplies an independent active
subset representing q-u with nonnegative coefficients. Its equations
are exactly (7.8). Conversely every retained candidate is feasible,
and its displayed nonnegative normal representation proves projection
optimality by expanding squared distances to any feasible point.

Starting from the specified x_0 and using (7.8) at each ORIGINAL t,
the literal update is x_(t+1)^(R)=x_t^(R)-h_t grad F_R(x_t^(R)).
Its last coordinate never changes. Ties, dependent rows and zero steps
are handled by the rule. For every pair of its actual samples, including
all minima and its terminal, the full smooth-convex interpolation
inequality also follows directly. For clarity, for any convex 1-smooth
F, the function F(x)-<grad F(x_i),x> is minimized at x_i. Applying
the descent inequality at x_j to this function yields

    F(x_i)-F(x_j)+<grad F(x_i),x_j-x_i>
               +||grad F(x_i)-grad F(x_j)||^2/2<=0.

Thus no remote row is assumed from a partial list, for the exact
envelope or for its fixed-coordinate realization.

## 9. Exact whole-law obstruction, including terminal heights

Return to any word with all h_i>1 and let T=2S+1. Subtracting T times
the numerator of (4.3) from its denominator gives the EXACT identity

    N-T[z+1+2b(1+zJ)]
      =J(1+zJ)(b-S/J)^2+D-T-S^2/J.                       (9.1)

For the hierarchy, J>=1 and (8.3) imply

    D-T-S^2/J >= D-(S+1)^2 >0.

    V_z,b(H_m)<1/(2S_m+1).                               (9.2)

The supremum is exactly that value, not merely bounded by it. Indeed
fix b=S_m/J and send z to infinity in the explicit scalar expression

    sup_(z>=0,b>=0) V_z,b(H_m)=1/(2S_m+1).                (9.3)

This proves that changing the common component or paying any terminal

It does not bound an arbitrary common-plus-fresh-direction transcript
with a different slope/height law, signed nonlocal memory, or any other
static function family.

The zero-common-component subfamily has a stronger quantitative failure.

    sum_(i<n) r_i^2+(r_n+b/r_n)^2=Q-1+(1+b)^2,

and its terminal numerator is 1+2b, elementary one-variable algebra

    V_0,b <= 2/[1+sqrt(4Q-3)], for every b>=0.             (9.4)

For example, maximizing (2t-1)/(Q-1+t^2), t=1+b>=1,
sets t=(1+sqrt(4Q-3))/2. On H_m, Q>3^(2^(m-1)), so

    sup_b V_0,b < 2*3^(-2^(m-2)).                         (9.5)

Thus this is a terminal-value obstruction, not just a remark about
growing dimension. Adding the common component can recover the Huber
scale but, by (9.3), cannot exceed it on the diagnostic hierarchy.

## 10. The full canonical/cut channel does not repair this law

      max_j (h_j-1)_+^2/[(1+P_j)^2(1+2T_j)]},

We establish its whole-hierarchy value afresh to avoid importing the
unverified shared draft's comparison.

    (d_l/p)^2 [1+2(p+d_l)/(2t-1)].                         (10.1)

For fixed t,d_l the expression decreases in p: expanding it gives

    d_h^2 [1+2(1+d_h)X_l/(2X_l-1)]
       <=d_h^2(3+2d_h)<1.                                (10.2)

This checks EVERY physical cut, not just a selected pair. Thus

    K_(n_m)(H_m)=1/(2S_m+1).                              (10.3)

Let V_R(h;z,b) be the actual normalized terminal value of the

    max{K_(n_m)(H_m), sup_(z,b>=0) V_R(H_m;z,b)}

The exact accepted SAME-m benchmark comparison in Section 1 and

    B_(n_m) max{K_(n_m)(H_m), sup_(z,b>=0) V_R(H_m;z,b)}

In particular this applies to the entirely deterministic selector
