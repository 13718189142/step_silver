## 1. Target, source audit, and the changed interface

2(f(x_n)-f_star)/(L||x_0-x_star||^2) over finite-dimensional convex
differentiable L-smooth functions with an attained minimum and GD
x_(t+1)=x_t-h_t grad f(x_t)/L. Thus W=2R for the project's R.

The original benchmark remains U_n^ZJ=1/B_n. For clarity its ALL-split
definition is C_0=B_0=1 and

    Omega(x,y)=(x+y+sqrt((x+y)^2+4xy))/2,
    G(x,y)=2x+y/2+sqrt(y^2+8xy)/2,
    C_n=max_(0<=j<n) Omega(C_j,C_(n-j-1)),
    B_n=max_(0<=j<n) G(C_j,B_(n-j-1)).

No recurrence or benchmark constant is modified in this draft.

## 2. Statement of the candidate checkpoint lemma

Fix 0<kappa<=epsilon<=1/2. Define

    Theta=arctan(1/epsilon),
    c_sp=sqrt(1+epsilon^2) exp(kappa Theta),
    d_sp=1-kappa^2,
    T_sp=[exp(d_sp Theta/kappa)-1]/d_sp,
    H_sp=c_sp[1+(1+kappa)T_sp]-1,
    A_sp=epsilon+(H_sp+1)/epsilon.                       (1)

All these quantities are independent of the word and its length.

Partition ANY prescribed finite nonnegative word, in its original order,
as

    (block_0,b_0,block_1,b_1,...,block_(k-1),b_(k-1),block_k),

where each ordinary block may be empty, s_i is its sum, and every selected
checkpoint satisfies b_i>=1+epsilon^(-2). The case k=0 is allowed.
For each i<k, Section3 constructs local vectors from the actual ordered
steps in block_i, with displacements P_i,Q_i satisfying

    0<=P_i<=c_sp s_i,  0<=Q_i<=H_sp,  P_i>=epsilon Q_i.

Set

    M_i=P_i+max{epsilon b_i,epsilon+(1+Q_i)/epsilon},
    sigma_i=1/M_i,
    D_0=1,  D_(i+1)=D_i sigma_i(b_i-1),
    ell_0=0,  ell_(i+1)=D_i sigma_i(1+Q_i).            (2)

For k>=1 there is one explicitly specified finite max-affine J on
R^(k+1), and one function f=env_1 J, such that the complete physical GD
run starts at e_0 and has

    2f(x_n)=D_k^2/(1+2s_k),
    min f=0,
    dist(e_0,argmin f)^2
      =sigma_0^2[(epsilon+P_0)^2+(1+Q_0)^2].           (3)

Consequently this exact true-distance payoff is

    W_n(h) >= (b_0-1)^2
               /{[(epsilon+P_0)^2+(1+Q_0)^2](1+2s_k)}
             times product_(i=1)^(k-1)[(b_i-1)/M_i]^2. (4)

In particular the following fully explicit mass-only bound holds:

    W_n(h) >= 1/(1+2s_k)
      times product_(i=0)^(k-1)
        [(b_i-1)/(c_sp s_i+max{epsilon b_i,A_sp})]^2. (5)

For k=0 use the one-dimensional Huber envelope, giving exactly
1/(1+2s_0). Multiplying the displayed functions by L gives the same
normalized payoffs and the prescribed steps h_t/L for every L>0.

The lemma covers every word and every checkpoint selection satisfying
its displayed condition. Zeros and arbitrarily large ordinary steps are
included. No cap is imposed on selected steps. Maximizing (4) or (5)
over allowed selections, and including k=0, is an explicit all-word
lower-bound channel; no comparison with B_n is assumed.

There is also a direct source-format corollary, proved in Section6A:
with a_sp=2A_sp/c_sp and checkpoints b_i>=2(1+epsilon^(-2)),

    W_n(h)>=1/[2(1+s_k)]
        times product_(i=0)^(k-1)
          [b_i/(c_sp(a_sp+s_i)+epsilon b_i)]^2.       (5a)

It preserves the source's numerator, terminal factor and threshold,
while replacing its certified auxiliary constant. Thus the improvement
is not obtained by concealing the b_i-1 cost of the separate formula(5).

## 3. The new support curve and a backward construction for arbitrary steps

For 0<=theta<=Theta define

    e(theta)=(cos theta,-sin theta),
    e_t(theta)=(-sin theta,-cos theta),
    R_sp(theta)=c_sp exp(-kappa theta),
    v(theta)=R_sp(theta)e(theta),
    n_sp(theta)=e(theta)+kappa e_t(theta).

Let K be the convex hull of 0 and this compact arc. Write
v(theta)=(p(theta),-q(theta)). The endpoint values are
v(0)=(c_sp,0), v(Theta)=(epsilon,-1). Differentiating gives

    p'=-R_sp(kappa cos theta+sin theta)<0,
    q'= R_sp(cos theta-kappa sin theta)>=0.

The second inequality uses kappa<=epsilon=cot Theta. Hence

    epsilon<=p<=c_sp, 0<=q<=1, q/p<=1/epsilon.         (6)

For delta=phi-theta in [-Theta,Theta],

    <n_sp(theta),v(phi)>/R_sp(theta)
       =exp(-kappa delta)(cos delta+kappa sin delta).

The derivative of the right side in delta is
-(1+kappa^2)exp(-kappa delta)sin delta. Its maximum on this interval is
therefore1, at delta0. Since <n_sp(theta),0>=0<R_sp(theta), n_sp(theta)
is an outward supporting normal to K at v(theta). At v(0), (0,1) is
also a supporting normal because every point of K has second coordinate
nonpositive. This proves all support inequalities directly; no theorem
about spiral convexity is imported.

Now let alpha_1,...,alpha_m be any finite nonnegative ordinary block.
Set r_m=v_m=v(Theta) and work backward. Given r_j=(u_j,-y_j) and
alpha=alpha_j, make the following choice.

If

    y_j<=kappa[u_j+(alpha-1)c_sp],                     (7)

put v_(j-1)=v(0), r_(j-1)=r_j+alpha v(0).
Otherwise choose the unique theta in (0,theta_j] satisfying

    <r_j,e(theta)-kappa^(-1)e_t(theta)>/R_sp(theta)
          =1-alpha,                                 (8)

put v_(j-1)=v(theta), r_(j-1)=r_j+alpha v(theta).
Here theta_j is the angle of v_j. Once a capped vector v(0) occurs,
(7) applies at every subsequent backward step, so (8) is only needed
when theta_j>0. Equation(8) defines a number uniquely; it is not an
oracle, numerical feasibility assumption, or executed solver.

We prove by backward induction that these rules are well-defined, that
v_j is the Euclidean projection of r_j on K, and that

    u_j>=epsilon, y_j>=1, y_j/u_j<=1/epsilon.          (9)

For a noncapped point the stronger representation is

    r_j=R_sp(theta_j)[(1+t_j)e(theta_j)
                            +kappa t_j e_t(theta_j)],
    t_j>=0.                                         (10)

It holds at j=m with theta_m=Theta,t_m=0. For a capped step let
mu=u_j+(alpha-1)c_sp. Condition(7) implies mu>=y_j/kappa>0, and

    r_(j-1)-v(0)=mu n_sp(0)+(0,kappa mu-y_j).

This is a nonnegative sum of the two supporting normals already checked,
so it certifies projection. It also yields
y_(j-1)<=kappa(u_(j-1)-c_sp), which implies(7) at every earlier step.

If(7) fails, the current point is not capped. By(10), its polar angle
is at least theta_j and the left side of(8) equals1 at theta_j.
At theta0 the left side is (u_j-y_j/kappa)/c_sp<1-alpha.
Its derivative is

    (kappa+kappa^(-1))(u_j cos theta+y_j sin theta)
       /R_sp(theta)>0.

Thus there is exactly one root theta in(0,theta_j]. The tangential
component <r_j,e_t(theta)> is nonnegative. Equation(8) says exactly
that r_j+(alpha-1)v(theta) is a nonnegative multiple of n_sp(theta).
Projection and(10) follow from the supporting inequality. Adding alpha
times a vector obeying(6) preserves u>=epsilon,y>=1; the ratio y/u is
a convex combination of its previous value and q/p, giving(9).
This completes the induction, including alpha=0.

Define

    P=sum_(j=1)^m alpha_j p(theta_(j-1)),
    Q=sum_(j=1)^m alpha_j q(theta_(j-1)), S=sum alpha_j.

Then r_0=(epsilon+P,-1-Q). Bounds P<=c_sp S and P>=epsilon Q follow
from(6). It remains to prove the word-independent bound for Q.

Consider consecutive noncapped angles theta_old<=theta_new and put
Delta=theta_new-theta_old. Taking the tangential component in the exact
GD identity, and using(10), gives

    t_old=B(Delta)+A(Delta)t_new,
    A(Delta)=exp(-kappa Delta)(cos Delta+sin Delta/kappa),
    B(Delta)=exp(-kappa Delta)sin Delta/kappa.          (11)

For 0<=Delta<=Theta<pi/2 both A,B are nonnegative. Put d=d_sp.
Direct differentiation yields

    A'-(d/kappa)A
       =-(1+kappa^(-2))exp(-kappa Delta)sin Delta<=0.

Since A(0)=1, A<=exp(d Delta/kappa). Also B<=Delta/kappa, so
1+dB<=1+d Delta/kappa<=exp(d Delta/kappa). Therefore(11) implies

    1+d t_old<=exp(d Delta/kappa)(1+d t_new).

Telescoping from theta_m=Theta,t_m=0 proves

    t_j<=[exp(d(Theta-theta_j)/kappa)-1]/d<=T_sp.      (12)

At each noncapped point(10) now gives

    y_j=R_sp(theta_j)[(1+t_j)sin theta_j
                              +kappa t_j cos theta_j]
       <=c_sp[1+(1+kappa)T_sp]=1+H_sp.

A capped backward step leaves y unchanged, so the same bound holds at
all capped points. Hence Q=y_0-1<=H_sp. Every inequality is uniform in
m and in all finite values of the alpha_j; no maximum stepsize was used.

## 4. Local finite planes and their exact zero/proximal regions

For one block suppress its index. Set

    A_loc=epsilon+(1+Q)/epsilon,
    M=P+max{epsilon b,A_loc}, sigma=1/M,
    z_circ=(1,0)-sigma(epsilon+P,-1-Q),
    U=1-sigma(P+epsilon b)>=0, Y=sigma Q.

Let V_fin consist of0,v(0),v_0,...,v_m and define

    phi(z)=sigma max_(v in V_fin)<v,z-z_circ>.         (13)

All objects are fixed by the supplied block and b. The smaller convex
hull conv(V_fin) contains every v_j and is contained in K; thus the
projection inequalities proved in Section3 still hold on it. At

    x_j=z_circ+sigma r_j, z_j=x_j-sigma v_j,

the plane with slope sigma v_j is active in phi, giving
sigma v_j in partial phi(z_j). Consequently z_j is the proximal point
of phi at x_j, and grad env_1 phi(x_j)=sigma v_j.

Every nonzero v=(p,-q) obeys p>=0,q>=0,q<=p/epsilon. At the origin,

    <v,-z_circ>
     =-p+sigma[p(epsilon+P)+q(1+Q)]
     <=-p+sigma p(P+A_loc)<=0.

Thus phi=0 on {z_1<=0,z_2>=0}. At(U,Y),

    <v,(U,Y)-z_circ>
       =sigma[(1-b)epsilon p+q]<=0                    (14)

because b>=1+epsilon^(-2). Increasing its second argument preserves
this inequality, so phi(U,y)=0 for y>=Y.

The local GD path starts at(1,0). Its ordinary block ends at
(U+sigma epsilon b,Y), with gradient sigma(epsilon,-1); checkpoint b
then reaches (U,Y+sigma b). Every ordinary/checkpoint-query proximal
point has first coordinate at least U and second coordinate in
[0,sigma(1+Q)]. To check the first claim, the normal representations give
u_j>=p_j: for an interior point this follows from(10), since
cos theta-kappa sin theta>=0, and for a capped point it follows from
the normal representation. Hence

    (z_j)_1-U=sigma[epsilon(b-1)+u_j-p_j]>=0.

For the second claim, (z_j)_2=sigma[1+Q-y_j+q_j]. The inequalities
1<=y_j<=1+Q and0<=q_j<=1 give the two endpoints.

These statements concern the nonsmooth planes, precisely where the
global maximum in the next section must be tested.

## 5. One static global function and ALL directed interpolation rows

Use coordinates0,...,k and quantities(2). For i<k put

    Phi_i(z)=D_i^2 phi_i((z_i-ell_i)/D_i,z_(i+1)/D_i).

Set delta_tail=D_k/(1+2s_k),

    Phi_k(z)=delta_tail max{z_k-ell_k,0},
    J(z)=max_{0<=i<=k} Phi_i(z),
    f(x)=min_z[J(z)+||x-z||^2/2].                     (15)

This is a finite maximum of specified affine planes, including the zero
plane. It is nonnegative and J(0)=0 by Section4 and ell_i>=0. The
quadratic term makes the proximal minimizer unique and attained.

For completeness, the minimizing equation is x-z in partial J(z).
The two subgradient inequalities at z,z' give
||g-g'||^2<=<g-g',x-x'> for g=x-z,g'=x'-z'. For an increment w,
using z as a trial proximal point gives
f(x+w)<=f(x)+<g,w>+||w||^2/2. If z' is the proximal point at x+w,
the inequality J(z')>=J(z)+<g,z'-z> gives, by expansion,
f(x+w)>=f(x)+<g,w>+||w-(z'-z)||^2/2. Consequently
0<=f(x+w)-f(x)-<g,w><=||w||^2/2. This proves differentiability,
the supporting inequalities prove convexity, and the preceding
monotonicity estimate proves1-smoothness. It has minimum0 and is
everywhere finite. No external interpolation theorem is required.

We verify the WHOLE actual trajectory. At the start of block i the
claimed coordinates are frozen x_j=ell_j+D_j U_j for j<i, current
coordinate x_i=ell_i+D_i, and all coordinates j>i equal0. Use the
Section4 trajectory in coordinates i,i+1. At each of its candidate
GLOBAL proximal points:

* the current component Phi_i has the prescribed subgradient;
* component i-1 has first input U_(i-1) and second input at least
  ell_i/D_(i-1)>=Y_(i-1), so is0 by(14);
* every earlier component is still in the same zero region;
* component i+1 has first input at most0, because the second proximal
  coordinate is at most D_i sigma_i(1+Q_i)=ell_(i+1), and second input0;
* every later component is also in its pre-activation zero region.

The terminal hinge is included in this last check. Thus Phi_i dominates
every other component at the candidate proximal point, even if all
values there are0. Its prescribed subgradient is a subgradient of J,
proving the actual global gradient. After the checkpoint, coordinate
i freezes at ell_i+D_i U_i, and coordinate i+1 equals

    D_i(Y_i+sigma_i b_i)=ell_(i+1)+D_(i+1).

This proves the induction, without removing any old or future plane.

During the terminal block, after accumulated steps u in[0,s_k], the
moving coordinate is ell_k+D_k-u delta_tail. Its proximal coordinate
is at least ell_k because
D_k-(u+1)delta_tail>=0. All earlier pieces remain0 there. The actual
gradient is consequently delta_tail e_k at every terminal-block query
and at x_n. Evaluation gives

    f(x_n)=delta_tail D_k-(s_k+1/2)delta_tail^2
           =D_k^2/[2(1+2s_k)].                        (16)

Here is also an exact check of the original ALL-directed constraints.
At every sample, including the unqueried terminal, put g_i=grad f(x_i)
and z_i=x_i-g_i. The preceding construction proves
g_i in partial J(z_i) and f_i=J(z_i)+||g_i||^2/2. For every ordered
pair of samples, direct cancellation yields

    Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2
        =J(z_i)-J(z_j)+<g_i,z_j-z_i><=0.              (17)

The same equation applies when either sample is any minimizer, with
g_star=0,J(z_star)=0. Thus all remote, prefix, terminal, and optimum
rows have been paid. Zero steps give identical actual jets and also
satisfy(17); no formal coordinate deletion is used to assert legality.

The Gram matrix is the Gram matrix of these explicit real vectors in
R^(k+1), hence is positive semidefinite. Within a block the gradient
inner products are positive multiples of cos(theta_i-theta_j)>0.
Neighboring components share one coordinate and can have negative
inner products. The construction is deterministic and retains these
temporal correlations; it averages no oracle branches.

## 6A. The same improved geometry in the original checkpoint formula

This is a second realization of the SAME spiral curve, to compare its
auxiliary allowance with the source without changing other factors.
It uses a half-sum of local envelopes, as in the audited source. Here
the specified genuine minimizer is0 and the actual initial norm about

closest-minimizer result for the one-envelope realization.

Choose a_sp>=2A_sp/c_sp. For each block i run Section3 on its ordinary
steps divided by2, and set S=s_i/2, b=b_i/2 and A=c_sp a_sp/2. In this
paragraph only, choose

    sigma=1/(c_sp S+epsilon b+A)

in(13). This possibly smaller scale still satisfies both inequalities
sigma(P+epsilon b)<=1 and sigma[P+epsilon+(1+Q)/epsilon]<=1, since

Section4 remain valid. Write f_i=env_1 phi_i, with its resulting U_i,Y_i.
Its local checkpoint transfer is sigma b, namely

    G_i=b_i/[c_sp(a_sp+s_i)+epsilon b_i].

For this realization use fresh lengths D'_0=1,
D'_(i+1)=D'_i G_i and thresholds ell'_0=0,

    F(x)=(1/2)sum_(i<k) (D'_i)^2
             f_i((x_i-ell'_i)/D'_i,x_(i+1)/D'_i)
          +(1/2)H_delta(x_k-ell'_k),
    delta=D'_k/(1+s_k),
    H_delta(z)=0 (z<=0), z^2/2 (0<=z<=delta),
               delta z-delta^2/2 (z>=delta).         (18a)

Each local f_i is convex and1-smooth by the envelope proof in Section5.
Each coordinate appears in at most two local terms, counting the
terminal Huber term. Summing their quadratic upper bounds, with weight
1/2, gives F(x+w)<=F(x)+<grad F(x),w>+||w||^2/2. This implies the
gradient is1-Lipschitz: apply that upper inequality to the convex
function F(z)-<grad F(y),z> at x and a unit negative-gradient step.
Since its minimum is at y, the result is
F(x)-F(y)-<grad F(y),x-y>>=||grad F(x)-grad F(y)||^2/2.
Add the exchanged inequality and use Cauchy--Schwarz.

The same local zero regions show F>=0,F(0)=0. At the beginning of
block i the frozen coordinates are ell'_j+D'_j U_j for j<i, the current

has nonzero gradient, and its factor1/2 converts each physical step to
the chosen half step. The checkpoint gives the next stated starting
state. This verifies the entire word for the ONE function(18a).

the specified minimizer0 have squared distance exactly1. Therefore
W_n(h)>=2F(x_n), proving(5a). For k=0 the same last Huber term proves
the empty product. The all-directed Q_ij<=0 follow from the smooth
convex inequality just proved, including the actual terminal and0.
The dimension is k+1 and all entries, including zeros, remain in the
physical run.

## 7. Quantitative improvement and an exact finite-word obstruction

First compare certified auxiliary costs at the SAME horizontal
coefficient. Take epsilon=kappa tending to0, retain c_sp from(1), and
in the circular source choose

    gamma_match=[c_sp^2-1-kappa^2]/2>0.

Then c_Y=c_sp, so this does not exchange the gain for a larger linear
coefficient of S. Elementary logarithmic limits in the displayed
formulas give

    lim_(kappa->0) kappa log A_sp=pi/2,
    lim_(kappa->0) kappa log(H_Y+1)/log(1/kappa)=2/pi. (18)

For the second limit, gamma_match/kappa->pi/2, R_Y->1, and the base
inside H_Y is asymptotic to2/kappa. For the first, log A_sp differs
from d_sp Theta/kappa by terms whose product with kappa tends0.
These are parameter asymptotics for explicit constants, not an o(1)
loss asserted in the target theorem. In particular A_sp/C_Y tends0.
The new certified cost removes the factor log(1/kappa) in the exponent
of the published circular allowance.

For the direct corollary(5a), a_sp=2A_sp/c_sp has the same limit
kappa log a_sp->pi/2. Its ratio to the source's sufficient choice
2C_Y/c_sp tends0. If the extra polynomial floors used by the source's
non-anytime recursion are imposed on both choices, these parameter
asymptotics do not change. This draft does not import or reprove that
recursion's resulting exponent theorem.

That improvement is not enough to make this geometry lossless. The
following finite family tests the ACTUAL activation budget, including
the slack c_sp S-P, rather than merely bounding Q.

Fix the parameters and an integer M>=1. Set Delta=Theta/M,
theta_l=Theta-l Delta for0<=l<=M, and define

    A_D=exp(-kappa Delta)(cos Delta+sin Delta/kappa),
    B_D=exp(-kappa Delta)sin Delta/kappa,
    t_0=0, t_l=B_D+A_D t_(l-1),
    r_l=R_sp(theta_l)[(1+t_l)e(theta_l)
                                   +kappa t_l e_t(theta_l)],
    alpha_l=1-exp(-kappa Delta)cos Delta+B_D
              +(1+kappa^2)B_D t_(l-1).              (19)

Every alpha_l is nonnegative. Tangential and radial components show
exactly that r_l-r_(l-1)=alpha_l v(theta_l). The normal inequality
from Section3 proves v(theta_l)=Pi_K(r_l). Thus the finite chronological
word (alpha_M,...,alpha_1), started at r_M, follows these states back
to r_0=(epsilon,-1). This is an exact family for every M, not an
executed sweep or a replacement by continuous-time dynamics.

Write S_M=sum alpha_l, P_M=(r_M)_1-epsilon,
Q_M=-(r_M)_2-1. Since theta_M=0,

    P_M=c_sp(1+t_M)-epsilon,
    1+Q_M=c_sp kappa t_M.                            (20)

For all sufficiently large M, A_D>1. The elementary geometric sum and
the recursion in(19) give

    t_M=B_D(A_D^M-1)/(A_D-1),
    S_M=M a_D+(1+kappa^2)B_D(t_M-MB_D)/(A_D-1),
    a_D=1-exp(-kappa Delta)cos Delta+B_D.

Using derivatives at Delta0, or the defining limits of exp, gives

    t_M -> T_sp,
    S_M -> (1+kappa^2)T_sp/d_sp
                 -kappa(1+kappa^2)Theta/d_sp.         (21)

The scaled endpoint plane at the origin in(13) imposes NECESSARILY

    sigma^(-1)>=epsilon+P+(1+Q)/epsilon.              (22)

Indeed substitute v(Theta)=(epsilon,-1) into phi(0)=0 to obtain it.
For the equal-angle family the additive excess left AFTER paying the
linear budget c_sp S_M is therefore

    E_M=epsilon+P_M+(1+Q_M)/epsilon-c_sp S_M
       -> E_sp
        =c_sp[1+(kappa/epsilon-2kappa^2/d_sp)T_sp
                       +kappa(1+kappa^2)Theta/d_sp]. (23)

When epsilon=kappa<=1/2 this becomes

    E_sp=c_sp[1+(1-3kappa^2)T_sp/d_sp
                       +kappa(1+kappa^2)Theta/d_sp]>0,
    lim_(kappa->0) kappa log E_sp=pi/2.               (24)

More precisely kappa A_sp/E_sp tends1 in this diagonal regime. The
upper allowance and this finite-family necessary allowance therefore
have the same exponential scale, with their displayed bounds differing
by a factor asymptotic to1/kappa. This is a statement about geometric
parameter costs only.

For each fixed kappa, convergence means that actual finite words force
E_M arbitrarily close to E_sp. Thus any universal additive allowance
A with inverse scale c_sp S+A and the SAME endpoint/minimum-reset
zero condition must satisfy A>=E_sp. If its denominator also contains
epsilon b, take the legal fixed checkpoint b=1+kappa^(-2); necessarily
A>=E_sp-kappa b, which has the same exponential parameter scale.
This is a quantitative obstruction to a polynomial additive activation
allowance in this new family. It does not infer an obstruction from Q
alone or forget the available horizontal slack.

The scope of(22)--(24) is the specified spiral support geometry with
its actual endpoint plane and a common zero region. It says nothing
against positive-height global gluings, changed endpoints, or other
support curves. It does not upper-bound W_n for these words.

## 8. Every paid loss and the exact remaining target obligation

There are no hidden dimension or PSD losses: dimension is k+1, the
function is fixed before the run, and(17) is exact. Formula(4) uses the
true closest minimizer. Passing from(4) to(5) pays only the explicitly
displayed displacement bounds and the first-factor norm bound.

The remaining costs in(5) are:

* the checkpoint restriction b_i>=1+epsilon^(-2);
* the exact numerator b_i-1, caused by the future-plane proximal buffer;
* the coefficient c_sp>1 of every s_i;
* max{epsilon b_i,A_sp}, including unbounded selected steps;
* the terminal Huber denominator1+2s_k, retained exactly.

In(5a) the buffer and numerator loss are replaced by the half-sum
construction: the threshold is doubled, the numerator is b_i, the
denominator is c_sp(s_i+a_sp)+epsilon b_i, and the terminal factor is
1/[2(1+s_k)]. Relative to the product with denominator s_i and the
SAME terminal factor, its exact logarithmic loss, when all s_i>0, is

    Loss_source=2sum_i log[c_sp+(c_sp a_sp+epsilon b_i)/s_i].

Thus it too retains at least2k log c_sp and an explicit, unbounded-in-b
term. There is no assertion that its remaining multiplicative loss
tends1 uniformly over words.

For partitions with all s_i>0, compare(5) only as a DIAGNOSTIC with the
formal product I=product(b_i/s_i)^2/(1+2s_k). The logarithm of I divided
by the right side of(5) is exactly

    Loss=2sum_i log[c_sp+max{epsilon b_i,A_sp}/s_i]
            -2sum_i log(1-1/b_i).                   (25)

In particular Loss>=2k log c_sp. Also

    Loss<=2k(c_sp-1)
        +2sum_i max{epsilon b_i,A_sp}/s_i
        +2sum_i 1/(b_i-1),                          (26)

by log(1+x)<=x and -log(1-u)<=u/(1-u). These explicit sums would have
to tend0 to use this sufficient near-unit comparison. They do not do
so uniformly over all words: ordinary blocks can have sum0, and
epsilon b_i/s_i is unbounded. Formula(5) itself remains defined and
valid when s_i=0; the diagnostic I is then simply not used. Neither
(25) nor(26) asserts that I is the original benchmark.

The actual outstanding task is to prove, with a threshold independent
of h, that an explicit legal adversary channel (this one, a stronger
one, or a combination with other accepted channels) reaches
(1-delta)/B_n for every delta>0 and every sufficiently large n and
every h in[0,infinity)^n. For this particular channel a concrete next
question is whether optimizing its displayed exact payoff(4), including
the Huber empty selection, controls the ORIGINAL all-split B_n with
that uniform relative error. This is a restricted, explicitly defined
family, not a restatement of unrestricted minimax feasibility.

No such comparison is proved here. The new support curve gives a
quantitatively smaller certified checkpoint cost, but its exact finite
obstruction(23) shows why sending its parameters to zero alone cannot
be presented as a lossless repair. No original-target completion,
new accepted fact, or formal verification is claimed.
