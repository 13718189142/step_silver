    Prod_i=product_(0<=r<i)(h_r-1)=beta^i*c^(-s_2(i)).              (2.1)

## 3. Law I: a flat minimizer interval with two unit-curvature sides

For any width w>=0 let I_w=[-w,0] and define the single static function

              (x+w)^2/2, x<-w,
    f_w(x)=   0,         -w<=x<=0,
              x^2/2,    x>0.

Let p_w(x)=min(0,max(-w,x)) be the interval projection, and put
g_w(x)=x-p_w(x). The derivative is continuous, nondecreasing, and has slopes
one, zero, one in spatial order. Consequently f_w is convex and1-smooth,
with exactly the minimizer set I_w and minimum0. For w>0 it is genuinely
nonlinear. We always normalize using distance to I_w, not distance to its
midpoint or another more distant chosen minimizer.

### 3.1 All directed inequalities and all integrated values

At every point, with y=p_w(x), we have

    x=g+y, f_w(x)=g^2/2, y in I_w.

For any two sampled points i,j, including ANY chosen optimum, the full
signed directed interpolation residual is

    Q_ij=f_i-f_j+g_i*(x_j-x_i)+(g_i-g_j)^2/2
        =g_i*(y_j-y_i)<=0.                                  (3.1)

Indeed g_i>0 implies y_i=0>=y_j; g_i<0 implies y_i=-w<=y_j;
and g_i=0 gives equality. This is an explicit all-pairs proof, including
both directions, all optimum rows, repeated points, and every plateau area.
The formula f=g^2/2 integrates the fixed derivative from the actual interval;
it is not a deletion of a previously accumulated area.

### 3.2 Actual GD, zero steps, capture, and exact revisits

Let Dist_i=dist(x_i,I_w), with Dist_0>0. At any outside point, a step h>=0 gives

    Dist_next=(1-h)*Dist_i,                     0<=h<=1,
    Dist_next=((h-1)*Dist_i-w)_+,               h>1.                (3.2)

For h<=1 the point stays on its current side or lands on its boundary.
For h>1 it either lands inside I_w or crosses to the opposite outside side.
An inside point has zero gradient and stays at precisely that same point.
This proves (3.2) directly on both sides of the interval. A zero step repeats
the complete jet. There is no restart at a different minimizer after capture.

Exact outside revisits are also legal: for any w>0,D>0, two consecutive
steps h=2+w/D take D to -w-D and then back to D. Each outside value is D^2/2.
This illustration uses the SAME law, not an additional candidate family.
Its only purpose is to make the exact-revisit convention explicit.

On our original word all h_i>1. Define Ret_0=0 and

    Ret_(i+1)=(h_i-1)*Ret_i+1,
    Ret_i=Prod_i*sum_(r=1)^i 1/Prod_r.                              (3.3)

Repeated application of (3.2) gives the exact formula

    Dist_i=(Prod_i*Dist_0-w*Ret_i)_+.                                 (3.4)

For the induction, ((b*z)_+-w)_+=(b*z-w)_+ when b,w>=0;
applying this to the preceding affine expression proves the next step.
If capture has occurred, the expression remains zero as required.

Consequently, for ALL w>=0 and every initial point outside I_w,

    V_w=(Dist_n/Dist_0)^2<=Prod_n^2=beta^(2*n)*c^(-2*m).             (3.5)

This is an upper bound on the actual error of the entire specified law,
including horizon-dependent widths, either initial side, and arbitrary
initial distances. It is not an upper bound on arbitrary scalar functions.

### 3.3 A concrete nonlinear, noncaptured trajectory and its full spatial order

Here is one exact choice with w>0 and every queried distance positive.
The Prod_i in (2.1) are pairwise distinct. To see this, all are in Q(sqrt(2)).
The nontrivial conjugation fixes beta and sends c to
c'=(1-sqrt(2))*(1+1/m). If beta^(i-j)*c^(-s_2(i)+s_2(j))=1,
divide by its conjugate. Since |c/c'|=lambda^2>1, the integer exponent
of c must be zero. Then beta^(i-j)=1 implies i=j.

Define the positive finite quantities

    Delta_sp=min_(0<=i<j<=n)|Prod_i-Prod_j|,
    Ret_max=max_(0<=i<=n) Ret_i,
    w=min{Prod_n/(2*Ret_n), Delta_sp/(4*Ret_max)}.                    (3.6)

These finite expressions define the width before the run; they are not a
numerical search or an unproved optimization problem. Start at x_0=1 and
choose the closest minimizer0, so the TRUE initial squared distance is1.
Since Ret_i/Prod_i is increasing in i, (3.6) implies

    Dist_i=Prod_i-w*Ret_i>=Prod_i/2>0,
    x_i=Dist_i if i is even, and x_i=-w-Dist_i if i is odd.         (3.7)

Every step thus crosses the whole minimizer interval. Equations (3.2)--(3.7)
give every physical point, derivative, value, and GD update without deleting
any interval or knot.

For completeness the spatial order, which is not chronological, is exact.
Put alpha=-log(beta)/log(c)>0 and order_i=s_2(i)+alpha*i. Then
Prod_i=c^(-order_i), and the order_i are distinct by the preceding argument.
For every pair, |w*(Ret_i-Ret_j)|<=Delta_sp/4<|Prod_i-Prod_j|, so Dist_i-Dist_j has exactly
the sign of Prod_i-Prod_j. Every odd-indexed point is less than -w; every
even-indexed point is positive. Among even indices, increasing order_i means
decreasing x; among odd indices, increasing order_i means increasing x.
This completely specifies the total order by explicit, distinct real keys
for each m, with NO guessed phase comparisons and no ties. The fixed
minimizer interval lies between the two groups. Other widths may have ties;
(3.1)--(3.4) still apply without changing any previously defined value.

The terminal point is -w-Dist_n, since n is odd, and

    f_w(x_n)=(Prod_n-w*Ret_n)^2/2,
    Prod_n^2/4<=V_w=(Prod_n-w*Ret_n)^2<Prod_n^2.                     (3.8)

Thus this nonlinear example has a proved exact payoff, not just a lower
certificate. Nevertheless (2.4)--(3.5) imply uniformly for the whole law

    B_n*V_w<=5*(3/lambda^2)^m ->0.                         (3.9)

The flat-minimum mechanism is weak on the original word even when its
width is chosen from the entire word and every queried point remains outside.

## 4. Law II: a one-sided Huber shelf with an actual inward return

Fix ONE function, independent of m:

              x^2/2, x<=1,
    F_sh(x)=  x-1/2, x>=1,
    g_sh(x)=min(x,1).

It is convex, differentiable, globally1-smooth, with unique minimizer0 and
minimum0. Its negative ray is quadratic and its positive ray eventually
has constant gradient. In particular the two outer ray gradients have no
finite multiplicative comparison bound of the type in e08ee0674ce368d3.
The complete function is fixed throughout the trajectory.

Define y(x)=(x-1)_+. Then at every x

    x=g+y, F_sh(x)=g^2/2+y, g<=1, y>=0, (1-g)*y=0.          (4.1)

The positive proximal value y is retained on every return to the shelf.
For ANY two points, including optimum0 and repeated queries,

    Q_ij=(1-g_i)*(y_i-y_j)=-(1-g_i)*y_j<=0.                (4.2)

This explicit factorization proves ALL directed inequalities for the entire
trajectory, including every incoming and outgoing terminal row. It uses
the integrated value in (4.1), not just the slope or a pointwise envelope.

### 4.1 Parameters of the selected return branch

Use exactly the enclosing-m d_0,d_1,d_2,d_3 from Section2 and put

    w_sh=beta^2*c,
    v_sh=w_sh-1-beta,
    R_sh=S_3+1=8+4*a+2*beta+beta*c.

For every m>=32 the following strict inequalities hold:

    0<v_sh<a*beta,
    c*v_sh<beta^2<1,
    1+beta<w_sh<2+a.                                     (4.3)

Here is an exact proof without numerical evaluation. Since beta>=31/32
and c>12/5, w_sh>(31/32)^2*(12/5)>2, the last inequality following from
12*31^2>10*32^2. Thus v_sh>0. Also

    c*v_sh-beta^2
      =lambda^2*(1-t^2)^2-lambda*(2+t-t^2)-(1-2*t+t^2)
      <=(2-lambda)*t-(lambda+2)*t^2<0.

The inequality uses (1-t^2)^2<=1-t^2 and lambda^2=2*lambda+1.
It proves c*v_sh<beta^2 and hence v_sh<a*beta. Finally

    c*(w_sh-2-a)
      =beta^2*c^2-2*c-beta
      <=lambda^2-2*lambda*(1+t)-(1-t)
      =-(2*lambda-1)*t<0.

Choose any delta in the FULL interval [0,v_sh/a] and start at

    X_0=R_sh+delta, z_sh=v_sh-a*delta>=0.                  (4.4)

The squared distance to the unique minimizer is exactly (R_sh+delta)^2.
The endpoint delta=v_sh/a is permitted and will give a genuine zero landing.
