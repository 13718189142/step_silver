    ||X_(i+1)-X_star||^2

## 3. A uniform movement bound before taking any small-mixture estimate

Select in (1) the component B=1 and all prefix signs common. Its mass is

    g_i = Gamma_i (sqrt(lambda)*u_i, sqrt(omega)*z_i),

Splitting a probability mixture proves this representation directly. An
isometry of gradient spans, extended to the initial displacement and any
orthogonal component, preserves every scalar product and the initial
distance. Thus it is legitimate for every realization in the statement.

Fix a block start t=4r, 0<=r<N/4. Before t put

    P_a = sum_(j<t,u_j=a_0) h_j*Gamma_j,
    P_b = sum_(j<t,u_j=b_0) h_j*Gamma_j, T=P_a+P_b.

    X_t = (x^c-sqrt(lambda)(P_a*a_0+P_b*b_0),

    A_x = <a_0,x^c>/sqrt(lambda)-P_a+beta_0*P_b,

The first two steps in the block have sizes l,k and both coarse directions
a_0. Their total amplitude-weighted mass is

    D_a = l*Gamma_t+k*Gamma_(t+1)>0.

    B_x+2(beta_0+e)A_x+[1+2(beta_0+e)](r_0+eT) >= 0.          (13)

    e<1/1000, 1/sqrt(lambda)<1001/1000,

       <= <b_0+2(beta_0+e)a_0,x^c>/sqrt(lambda)
                           +[1+2(beta_0+e)]r_0,

Both coefficients exceed 1/64: their lower bounds at 0<=e<=1/1000

## 5. Every consecutive odd-b return can grow only by O(eta K_path)

Let j=4r+3 and suppose j+4<=n. These are consecutive odd-b samples.

    j+1:   l on a_e, amplitude A_0=Gamma_(j+1),
    j+2:   k on a_o, amplitude C_0=Gamma_(j+2),
    j+3:   l on b_e, amplitude E_0=Gamma_(j+3),
    j+4:            b_o, amplitude D_0=Gamma_(j+4).

The physical H may be arbitrarily large. No amplitude ratios are imposed.
Temporarily set a=a_o, b=b_o, tau=<a,b>=z=-beta, and

    Z = -H B_0 b-l A_0 a_e,
    X = <b,Z>, Y=<a-tau*b,Z>.

By (18),(19),

    Y = -l A_0(p-z*w) <= 0.                                (22)

The ORIGINAL pair between j and j+2, together with (21), therefore gives

    C_0^2+B_0^2-2tau B_0 C_0
       <= (tau C_0-B_0)X+C_0 Y+e_0(C_0+B_0)
       <= (tau C_0-B_0)X+e_0(C_0+B_0),                     (23)

where e_0=4 eta K_path. Suppose Delta=D_0-B_0>0. The ORIGINAL pair
between j and j+4 similarly gives

    Delta[X-k C_0 tau-l E_0 p]
       >= Delta^2-e_0(D_0+B_0).

Consequently

    X >= Delta+k C_0 tau+l E_0 p-e_0(D_0+B_0)/Delta.         (24)

Substitute (24) into (23). Its coefficient tau C_0-B_0 is negative.
Putting M=B_0+beta C_0>0 gives exactly

    (1-k*tau^2)C_0^2 + tau(k-2)B_0 C_0+B_0^2
           +M Delta+M l E_0 p
       <= e_0(C_0+B_0)+M e_0(D_0+B_0)/Delta.                (25)

Every discarded term on the left is nonnegative: tau<0, k<2, p>0.
It follows from (19) that the left side is at least

    (C_0^2+B_0^2)/100 + M Delta.

On the right, D_0+B_0=Delta+2B_0, so it is at most

    e_0[(1+beta)C_0+2B_0] + (2e_0/Delta)B_0 M
       <= 4e_0 M + (3e_0/Delta)(C_0^2+B_0^2).             (26)

Here beta>=69/100 proves the first bound, and
B_0 M <= (3/2)(B_0^2+C_0^2) proves the second.
If Delta>=1024e_0, (26) is at most

    M Delta/256 + (3/1024)(C_0^2+B_0^2),

strictly less than the lower bound for (25). This is a contradiction.
Thus every such return obeys

    Gamma_(j+4) <= Gamma_j + d_amp,
    d_amp = 4096 eta K_path <= 2^22 eta R.                  (27)

This uses two pairs of ORIGINAL samples and their actual displacement

the same argument with zero error directly rules out Delta>0, so the
exact four-ray boundary has nonincreasing odd-b amplitudes.

## 8. The fixed finite class is nonempty

This is a nonvacuity construction, not a competitive lower witness. It also
shows that the obstruction does not mean the Gram itself is illegal.
For the SAME full-support weights (1) and the SAME actual word, put

    H_max=max_i h_i,
    kappa_chr=min(1,(min_i h_i-1)/4)>0,
    q_decay=kappa_chr/[8(H_max+kappa_chr+1)],
    Gamma_i=q_decay^i, f_i=(kappa_chr+1/2)Gamma_i^2.

All N characters are present and have positive weight, so the character
matrix is invertible. Indeed
sum_s chi_s(i)chi_s(j)=N for i=j and 0 otherwise, by pairing the sign
of a differing bit. Hence the Gram K_ij=<g_i,g_j> is positive definite.
Define

    b_i=(kappa_chr+1)Gamma_i^2+sum_(t<i)h_t K_ti,
    X_0=sum_i (K^(-1)b)_i g_i,
    X_i=X_0-sum_(t<i)h_t g_t.

These finite formulas fix one anchor before GD is run. They give
<X_i,g_i>=(kappa_chr+1)Gamma_i^2. For i<j direct expansion gives

    Q_ij=(kappa_chr+1-h_i)Gamma_i^2-kappa_chr Gamma_j^2
                          -sum_(i<t<j)h_t K_it-K_ij,
    Q_ji=(kappa_chr+1)Gamma_j^2-kappa_chr Gamma_i^2
                          +(h_i-1)K_ij+sum_(i<t<j)h_t K_jt.

Using |K_it|<=Gamma_i Gamma_t, q_decay<1/2 and h_i-1>=4kappa_chr,
the first residual divided by Gamma_i^2 is at most

    -3kappa_chr+H_max*q_decay/(1-q_decay)+q_decay
       <= -3kappa_chr+2(H_max+1)q_decay
       <= -11kappa_chr/4 < 0.

The second is at most

    -kappa_chr+(kappa_chr+1)q_decay^2
                             +H_max*q_decay/(1-q_decay)
       <= -kappa_chr+(2H_max+kappa_chr+1)q_decay
       <= -3kappa_chr/4 < 0.

For the optimum jet (0,0,0), Q_(i,star)=0 and
Q_(star,i)=-kappa_chr Gamma_i^2<0. Thus ALL original ordered pairs,
including the optimum and terminal, have been checked.

For an explicit one-function realization, let

    C_poly=conv(0,g_0,...,g_n),
    F_poly(X)=max_(v in C_poly) [<X,v>-||v||^2/2].

The unique maximizer is the nearest-point projection P_(C_poly)(X).
Its normal condition is <X-P_(C_poly)(X),v-P_(C_poly)(X)><=0 for every
v in C_poly. Adding this condition at X and Y proves that the projection
is 1-Lipschitz. Comparing the two maximizing expressions bounds the
first-order remainder for F_poly between 0 and ||X-Y||^2, so F_poly is
differentiable with gradient P_(C_poly), convex, finite, and 1-smooth.

At each prescribed sample the displayed residuals say
<X_i-g_i,g_j-g_i>=Q_ji<=0, and
<X_i-g_i,-g_i>=-kappa_chr Gamma_i^2<0. Thus the normal condition holds
on every vertex and their convex hull: grad F_poly(X_i)=g_i and
F_poly(X_i)=f_i. The fixed function therefore follows the stated GD.
It is nonnegative and minimized at zero. Its full minimizer set is
{Z:<Z,g_i><=0 for all i}, by the same normal condition at the zero vertex.
The true distance to this polar cone is positive and is the R used in
(3)--(7); no claim equates it with ||X_0||.
