## 1. Full input, function, values, and true distance

A child consists of a nonnegative finite word p of length ell, actual vectors
x_i,g_i for 0<=i<=ell, and the optimum sample x_*=g_*=0. Set

    K=conv(0,g_0,...,g_ell), y_i=x_i-g_i,
    x_(i+1)=x_i-p_i g_i,
    <g_j-g_i,y_i><=0 for every i,j,
    <g_i,y_i>>=0 for every i,
    x_ell=g_ell=w !=0.                                      (1)

Include ALL samples, even duplicated ones or those at zero steps. Nonzero
residuals y_i are permitted. Set

    A=1+sum p_i, R_i=sum_(t=i)^(ell-1) p_t,
    R^2=||x_0||^2, Delta=A^2||w||^2-R^2,
    kappa=A^2||w||^2/R^2.

The values and common function are specified, not freely shifted:

    F_K(x)=max_(g in K)(<x,g>-||g||^2/2),
    f_i=<x_i,g_i>-||g_i||^2/2, f_*=0.                        (2)

The maximizer is the unique Euclidean projection onto K: completing the
square proves this, and its exact optimality condition is (1). Adding the
two projection normals for x,x' proves
||P_K(x)-P_K(x')||^2<=<x-x',P_K(x)-P_K(x')>. Thus it is 1-Lipschitz.
Evaluation of the defining maxima at each other's maximizers bounds the
differentiability remainder by ||x-x'||^2. Consequently F_K is finite,
convex, differentiable and 1-smooth, with gradient P_K and values (2).

The minimizer set is exactly K^polar={z:<z,g><=0 for every g in K}.
The forward implication follows by using epsilon*g in (2) and sending
epsilon down to0; the reverse implication follows directly from (2).
The endpoint and GD identity give

    x_0=w+sum_(i<ell) p_i g_i.                              (3)

Hence <x_0,z><=0 for every minimizer z, so 0 is a closest minimizer and
dist(x_0,argmin F_K)^2=R^2. No arbitrary chosen-optimum normalization is used.
For the original signed interpolation residual of fact1252b8823bb5a290,
substitution of (2) gives the identity

    Q_ij=<g_i-g_j,y_j>.                                    (4)

This includes Q_i*=0 and Q_*i=-<g_i,y_i><=0. Thus checking (1) checks EVERY
original interpolation inequality, including both optimum incidences.

The input contains these vectors and values, not merely Delta or kappa.

## 2. A real cross Gram block and its exact legality conditions
