# C60: a nonlinear two-state legal trajectory with dense turning gradients

Status: **new explicit construction and scoped calculations; UNVERIFIED**.
No solve or verifier call is used. The numerical allocation is closed.
This branch follows the finite C59 reconstruction by seeking an analytic
correlated core. It does not assume that the C59 data belong to this family.

The construction works for every finite nonnegative schedule, including
zeros and arbitrarily large entries, with one fixed globally convex
1-smooth function. Its all-pair law and true closest-minimizer distance
are explicit. The quantitative payoff on the critical H_m remains open.

## 1. The global function, its gradients, and its minimizers

In R^2 let

    C_par={(t,s): s>=t^2/sqrt(2)},
    F_par(X)=sup_(g in C_par) [<X,g>-||g||^2/2].      (1)

The set is closed, convex and nonempty, and contains0. Completing the
square shows that the maximizer in (1) is the Euclidean projection of X
onto C_par. It exists and is unique because the squared-distance objective
is coercive and strictly convex. Thus F_par is finite and convex.

For completeness write p(X) for that projection. Its elementary normal
condition is

    <X-p(X),g-p(X)><=0 for every g in C_par.

Applying this twice and adding gives

    ||p(X)-p(Y)||^2<=<p(X)-p(Y),X-Y>.

Thus p is1-Lipschitz. Comparing the two maximizers in (1) gives

    0<=F_par(Y)-F_par(X)-<p(X),Y-X>
        <=<p(Y)-p(X),Y-X><=||Y-X||^2.

Consequently F_par is differentiable, its gradient is p, and it is
1-smooth. This proof supplies the needed global property directly.

The minimizers are exactly

    argmin F_par={(0,y):y<=0}.                      (2)

Indeed F_par>=0 by the feasible vector0. For X=(0,y), y<=0, every g=(t,s)
in C_par has <X,g>=ys<=0, so the maximum in (1) is0. Conversely, if
the vertical coordinate y>0, taking g=(0,s) with small s>0 makes the
objective positive. If the horizontal coordinate x is nonzero and y<=0,
take g=(t,t^2/sqrt(2)) with sufficiently small nonzero t of the sign of x:
the linear term xt dominates the terms of quadratic and quartic order.
This again makes the objective positive. These cases prove (2).

This F_par is not a convex quadratic: its minimizer set is a ray, whereas
the nonempty minimizer set of an unconstrained convex quadratic is an
affine subspace.

## 2. Coordinates that make every interpolation pair explicit

For a real r and v>=0, put

    u=v+r^2,
    X(r,v)=(r(1+v+r^2), -v/sqrt(2)),
    G(r)=(r,r^2/sqrt(2)).                           (3)

Then X-G=u(r,-1/sqrt(2)). For any (t,s) in C_par,

    <X-G,(t,s)-G>
      =u[r(t-r)-(s-r^2/sqrt(2))/sqrt(2)]
      <=-(u/2)(t-r)^2<=0.

The normal condition therefore proves that G(r) is the actual gradient
of F_par at X(r,v). Direct substitution into (1) gives

    F_par(X(r,v))=r^2(1+v)/2+3r^4/4.                (4)

For any two sampled states r_i,v_i and r_j,v_j, with the actual values
from (4), the complete signed interpolation residual is

    Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<G_i,X_j-X_i>+||G_i-G_j||^2/2
        =<X_j-G_j,G_i-G_j>
        =-(u_j/2)(r_i-r_j)^2<=0.                   (5)

The first equality after Q follows by substituting
f_i=<X_i,G_i>-||G_i||^2/2. The second uses the displayed parabolic
coordinates. This proves EVERY ordered pair simultaneously for one
function; no local, adjacent, or cycle relaxation is being used.

For an initial nonzero r_0 and v_0>=0, the closest minimizer in (2) is

    X_star=(0,-v_0/sqrt(2)),
    R_0^2=dist(X_0,argmin F_par)^2
            =r_0^2(1+v_0+r_0^2)^2.                (6)

The optimum sample fits the same formulas with r_star=0, v_star=v_0,
u_star=v_0. Thus (5) includes both of its directions. Choosing the origin
instead would generally overestimate the true initial distance; (6) is
the normalization used throughout.

## 3. The exact GD transition, with no interpolation optimization

For ANY step h>=0, starting from state(r,v), define

    v_plus=v+h r^2,
    r_plus^3+(1+v_plus)r_plus
                      =r(1+v+r^2-h).              (7)

The cubic's left side is strictly increasing from minus infinity to
plus infinity because its derivative is1+v_plus+3r_plus^2>=1. Thus (7)
defines exactly one real r_plus, by an explicit scalar algebraic rule.
There is no nearest-feasible-trajectory problem hidden in this definition.

The vertical coordinate of X-hG is -v_plus/sqrt(2), and its horizontal
coordinate is the right side of (7). Hence

    X(r_plus,v_plus)=X(r,v)-h G(r).

This proves actual GD for the same global function. In particular h=0
leaves the state unchanged, and every finite positive step gives finite
new state. All v_i remain nonnegative, regardless of gradient sign changes.

For a supplied finite nonnegative schedule h_0,...,h_(n-1), iterate (7)
from any r_0!=0,v_0>=0. Its exact normalized payoff is

    P_par(h;r_0,v_0)
       =[r_n^2(1+v_n)+(3/2)r_n^4]
                        /[r_0^2(1+v_0+r_0^2)^2].  (8)

The function (1), all values(4), gradients(3), positions(3), and initial
distance(6) are fixed together. Thus (8) is an actual lower witness for
W_n(h), albeit not yet a profile-strength one on the critical schedules.

Some immediate identities expose the changing geometry:

    u_plus-u=(h-1)r^2+r_plus^2,
    X_plus,horizontal=X_horizontal[1-h/(1+u)].      (9)

In particular, for schedules with all h>=1, the normal parameter u is
nondecreasing, but r and the horizontal gradient can turn repeatedly.
The Hessian direction changes with r; this is not a fixed linear spectral
transcript. The vertical state accumulates h r^2 rather than discarding
previous turns.

## 4. Exact dense-jump calibration on a different schedule

Take any sigma in(0,1), v_0>=0, and define

    U_0=1+v_0+sigma,
    rho=(1+sigma)/(1-sigma),
    h_i=2U_0 rho^i/(1-sigma),
    r_i=(-1)^i sqrt(sigma),
    v_i=U_0 rho^i-1-sigma.                         (10)

These v_i are nonnegative. Substitution into (7) gives
v_(i+1)-v_i=h_i sigma and
r_(i+1)(1+v_(i+1)+sigma)=r_i(1+v_i+sigma-h_i),
so (10) is an exact trajectory of F_par. Its payoff is

    P_par=rho^n/U_0+sigma/(2U_0^2).                 (11)

This calibration is legal and can have large payoff. It is a geometric
step sequence, NOT the critical ruler word, so (11) proves no claim about
H_m or the original profile. It only checks that the mechanism supports
correlated turns without a feasibility repair.

There are n nonzero collinear gradient jumps in (10):

    G_i-G_(i-1)=(2(-1)^i sqrt(sigma),0), 1<=i<=n.

Their Gram J has trace4sigma n and twice the absolute off-diagonal sum
4sigma n(n-1). For any Gram realizable with at most k nonzero jumps in
each orthonormal coordinate, Cauchy--Schwarz coordinate by coordinate
implies2sum_(i<j)|J_ij|<=(k-1)trace(J). Hence this trajectory requires
some coordinate with at least n jumps in every orthonormal realization.
Dense chronological correlation does not require a large ambient dimension.

For a completely rational parameter example, sigma=1/4 and v_0=0 give
rho=5/3, h_0=10/3, h_1=50/9, and r_i=(-1)^i/2. Formula (11) is then
an exact expression, not a numerical simulation.

## 5. Two simple parameter limits do not yet give the desired mechanism

First fix a finite schedule and fix v_0=A>=0. Let r_0=t tend to zero.
Induction in (7) gives

    v_i=A+O(t^2),
    r_i=t product_(j<i)[1-h_j/(1+A)]+O(t^3).

At each induction step, the inverse cubic has nonzero derivative1+A
at zero, and its cubic term is O(t^3), giving the displayed expansion.
Thus the payoff tends to the ordinary scalar quadratic value

    [1/(1+A)] product_(j<n)[1-h_j/(1+A)]^2.         (12)

The implicit constants here depend on the entire fixed finite schedule.
They have NOT been bounded uniformly along growing H_m. Consequently
(12) is a calibration and an explanation of why an infinitesimal core
does not itself add a new mechanism; it is not a class obstruction.

Second put S=sum h_i and sigma=r_0^2. If v_0>=S, no horizontal sign flip
occurs and |r_(i+1)|<=|r_i|. Indeed h_i<=v_i and the right side of (7)
has the sign of r_i. Evaluating the increasing cubic at r_i shows that
its excess over that right side is h_i r_i(1+r_i^2), with the appropriate
sign, so the claimed decrease in absolute value follows. Hence

    v_n<=v_0+S sigma,
    P_par <= [1+v_0+S sigma+(3/2)sigma]
                                  /(1+v_0+sigma)^2. (13)

Along any growing-horizon choices with
v_0/[S(1+sigma)] tending to infinity, this gives S P_par tending to zero.
Thus placing the initial state arbitrarily far down the flat minimizer
direction also loses the desired scale. This rules out only the displayed
parameter regime; intermediate coupled regimes remain open.

## 6. The natural geometric calibration is small on the entire critical word

One tempting choice is to match the geometric growth ratio rho in(10)
to the physical critical parameter c_m. This means

    m>=128, epsilon=1/m,
    c=(1+sqrt(2))(1+epsilon), a=(1-epsilon)/c,
    sigma=(c-1)/(c+1), r_0=sqrt(sigma), v_0=0.       (14)

For THIS particular starting rule, the full actual H_m trajectory obeys

    P_par(H_m;r_0,0)
              < (2500/m^2) 2^(-2^m).              (15)

The same is true with the opposite initial horizontal sign, by symmetry.
This does not bound the family over other choices of r_0,v_0.

Here is a proof that tracks all later queried states, rather than inferring

## 7. The concrete remaining critical-word problem

For the actual critical word fix, within each horizon m,

    c_m=(1+sqrt(2))(1+1/m), a_m=(1-1/m)/c_m,
    H_0=(), H_(j+1)=(H_j,1+a_m c_m^j,H_j).

Let T_h denote the explicit two-state transition (7). The full block map
is defined EXACTLY by

    Phi_0=identity,
    Phi_(j+1)=Phi_j composed with T_(1+a_m c_m^j)
                                     composed with Phi_j.

The central task in this branch is to select explicit r_0(m),v_0(m), or
prove a useful uniform upper bound for this mechanism, while controlling
the exact payoff (8) under Phi_m. The model is already an actual legal
function for every such selection, so no separate interpolation optimization
is required. What is missing is the QUANTITATIVE estimate on that nonlinear
recursion. Neither (11), the small-amplitude calibration, nor (13) fills it.

An open subgoal is to compare the increase in v across both copies of a
dyadic block with the horizontal sign and gain at its central bridge.
The state v must remain shared between the copies; restarting it, freezing
the curvature, or replacing the two copies by independent instances would
change the function and lose the construction.

## 8. Source grounding and verification scope

Jiang, Yue and Zhou, arXiv:1911.12545v2 (2021), Theorem2.2, gives the
Euclidean projection onto S={(x,y):||x||^2<=y}: inside S it is the point,
and outside it is(x/(1+mu),y+mu/2), where mu>=max(0,-2y) is the unique
solution of ||x||^2/(1+mu)^2=y+mu/2. The paper's surrounding definition
of S and full proof were read. It supplies a closely matched projection
mechanism, not a GD complexity result for the present function. Its
cubic-regularization convergence theorem is not invoked.

Sections1--6 derive the two-dimensional specialization and all required
properties directly. No external theorem is used as a black box, and no
unverified earlier project result is a premise. The finite C59 data merely
motivated seeking dense turning gradients. Every new statement here remains
a draft until accepted by the verifier; no claim is made that this family
solves the all-schedule exact-profile problem.
