## Static model and exact legality

For positive radii r_- and r_+, let phi(0)=0 and
phi'(s)=max(-r_-,min(s,r_+)). Thus phi=s^2/2 on the core,
phi=r_+ s-r_+^2/2 to the right, and phi=-r_- s-r_-^2/2 to the left.
Let A be a symmetric PSD matrix, u_j unit vectors, and alpha_j>0.
Define one static function

    F(x)=x^T A x/2 + sum_j alpha_j phi_j(u_j^T x),
    C=A+sum_j alpha_j u_j u_j^T,   0<=C<=I.

Every scalar clip is monotone and 1-Lipschitz. For any x,y its difference
is theta_j u_j^T(x-y), with 0<=theta_j<=1. Therefore

    grad F(x)-grad F(y)=(A+sum_j alpha_j theta_j u_j u_j^T)(x-y).

The matrix in parentheses lies between zero and I, so its norm is at
most one. This proves global 1-smoothness; convexity also follows termwise
from monotonicity of each scalar derivative and A>=0. No local Hessian
rescaling of the physical steps is permitted or needed.

Every summand is nonnegative, and phi_j(s)=0 iff s=0. Consequently

    argmin F=ker A intersect intersection_j u_j^perp=ker C,
    min F=0.

The initial distance is the norm of the orthogonal projection onto
(ker C)^perp. When C is positive definite this is exactly ||x_0||;
the one-dimensional calibration also has its unique minimizer at zero.
All matrices below have explicit rational certificates, with no eigenvalue
clipping. A small eigenvalue is not silently removed from the distance.

Useful exact planar directions are

    u_+=(4/5,3/5), u_-=(4/5,-3/5), v=(0,1).

Weights alpha_+=alpha_-=25/32 give total matrix diag(1,9/16).
Adding weight 7/16 on v gives I. With A=mu I and multiplying every
ridge weight by 1-mu, 0<=mu<1, these become respectively
diag(1,mu+(1-mu)9/16) and I. The background floor can be zero or
arbitrarily weak. A fixed gate change changes one rank-one matrix and
can therefore change the principal axes; no independent switching matrix
is supplied to gradient descent.

Here is a direct sufficient test for the complete accepted ray family to
equal K. For i<j put P=sum_(t<i)h_t and D=sum_(i<t<j)h_t. If

    (h_i-1)(h_j-1)<(1+P)(1+D)                 (R)

for every i<j, no feasible accepted ray selection has two checkpoints.
Indeed its first two checkpoint gradients are
m_0=1/(1+P), m_1=(h_i-1)m_0/(1+D), with old offset P*m_0.

## Exact finite-word ray comparison, with no exponential enumeration

Write r_j=a*c^j and M_j=1+sum H_j. Then

    M_0=1, M_(j+1)=2*M_j+r_j,
    t_j=r_j/M_j, t_0=a, t_(j+1)=c*t_j/(2+t_j).

For m>=6, c>1+sqrt(2) and beta<1 imply a<c-2. Hence t_j increases
and stays below c-2. Also

    beta-(c-2) = 3-(1+sqrt(2))-(2+sqrt(2))/m
       >= (10-7*sqrt(2))/6 >0,

so t_j>=a>(c-2)/c.

For any two chronological indices i<j with levels u,v, define the ratio
R_ij in (R) with the right side as its denominator. If u>=v, the prefix
before i contains H_u and the gap before j contains H_v; thus
R_ij<=t_u*t_v. The largest level m-1 occurs only once, so at least one
of u,v is at most m-2. It follows that

    R_ij<=t_(m-1)*t_(m-2)<c*t_(m-2)^2.

If u<v, both the prefix before i and the gap from i to j contain H_u.
Their total mass is at least sum H_v-(1+r_u), since the prefix before
j contains H_v. Consequently, putting x=1+P and y=1+D,
x,y>=M_u and x+y>=M_v-r_u. From
(x-M_u)(y-M_u)>=0 we obtain

    x*y>=M_u*(M_v-M_u-r_u).

Let d=v-u>=1 and t=t_u. Directly iterating the recurrence for M gives

    (M_v-M_u-r_u)/M_u
      =2^d-1+t*((c^d-2^d)/(c-2)-1)
      >=c^(d-1)+(c-1)*(2^d-2)/c>=c^(d-1).

The first inequality uses t>=(c-2)/c and the nonnegative coefficient
((c^d-2^d)/(c-2)-1). Therefore R_ij<=c*t_u^2<=c*t_(m-2)^2.
Equality in the complete maximum occurs at the first level-(m-2) bridge
and the immediately following level-(m-1) bridge: both intervening
prefix/gap masses are sum H_(m-2). Thus

    max_(i<j) R_ij = c*t_(m-2)^2.                         (C95.1)

This proves the full pair test symbolically; it does not rely on sampled
pair scans. Here are short rational upper certificates for the three
authorized horizons. Monotonicity of c*t/(2+t) in both positive variables
allows use of the displayed upper parameters throughout.

* m=6: c<17/6 and a<3/10. Four recurrence steps give
  t_4<83521/144462<7/12. Thus c*t_4^2<833/864<1.
  The intermediate bounds are 17/46,289/654,4913/9582,83521/144462.
* m=7: c<83/30 and a<5/16. Bounds for t_1,...,t_5 are respectively
  3/8,7/16,1/2,83/150,3/5. For the last step,
  6889/11490<3/5; hence c*t_5^2<747/750<1.
* m=8: c<68/25 and a<13/40. The upper recurrence has reciprocal at
  its sixth step equal to
  25/18+(395/234)*(25/34)^6. This is greater than 33/20 because
  (25/34)^3>79/200 and (79/200)^2>611/3950. Therefore t_6<20/33,
  and c*t_6^2<27200/27225<1.

All parameter enclosures follow by squaring positive rational bounds for
sqrt(2). In particular sqrt(2)<10/7, sqrt(2)>29/21 prove the m=6
bounds; sqrt(2)<341/240 and sqrt(2)>7/5 prove m=7;
sqrt(2)<319/225 and sqrt(2)>163/117 prove m=8.
Combining (C95.1) with the accepted ray feasibility conditions proves

    F_ray_(2^m-1)(H_m)=K_(2^m-1)(H_m), m=6,7,8.           (C95.2)

No m>=128 result is used here. This is a new UNVERIFIED finite symbolic
comparison derived jointly, not an accepted graph fact.

For completeness the largest single-cut term is the central cut. For a
fixed level u put p=1+P, q=1+T; both are at least M_u and
p+q=M_m-r_u. The denominator p^2*(2q-1) has no interior minimum on
that interval: its derivative is 2p*(2*(M_m-r_u)-3p-1), with at most
one interior critical point, a maximum. The two endpoint denominators
are ordered by monotonicity of z^2/(2z-1) for z>=1, so the earliest
occurrence of level u is best. Its value is

    t_u^2/[2*M_m-2*M_u-2*r_u-1].

The numerator increases and the positive denominator decreases with u.
Thus the central level m-1 maximizes it. In particular K is exactly
the larger of the global Huber term and the central single-cut term;
no choice of untested checkpoints is hidden in the numerical reference.

## Exact capture criterion for a saturated core curvature budget

Assume C=A+sum_j alpha_j u_j*u_j^T=I, and let D be the closed convex
intersection of all core strips -r_(j,-)<=u_j^T x<=r_(j,+).
At every point in D, grad F(x)=x and 2F(x)=||x||^2.

Fix any k>=1 with k<=m, retaining the same enclosing-m parameters. A
run of H_k starting at z stays in D at every query AND at the terminal
point if and only if both z and -a*z lie in D. In that event its exact
iterate after t steps is

    x_t=(-1)^t*beta^t*c^(-s_2(t))*z, 0<=t<=2^k-1,         (C95.3)

where s_2(t) counts binary ones and s_2(0)=0. In particular

    x_end=-beta^(2^k-1)*c^(-k)*z,
    2F(x_end)=beta^(2*(2^k-1))*c^(-2k)*||z||^2.           (C95.4)

Proof: the level at step t-1 is v_2(t), the exponent of 2 dividing t.
The identity sum_(j=1)^t v_2(j)=t-s_2(t) follows either by summing
floor(t/2^q), or by writing t as a sum of distinct powers of 2. Multiplying
the actual scalar factors -a*c^(v_2(j)) yields (C95.3). For positive t,
its absolute multiplier is at most beta/c=a because s_2(t)>=1 and
beta^t<=beta. When t is even the multiplier is nonnegative and at most1;
when t is odd it lies between -a and0. Thus every candidate x_t lies
on one of the segments [0,z] and [0,-a*z], both contained in D. Induction
now proves the actual recursion never leaves D, including the terminal
point. Necessity follows from the first actual step, whose multiplier is
-a when z is in D. At t=2^k-1, s_2(t)=k, giving (C95.4).

The reflection condition is essential for asymmetric ridges. Take one
scalar ridge with r_-=1,r_+=4,A=0,alpha=1,z=4. On any m>=6,
a>=5/(7*(1+sqrt(2)))>1/4. The first actual point -4a lies below -1.
The objective is legal and z is in the core, but all-core invariance fails.
This is an analytical counterexample, not a numerical case or a new fact.

## A scalable, conditional weak-background obstruction

The preceding criterion has an explicit normalization cost. Let d0=||x0||,
which is the true initial distance because C=I, and put

    Gcap=sum_j alpha_j*max(r_(j,-),r_(j,+)),
    hmax=max h_i, Gcap*S_m<=Ccap*d0,
    0<=A<=2*I/hmax,

where Ccap>=0 is a constant independent of m. The radii may be asymmetric.
Since ||I-h_i*A||<=1 and the clipped-ridge gradient has norm at most Gcap,
the true recursion gives ||x_i||<=d0+Gcap*sum_(j<i)h_j<=(1+Ccap)*d0.

Suppose the actual state z at the start of the FINAL H_k suffix satisfies
z,-a*z in D. No condition is imposed on any earlier queried regions.
By (C95.4), the normalized payoff obeys

    V<= (1+Ccap)^2*beta^(2*(2^k-1))*c^(-2k),
    V/K <=2*(1+Ccap)^2*c^(m-2k)*beta^(2*(2^k-1)).         (C95.5)

Here K>=1/(2S_m+1), and M_m<=c^m: the latter follows by induction from
M_(j+1)=2M_j+a*c^j and 2+a<c, proved above.

For m>=16 take k=ceil(log_2(m^2)). Then k<=m, 2^k>=m^2, and the final
suffix has fewer than 2m^2 steps. The elementary inequality log(1-1/m)
<=-1/m and c<3 imply

    V/K <=2*(1+Ccap)^2*exp(2/m)*(3/exp(2))^m ->0.        (C95.6)

The ratio 3/exp(2) is below1, since exp(2)>1+2+2=5. Thus a family meeting
these hypotheses that remains scalar-competitive cannot pass this capture
test at the start of that final suffix for infinitely many unbounded horizons.
This prices weak (even zero) backgrounds and arbitrarily many preceding
gate changes. It does not prove that capture occurs. Cases that stay
outside D, fail the reflected-point condition, have C!=I, or violate the
displayed cap/background hypotheses remain outside the conclusion.
