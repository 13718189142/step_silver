## 3. One compact convex set and EVERY original normal inequality

Let K=conv{0,u,v,w,k}. The vectors u,v are independent and
z*u+a*v+w=0 with all three coefficients positive. Therefore0 lies in the
interior of the triangle conv{u,v,w}, and hence in the interior of K.

Use the proposed full trajectory

 x0=(a+1)u, g0=u;
 x1=u,     g1=u;
 x2=-z*u,  g2=v;
 x3=w,     g3=w;
 x4=-d*w=4k, g4=k;
 x5=(5/2)k,  g5=k.                                 (10)

Every equation x_(i+1)=x_i-h_i*g_i follows immediately from (1) and (5).
To prove g_i=Proj_K(x_i), it suffices to check
<r-g_i,x_i-g_i><=0 for all vertices r of K. Linear interpolation then
gives it for every r in K. These checks cover every ordered original pair,
including optimum and terminal pairs, with one common K.

At samples1 and3 the residual x_i-g_i is zero. At sample0 it is a*u.
We have <v,u>/U=kappa<0, <w,u>/U=r_u<0 and
<k,u>/U=-(d/4)r_u<(2.57/4)*.1<1; the vertices0 andu also satisfy the
normal inequality. This checks sample0.

At sample2 its residual is y2=-z*u-v. In units of U, the inequalities
for vertices0 andu are respectively

 <-v,y2>/U=z*kappa+1/2<0,
 <u-v,y2>/U=-z+(z-1)kappa+1/2<=-1/2.

The vertex w has equality by (7), and the vertex v has equality trivially.
Write T=<v,y2>=(-z*kappa-1/2)U>0. By (7), <w,y2>=T. Thus
<k-v,y2>=-(1+d/4)T<0, which checks the last vertex.

At samples4 and5 the residuals are3k and(3/2)k. It is enough to check

It follows from (9), since d*J/4>2.55*.169/4>.1. Every original
normal inequality has now been checked without dropping remote pairs.

## 4. A global smooth function with the correct initial distance

Define

 F_t(x)=max_(r in K){<x,r>-||r||^2/2}.              (11)

The maximizer is unique and equals Proj_K(x), by completing the square.
If P and P' are the projections of x and x', their normal inequalities
give ||P-P'||^2<=<x-x',P-P'>, so the projection is1-Lipschitz. Evaluating
the maxima in (11) at each other's maximizers yields

 0<=F_t(x')-F_t(x)-<P,x'-x>
      <=<P'-P,x'-x><=||x'-x||^2.

This proves differentiability with gradient P and1-Lipschitz gradient.
It is finite and convex because K is compact and (11) is a maximum of
affine functions of x. Since0 is interior to K, for every x!=0 a sufficiently
small positive multiple r=epsilon*x belongs to K and makes
<x,r>-||r||^2/2 positive. Therefore F_t(0)=0 and0 is its UNIQUE minimizer.
The initial point in (10) is(1,0), so its true initial distance is exactly1.

Section3 now proves that (10) is the actual GD trajectory of this single
global function. At x5=(5/2)k, (11) gives F_t(x5)=2||k||^2. Thus
2F_t(x5)=4||k||^2=d^2||w||^2/4, proving (2). Replacing F_t by L*F_t
for any L>0 preserves the project's normalized performance under h_i/L.

## 6. What this explains and does not explain

The constructed path increases total step mass, and its tested finite case
passes the earlier necessary scalar screens. A planar convex body now gives
a direct, exact obstruction on the whole interval0<t<=1/50, even when the
entire upper certificate is reselected. The construction uses a true common
projection body and all original pairs; it is not a formal Gram test alone.

It does not claim to attain the full SDP optimum or to require dimension2
in every worst case. No automatic recursive lift to n15,n31 or unbounded n
is established here. The separate common-offset mass argument covers the
asymptotic limitation of that particular dense family; arbitrary coordinated
redistribution and the original minimax conjecture remain open.
