## 1. Exact coefficients at every depth

Set rho=1+sqrt(2), n_j=2^j-1, and define the undeformed word by

 H_0=(), H_(j+1)=(H_j,1+rho^(j-1),H_j).

Its mass is M_j=1+sum H_j=rho^j, since M_(j+1)=2M_j+rho^(j-1).

 x_i=x_0-sum_(t<i)h_t g_t,

Here is one finite-node array L^(j), without optimum entries. At depth0 it
is zero. In a join let p=n_j, q=2n_j+1, M=rho^j, d=M/rho, and b=1+d.

w_i=h_i for p<i<q and w_q=1. For every p<i<=q add rho w_i to L_(p,i)
and to L_(q,i), omitting the diagonal entry L_(q,q). Add M to L_(q,p).
All coefficients are explicitly nonnegative. Repeated contributions add.

Define

 J_j=2sum_(i<n_j)h_i(f_i-f_(n_j))
                       +sum_(i<n_j)h_i(1-h_i)||g_i||^2.

The full finite identity is

 J_j=sum_(i!=k)L^(j)_ik C_ik+M_j(M_j-1)||g_(n_j)||^2.       (1)

Here is an induction with every nontrivial cancellation displayed. It is
zero=zero at j=0. In a join write g=g_p, e=g_q,
P=sum_(p<i<q)h_i g_i, W=P+e, and S=M-1. The quadratic parts of the two
weighted fans, before multiplication by rho, are respectively

                                      +sum_right h_i(h_i-1)||g_i||^2.

(1+2d)||g||^2-||e||^2+2<g,W>.
These formulas follow by expanding the definitions and using

rho^2 times the right child. Their value coefficients are

 f_p: 2M(rho-1)=2(S+b),

They therefore equal all value coefficients of J_(j+1)-J_left-rho^2 J_right.
After using both child identities, the g diagonal left over is

 M(M-1)-d(1+d)-M(1+2d-rho)=0,

and the e diagonal is
rho^2 M(M-1)+(rho+1)M=(rho M)(rho M-1).
This proves (1) at every depth.

## 2. Full coordinates and exact singular conditions

Let N=n+1, e the vector of N ones, e_n the last coordinate, and
V=I+T with T_it=h_t for t<i. For an arbitrary real N by N array A put

 b=Ae, a=A^T e-2e_n,
 G=sym(AV)-e_n e_n^T, sym(D)=(D+D^T)/2.

The actual multipliers are Lambda_ij=-A_ij (i!=j), Lambda_(i,star)=b_i,
Lambda_(star,i)=a_i. All actual signs and flows hold precisely when

 A_ij<=0 (i!=j), b>=0, a>=0.                              (4)

Expanding Q, or first canceling its value coefficients by these flows,
gives the complete matrix

 Mcert(q,A,h)=[[q,-b^T/2],[-b/2,G]].                       (5)

At either baseline put w0=(h_0,...,h_(n-1),tau), where tau=M or t,
and q0=1/v or1/t, as appropriate. Make the invertible fixed-coordinate
substitution x_0=z+w0^T g. At any nearby parameters the matrix becomes

 [[q,r^T],[r,D]],
 r=q w0-b/2,
 D=G+q w0w0^T-(w0b^T+bw0^T)/2.

For q>0 its PSD condition is EXACTLY

                    D-r r^T/q >=0.                     (6)

At the baseline r=D=0. If q,A,h are analytic in s, write
r=s r1+s^2 r2+..., D=s D1+s^2 D2+.... Then D1>=0 is necessary, but
on ker D1 one also needs D2-r1 r1^T/q0>=0. At any subsequent singular
block [[B,C],[C^T,E]], the exact conditions are B>=0,
range(C) contained in range(B), and E-C^T B^dagger C>=0. Here B^dagger
inverts B on its range and is zero on its kernel. To see the range
condition, a vector in ker B must have zero mixed product with every
other vector under a PSD form; completing squares on range B then gives
the last condition. Thus there is no license to discard mixed blocks.

At an initially zero A_ij, a one-sided feasible derivative requires
dot A_ij<=0; at an initially zero component of a or b, the corresponding
derivative must be nonnegative. Positive coefficients allow signed
variations only while their FINAL values satisfy (4).

## 3. An explicit dense rank-one transport, with no unknown PSD

Here we attempt continuation from the sharp baseline (2). The chosen
physical path is exactly the assignment, with epsilon=s/m:

 c=rho(1+epsilon), a=(1-epsilon)/c,
 H_(j+1)=(H_j,1+a c^j,H_j), j<m.

Let h be that word, M=1+sum h, v=2M-1, q=1/v, and
p=V^(-1)e. Thus p_0=1 and p_(i+1)=(1-h_i)p_i.
In a neighborhood of epsilon=0 define

 mu=1-sqrt(1-v p_n^2),
 wphys=(h_0,...,h_(n-1),M),
 delta=(mu-wphys^T p)/h_0,
 w=wphys+delta(e_0-e_1).                                (7)

This is analytic at zero, since there 1-v p_n^2=(M-1)^2/M^2>0,
and delta(0)=0. It satisfies e^T w=v and w^T p=mu. All w_i are

Put S0=e_n e_n^T+q w w^T and define

 U=[e,p], H=U^T U,

 Kpart=R H^(-1)U^T-U H^(-1)R^T
                             -U H^(-1)(U^T R)H^(-1)U^T.

The two columns of U are independent because h_0>0. Directly,
U^T R is skew: its first diagonal uses sum w=v=1+2sum h, the second
uses q mu(2-mu)=p_n^2, and its mixed entries sum to zero using Vp=e.
It follows by multiplication that Kpart is skew and Kpart U=R.

At the undeformed baseline let A0 be the exact array from (2), V0 its
V, and K0=(A0 V0-V0^T A0^T)/2. Define the ACTUAL proposed full array

 K=Kpart+(I-P)K0(I-P),  A=(S0+K)V^(-1).                  (8)

entries. It preserves A(0)=A0. Indeed every skew matrix with KU=R has

K0 at zero by this decomposition. Also

 Ae=2q w, A^T e=2e_n, sym(AV)=e_n e_n^T+q w w^T.

Consequently the FULL matrix (5) is identically

## 5. All-depth stiffness of any rank-one continuation

There is a separate quantitative necessity that does not fix the two-weight
choice (7) or the free skew part. It describes rank-one continuation at the
sharp Huber rate, not arbitrary PSD continuations.

At depth m let N=2^m and M0=rho^m. For an integer i let pop(i) be the
number of ones in its binary expansion. On the undeformed Silver word,

 p_i=(-1)^i rho^(-pop(i)),
 dot p_i=-(i+pop(i))p_i,                                (11)

where dots denote derivatives in epsilon=s/m at zero. To verify this,
sum_(t=1)^i nu_2(t)=i-pop(i): summing floor(i/2^k) over k gives the
left side and summing the binary expansion gives the right side.
The product of h_t-1 therefore has exponent -pop(i). Under the physical
path the logarithmic derivative of h_t-1 is nu_2(t+1)-2, whose sum is
-i-pop(i). This proves (11), including p_n=-1/M0 and
dot p_n=(N+m-1)/M0.

Let D=rho^2+2=2rho+3. The physical mass has exact formula

 M(epsilon)=A(epsilon)c^m+(1-A(epsilon))2^m,
 A(epsilon)=a/(c-2).

Here A(0)=1, dot A(0)=-D, and hence

                    dot M=M0(m-D)+D N.                (12)

Suppose ANY differentiable full certificate, with every pair available,
has q=1/(2M(epsilon)-1), a rank-one remainder q||x_0-w^Tg||^2, and
starts at (2). No multiplier formula or support pattern is assumed.
On the actual full-curvature quadratic f(x)=x^2/2, all Q_ij are zero,
x_0=1 and g_i=p_i. Thus

 q(1-w^T p)^2=q-p_n^2.

The baseline selects the analytic minus branch, so w^T p=mu in (7).
Let dot wphys=(dot h_0,...,dot h_(n-1),dot M), and let
zeta=dot w-dot wphys. Differentiating the preceding scalar equality gives

 p^T zeta=(dot M-M0(N+m-1))/(M0-1)=:Delta_m.             (13)

For detail, dot mu=[dot M-(2M0-1)(N+m-1)]/[M0(M0-1)].
Also wphys^T p=1+(M-1)p_n, whose derivative is
-dot M/M0+(M0-1)(N+m-1)/M0. Subtracting gives (13).

Tightness at q=1/(2M-1) additionally forces every optimum-source
multiplier to vanish: on the attaining Huber trajectory every Q_(star,i)
is strictly negative, while all finite Q and all Q_(i,star) are zero.
The PSD certificate must have zero value there, so complementarity gives
this assertion. Then A^T e=2e_n and sum w=2M-1, whence sum zeta=0.
Since max_i p_i=1 and min_i p_i=-1/rho, this also yields

 sum_i |zeta_i| >=sqrt(2)|Delta_m|.                    (14)

Indeed subtract the midpoint of the range of p before applying the
triangle inequality, and use sum zeta=0. The two-weight derivative (7)
attains (14), so its failure in Section4 is not caused by excessive
total redistribution in this particular norm.

More significantly, put w0=(h_0,...,h_(n-1),M0). Since all h_i>1,
h_i|p_i|=|p_i|+|p_(i+1)|. Also
sum_(i=0)^n |p_i|=(1+1/rho)^m=(sqrt(2))^m. Therefore

 sum_i (w0)_i |p_i|=2(sqrt(2))^m-1/M0.

It follows that every such rank-one continuation satisfies

 max_i |zeta_i|/(w0)_i
       >= |Delta_m|/[2(sqrt(2))^m-1/M0].               (15)

By (12), Delta_m/N tends to -1 as m tends to infinity. Consequently,
in the original s parameter the right side of (15) is asymptotic to

                         (sqrt(2))^m/(2m).            (16)

Thus no horizon-uniform bound on the componentwise relative FIRST
derivative of this rank-one boundary vector is possible, even with the
complete coefficient array available. Natural physical weights have
bounded relative s derivatives at zero, so the same divergence cannot
be hidden by merely counting their physical variation.

This is NOT a bound on total finite-path change: an analytic path may
have a very thin boundary layer. It is NOT an obstruction to PSD
remainders of higher rank. Such a remainder can absorb the quadratic
probe, so (13) need no longer hold as an equality.

The same phenomenon occurs at the actual canonical source baseline (3).
Keep q(epsilon)=1/t(epsilon), t^2-t+1=M(epsilon)^2, and assume the
complete remainder remains rank one starting at (3). Set
wphys=(h_0,...,h_(n-1),t). The quadratic calibration again forces

                   =(dot M-M0(N+m-1))/(t0-1),         (17)

where t0=(1+sqrt(4M0^2-3))/2. To check, use
dot t=2M0 dot M/(2t0-1),

and (t0-1)^2+t0=M0^2. The weighted denominator is now
2(sqrt(2))^m-1+(t0-1)/M0, so the same asymptotic lower bound (16)
holds. This calculation allows both directions of optimum incidence;
it uses only the rank-one full remainder and the specified rate.

## 6. A complete higher-rank analytic repair, with unaffordable exact cost

There is no qualitative obstruction to analytic continuation when the
objective coefficient is unrestricted. The following construction gives
an explicit full-array path from EITHER baseline, at every m>=2, with
all actual pairs and both optimum directions strictly positive for s>0.
Its objective cost is far too large. This is a quantified failed repair,
not the uniform rate conclusion requested in TASK.

Use N=2^m, n=N-1, M0=rho^m, and set

 Hmax=1+3M0, Lmax=6M0, R=(1+4Hmax)^2,
 d_i=8R^(n-i), D=diag(d_i), Qsafe=4sum_i d_i,

 Asafe=D+eta0(NI-ee^T).

All physical h_i(s) lie below Hmax, and |h_i(s)-h_i(0)|<=Lmax s.
For the latter, differentiate
rho^(j-1)(1-s/m)(1+s/m)^(j-1), j=0,...,m-1. The j=0 derivative is
at most2/(m rho) in magnitude. For j>=1 its magnitude is at most
(j/m)rho^(j-1)(1+1/m)^(j-1)<=3M0. Both asserted bounds follow.
The bound (1+1/m)^m<3 follows by expanding the binomial and using
binom(m,k)/m^k<=1/k! and sum_(k>=0)1/k!<3.

First omit the eta0 term. The gradient block is sym(DV)-e_n e_n^T.
After congruence by D^(-1/2), its off-diagonal entries have absolute
values at most (Hmax/2)R^(-|i-j|/2). The sum of absolute off-diagonal
entries in each row is at most Hmax/(sqrt(R)-1)=1/4. Its diagonal is1,
except the terminal value1-1/8=7/8. The inequality
2|z_i z_j|<=z_i^2+z_j^2 therefore proves

 sym(DV)-e_n e_n^T >= D/4.

For every scalar z and gradient vector g, weighted Cauchy--Schwarz gives

 Qsafe z^2-z sum_i d_i g_i+g^T[sym(DV)-e_n e_n^T]g
 >=(Qsafe/2)z^2+[1/4-(sum_i d_i)/(2Qsafe)]sum_i d_i g_i^2
 >=z^2+sum_i g_i^2.

Here Qsafe=4sum d_i and min d_i=8. Thus the complete matrix before
the eta0 term is at least the identity, including its initial row.
The eta0 term has zero row and column sums, so it changes only the
gradient block by eta0 sym((NI-ee^T)V). Its norm is at most
eta0 N(1+n Hmax)=1/2: NI-ee^T has norm N, and
||V||<=1+||T||_F<=1+n Hmax. Consequently

                    Mcert(Qsafe,Asafe,h(s)) >= I/2.     (18)

Its actual finite multipliers are all eta0>0. Its optimum multipliers
are b_i=d_i and a_i=d_i-2*1_(i=n), all strictly positive. This proves
the full signs and PSD directly, without an appeal to Slater theory.

Let A0,q0 be either FULL baseline in Section1, and let
C_A=sum_(i,j)|A0_ij|, K=2 C_A N Lmax. Define

 A(s)=[A0+K s Asafe]/(1+K s),

The physical h(s) stays the assigned word. The frozen baseline array
changes its full matrix by a gradient block sym(A0(T(s)-T(0))). Its
norm is at most C_A N Lmax s=K s/2. Hence

 Mcert(q0,A0,h(s))>=-(K s/2)I.

Convexly combining this with (18) proves FULL PSD of (19) at every
s in[0,1]. All final multiplier signs hold by convex combination, and
every pair is positive for s>0. The matrix and array are real analytic;
the baseline singular kernel and all mixed directions are included in
the displayed bounds. Everything is predetermined by m and s.

The exact cost explains why this does not solve the research task.

 q(s)>=Qsafe/2>=16(1+4Hmax)^(2n).                       (20)

The resulting certificate level diverges rather than being O(1/M_m).
This specific dense repair is feasible but fails the scalar comparison

No optimality is claimed for (19), and its cost is not a lower bound on

## 7. What remains open

The canonical full baseline has now been calibrated at every depth, and
the sharper full baseline is separately explicit. The dense proposal
(7)--(8) solves its full PSD and all mixed-block conditions identically,
but its ACTUAL coefficient signs fail. Even arbitrary free skew changes
cannot fix its prescribed boundary-weight derivative at three steps.
Any rank-one continuation at either displayed rate also has the growing
relative-derivative requirement (16). These are scoped obstructions,
not a claim that every optimal certificate has either shape.

The explicit higher-rank path (19) uses every such sign direction and
is feasible at every depth, but its actual objective coefficient (20)
is unaffordable. The assignment permits other higher-rank PSD remainders,
different boundary weights, nonzero optimum sources, and a relaxed q.
Those freedoms are not excluded here. To obtain a separation, one must control the FINAL
signs and the exact finite-path Schur remainder uniformly in m, with
limsup q_m M_m<e/rho at s=1 (or the corresponding strict fixed-s gap).
Neither a derivative, the rank-one scalar moment, nor the real-skew
parameterization supplies that remaining estimate. No such certificate
or unrestricted obstruction has been proved in this round.
