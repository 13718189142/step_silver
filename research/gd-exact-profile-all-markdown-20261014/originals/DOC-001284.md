## 1. Original schedule, class, and claims

For each integer m>=4 set

    N=2^m, n=N-1, sigma=1+sqrt(2), delta=1-1/m,
    c=sigma(1+1/m), a=delta/c,
    H_0=(), H_(j+1)=(H_j,1+a*c^j,H_j), 0<=j<m.

Use exactly h=H_m, so h_t=1+a*c^(nu_2(t+1)) for 0<=t<n.
All occurrences of a and c retain the enclosing m. Put

    ell=1+a=h_0=h_(n-1), S=sum_(t<n)h_t,
    T=sum_(t=1)^(n-1)h_t=S-ell,
    K_first_m=a^2/(1+2T).

Here K_first_m is the actual first-cut scalar payoff from accepted
`b98bb807d514e80d`. It is not a new benchmark. B_n is the ORIGINAL
Zhang--Jiang denominator, so the benchmark objective is 1/B_n.
The elementary bounds 6/25<a<1/2 give 1<ell<3/2.

Define the following restricted class of legal instances on this schedule.
Normalize smoothness to 1, choose a closest minimizer as 0, and normalize
the true initial distance to ||X_0||=1. Write g_i=grad F(X_i),
f_i=F(X_i)-F(0), with the actual GD displacement

    X_(i+1)=X_i-h_i*g_i.

If a queried gradient is zero, the terminal gap is zero. Otherwise write
g_i=A_i*u_i, A_i>0, ||u_i||=1, for 0<=i<n. The sole queried-Gram
restriction is that there is a number tau in (0,1) with

    <u_0,u_i>=-tau                  (1<=i<n),
    <u_i,u_j>=tau                   (1<=i<j<n).          (1)

Every amplitude, initial anchor, sampled value, and actual function is
otherwise free. Tau may depend arbitrarily on m. In particular the
unqueried terminal gradient g_n is unrestricted. The Gram in (1) is
(1-tau)I+tau*eta*eta^T, where eta_0=1 and eta_i=-1 for i>=1.
It has no orthogonal queried pair and has a negative initial correlation
with every later query. Thus C89's positive-history premise fails and its
large orthogonal selection is absent.

Let V_best_m be the supremum of 2f_n over this class, including all dimensions.
The proposed joint conclusions are:

1. A fully explicit dense character construction, all original pair
   constraints, one static function, unique minimizer, and radius 1 are

2. For every m>=4, one specified construction has B_n*2f_n>1/150 and
   minimum character atom exactly 1/[8n(n+1)]. No scalar strictification
   or unpriced anchor component is required.
3. More strongly, the ENTIRE class (1) has the exact asymptotic strength

       lim_(m->infinity) B_(2^m-1)*V_best_m
          = Z_1/[2e*sigma^2] < 1/5,                    (2)

   where Z_1=lim sigma^(-m)B_(2^m-1) is the accepted ray constant and
   e=lim (1+1/m)^m. This includes arbitrary horizon-dependent tau and

The upper bound in (2) is about this family of lower witnesses. It is

## 2. A direct construction on an arbitrary eligible physical word

First let n>=2, h_0=1+a with a>0, and choose 0<tau<1 such that

    h_i>=1/tau, 1<=i<n.                                (3)

Choose unit vectors u_0,...,u_n with exactly the signed Gram (1), now
including i=n with negative sign. Such vectors are realized explicitly
in Section 6. Choose q>0 and define

    A_0=1, A_i=q*tau^(i-1) for 1<=i<=n,
    g_i=A_i*u_i, X_0=g_0,
    X_i=X_0-sum_(t<i)h_t*g_t.

Define the actual tail moments

    P_i=sum_(t=1)^(i-1)h_t*A_t,
    E_i=sum_(t=i)^(n-1)h_t*A_t^2,  1<=i<=n.

Empty sums are zero. Let b>0 satisfy

    A_n+b/A_n+tau*P_n <= tau*a.                        (4)

Set f_0=1/2 and

    f_i=A_i^2/2+b+tau^2*E_i, 1<=i<=n,                 (5)

and include the optimum jet (0,0,0). These are values on original indices;
no trajectory is compressed or reset. The following sections verify EVERY
ordered interpolation residual

    Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,X_j-X_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2.         (6)

The necessity and sufficiency of Q_ij<=0, with the explicit construction
of one function, are accepted in `1252b8823bb5a290`.

## 3. Every finite tail pair, with the original displacement

For 1<=i<j<=n put

    W_ij=sum_(t=i)^(j-1)h_t*A_t,
    D_ij=f_i-f_j=(A_i^2-A_j^2)/2
                           +tau^2*sum_(t=i)^(j-1)h_t*A_t^2.

The identity for D_ij follows from (5), not from a compressed update.
The actual signed inner products give the following exact allowed interval:

    L_ij <= D_ij <= U_ij,
    L_ij=tau*A_j*W_ij+(A_i^2+A_j^2-2tau*A_i*A_j)/2,
    U_ij=h_i*A_i^2+tau*A_i*sum_(t=i+1)^(j-1)h_t*A_t
                         -(A_i^2+A_j^2-2tau*A_i*A_j)/2. (7)

Here Q_ji=L_ij-D_ij and Q_ij=D_ij-U_ij. In particular every skipped
history term h_t*<g_i,g_t> is present in (7).

The lower inequality follows from the identity

    D_ij-L_ij
      =sum_(t=i)^(j-1)tau*h_t*A_t*(tau*A_t-A_j)
                          +A_j*(tau*A_i-A_j) >=0.      (8)

For each summand t<=j-1, tau*A_t>=A_j, because A_(t+1)=tau*A_t.
The last term is also nonnegative. For j=i+1 both terms vanish, giving
the exact reverse-row equality Q_(i+1,i)=0.

For the upper inequality put d=j-i. Direct expansion gives

    (U_ij-D_ij)/A_i^2
      =(h_i-1)(1-tau^2)
       +sum_(k=1)^(d-1)(h_(i+k)-1)*tau^(k+1)*(1-tau^(k+1))
       +J_d,

    J_d=-tau^2+tau^(d+1)
                   +sum_(k=1)^(d-1)(tau^(k+1)-tau^(2k+2)). (9)

One has J_1=0 and

    J_(d+1)-J_d=tau^(d+2)*(1-tau^d)>=0.

All coefficients in the first two terms of (9) are nonnegative because
h_i>=1. Thus U_ij-D_ij>=0. This proves both orientations for every
tail pair, not just adjacent samples.

## 4. Every optimum and initial pair; the exact scalar boundary

The negative initial history is retained exactly:

    <g_i,X_i>=tau*A_i*(a-P_i), 1<=i<=n.               (10)

The contribution of the first displacement is +h_0*tau*A_i, while
<g_i,X_0>=-tau*A_i. Deleting that signed initial term would change (10).

The optimum residual Q_(i,star) is nonpositive precisely when

    R_i:=A_i+b/A_i+tau*P_i+tau^2*E_i/A_i <= tau*a.

For 1<=i<n, expansion using A_(i+1)=tau*A_i gives

    R_(i+1)-R_i
      =(1-tau)[(tau*h_i-1)A_i
                    +tau*E_(i+1)/A_i+b/(tau*A_i)] >=0. (11)

Condition (3) is used here. Consequently every optimum upper row follows
from the SINGLE final boundary (4). Conversely the final row requires
(4), so this is an exact elimination for this chosen construction.
The other optimum orientation is

    Q_(star,i)=-b-tau^2*E_i<0.

At i=0, X_0=g_0 and f_0=1/2 give both optimum residuals zero.

For i>=1, direct calculation gives

    Q_(i,0)=Q_(i,star),
    Q_(0,i)=-a-b-tau^2*E_i+tau*(P_i+A_i).             (12)

If i<n, then P_n-P_i>=h_i*A_i>=A_i. Hence
tau*(P_i+A_i)<=tau*P_n<=tau*a by (4).
If i=n, (4) gives tau*P_n+A_n<=tau*a, so
tau*(P_n+A_n)<=tau*a-(1-tau)A_n<=tau*a.
In either case Q_(0,i)<=-(1-tau)a-b-tau^2*E_i<0.

Equations (7)--(12), together with the two zero rows at i=0, check ALL
original directed pairs including the terminal. For the smallest physical
block, indices 0,1 and star, they give Q_(1,0)=Q_(1,star)<=0 and
Q_(0,1)<0 with <g_0,g_1>=-tau*q<0. This block is part of the original
word with its actual h_0, and already has genuinely signed history.

## 5. One static function, values, and exact closest distance

Put y_i=X_i-g_i and d_i=f_i-||g_i||^2/2. Then y_0=0,d_0=0 and
d_i=b+tau^2*E_i>0 for i>=1. Define on the same Euclidean space

    H(v)=||v||^2/2+max{0,max_(1<=i<=n)(<y_i,v>-d_i)},
    F(X)=sup_v(<X,v>-H(v)).                            (13)

By the explicit all-pair interpolation construction in accepted
`1252b8823bb5a290`, F is everywhere finite, convex, differentiable and
1-smooth, and F(X_i)=f_i, grad F(X_i)=g_i for every i, including 0 and n.
Thus its actual GD is exactly the prescribed original trajectory.

All d_i with i>=1 are strictly positive, so H(v)=||v||^2/2 in a
neighborhood of zero. Also H(v)>=||v||^2/2 everywhere. It follows that
F(0)=0. For any X!=0, take a sufficiently small t in (0,1) with tX in
that neighborhood. Then

    F(X)>=(t-t^2/2)||X||^2>0.

Therefore argmin F={0}. Its true initial squared distance is exactly
||X_0||^2=||g_0||^2=1. No inverse-Gram or orthogonal-anchor radius cost is
hidden. Multiplication of F by L and use of physical steps h_i/L give
the same normalized payoff for arbitrary L>0.

## 6. A positive finite character law

Let d be any integer with 2^d>=n+1. In the (d+1)-bit label space use

    lambda_0=(0,binary(0)),
    lambda_i=(1,binary(i)), 1<=i<=n.

These labels do not permute the physical indices. Let s range over this
binary space, let e_sig have only its first bit equal to 1, and put

    D=2^(d+1),
    w_s=tau*1_(s=e_sig)+(1-tau)/D,
    (u_i)_s=sqrt(w_s)*(-1)^(s dot lambda_i).            (14)

All weights are positive and sum to one. Summing the binary characters
by pairing a differing bit gives

    <u_i,u_j>=tau*eta_i*eta_j+(1-tau)*1_(i=j).

This is the required full signed Gram, not a PSD-only existence claim:
positions, all values, all original residuals and F were supplied above.
The explicit anchor is simply X_0=u_0. On H_m one may take d=m,D=2N.
The least atom is (1-tau)/(2N).

## 7. Exact payoff and an associative physical block rule

Put

    r=tau^(n-1), H_tail=sum_(t=1)^(n-1)h_t*tau^(t-1).

Then A_n=q*r and P_n=q*H_tail. For fixed q, the largest permitted b in
(4) is

    b=q*r*(tau*a-q*(tau*H_tail+r)).                   (15)

When b>0, (13) has the unique minimizer just proved. Its normalized
terminal payoff is

    V=2f_n=2q*r*tau*a-q^2*r*(2tau*H_tail+r).

Completing the square in q gives the maximum, at

    q=tau*a/(2tau*H_tail+r),
    b=q^2*r*tau*H_tail>0,
    V_tau=tau^2*a^2*r/(2tau*H_tail+r).                 (16)

This is an algebraic formula, not a numerical parameter evaluation.

For a consecutive tail block p=(p_0,...,p_(k-1)), define

    alpha_p=tau^k,
    H_p=sum_(j=0)^(k-1)p_j*tau^j,
    J_p=sum_(j=0)^(k-1)p_j*tau^(2j).

If it starts at tail amplitude A, cumulative moment P, and value f, its
actual end state is

    A_out=alpha_p*A,
    P_out=P+A*H_p,
    f_out=f-A^2*(1-alpha_p^2)/2-tau^2*A^2*J_p.         (17)

For the physical concatenation p followed by qword,

    alpha_(p qword)=alpha_p*alpha_qword,
    H_(p qword)=H_p+alpha_p*H_qword,
    J_(p qword)=J_p+alpha_p^2*J_qword.                 (18)

These identities follow by splitting the defining sums at the actual
index k. They prove associativity without resetting P, A, or the enclosing
schedule parameters. All cross-block Q rows are already covered by
(7)--(12). The final boundary (4) prices the complete history. This is a
legal iterable rule for this restricted tail geometry, not an improving
primitive-capacity composition theorem.

There is an explicit reserve test when appending to a fixed existing
prefix. Write d_in=f-A^2/2. The inherited values remain unchanged, while

    d_out=d_in-tau^2*A^2*J_p.

A permitted append must have d_out>0 and

    A_out+d_out/A_out+tau*P_out<=tau*a.

With these tests, the enlarged transcript has the same form (5), with
b=d_out: splitting its future sum shows that every old value is exactly
its previous value. Thus the all-pair proof applies to the old and new
samples together. If d_out<0 the original optimum lower row fails; if
the displayed boundary fails, the original optimum upper row fails.
No claim is made that an arbitrary further block passes these paid tests.

Since tau*H_tail>=r*T, (16) also gives

    V_tau<=tau^2*a^2/(2T+1)<K_first_m.                       (19)

Thus even before the wider obstruction below, the exact block rule does
not improve the scalar first-cut lower bound.

## 8. Quantitative calibration on every original H_m

Choose tau=1-1/(4n). For n>=15,
1/tau<=60/59<31/25<ell, so (3) holds at every original tail step.
The character law's minimum atom is exactly 1/[8nN]. Since H_tail<=T
and r<=1, (16) and Bernoulli's inequality yield

    V_tau >= tau^(n+1)*K_first_m
           >=[1-(n+1)/(4n)]*K_first_m >=(11/15)*K_first_m.        (20)

For completeness the original benchmark comparison uses its exact mass
recurrences, not the physical mass as a substitute. The accepted joins are

    Omega(u,v)=(u+v+sqrt((u+v)^2+4uv))/2,
    G(u,v)=2u+v/2+sqrt(v^2+8uv)/2,
    C_0=B_0=1,
    C_n=max_(k+l=n-1)Omega(C_k,C_l),
    B_n=max_(k+l=n-1)G(C_k,B_l).

Both joins are increasing and G>=Omega: after squaring the nonnegative
quantities 3u+sqrt(v^2+8uv) and sqrt(u^2+6uv+v^2), their squared difference
is 8u^2+2uv+6u*sqrt(v^2+8uv)>=0. Induction gives B_n>=C_n, and the
balanced primitive split gives B_(2^m-1)>=sigma^m.

Counting 2^(m-k-1) occurrences of level k in the actual word gives

    S=N-1+r_sum*(c^m-N),
    r_sum=delta/[c(c-2)], 0<r_sum<1.                  (21)

Here c>sigma>2, and c(c-2)>sigma(sigma-2)=1>delta. Hence
S<c^m-1 and 2T+1<2c^m<6sigma^m. The last inequality uses
(1+1/m)^m<3, obtained by the binomial expansion and sum 1/k!<3.
Finally a=(sqrt(2)-1)(m-1)/(m+1)>6/25 for m>=4. Therefore

    B_n*V_tau >(11/15)*a^2/6
              >396/56250 >1/150.                    (22)

This proves a fully realized, quantitatively priced signed construction
on every original enclosing horizon, while (19) records its limitation.

## 9. Arbitrary amplitudes: an original tail-pair restriction

We now return to the ENTIRE class (1), with arbitrary amplitudes, values,
anchor, and free terminal. Set k=n-1, so the last queried sample is k.
Normalize using the true closest-minimizer distance as in Section 1.

For 1<=i<j<=k, add Q_ij and Q_ji. The accepted necessary inequality is

    <g_i-g_j,X_i-X_j> >= ||g_i-g_j||^2.

Its LEFT side is exactly

    h_i*A_i*(A_i-tau*A_j)
      +tau*(A_i-A_j)*sum_(t=i+1)^(j-1)h_t*A_t.        (23)

If A_j>=A_i/tau, both terms in (23) are nonpositive. The right side is
A_i^2+A_j^2-2tau*A_i*A_j>0 because 0<tau<1 and both amplitudes are
positive. This contradiction proves

    A_i>tau*A_j, 1<=i<j<=k.                           (24)

No nonnegative-history simplification was made across the initial sample.
The assertion is only within the tail where its pair correlations are
actually positive.

## 10. The true anchor and a free terminal: a sharp limiting upper bound

Write A_0=||g_0||. Optimum cocoercivity gives
<X_0,g_0>>=A_0^2, and A_0<=||X_0||=1. Set z=<X_0,u_0>, so z>=A_0.
Resolve the unit vector u_k=-tau*u_0+sqrt(1-tau^2)*v with v perpendicular
to u_0 and ||v||=1. Then

    <X_0,u_k> <= -tau*z+sqrt(1-tau^2)*sqrt(1-z^2).

The right side is decreasing for 0<=z<=1, so it is at most its value at
z=A_0. Put T_pre=sum_(t=1)^(k-1)h_t=T-ell. The original optimum row at k,
including the negative contribution of g_0 and every positive tail term,
therefore gives, using (24),

    f_k <= A_k[tau*a*A_0+sqrt(1-tau^2)*sqrt(1-A_0^2)]
                               -(tau^2*T_pre+1/2)A_k^2.

Cauchy--Schwarz bounds the bracket by

    M_tau=sqrt(tau^2*a^2+1-tau^2).

The actual final step has length ell<2. Smoothness at X_k gives

    f_n<=f_k-ell*(1-ell/2)*A_k^2.

Maximizing the resulting quadratic in A_k proves the uniform class bound

    2f_n <= U_angle(tau)
      :=[tau^2*a^2+1-tau^2]
                    /[1+2tau^2*T_pre+ell*(2-ell)].    (25)

This does not impose any law on g_n. Its initial norm was the TRUE radius,
and neither a translated reset nor an unrecorded anchor adjustment occurs.
Relative to the first-cut payoff, (25) implies

    U_angle(tau)/K_first_m
      <=[1+(tau^(-2)-1)/a^2]
                     *(1+2T)/(1+2T-ell^2).           (26)

In particular, as tau tends to 1 this ratio tends to 1 uniformly over m
because a>6/25, ell<3/2 and T>=n-1.

## 11. Small correlations: a priced moment bound on the actual tail

There are M=n-1 tail queries 1,...,k. For consecutive tail samples, the
original reverse interpolation inequality reads

    f_i-f_(i+1) >= (A_i^2+A_(i+1)^2)/2
                          +tau*(h_i-1)*A_i*A_(i+1)
                    >= (A_i^2+A_(i+1)^2)/2.           (27)

At each tail sample, the optimum row and the actual displacement from X_1
give

    <X_1,g_i> >= f_i+A_i^2/2.

All added terms in this last relation are tau*h_t*A_i*A_t>=0 for
1<=t<i. Write b_tail=f_k-A_k^2/2>=0. Summing (27) gives

    <X_1,u_i> >= [b_tail+sum_(j=i)^k A_j^2]/A_i >0.   (28)

For real coefficients t_i, (1) gives the elementary Gram estimate

    ||sum_(i=1)^k t_i*u_i||^2
       =(1-tau)sum_i t_i^2+tau*(sum_i t_i)^2
       <=[1+(M-1)tau]*sum_i t_i^2.

Apply Cauchy--Schwarz with t_i=<X_1,u_i>. It follows that

    sum_i <X_1,u_i>^2 <=[1+(M-1)tau]||X_1||^2.        (29)

The original first step costs no hidden radius: since ell<2 and
<X_0,g_0>>=A_0^2,

    ||X_1||^2<=1-ell*(2-ell)*A_0^2<=1.

The scalar elimination proved in accepted `0e7e78056dc6258c`, proof
Section 5, applies to (28)--(29): if q_i=A_i^2 and T_i=b_tail+sum_(j>=i)q_j,
it bounds 2f_k by beta_(M-1) times the upper budget for sum T_i^2/q_i.
Here beta_j=4/[1+sqrt(1+2c_j)]^2, c_0=0,
c_(j+1)=c_j+2+2sqrt(c_j+1), with beta_j<=2/j^2 for j>=1.
The actual last step cannot increase f. Hence

    2f_n <= [1+(n-2)tau]*beta_(n-2)
           <= 2[1+(n-2)tau]/(n-2)^2.                 (30)

This is an elementary extension with its full Gram cost written out.
No orthogonality of these actual tail gradients is asserted.

## 12. Intermediate correlations: exact adjacent ratios

For two consecutive tail queries the same cocoercivity inequality gives

    (h_i-1)A_i^2-A_(i+1)^2
                    +(2-h_i)tau*A_i*A_(i+1)>=0.

For z>0 define the positive root

    r_tau(z)=[-tau*(z-1)+sqrt(tau^2*(z-1)^2+4z)]/2.

Then A_(i+1)/A_i<=r_tau(h_i-1). The root obeys r_tau(z)>tau and is
strictly increasing in z. A convenient exact formula, with natural log,
is

    phi_tau(x):=log r_tau(exp x)
       =x/2-asinh(tau*sinh(x/2)).                     (31)

Indeed divide r^2+tau(z-1)r-z=0 by r*sqrt(z) and solve the resulting
hyperbolic-sine identity. Thus phi is odd, phi(0)=0, and

    phi'(x)=1/2-tau*cosh(x/2)
                              /[2sqrt(1+tau^2*sinh^2(x/2))]>0,
    phi''(x)=-tau*(1-tau^2)*sinh(x/2)
                              /[4(1+tau^2*sinh^2(x/2))^(3/2)]. (32)

The derivative positivity follows by squaring: the denominator under its
square root exceeds tau^2*cosh^2(x/2) by 1-tau^2. On x>0, phi is
strictly concave. These formulas also prove r_tau(z)>tau by its limit
as z decreases to zero and strict monotonicity.

The first queried norm is bounded without assuming it shares a positive
correlation. Write d=g_1-g_0. Cocoercivity and X_1-X_0=-ell*g_0 give
-ell*<g_0,d>>=||d||^2, whence

    ||g_1||^2<=||g_0||^2+(1-2/ell)||d||^2<=||g_0||^2<=1.

Define the product over ALL original steps

    P_all=product_(t=0)^(n-1) r_tau(h_t-1).

The first and last physical steps both have excess a. Multiplying only
the legitimate tail inequalities i=1,...,n-2 therefore gives

    A_k <= P_all/r_tau(a)^2 <= tau^(-2)*P_all.         (33)

This product does not assume any condition on the free terminal gradient.

## 13. Ruler counts create a uniform strict product loss

Put Lc=log c. Since the physical excess at level j is delta*c^(j-1)
with delta<1, monotonicity in (31) and the original counts give

    log P_all
      <=(N/2)[-phi(Lc)+sum_(j=2)^(m-1)2^(-j)*phi((j-1)Lc)].

Concavity and phi(0)=0 imply phi(r*Lc)<=r*phi(Lc) for integer r>=1.
Keep the strict deficit at j=3 separately and write

    Delta_tau(c)=2phi(Lc)-phi(2Lc)>0.

The finite identity sum_(j=2)^(m-1)(j-1)/2^j=1-m/2^(m-1) yields

    log P_all <= -m*phi(Lc)-(N/16)*Delta_tau(c).        (34)

This retains all original levels and their actual multiplicities.

For an explicit bound, c>=sigma>2 and c<4 for m>=4. Hence
Lc>1/2 and cosh(Lc)<5/2. The identity

    Delta_tau(c)=integral_(t=0)^Lc integral_(s=t)^(t+Lc)
                                           [-phi''(s)] ds dt

contains the rectangle Lc/2<=t<=Lc, Lc<=s<=3Lc/2. On this rectangle,
sinh(s/2)>1/4, and
(1+tau^2*sinh^2(s/2))^(3/2)<=cosh(Lc)^3<125/8.
