# Arbitrary even profiles with discrete radial homogeneity are too weak on H_m

Status: complete scoped candidate proof, not verifier-accepted. No verifier
call. This extends the fixed-staircase analysis to every shape within a radial
period. Evenness and the stated exact discrete homogeneity are essential
hypotheses; the full scalar and multidimensional classes remain unresolved.

## 1. Statement

Let c=13/5, a=c^(-17), b_u=a c^u, and

    H_0=(),  H_(m+1)=(H_m,b_m,H_m),
    N_m=2^m-1,
    s_m=sum H_m=a(c^m-2^m)/(c-2).

Consider ANY nondecreasing 1-Lipschitz function g:[0,infinity)->[0,infinity)
with g(0)=0 and

    g(cR)=c g(R),                         R>=0.              (1)

Extend it oddly to the real line and set
f(x)=integral_0^|x| g(R)dR. This gives an even convex differentiable
1-smooth function with fixed minimum f(0)=0. No prescribed plateau, ramp,
curvature pattern, knot count, or sign-crossing history is assumed.

For every m>=17, every x_0!=0, and the complete GD schedule H_m,

    2f(x_(N_m))/|x_0|^2 <= [1/(a c)]c^(-m).                (2)

The function g itself may depend on the entire horizon m. Consequently the
supremum of these objective ratios over the full class (1), divided by the
exact U_(N_m)^ZJ, tends to zero. This is a class obstruction, not a bound
on the unrestricted W_(N_m)(H_m).

## 2. A scalar clock and the quantitative closure inequality

The identically zero g has zero objective. Otherwise g(R)>0 for every R>0:
if it vanished at one positive radius, (1) and monotonicity would make it
vanish on arbitrarily large intervals. The continuous ratio g(R)/R is
periodic in log R. Thus its maximum M>0 is attained on any multiplicative
period, and M<=1 by smoothness. Put ell=1/M>=1.

Define a flow clock

    F(R)=integral_1^R dt/g(t),        R>0.

The arbitrary reference 1 affects only an additive constant. Equation (1)
implies that

    F(cR)-F(R)=tau>0                                        (3)

is independent of R: differentiate in R, or substitute t=cu. Choose r>=17
minimal with b=b_r>=ell. Then b/c<ell<=b. Define

    D=max_(R>0) [F(b g(R))-F(R)].                           (4)

The expression in brackets is periodic under R->cR, so the maximum exists
on a compact radial period. At a maximizer of g(R)/R=1/ell, one has
b g(R)>=R. Therefore D>=0.

The key proved inequality is

    tau+D <=(c-1)b.                                        (5)

Here is a direct proof for every possible input radius; no functional
inequality is left as an assumption. Fix R>0, put u=g(R)>0 and q=R/u>=1.

If q<=b, monotonicity gives

    F(bu)-F(R)<=b-q.                                       (6)

On the period [R,cR], the endpoint gradients are u and cu. Monotonicity and
the 1-Lipschitz condition give the pointwise lower bound

    g(t)>=max(u, cu-(cR-t)).

The switch occurs at t=cR-(c-1)u>=R. Integrating the reciprocal of this
lower bound yields

    tau<= (c-1)(q-1)+log c.                                (7)

Adding (6)-(7) gives

    tau+F(bu)-F(R)
       <=b+(c-2)q-(c-1)+log c
       <=(c-1)b-(c-1)+log c <(c-1)b,

because q<=b, c>2 and log c<c-1.

If q>=b, use (3) to write the same quantity as

    tau+F(bu)-F(R)=F(cbu)-F(R).

When cbu<R this quantity is negative. Otherwise every point of integration
has gradient at least u, so it is at most cb-q<=(c-1)b. This proves (5)
uniformly in R, and hence for the actual maximum D in (4).

## 3. Actual block and checkpoint updates of the same clock

Every step of H_r is at most b/c<ell. Since g(R)<=R/ell, these steps cannot
cross zero from a nonzero point. For a positive-radius step
R'=R-hg(R)>0, monotonicity gives

    F(R)-F(R')=integral_(R')^R dt/g(t)
        >=(R-R')/g(R)=h.                                  (8)

The same calculation applies after reflecting a negative point, because f
is even. Therefore each actual H_r block decreases the clock of the absolute
radius by at least S=s_r.

For a checkpoint of level k, h=b c^k, consider the actual next radius.
If it does not cross zero, its clock decreases. If it crosses, then

    R'=h g(R)-R <= c^k b g(R).

By (3)-(4), this gives

    F(R')-F(R)<=k tau+D.                                   (9)

Since D>=0, (9) also bounds a same-sign checkpoint. A zero landing has zero
terminal objective and can be excluded from the clock calculation.

For r>=17, one has S>=eta b with eta=13/8. To check this directly, note
(10/13)^3<1/2 and (10/13)^2<4/5, hence (10/13)^17<1/40; substitute in
s_r/b_r=[1-(2/c)^r]/(c-2). Since eta>c-1=8/5, (5) gives

    S-tau-D>0.                                            (10)

Thus the growing-state flow description has a genuine, uniformly profitable
update. It is not just a restatement of the unknown scalar optimization.

## 4. Sum the exact recursive word and retain the original normalization

First suppose m>=r and put J=m-r. The word H_m contains 2^J copies of H_r.
Its checkpoint levels k=0,...,J-1 occur 2^(J-k-1) times. Their total level
sum is 2^J-J-1, and their number is 2^J-1. Adding (8)-(9) along the full
actual trajectory gives

    F(|x_(N_m)|)-F(|x_0|)
       <=-2^J S+(2^J-J-1)tau+(2^J-1)D
       =-2^J(S-tau-D)-(J+1)tau-D
       <=-(J+1)tau.                                      (11)

Equation (3) and monotonicity of F imply

    |x_(N_m)|<=c^(-J-1)|x_0|.                             (12)

The global secant bound g(R)<=R/ell supplies the complete area bound
f(x)<=x^2/(2ell). Since ell>b/c,

    2f(x_(N_m))/|x_0|^2
       <=ell^(-1)c^(-2J-2)
       <=b^(-1)c^(-2J-1)
       =a^(-1)c^(-m-J-1)
       <=[1/(a c)]c^(-m).                                (13)

If m<r, every step in H_m is smaller than ell and the entire trajectory
stays on its initial ray. Let w=g(|x_(N_m)|) and R=|x_(N_m)|. Monotonicity
and smoothness give R+s_m w<=|x_0| and f(x_(N_m))<=Rw-w^2/2. Thus

    2f(x_(N_m))/|x_0|^2<=1/(1+2s_m)
       <=[1/(2eta a)]c^(-m)
       <=[1/(a c)]c^(-m),

using m>=17 and 2eta>c. This proves (2) in every case. All distances refer
to the same fixed minimizer zero. Scaling f by L gives exactly the project's
normalization and GD steps h_t/L.

## 5. Explicit exact-profile comparison and finite-knot realizations

For completeness set C_n=S_circle(n)+1 and D_n=2S_bullet(n)+1. Their shifted
joins are Omega(A,B)=(A+B+sqrt((A+B)^2+4AB))/2 and
G(A,D)=2A+D/2+sqrt(D^2+8AD)/2. The bound
Omega(A,B)<=(A^(3/4)+B^(3/4))^(4/3) reduces to
t^(4/3)+(1-t)^(4/3)+[t(1-t)]^(4/3)<=1. With x=t^(1/3),y=(1-t)^(1/3),
the difference from 1 is x^3 y^3[1/(1+x+x^2)+1/(1+y+y^2)-xy]>=0:
both reciprocal terms are at least 1/3 and xy<=2^(-2/3)<2/3. Induction gives
C_n<=(n+1)^(4/3). Since G(A,D)<=D+4A and the power 4/3 is superadditive on
nonnegative inputs, the dominant DP gives D_n<=1+4n^(4/3). Multiplying (2)
by D_(N_m) proves the claimed vanishing ratio, because c^3>16.

If the profile has finitely many affine pieces within one radial period,
any one finite nonzero trajectory has a finite-knot realization preserving
all its values and gradients. Choose a radius R below every nonzero query
and terminal radius, and put u=g(R)>0 and A=f(R). Smoothness and monotonicity
give

    u^2/2<=A<=Ru-u^2/2.

Replace the inner profile by zero up to
delta=R-u/2-A/u, then a unit-slope ramp of length u, then a plateau at u up
to R. The displayed bounds imply 0<=delta<=R-u. Its terminal gradient is u
and its area is Ru-delta u-u^2/2=A. All larger-radius gradients and values
are therefore unchanged. Apply the same replacement on both rays and cap
outside the largest sampled radius. The original finite GD trajectory and
its exact objective normalization are preserved. This does not claim that
an arbitrary nonhomogeneous core replacement obeys the same global theorem.

The new obstruction includes arbitrary legal within-period geometry,
horizon-dependent shape and scale, dense gradient values, arbitrarily many
knots, and every crossing pattern. It is still restricted to even functions
with the exact radial period c=13/5. The independently phased staircase
result in research/max3_multiscale_flow_obstruction.md remains a separate
asymmetric extension. Neither result decides the full scalar class or the
project's unrestricted all-schedule minimax problem.
