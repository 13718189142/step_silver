## 1. The general signed statement

Let F be a convex differentiable function on a finite-dimensional Euclidean
space, with 1-Lipschitz gradient and a minimizer. Run actual GD with a
finite nonnegative schedule h of length n>=1. Write X_i for its positions,
g_i=grad F(X_i), f_i=F(X_i)-min F, and

    X_(i+1)=X_i-h_i g_i, R=dist(X_0,argmin F).

Suppose the nonzero QUERIED gradients g_0,...,g_(n-1) belong to M mutually
orthogonal subspaces V_1,...,V_M, each of which is used at least once.
The gradients within a subspace can have arbitrary signed correlations,
arbitrary norms, and arbitrary linear dependencies. No condition is imposed
on the unqueried terminal gradient. A run is a maximal consecutive interval
of queried indices having the same subspace label. Let r be the first index
of the final run, and set

    A_tail=product_(t=r)^(n-1) max{1,(h_t-1)^2}.

The candidate conclusion is

    2 f_n/R^2 <= A_tail beta_(M-1),                     (1)
    c_0=0, c_(j+1)=c_j+2+2sqrt(c_j+1),
    beta_j=4/(1+sqrt(1+2c_j))^2.

If any queried gradient is zero, the trajectory remains at that minimizer
and the terminal gap is zero. Thus excluding zero gradients loses no
positive payoff. In the nonzero case R>0.

The important difference from C89 is that (1) does NOT assume nonnegative
correlations with earlier gradients. Those terms remain signed; the proof
chooses intervals on which they vanish by orthogonality of whole subspaces.

## 2. First occurrences: every skipped term is retained

Translate a closest minimizer to zero. Such a minimizer exists because its
set is nonempty and closed, and a bounded distance-minimizing sequence has
a convergent subsequence. Accepted `1252b8823bb5a290` gives every original
ordered inequality

    Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,X_j-X_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2<=0.       (2)

List the first occurrence of each subspace, in chronological order, as
j_0<...<j_(M-1). Put v_a=g_(j_a), q_a=||v_a||^2, and p_a=f_(j_a).
The vectors v_a are pairwise orthogonal. Moreover

    <v_a,g_t>=0 for EVERY t<j_a,                       (3)

because none of those earlier gradients belongs to its subspace.
There are no compressed GD steps in this argument. Applying the original
Q_(j_(a+1),j_a) gives exactly

    p_a >= p_(a+1)+(q_a+q_(a+1))/2
              +sum_(t=j_a)^(j_(a+1)-1)h_t<v_(a+1),g_t>
        = p_(a+1)+(q_a+q_(a+1))/2.                    (4)

The original optimum row, retaining its past displacement, gives

    <X_0,v_a> >= p_a+q_a/2
                       +sum_(t<j_a)h_t<v_a,g_t>
                 =p_a+q_a/2.                         (5)

Also p_(M-1)>=q_(M-1)/2. Define
b=p_(M-1)-q_(M-1)/2>=0 and T_a=b+sum_(s=a)^(M-1)q_s.
Equations (4)-(5) imply <X_0,v_a>>=T_a>0. Expanding the squared norm
after subtracting the orthogonal projections onto v_a yields

    R^2>=sum_a T_a^2/q_a.                              (6)

For completeness, the scalar elimination in accepted
`0e7e78056dc6258c` applies as follows. Inductively
sum_(a<k)T_a^2/q_a>=c_k T_k. Adding the next term and using
T_k=q_k+T_(k+1) reduces this to

    (c_k+1)q_k+(c_k+2)T_(k+1)+T_(k+1)^2/q_k
       >=[c_k+2+2sqrt(c_k+1)]T_(k+1),

which is the nonnegativity of a square. With c=c_(M-1),
z=T_(M-1)/q_(M-1)>=1 and u=(1+sqrt(1+2c))/2, we get
R^2>=q_(M-1)z(z+c), 2p_(M-1)=q_(M-1)(2z-1), and

    z(z+c)-u^2(2z-1)=(z-u)^2>=0.

Therefore 2p_(M-1)/R^2<=beta_(M-1). The same proof includes M=1.
Induction gives c_k>=k^2 and hence beta_k<=2/k^2 for k>=1.

## 3. Run starts cannot transmit an increase to another subspace

Consider consecutive run starts u<v. All gradients g_t for u<=t<v
belong to the old subspace; g_v belongs to a different, orthogonal one.
The actual original pair Q_(v,u) therefore says

    f_u>=f_v+||g_u-g_v||^2/2
                         +sum_(t=u)^(v-1)h_t<g_v,g_t>
        =f_v+(||g_u||^2+||g_v||^2)/2.                  (7)

This identity keeps every physical step of the old run. In particular,
even a large increase INSIDE that run cannot be passed to the next run
start. A first occurrence is a run start. Applying (7) successively after
the last first occurrence proves f_r<=p_(M-1).

For any actual step of a 1-smooth convex function, the segment smoothness
bound and ||g||^2<=2f give

    f(X-hg)<=max{1,(h-1)^2}f(X), h>=0.                 (8)

For h<=2 this is descent. For h>2, use
f(X-hg)<=f(X)+h(h-2)||g||^2/2<=(h-1)^2f(X).
Apply (8) to the complete physical suffix beginning at r. This proves (1).
No sign assumption has been made within any V_a.

## 4. Consequence for arbitrary signed finite-support character kernels

Fix m>=4, N=2^m, n=N-1, and retain the original enclosing parameters

    sigma=1+sqrt(2), delta=1-1/m,
    c_chr=sigma(1+1/m), a_chr=delta/c_chr,
    h_i=1+a_chr*c_chr^(nu_2(i+1)), 0<=i<n.             (9)

Here nu_2 is the exponent of 2 dividing a positive integer. These are
exactly H_0=(), H_(j+1)=(H_j,1+a_chr*c_chr^j,H_j), with the SAME enclosing
m in every child. The last physical step is 1+a_chr<2.

Let w_s be any nonnegative probability law on s in {0,1}^m, and define
chi_s(i)=(-1)^(sum_l s_l i_l), k(d)=sum_s w_s chi_s(d). Suppose k(d)=0
outside a binary subgroup H of cardinality K. Prescribe queried gradients

    g_i=(Gamma_i sqrt(w_s)chi_s(i))_s, Gamma_i>0.

Then <g_i,g_j>=Gamma_i Gamma_j k(i XOR j). Different H-cosets span
orthogonal subspaces; all within-coset correlations may be negative.
If K>=2, deleting the one unqueried label N-1 leaves all M=N/K cosets
represented. Each coset has at most K queried labels, so the final run
contains at most K physical steps.

For that final suffix, write its indices as N-1-j with 1<=j<=q<=K.
Then nu_2(N-j)=nu_2(j). Levels zero and one have offsets a_chr<1 and
delta<1. For a level k>=2 the offset delta*c_chr^(k-1) is at most
c_chr^(k-1). Consequently

    A_tail <= c_chr^(2 sum_(j=1)^K (nu_2(j)-1)_+)
           = c_chr^(2 sum_(k>=2) floor(K/2^k))
           <= c_chr^K <= [5sigma/4]^K.                (10)

The displayed sum is finite and the middle identity just counts, for each
j, how many powers 2^k with k>=2 divide it. It is NOT an enumeration or a
numerical computation. If M>=2, (1) yields

    2f_n/R^2 <= [5sigma/4]^K beta_(N/K-1)
              <=8[5sigma/4]^K K^2/N^2.               (11)

For K=1 every queried gradient is orthogonal to every other, so M=n,
the final run is one step, and (1) applies directly with beta_(n-1).

Thus every fixed-size correlation subgroup fails to supply a nonvanishing
original-B payoff on (9), regardless of where its binary generators lie,
of any signs in its kernel, of its amplitudes, or of its terminal extension.
Indeed accepted `b98bb807d514e80d` gives
B_n<=1+4n^p, p=log_2(3)<2, so multiplying (11) by B_n tends to zero.
This also covers high-bit subgroups: moving a fixed support to a high
bridge is not an escape. The proof prices the actual FINAL run, rather
than bounding the entire interval after the last first occurrence.

More generally (1) holds without characters whenever the queried
gradients split into orthogonal subspaces with a controlled final run.
It does not exclude a dense correlation graph, growing components whose
actual suffix cost defeats (11), or geometries with correlations between
the proposed components.

## 5. A different full-support law with genuine negative history

For m>=4 define the real signs B=(-1)^s_0, C=(-1)^s_1, D=(-1)^s_2 and

    w_s=N^-1[1-B/2-CD/3+BCD/4].                        (12)

For (B,CD)=(+,+),(+,-),(-,+),(-,-), the density factors in brackets are
5/12,7/12,11/12,25/12. Each occurs equally often, their average is one,
and every atom is positive. The only nonzero nonconstant moments are

    k(1)=-1/2, k(6)=-1/3, k(7)=1/4.                   (13)

All other moments vanish by pairing a free character bit. Thus H={0,1,6,7}.
The moments do not form a product law in any linear sign basis on H:
none of -1/2,-1/3,1/4 equals the product of the other two. For example
1/4-(-1/2)(-1/3)=1/12. This is a different law from C89.

Its negative history is explicit. Every original leaf has correlation
-1/2; at the first level-two bridge the triple at indices 2,3,4 has
correlations -1/2,-1/3,+1/4 for pairs 23,24,34. A largest-representative
selection of the kind used in C89 has negative correlations with skipped
earlier members of its own coset. In particular the last queried gradient
g_(N-2) has correlation -1/3 with g_(N-8). C89's nonnegative-history
hypothesis fails; those negative terms cannot be discarded.

In every eight-label slab the coset labels occur as A,A,B,B,B,B,A,A.
The missing terminal label removes the last A of the last slab. Hence the
final queried run contains only index N-2. Its last physical step is less
than two, so A_tail=1. Applying the complete argument above gives

    V_sig=2f_n/R^2<=beta_(N/4-1)<=128/N^2,
    0<=B_n V_sig<=128(1+4n^p)/(n+1)^2 ->0.             (14)

This is uniform over all positive queried amplitudes, anchors, values,
functions, and free terminal gradients realizing this law.

## 6. Actual nonemptiness, all original pairs, and the true radius

The following explicit construction applies to (12), including a
prescribed terminal gradient, on the complete physical word (9).
In fact its pair checks work for any finite schedule with every h_i>1.
Define

    H_max=max_(i<n)h_i,
    kappa=min{1, min_(i<n)(h_i-1)/4},
    eta=kappa/[16(H_max+kappa+1)],
    Gamma_i=eta^i, f_i=(kappa+1/2)Gamma_i^2, 0<=i<=n,
    K_ij=<g_i,g_j>.

Use (12) and the character gradients for ALL labels 0,...,n. Set

    b_i=(2kappa+1)Gamma_i^2+sum_(t<i)h_t K_ti,
    (X_0)_s=[1/(N sqrt(w_s))]
                    sum_(i=0)^n(b_i/Gamma_i)chi_s(i),
    X_i=X_0-sum_(t<i)h_t g_t.                          (15)

The elementary character identity sum_s chi_s(i)chi_s(j)=N 1_(i=j)
follows by pairing a differing bit. Thus
<X_0,g_i>=b_i and <X_i,g_i>=(2kappa+1)Gamma_i^2.

For EVERY original pair i<j, direct substitution gives

    Q_ij=(kappa+1-h_i)Gamma_i^2-kappa Gamma_j^2
                        -sum_(i<t<j)h_t K_it-K_ij,
    Q_ji=(kappa+1)Gamma_j^2-kappa Gamma_i^2
                        +(h_i-1)K_ij+sum_(i<t<j)h_t K_jt. (16)

No signed term has been removed from these identities. For estimates use
|K_it|<=Gamma_i Gamma_t, which follows from the explicit unit character
directions and the nonnegative probability weights. Since eta<1/2 and
h_i>=1+4kappa, the first line divided by Gamma_i^2 is at most

    -3kappa+H_max eta/(1-eta)+eta
       <=-3kappa+2(H_max+1)eta <=-23kappa/8<0.

Writing d=j-i>=1, the second line divided by Gamma_i^2 is at most

    -kappa+(kappa+1)eta^2+H_max eta/(1-eta)
       <=-kappa+2(H_max+kappa+1)eta=-7kappa/8<0.

The original optimum rows are exactly

    Q_(i,star)=Q_(star,i)=-kappa Gamma_i^2<0.           (17)

In particular the smallest actual nonempty block, the first leaf
h_0=1+a_chr, has

    Q_01=kappa-a_chr-kappa eta^2+eta/2<0,
    Q_10=(kappa+1)eta^2-kappa-a_chr eta/2<0.            (18)

These are its genuine negative correlation terms, not a positive-history
surrogate. Equations (16)-(17) simultaneously check every later block,
both orientations of every remote pair, and all terminal pairs.

Here is ONE function fixed for the entire run. Put

    y_i=X_i-g_i, delta_i=f_i-||g_i||^2/2=kappa Gamma_i^2,
    H(v)=||v||^2/2+max{0,max_(0<=i<=n)(<y_i,v>-delta_i)},
    F_sig(X)=sup_v(<X,v>-H(v)).                        (19)

The constructive interpolation proof in accepted `1252b8823bb5a290`,
applied to this whole finite jet set, proves that F_sig is everywhere
finite, convex, differentiable, and 1-smooth, and has exactly all the
positions, gradients, and values (15)-(17). Its maximizer is unique
because H is 1-strongly convex. Thus the same F_sig realizes the original
GD recurrence for every physical step, without resets or padding.

Every delta_i is strictly positive. Consequently H(v)=||v||^2/2 in a
neighborhood of zero. Also H>=||v||^2/2, so F_sig(0)=0. For X!=0, choose
v=tX in that neighborhood with 0<t<1; then
F_sig(X)>=(t-t^2/2)||X||^2>0. Hence argmin F_sig={0}.
The TRUE radius is therefore exactly

    R^2=||X_0||^2
       =N^-2 sum_s w_s^-1
                       [sum_i(b_i/Gamma_i)chi_s(i)]^2. (20)

It is finite and at least one, since ||g_0||=1 and the gradient is
1-Lipschitz relative to its unique minimizer. The positive actual payoff is

    2F_sig(X_n)/R^2=(2kappa+1)eta^(2n)/R^2.            (21)

This construction proves nonemptiness and makes its radius cost explicit;
it is not claimed to be a competitive amplitude choice. Bound (14) applies
to every other legal amplitude choice as well.

## 7. Scope, literature, and remaining direction

The selected route was screened by direct proving. Equations (3)-(7) close
the signed component obstruction; (15)-(21) close full interpolation and
normalization of an actual signed law. The attempted positive normalized
payoff for a fixed-size component law is therefore excluded at joint-draft
level. This is not a verifier verdict.

Matlas was queried for negatively correlated GD block constructions and
strict finite interpolation; its hits did not supply the missing result.
An early web search recovered the finite interpolation literature. The
exact local text of Taylor--Hendrickx--Glineur, arXiv:1502.05666,
Section2.5, Theorem4, its proof, construction, Corollary1 and Remark3 was
read. The needed interpolation result is already proved in accepted
1252b8823bb5a290, so no external theorem is imported as a premise.

An actual uncovered freedom is a DENSE signed correlation geometry whose
query graph has one large component. Neither a different fixed-size
subgroup, higher-order dependence within it, nor moving it to high bits
escapes (11). A promising concrete next test is to strictify a known scalar
signed trajectory and add a small full-support orthogonal perturbation.
Its common function and radius must be built jointly: a covariance matrix
alone does not provide either. Such a lift would calibrate the dense law;
it would not automatically improve the known scalar payoff or supply the
uniform arbitrary-h target.
