## 2. The named dense family, enlarged beyond the experiment

Let h^0 be any exact dominant word attaining S_bullet(n), and let k be its
root bridge index. Its root children are exact primitive and dominant source
words, possibly empty. On each nonempty child I of length m_I put

 d_i=h_i^0-1>0,        1/2<=w_i<=3/2,
 W_I=sum_(i in I)w_i,
 A_I=sum w_i*d_i/W_I,  H_I=W_I/(sum w_i/d_i),
 c_I=(A_I+H_I)/2,     v_i=w_i(d_i-c_I),
 V_I=sum_(i in I)v_i.

Choose independent REAL amplitudes t_I for the children, and define

 h_i=h_i^0+t_I*v_i                    (i in child I),
 h_k=h_k^0-(1/4)sum_I t_I*V_I.        (root)

Require every actual h_i>=0. The amplitudes, weights, chosen maximizing
tree, and horizon may all vary. The experiment used one common positive
amplitude and w_i=1+tilt*(2j/(m_I-1)-1), with singleton weight1.
No certificate restriction is made in this definition.

**Claim.** Every such schedule satisfies, for n>=1,

 sum h_i <= S_bullet(n)+(27/8)(n-1),
 W_n(h) >= 1/[B_n+(27/4)(n-1)].                    (1)

In particular, uniformly over this entire enlarged family,

 W_n(h)/U_n^ZJ >= [1+27(n-1)/(4B_n)]^(-1) ->1.    (2)

This is a lower bound for unrestricted worst-case performance on the named
physical schedules. Full dual reselection, higher-rank remainders, both
optimum incidences and arbitrarily many new edges cannot evade it.

## 3. A block inequality from nonnegativity

Consider any nonempty base block h_i^0=1+d_i>1 of length m. Let its minimum
step be h_min, attained at j, and assume weights in [w_min,w_max] with
w_min>0. Define A,H,c,v,V as above. Write d_min=h_min-1.

The elementary weighted arithmetic/harmonic inequality H<=A follows from

 (sum w_i*d_i)(sum w_i/d_i) >= (sum w_i)^2:

expand the difference as sum_(i<j)w_i*w_j*(d_i/d_j+d_j/d_i-2), whose terms
are nonnegative because (d_i-d_j)^2/(d_i*d_j)>=0. Also H>=d_min, since
sum w_i/d_i <= (sum w_i)/d_min. Therefore, with W=sum w_i,

 V=W(A-H)/2=W(c-H)>=0,
 V<=W(c-d_min).                                    (3)

Put delta=c-d_min>=0. If delta=0, (3) forces V=0. If delta>0 and t>=0
keeps the jth step nonnegative, then

 0<=h_min-t*w_j*delta,
 t*delta<=h_min/w_j,
 t*V<=W*h_min/w_j <=m*(w_max/w_min)*h_min.          (4)

If t<0, t*V<=0, so the same upper bound holds. This is valid for each block
separately and permits independent amplitudes. It uses one actual small
step in that block; it is not a bound on a compressed recursion.

More generally the same argument works for any center c satisfying
H<=c<=A and A-c<=c-d_min. The midpoint rule meets these conditions.

## 4. Source words have a bounded small step in every nonempty child

Every source step is strictly greater than1. For a primitive bridge, writing
A=1+sum(left), C=1+sum(right), the positive number b-1 solves
(b-1)^2+(A+C)(b-1)-AC=0. For a dominant bridge it has the form
b=1+A/r with r=(1+sqrt(1+8A/D))/2>1 and D>0.

Every nonempty primitive source word contains a step sqrt(2): descend its
finite binary tree to a node with two empty children, where phi(0,0)=sqrt(2).
For a nonempty dominant word, if any primitive child on its dominant spine
is nonempty it contains such a step. Otherwise the last node on that spine
has two empty children, giving psi(0,0)=3/2. Thus every nonempty source word
has h_min<=3/2, without any assumption about maximizing split patterns.

Apply (4) to each root child, with w_max/w_min<=3 and h_min<=3/2. Then

 sum_I t_I V_I <= (9/2)sum_I m_I=(9/2)(n-1).

The root compensation makes the change of the TOTAL physical step sum
exactly (3/4)sum_I t_I V_I. This proves the first inequality of (1), even
when one child is empty or both children are singletons.

For any nonnegative schedule of total sum S, the legal scalar Huber
function of threshold a=1/(1+2S), started at1, has all iterates >=a,
x_n=1-aS and2f(x_n)=a. It has smoothness1 and unique minimizer0.
Consequently W_n(h)>=1/(1+2S), as also proved in b98bb807d514e80d.
Substitute the sum bound to get the second inequality of (1).

## 5. Uniform asymptotics from the exact recurrence

No unverified decimal asymptotic constant is needed. Put lambda=1+sqrt(2)
and P_j=1+S_circle(j). The legal balanced primitive construction gives

 P_(2^m-1)>=lambda^m,

by induction from P_0=1 and the primitive mass join
Omega(A,A)=lambda*A. The dominant mass join is
G(A,D)=2A+D/2+sqrt(D^2+8AD)/2. Taking a primitive prefix of length2^m-1
and an empty dominant suffix gives B_(2^m)>=2lambda^m.
Moreover B_(j+1)>=G(1,B_j)>B_j, by the allowed empty-prefix split.
For2^m<=n<2^(m+1),

 B_n/n >= (lambda/2)^m ->infinity.

Together with (1), this proves the uniform lower-bound ratio (2).
No conclusion is asserted about W_n(h) having a matching upper bound.

## 6. Scope, stress tests, and what remains

The calculation covers changes at every index, nonsymmetry, arbitrary
amplitudes for both children, and arbitrary bounded positive weights. Its
obstruction is the common subtractive center and the small source step,
not a fixed terminal library, sparse exception set, fixed support, or Huber
tightness of an upper certificate.

The proof identifies its load-bearing assumptions. An unbounded ratio
w_max/w_min only gives O((w_max/w_min)*n) added mass. Groups without a
bounded small step, a different center failing (3), or index-dependent
amplitudes inside one group leave this argument. Repeated separate
nonnegative mutations also accumulate their own cost and are not silently
covered by the single-round estimate. Removing those assumptions requires
a new construction and a new full-model/certificate argument; the generic
unknown SDP remains blocked as an asymptotic proof route.

This finding does not claim that all dense perturbations fail, or that the
exact benchmark is a local/global minimum over unrestricted schedules.
The original all-horizon/all-schedule target remains open.
