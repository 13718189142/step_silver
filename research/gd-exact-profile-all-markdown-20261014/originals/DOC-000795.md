## 1. The minimizer set and a projection certificate
For any finitely many vectors w_i and nonnegative offsets eta_i let
J(z)=max(0,max_i(<w_i,z>-eta_i)),
f(x)=min_z(J(z)+||x-z||^2/2).
The minimum defining f is attained because its nonnegative first term plus the quadratic is coercive. Thus f(x)=0 if and only if both nonnegative terms vanish, equivalently x=z and J(x)=0. Hence
argmin f = P := {z:<w_i,z><=eta_i for every i}.
In particular 0 is a minimizer but need not be closest to x_0.

A direct projection certificate is: y_proj belongs to P and
x_0-y_proj=sum_i lambda_i w_i, lambda_i>=0,
with lambda_i=0 unless <w_i,y_proj>=eta_i.
For any z in P, <x_0-y_proj,z-y_proj><=0; expanding ||x_0-z||^2 then proves ||x_0-z||^2>=||x_0-y_proj||^2. This proves the certificate without invoking an external projection theorem. A feasible y_proj alone gives the upper bound dist(x_0,P)^2<=||x_0-y_proj||^2, sufficient to strengthen a normalized lower bound.

For completeness, each use of a Moreau relay below is certified by this direct rule: if the indicated affine plane is active at z=x-w_i, then w_i is a subgradient of J there, so z is the unique minimizer and grad f(x)=w_i, f(x)=<w_i,x>-||w_i||^2/2-eta_i. Subgradient monotonicity proves that grad f is1-Lipschitz. Convexity follows by partial minimization, and uniqueness plus the two minimizing inequalities gives differentiability.
