# Two-curvature matrix mechanism — unverified screening

UNVERIFIED EXACT REALIZATION AND PAYOFF. For a,b in(0,1], let J_{a,b}(x)=a x^2/2 on x>=0 and b x^2/2 on x<=0. Its derivative is a x or b x on the two half-lines, agrees at0, is nondecreasing, and is1-Lipschitz (for x<=0<=y, a y-b x<=y-x). It is convex and differentiable, with minimizer0. Start at x0=+1 or-1 with probability1/2, so the initial squared distance is1 in every sample.

Define a nonnegative two-by-two matrix
N_h(a,b)=[[(1-ha)_+^2,(hb-1)_+^2],[(ha-1)_+^2,(1-hb)_+^2]].
If a column represents the expected squared magnitude currently on its indicated sign, multiplying by this matrix gives the next signed squared-magnitude masses. This holds because the step is x -> (1-ha)x on the positive half-line and x -> (1-hb)x on the negative half-line. A zero iterate contributes zero mass, so the sign convention at0 is irrelevant.
Thus for every predetermined nonnegative schedule h the exact expected normalized objective under this initial-sign law is
P_h(a,b)=(1/2)[a,b] N_{h_{n-1}}(a,b)...N_{h_0}(a,b)[1,1]^T.
For any Borel probability law nu_h on(0,1]^2, selected after the complete schedule is known, the joint law on (a,b,initial sign) consists only of legal normalized instances and yields
W_n(h)>=integral P_h(a,b)dnu_h(a,b).
The integrand is bounded for fixed finite h, since each normalized error is at most product_i(1+h_i)^2. No infimum is moved through expectation. The matrices retain order and state-dependent curvature; at a=b they reduce to the usual quadratic polynomial model.

Direct attack1 refutes the fixed-a=1 subclass as a universal hard family. For h=(1,H,1), H>=1, the positive initial point is killed at the first step. From x0=-1 one has x1=-(1-b). If Hb>=1, the second step reaches the nonnegative half-line and the final unit step kills it. If Hb<=1, x3=-(1-Hb)(1-b)^2. Therefore
P_{(1,H,1)}(1,b)=(b/2)(1-Hb)_+^2(1-b)^4<=2/(27H),
because max_{0<=t<=1}t(1-t)^2=4/27. The supremum over either deterministic initial sign is at most4/(27H). Every distribution over b has the same upper cap and it tends to0 with H at horizon3.

Direct attack2: let Lambda be any fixed finite subset of(0,1]. List its reciprocals in nondecreasing order and use them as the steps. Every objective J_{a,b} with a,b in Lambda is solved exactly from either initial sign. Before the first step equal to the reciprocal of the starting-side curvature, all steps are smaller, so the iterate retains its sign. At that reciprocal step it reaches0 and remains there. Appending zero steps gives exact solution for all larger horizons. Thus a fixed finite support of curvatures cannot furnish the sought all-schedule lower bound.

Neither attack settles the genuinely continuous two-curvature class. The unresolved statement would be: for every epsilon>0 there exists N_epsilon independent of h such that for every n>=N_epsilon and h in[0,infinity)^n there is a probability nu_h on(0,1]^2 with integral P_h dnu_h >=(1-epsilon)U_n^ZJ. Equivalently replace the integral by sup_{a,b} P_h(a,b), since point masses are allowed (no minimax interchange). This is a restricted-family theorem of the full desired lower-bound strength and is currently BLOCKED; the explicit matrix formula alone is not a bridge proving it.
