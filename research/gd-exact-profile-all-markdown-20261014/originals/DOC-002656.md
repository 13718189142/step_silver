# Four equality instances forbid positive intrinsic quadratic storage at a benchmark schedule

Status: unverified exact counterexample argument. No new solver or verifier call. This is a limitation of positive quadratic remainders in the intrinsic objective energy, not of all certificates or of the unrestricted minimax problem.

Put t=sqrt(2), lambda=1+t, and consider the known three-step schedule

    h=(t,lambda,3/2), S=5/2+2t, D=2S+1=6+4t=2lambda^2.

For nonnegative proof weights u=(u_0,u_1,u_2,u_3) with sum D, define

    d=x_0-x_3, U=sum_i u_i g_i, e=U-d,
    E_u=2sum_i u_i(f_i-f_3)+sum_i u_i||g_i||^2
          +2sum_i u_i<g_i,x_0-x_i>-||U||^2.

Use Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2<=0.

## Claim

If a symmetric PSD matrix P of size4 satisfies

    E_u >= sum_(i,j=0)^3 P_ij <g_i,g_j>               (1)

for every legal smooth convex trajectory of this schedule, then P=0.

This applies to any positive quadratic storage formed from fixed linear combinations of the four gradients: substituting those combinations into the storage matrix gives a PSD matrix P in (1). A nonzero matrix on redundant coordinates is possible only if its quadratic form vanishes identically on the GD coordinate subspace.

## 1. Why equality instances constrain every choice of proof weights

For every minimizer x_star, expansion gives

    ||x_0-x_star||^2+2sum_i u_i Q_(i,star)
       -2D(f_3-f_star)=||x_3-x_star-e||^2+E_u.      (2)

Each one-dimensional instance below has x_star=0, f_star=0, x_0=1, Q_(i,star)=0 at every query, and 2f_3=1/D. Thus (2) implies

    E_u=-|x_3-e|^2<=0.

Under (1), PSD gives E_u>=0, so E_u=0 and the gradient vector belongs to the kernel of P. This argument does not assume the standard proof-weight vector or any completeness theorem for interpolation certificates.

## 2. Four explicit legal equality instances

Every derivative displayed below is continuous, nondecreasing and1-Lipschitz. Its integral from zero is therefore differentiable, convex, 1-smooth and minimized at zero. The formulas also directly show f_i=x_i g_i-g_i^2/2 at the sampled points, giving Q_(i,star)=0.

### A. Global Huber plateau

Let c=1/D and set g(x)=max(-c,min(x,c)). All four sampled points remain on the positive affine piece, so their gradients are(c,c,c,c). The endpoint is x_3=1-Sc=(S+1)/D, and 2f_3=1/D. After division by the nonzero initial gradient, the gradient row is

    v_H=(1,1,1,1).

### B. Full quadratic

Take f(x)=x^2/2. The gradient row, with g_0=1, is

    v_Q=(1,1-t,2-t,-1+t/2).

The last entry is -(2-t)/2=-1/(2+t), and (2+t)^2=D, so 2f_3=1/D.

### C. Middle-cut asymmetric Huber

Let c=1/lambda and a=t/(4lambda). Set g(x)=max(-a,min(x,c)). Its exact points are

    x_0=1, x_1=c, x_2=-4a, x_3=-(5/2)a,

and its gradients are(c,c,-a,-a). Hence f_3=2a^2 and 2f_3=4a^2=1/D. Dividing the gradients by c gives

    v_M=(1,1,-t/4,-t/4).

### D. Capped quadratic with an inner return

Again let c=1/lambda, but use the different fixed derivative

    g(x)=x for x<=c, and g(x)=c for x>=c.

The exact points are

    x_0=1, x_1=c, x_2=-tc, x_3=(t/2)c.

The first step stays on the upper affine piece; the bridge enters the quadratic core, and the last step returns to its positive side. All points from x_1 onward are in the quadratic piece. Thus f_3=t^2 c^2/8=c^2/4 and 2f_3=1/(2lambda^2)=1/D. Dividing the gradients by c gives

    v_C=(1,1,-t,t/2).

This fourth equality case is essential. Using only the previous three rows would incorrectly leave room for a square proportional to

    ||g_2-g_3-(3/2)(t-1)(g_0-g_1)||^2.

For the capped quadratic, g_0=g_1 but g_2-g_3=-(3t/2)c is nonzero, whereas E_u=0 under any certificate (1).

## 3. The kernel spans the entire gradient space

Form the4-by4 matrix whose rows are v_H,v_Q,v_M,v_C. Subtract its first row from the other three. Expansion down the first column gives determinant

    (-t)(-1-t/4)(3t/2)=3(1+t/4)>0.                  (3)

The four rows therefore span R^4. For a PSD matrix, v^T P v=0 implies P v=0, as follows by diagonalizing P with nonnegative eigenvalues. Part1 puts all four independent rows in its kernel, proving P=0.

## 4. The obstruction occurs at an actual valid tight certificate

The usual weights are

    u=(t,2+t,2+t,2+t), sum u_i=D.

Their intrinsic energy has the explicit identity

    E_u=2lambda(-Q_10)+2(-Q_01)+D(-Q_32)
          +2lambda(-Q_21)+2lambda(-Q_31).            (4)

All coefficients are positive, so E_u>=0 and (2) yields W<=1/D. The four instances above attain this bound. Equation(4) can be checked directly from the GD differences x_1=x_0-tg_0, x_2=x_1-lambda g_1 and x_3=x_2-(3/2)g_2. This is also the primitive-one-step/dominant-one-step ConPD certificate, but no external closure theorem is needed for the displayed identity.

## Implication for retained-state propagation

The finite pair residual in max4_finite_pair_residual_transport.md is valid: that two-step objective bound leaves room for a positive quadratic remainder. Such a remainder cannot be carried as a nonzero PSD intrinsic-energy term through every exactly calibrated dominant schedule. At this three-step benchmark it must disappear, or become a structural zero, regardless of which nonnegative tight proof weights are selected.

The result does not exclude an indefinite storage coupled to function values, a state retaining actual interpolation slacks, a certificate at a smaller denominator, a different schedule, or a complete objective certificate in which the initial-distance square is still present. It rules out the specific plan of obtaining exact benchmark calibration and retaining a uniformly positive, nontrivial quadratic penalty depending on gradient-history linear aggregates alone. The complete original minimax target remains unresolved.

## 5. Further rigidity inside the intrinsic certificate class

The four equality instances also determine the proof weights uniquely. Equation(2) forces U=x_0 on each instance, since e=x_3 and d=x_0-x_3. The four independent gradient rows therefore determine u uniquely; the vector u=(t,2+t,2+t,2+t) satisfies these four equations and is the only possible choice.

Suppose in addition that an intrinsic certificate is written in the full finite-edge form

    E_u=sum_(i!=j) a_ij(-Q_ij)+g^T P g,
    a_ij>=0, P>=0.

Part3 gives P=0. Every edge with positive a_ij must have Q_ij=0 on all four equality instances. Direct substitution gives the intersection of allowable edges

    (0,1), (1,0), (2,1), (3,1), (2,3), (3,2).

For clarity, the global Huber and full quadratic make every Q zero. On the middle-cut asymmetric Huber, all edges between the two upper and two lower samples are strict except the lower-to-upper-corner edges(2,1),(3,1); within each affine ray both directions are zero. Its zero-edge set is exactly the six edges displayed above. The capped-quadratic instance keeps all six zero and rules out a nonzero PSD remainder as already proved.

Write the six coefficients as a,b,c,d,e,f in the displayed order. The f_0 coefficient and the g_0 g_1 coefficient give

    b-a=2t, a+(1-t)b=0,

so a=2 and b=2lambda. The g_1 g_2 and g_1 g_3 coefficients each give -t c=-2(2+t) and -t d=-2(2+t), respectively. Hence c=d=2lambda. The f_2 and g_3^2 coefficients then give f-e=D and f+e=D, so e=0 and f=D. Thus equation(4) is the UNIQUE certificate in this intrinsic finite-edge-plus-PSD class at the tight mass.

This is not a uniqueness theorem for arbitrary full PEP dual certificates with optimum-outgoing edges or for other representations involving the initial distance. It concerns precisely the intrinsic nonnegative-weight certificate class used by the current block-composition program.

## 6. A nontrivial indefinite quantity survives the obstruction

The absence of a PSD gradient remainder does not erase all usable state. Define

    T=<g_2-g_3, t g_1+g_2>.

The exact three-point identity is

    T=(-Q_31)-(-Q_21)-(-Q_32).                       (5)

Let E_prefix=2lambda(-Q_10)+2(-Q_01). Equation(4) gives, for every real theta with |theta|<=2lambda,

    E_u-theta T
      =E_prefix+(2lambda+theta)(-Q_21)
          +(D+theta)(-Q_32)+(2lambda-theta)(-Q_31)
      >=0.                                         (6)

In particular E_u>=2lambda|T|. The quadratic form T is indefinite, with its full matrix on(g_1,g_2,g_3) equal to

    [ 0,    t/2, -t/2 ]
    [ t/2,  1,   -1/2 ]
    [ -t/2,-1/2,  0   ].

It vanishes on all four equality modes, so it is consistent with the full-rank gradient obstruction. This gives an explicit interval of signed quadratic storage terms carried by actual interpolation slacks. The next composition must match a surviving cross term to this state, or retain its anchor function value; treating T as a squared norm would be invalid. A sign error in the initially published version of (5) was corrected on direct expansion: the correct order is I_31-I_21-I_32. The absolute-value bound and all preceding rigidity claims are unaffected.
