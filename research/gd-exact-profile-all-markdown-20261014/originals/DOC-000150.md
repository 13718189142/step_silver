## Controlled repair and inherited-component interference

Liu--Ye, [Silver Rate Is (Almost) Optimal for Gradient Descent: The Strongly Convex Case](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26873v1), v1 September22,2026.

Source scope: Theorem1.1 and Corollary1.2 concern strong convexity, relative
distance/function reduction and a subpolynomial condition-number loss. They do
not prove the convex exact constant/profile target. Sections2.2,3.2--3.3,4.2
and5.2 track activation level relative to remaining amplitude. The repair
construction controls this ratio; Lemma3.3 inserts a finite triangle-envelope
bridge that tolerates a nonnegative contribution from the previous component,
then removes interference in the next component's input coordinate. Complete
inactivity of an older component is not required. The stated bridge assumes
kappa>=4, small gap mass relative to kappa, positive available amplitude and
checkpoint size greater than4. Its transfer pays an explicit constant loss.

Main inference to test, not a theorem from this paper: the geometric lane may
benefit from tolerating a controlled inherited gradient rather than demanding
zero contribution everywhere. First compare this with the actual retained
Phi/Psi/Dist/G/Xi interface. A direct citation cannot remove the constant loss
or import a strong-convex limit into the current convex family. If the number
of repairs grows or the loss already defeats the near-critical scale, record
that exact failure and do not rebuild the exponent proof.

## Graph-structured full slack certificates

Bok--Altschuler, [Accelerating Proximal Gradient Descent via Silver Stepsizes](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/proxgd_stepsize_accel.pdf).

Source scope: Sections1.2 and3, Theorem3.1, Definition3.5 and Lemma3.6 concern
the specified silver proximal-GD construction. Two sets of nonnegative
interpolation multipliers and a full slack matrix certify multi-step descent.
After taking a Schur complement, the relevant matrix has Laplacian structure;
nonpositive off-diagonal entries and zero row sums give its PSD certificate.
The construction is recursive and does not establish completeness for arbitrary
GD certificates, general signed-terminal-credit re-selection, or minimax
optimality of arbitrary schedules. Its sign convention for interpolation is
opposite to this project's Q<=0 convention.

Main inference to test, not a result of the source: max4's re-selection problem
could be screened for an actual graph/Schur-complement repair which preserves
value flow and every coefficient of the full gradient matrix. A PSD graph
identity alone does not repair negative coefficients on actual interpolation
inequalities. The existing tight intrinsic-memory rigidity still applies to
its stated class; merely adding positive quadratic memory is not a new route.
