# P5 extension: exact history-aware plateau actions
Status: new complete construction and quantitative tests; unverified. No new verifier allocation requested. The uniform exact-profile comparison is still BLOCKED.

## 1. History and orientation
Work at L=1, minimizer0, initial point1. A finite history H contains(0,0) and gradient knots(y,v) satisfying 0<=v-w<=y-z whenever y>=z. Define
ell_H(r)=max_(y,v in H)[v-(y-r)_+],
u_H(r)=min_(y,v in H)[v+(r-y)_+].
The extrema of nondecreasing1-Lipschitz functions are again nondecreasing1-Lipschitz. The history inequalities show that both envelopes interpolate every knot. Hence a new gradient at r is legal exactly in[ell_H(r),u_H(r)]. Integrating either envelope gives a global convex1-smooth function with minimizer0. For a completed path ending at x, its largest possible normalized value is
Phi(H,x)=2 integral_0^x u_H(r)dr for x>=0,
Phi(H,x)=-2 integral_x^0 ell_H(r)dr for x<=0.
This follows by the pointwise envelope inequalities and is attained by integrating the indicated entire envelope. Every previous queried gradient is preserved.

For a nonzero current point, reflect if necessary so that it becomes a positive radius x: replace every knot(y,v) by(sigma y,sigma v), where sigma is the sign of the current point. All formulas below concern this reflected positive coordinate. Multiply selected magnitudes by sigma to obtain the original gradient. At x=0 choose gradient0.

## 2. Exact feasibility of a constant-gradient future block
Fix a nonnegative proposed block mass S. A magnitude q>=0 can be imposed on the entire interval[x-Sq,x] consistently with the old history exactly when
q belongs to I(H,x,S):=[ell_H(x), C(H,x,S)],
where
C(H,x,S)=min_(y,v in H) max{v,(x-y+v)/(1+S)}.                 (1)
The interval may be empty.

To prove this, add endpoint knots(x,q) and(z,q), where z=x-Sq. Monotonicity of the envelopes makes their joint feasibility equivalent to
q>=ell_H(x), q<=u_H(z).
The origin knot implies that the latter inequality with q>0 forces z>=q>0. For q=0, z=x>0. Thus the imposed plateau stays on the selected ray. For one old knot the upper inequality is
q<=v+(x-y-Sq)_+.
The continuous function on the right minus q is strictly decreasing, with unique zero
max{v,(x-y+v)/(1+S)}.
Indeed if x-y<=Sv the zero is v; otherwise it is(x-y+v)/(1+S). Intersecting these inequalities proves(1). The origin knot also gives ell_H(x)>=0 and C(H,x,S)<=x/(1+S).

Once both endpoint knots are feasible, the derivative has to be constant q between them; adding any intermediate knot with value q preserves legality. Hence every nonnegative step block of total mass S follows this plateau, in its actual chronological order. No individual stepsize bound is needed.

## 3. Exact optimal completion by a constant-gradient tail
For a tail of total mass S, any q in I(H,x,S) has exact maximal normalized terminal value
A(H,x,S;q)=2 integral_0^(x-Sq) min{u_H(r),q}dr.             (2)
Adding the two endpoint knots makes the new upper envelope on[0,x-Sq] exactly min{u_H(r),q}. Integrate that global new envelope. The resulting function attains(2), realizes the entire constant-gradient tail and every old gradient, and has initial squared distance1.

This optimum is a concrete finite calculation from the old knots. The function u_H is continuous piecewise affine with slopes0 or1 and finitely many pieces. On a rising piece write u_H(r)=r-d. For q whose level lies in that piece, put
E=integral_0^(q+d)u_H(r)dr-q^2/2;
E is constant as q varies through that rising piece. Formula(2) becomes
A=2E+2q(x-d)-(1+2S)q^2.                                 (3)
Its only interior stationary point is
q=(x-d)/(1+2S).                                          (4)
An optimizing q can therefore be selected by checking:
- the endpoints of the nonempty interval I(H,x,S);
- the finitely many plateau heights of u_H that belong to I;
- the values(4) whose level lies in the associated rising piece and in I.
The formula also covers zero levels by including the interval endpoints. Comparing these finitely many exact values selects q_stop(H,x,S). No numerical enumeration of trajectories or optimization over all functions is being assumed.

The offsets d and retained areas E in(3) are compulsory history data. The former proposal x/(1+2S) silently replaced d by0 and need not be optimal even when it is feasible.

## 4. A specified finite enlarged action tree
At time t, with current point x_t and full gradient history H_t, retain the old P5 actions:
(a) the signed steep envelope;
(b) the projection of x_t/(1+2S_t) onto the complete feasible interval, where S_t=sum_(r=t)^(n-1)h_r.

Add the following actions:
(c) the signed value q_stop for S=S_t, whenever I is nonempty;
(d) for each future checkpoint j in{t,...,n-1}, the signed value C(H,|x_t|,S_(t,j)), whenever I is nonempty, where
S_(t,j)=sum_(r=t)^(j-1)h_r.
History is reflected as in Section1 for(c),(d). Choose any listed action, add its knot, and apply the given h_t. At the end use Phi.

This is an explicit finite tree, with at most n+3 listed actions at each time, at most(n+3)^n leaves, and no free continuum of gradient choices. It starts from H_0={(0,0)}, x_0=1, rather than requiring a preselected seed. Every action is pathwise legal by the formulas above. Thus every completed leaf is a single globally defined legal one-dimensional function, and its displayed payoff is at most W_n(h).

When action(c) chooses a constant-tail magnitude q, the rest of that tail is present in the tree: choose the signed steep action thereafter. All its future radii lie in[z,x], where z=x-S_t q. The old upper envelope is at least q throughout this interval by feasibility at z, while the newly added knot(x,q) bounds the new upper envelope by q at all smaller radii. Hence every following signed steep choice is exactly q and realizes the completion in(2).

Let M_new(h) be the maximum leaf payoff. A genuine probability law whose expectation loses a uniformly vanishing factor is obtained by giving a leaf of value Z>=0 mass proportional to Z^(n^2), for n>=1. If all values vanish, use any law. With N leaves and p=n^2, Jensen's inequality for a->a^(1+1/p) gives
[sum Z^(p+1)]/[sum Z^p] >= [N^(-1)sum Z^p]^(1/p)
 >= N^(-1/p) max Z.
Consequently the expected normalized payoff T_new satisfies
(n+3)^(-1/n) M_new(h) <= T_new(h) <= M_new(h) <= W_n(h).   (5)
The factor tends to1 independently of h. This distribution can be sampled as conditional branch probabilities using sums of descendant pth powers; their products telescope to the leaf law. This is a symbolic specification, not a computation performed here. The schedule is fixed before the law or any function is chosen.

For n=0 take f(x)=x^2/2, with normalized error1.

## 5. Inclusion of the old policy and the accepted ray construction
The tree contains all original forced-prefix P5 leaves. Indeed choosing checkpoint j initially gives C=1/(1+P_j), where P_j=sum_(r<j)h_r. If q=C was chosen for a block of mass S and a first step of mass a<=S has been performed, q is still feasible for the residual block from x-aq. The new knot(x,q) gives the residual upper bound C_new<=q, while feasibility gives C_new>=q. Thus C_new=q. Repeating the same lookahead checkpoint keeps exactly the old constant-gradient prefix. At the checkpoint itself S=0, so C=u_H at the query, again equal to q. Thereafter the two original actions are retained.

It also contains the exact alternating-ray leaves in accepted fact1fca3e5dd555cb4a. At a new outward stage let(A_old,w_old) be the previous record on that ray, d=A_old-w_old, and let the current radius satisfy x=d+(1+S)m with m>=w_old. Every earlier same-ray knot(y,v) satisfies y-v<=d and v<=w_old. The opposite-ray knots satisfy y-v<=0<=d. Formula(1) therefore gives C>=m, while the record knot gives C<=max(w_old,(x-d)/(1+S))=m. Thus the future-checkpoint action is exactly m. The residual-block argument then follows every plateau in that fact. At the terminal stage the stored knots force the same old upper derivative envelope as its slope1 ramps and flat segments. Formula(3) has its exact retained area e_term and offset d; q_stop is precisely its q_opt. Therefore
M_new(h)>=F_ray_n(h)>=K_n(h)
using the accepted fact, without discarding any ray feasibility condition.

This inclusion is a comparison of constructions, not a second independent claim that the ray family solves the target. The added actions are also available at non-record states, and the old P5 inner-return branches are preserved.

The last old steep action takes gradient7/2 and ends at1/2, giving1/4. The last old interior action takes gradient4/3 and ends at8/3. Its maximal normalized value is
