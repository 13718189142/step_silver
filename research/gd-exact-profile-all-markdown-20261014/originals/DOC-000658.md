## 2. One function and the exact inverse operations

On a finite probability space with strictly positive weights, use the Euclidean Hilbert space

\[
 E=\{X:\mathbb EX=0\},\qquad
 \|X\|^2=\mathbb EX^2,
 \qquad K=\{g\in E:g_i\ge-1\}.
\]

The coordinate map X_i -> sqrt(p_i) X_i identifies E with an ordinary finite-dimensional Euclidean subspace. The function

\[
 F(X)=\max_{g\in K}\{\mathbb E[Xg]-\tfrac12\mathbb E g^2\}
\]

is convex and differentiable with gradient P_K X. The projection is nonexpansive, hence F is globally 1-smooth. Also F >= 0 and F(0)=0. Since 0 lies in the relative interior of K, the only X in E with P_K X=0 is X=0. Thus the selected minimizer is the original point 0, uniquely.

The actual projection and update are

\[
 g=(X-t)_+-1,\qquad \mathbb E(X-t)_+=1,
 \qquad Y=X-hg.
\]

In the inverse formulas, a denotes the threshold parameter; the separately scoped hierarchy parameter in Section 3 is named a as in the task. For h=1+d>1, choose a >= max(d,max Y), put Z=a-Y, and select conditional probabilities omega in [0,1] with E[omega Z]=d. Replace each atom by the two preimages

\[
 X_L=Y-h=a-h-Z,\qquad
 X_R=a-h+Z/d.
 \tag{2.1}
\]

Their probabilities are the old probability times 1-omega and omega. Their mean is zero. At threshold a-h their positive excess has mean E[omega Z]/d=1, so (2.1) gives the actual projection. Both branches map exactly to the old Y. Zero-weight branches are omitted. Duplicating every already-built future value on each split preserves all future thresholds, gradients, and objectives. After finitely many splits there is one fixed finite function and one fixed full physical trajectory.

These branches also exhaust the preimages for this fixed simplex geometry. Any physical threshold t obeys t>=-1, since 1=E(X-t)_+>=-t. On either branch the output is at most t+h; thus a=t+h>=max(d,max Y). Solving the inactive and active update formulas gives exactly (2.1), and mean zero gives E[omega Z]=d.

Writing J=E[omega Z^2], direct expansion gives

\[
 V(X)=V(Y)+2ha-h^2+(d^{-2}-1)J.
 \tag{2.2}
\]

More generally, for A,B >= 0,

\[
 \mathcal C_{A,B}(X)=V(X)+2AF(X)+B\|P_KX\|^2
\]
\[
 =V(Y)+2(h+A)a-h^2-2Ah+A-B
   +\left(\frac{1+A+B}{d^2}-1\right)J.
 \tag{2.3}
\]

All costs in this report are computed at the original minimizer. No physical rescaling of a child is used.

### 2.1 Exact facts about minimizing (2.2)

At fixed a, minimizing J fills the highest Y values first; maximizing J fills the lowest Y values first. This follows because the amount of constraint filled by an atom is omega(a-Y), while its contribution to J divided by that amount is a-Y. There is at most one fractional cutoff atom, so a fixed-step minimizer requires at most one extra atom.

For a cutoff value z and the fully selected set A, on each interval with the same cutoff,

\[
 J'(a)=d+\mathbb E[1_A(z-Y)].
 \tag{2.4}
\]

For 1<h<2, the minimizing allocation is the upper tail. Consequently

\[
 \frac{d}{da}\min_\omega V(X)
 =\frac{h^2}{d}-(d^{-2}-1)\mathbb E(Y-z)_+.
 \tag{2.5}
\]

Define

\[
 \gamma_h=\frac{hd}{1-d}=\frac{h(h-1)}{2-h}.
 \tag{2.6}
\]

If a minimizing allocation used a positive fraction of a minimum atom y_min, then z=y_min and E(Y-z)_+=-y_min. If -y_min > gamma_h, (2.5) is strictly negative. Increasing a slightly would reduce the cost while that fraction remains positive. Therefore **no minimizing allocation selects the minimum atom whenever -y_min > gamma_h and max Y>d**. This conclusion includes breakpoint minimizers: a minimum atom with zero selected fraction remains entirely on the left branch.

For h>2, the minimizing allocation is the lower tail. In (2.4),

\[
 0\le \mathbb E[1_A(z-Y)]\le d,
\]

because z<=a and the selected constraint mass is d. Thus d<=J'(a)<=2d. Since d^{-2}-1<0, the derivative of the optimized cost is at least

\[
 2h+(d^{-2}-1)2d=2+2/d>0.
\]

Hence the optimal threshold is always a=max(d,max Y). If M=max Y>d and the minimum atom has enough capacity, the optimum selects only that minimum atom and takes a=M.

These are one-step optimization statements, not statements about the least original radius over a whole prefix.

## 3. The complete inherited hierarchy and useful elementary bounds

Fix any m>=1536, and keep these same enclosing parameters in every child:

\[
 \sigma=1+\sqrt2,\quad \epsilon=1/(4m),\quad
 \beta=1-\epsilon,\quad c=\sigma(1+\epsilon),\quad a=\beta/c,
\]
\[
 H_0=\varnothing,\qquad H_{j+1}=(H_j,1+a c^j,H_j).
\]

Let n_m=2^m-1, S_j=sum H_j, D_m=1+2S_m, and abbreviate B=B_(n_m). The terminal law in both approaches is

\[
 Y_n=\pm Q\quad\hbox{with equal probability},
 \qquad Q=(B+1)/2.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

Here Q>1, the terminal projection is +/-1, and 2F(Y_n)=2Q-1=B.

The following estimates use no numerical calculation.

First, C_(2^j-1)>=sigma^j follows by balanced splits. Also B_n>=C_n: indeed Omega(x,y)<=2x+y<=G(x,y), and induction uses monotonicity of G. Therefore

\[
 B\ge G(C_{n_{m-1}},C_{n_{m-1}})
 =4C_{n_{m-1}}\ge4\sigma^{m-1},
 \qquad Q>2\sigma^{m-1}>2^m.
 \tag{3.2}
\]

Second,

\[
 (1+1/(4m))^m<\sum_{k\ge0}(1/4)^k=4/3.
 \tag{3.3}
\]

The largest bridge excess is d_max=a c^(m-1)=beta c^(m-2), whence

\[
 \frac{d_{\max}}{Q}<\frac{2}{3\sigma}<\frac13.
 \tag{3.4}
\]

The steps at levels 0 and 1 are h_0=1+a<sqrt(2) and h_1=1+beta=2-epsilon. Their gamma values satisfy gamma_(h_0)<1 and gamma_(h_1)<8m. Every step at level at least 2 is >2. Its smallest excess is

\[
 d_{\rm low}=a c^2=\beta c=\sigma(1-\epsilon^2),
 \qquad 2<d_{\rm low}<5/2.
 \tag{3.5}
\]

The upper bound c<5/2 is immediate for m>=1536, for example from sqrt(2)<17/12. We also have the stronger c<17/7 used later.

Finally, c(c-2)>1>beta, so 0<a/(c-2)<1. Solving the exact sum recurrence gives

\[
 S_j+1=\frac{a}{c-2}c^j+
       \left(1-\frac{a}{c-2}\right)2^j,
 \qquad S_j<c^j-1\quad(j\ge1).
 \tag{3.6}
\]

In particular D_m<2c^m. The accepted comparison used below is

\[

## 4. Approach 1: minimize the true one-step variance at every reverse step

### 4.1 Policy and statement

Start at (3.1), process the complete physical word H_m backward, and at each step minimize the actual predecessor variance (2.2) over all admissible a and omega. Choose an extreme fractional-knapsack allocation; aggregate equal-value atoms. The optimum exists: for 1<h<2 the objective is continuous piecewise affine and eventually has positive slope, while for h>2 its minimum is at the lower endpoint as shown above. Any remaining threshold tie can be resolved by taking the least minimizing a.

This is a fully specified finite-law construction, with at most n_m+2 atoms after all splits. The following bound is obtained for its actual payoff, denoted P_G(m):

\[
 V_0>Q(7/5)^{\,2^{m-3}-1},
 \qquad
 P_G(m)=\frac{B}{V_0}
 <2(7/5)^{-\,2^{m-3}+1}.
 \tag{4.1}
\]

Consequently

\[
 B P_G(m)
 <\frac{16}{3}\sigma^m(7/5)^{-\,2^{m-3}+1}
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{4.2}
\]

This is a failure of this actual witness policy, even with the benchmark-scaled terminal law. It does not bound W_(n_m)(H_m) from above.

### 4.2 Exact cap dynamics

Write the current minimum and maximum as y_min and M, their separation as Delta=M-y_min, and the probability of the minimum as p_min.

At a step h=1+d>2, suppose M>d and d/Delta<p_min. The unique optimal threshold is a=M and the lower-tail allocation selects only the minimum value. The total probability sent to the right branch is

\[
 \theta=d/\Delta.
\]

The new minimum and maximum are respectively

\[
 y_{\min}'=y_{\min}-h,\qquad
 M'=M-h+\Delta/d.
\]

The remaining minimum probability is p_min-theta, and

\[
 \boxed{\Delta'=(1+1/d)\Delta.}
 \tag{4.3}
\]

All other atoms move left by h. The new maximum has mass theta. Thus a small removed bottom mass creates a very distant upper atom. The next physical laws remain exactly the previously fixed laws.

At either of the two smaller step types, as long as the minimum is <=-Q, Section 2.1 shows it is never selected: Q>8m exceeds both gamma values. It moves left by h with unchanged mass. Every old maximum has some preimage at least M-h, irrespective of which branch is used. Thus

\[
 \Delta'\ge\Delta
 \tag{4.4}
\]

at a smaller step.

### 4.3 Bottom-mass bootstrap over the entire word

The word H_m is a palindrome. Reading it backward gives the same hierarchy. There are

\[
 N=2^{m-2}-1
\]

steps >2. In their chronological order within the reversed word, indexed k=1,...,N, their excesses are

\[
 d_k=\beta c^{1+v_2(k)}.
 \tag{4.5}
\]

In particular every odd k is a lowest cap with d_k=d_low. Since c<5/2<4,

\[
 d_k<(5/2)k^2.
 \tag{4.6}
\]

Assume the formulas have applied through the preceding caps. Equations (4.3)--(4.4), the initial separation 2Q, and d_low<5/2 give, before cap k,

\[
 \Delta_k\ge2Q(7/5)^{\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor}.
 \tag{4.7}
\]

Therefore its prospective removed mass satisfies

\[
 \theta_k=\frac{d_k}{\Delta_k}
 <\frac5{4Q}\,k^2(5/7)^{\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor}.
\]

The entire infinite majorant is finite and explicit:

\[
 \sum_{k\ge1} k^2(5/7)^{\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor}
 =\sum_{j\ge0}(8j^2+12j+5)(5/7)^j
 =1085/2.
\]

Hence

\[
 \sum_k\theta_k<\frac{5425}{8Q}<\frac{700}{Q}<\frac14.
 \tag{4.8}
\]

This closes the bootstrap, rather than presupposing feasibility. Before every step the surviving minimum mass exceeds 1/4. The minimum equals -Q minus the sum of all already inverted steps. Mean zero then gives

\[
 M\ge\frac{p_{\min}(-y_{\min})}{1-p_{\min}}
 \ge Q/3>d_{\max}.
 \tag{4.9}
\]

Thus the cap threshold is indeed M. At the next cap, its prospective theta is less than 1/4 by (4.8), so it is less than the available minimum mass. The cap formula applies, and the same cumulative bound leaves the minimum mass greater than 1/4 afterward. At a smaller step, Q>8m and M>d permit Section 2.1, which leaves that mass unchanged. Induction covers the complete word.

No minimum is exhausted. In particular (4.3) is a real law update at every cap, not a recurrence assumed without feasible weights.

### 4.4 Original radius and actual payoff

There are 2^(m-3) lowest caps. Before the last cap,

\[
 \Delta\ge2Q(7/5)^{2^{m-3}-1}.
\]

That last cap has d=d_low>2. After it, the surviving minimum has mass >1/4, the new maximum has mass theta=d/Delta, and their separation is (1+1/d)Delta. The pairwise variance identity gives

\[
 V\ge p_{\min}'\theta\bigl((1+1/d)\Delta\bigr)^2
 >\frac{d\Delta}{4}>\frac\Delta2.
 \tag{4.10}
\]

The remaining reversed suffix is H_2, whose three steps all lie in (0,2). Such an inverse cannot decrease the variance: projection optimality against 0 gives <X,P_K X>>=||P_K X||^2, and therefore

\[
 V(X)-V(X-hP_KX)
 \ge h(2-h)\|P_KX\|^2\ge0.
\]

Thus (4.10) lower-bounds the original variance V_0 and proves (4.1). Since B/Q<2, B<D_m<2c^m, and c^m<(4/3)sigma^m, it also proves (4.2).

The decisive failure mechanism is not a lack of feasible inverse laws. All weights and all physical iterates exist. Repeated lowest caps retain almost all the original bottom mass while their rare upper atoms force exponentially growing support and original variance.

## 5. Approach 2: retain the primitive prefix potential and propagate a flat upper face

The first approach cannot treat local variance as whole-prefix cost. This second approach changes the optimized objective to the exact primitive-form potential. Its proposed invariant is a symmetric two-point endpoint, for which the whole prefix has a directly realizable constant-gradient completion. The invariant does propagate, but on H_m it propagates precisely the deficient Huber trajectory.

### 5.1 Exact inverse minimizer on a two-point law

For any capacity C>=1, use

\[
 A=C-1,\qquad B_{\rm cost}=C(C-1).
\]

Here B_cost is a local coefficient and is not B_n. Consider a physical step h=1+d with 0<d<C, and a next law Y=+/-q with equal probabilities. Put

\[
 w=C^2/d^2-1>0,\quad T=C+d,\quad
 \gamma=\frac{d(C+d)}{C-d}=d+2T/w.
\]

Assume q>gamma. In particular q>d, so a>=q. At fixed a, (2.3) selects the upper tail. The optimized J is explicitly

\[
 J(a)=
 \begin{cases}
 (d-q)a+dq+q^2,&q\le a\le q+2d,\\
 d(a-q),&a\ge q+2d.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{5.1}
\]

The slope of the full cost on the first interval is

\[
 2T+w(d-q)=w(\gamma-q)<0;
\]

on the second interval it is 2T+wd=w gamma>0. Hence the **unique minimizing threshold** is

\[
 a=q+2d.
\]

At this threshold the old positive atom is entirely on the right branch and the negative atom entirely on the left. There is no fractional split, and the actual predecessor law is exactly

\[
 \boxed{X=\pm(q+h)\quad\hbox{with equal probabilities}.}
 \tag{5.2}
\]

This is an attained, whole-law result. It uses the same cost coefficients that generate the PP root identity, and it shows when that objective unavoidably selects the nonbranching Huber inverse.

### 5.2 The benchmark capacity also stays in this regime at every inherited level

Index the physical steps by i=0,...,n_m-1. A step at level j has d=a c^j, and its prior prefix has length at least 2^j-1. The benchmark C_i is nondecreasing, since Omega(C_i,1)>C_i is an allowed next split. Thus

\[
 C_i\ge C_{2^j-1}\ge\sigma^j.
\]

By (3.3), the ratio at every such step obeys

\[
 \frac d{C_i}<\frac4{3\sigma}<1.
 \tag{5.3}
\]

Together with (3.4), this gives

\[
 \frac\gamma Q
 <\frac{2(3\sigma+4)}{3\sigma(3\sigma-4)}<1.
 \tag{5.4}
\]

The last strict inequality has an exact algebraic check. For x=3sigma, sigma^2=2sigma+1 gives x^2-6x-8=1>0, which is precisely x(x-4)>2(x+4).

Starting at q=Q, (5.2) increases q by each reversed physical step. Therefore q>=Q>gamma remains true at every stage. The policy that minimizes (2.3) using the true benchmark capacity C_i at each prefix consequently has the actual invariant

\[
 X_i=\pm\left(Q+\sum_{t=i}^{n_m-1}h_t\right).
 \tag{5.5}
\]

It never produces a new atom or a new active face. Evaluating the benchmark potential at a nonprimitive prefix is a policy choice here; it is **not** a proved representation of that prefix's globally minimum inverse radius. The all-step construction (5.5), however, is real and its radius is exact.

### 5.3 The actually realizable prefix-sum potential has the same outcome

For completeness, another natural capacity is C=1+P_i, where P_i is the actual sum of the preceding physical steps. It should not be claimed that C>=sigma^j: at small inherited levels that inequality can fail. Instead, the immediately preceding left child gives P_i>=S_j. Equation (3.6) yields

\[
 \frac{d}{1+P_i}\le\frac{a c^j}{S_j+1}\le c-2<3/7.
\]

Thus gamma<(5/2)d <=(5/2)d_max<Q, by (3.4). This policy therefore also obeys (5.5).

On every selected endpoint +/-r, its entire preceding word is physically realizable by a constant gradient, and the exact radius is

\[
 \mathcal C_{P_i,P_i(P_i+1)}(\pm r)
 =r^2+2P_i(r-1/2)+P_i(P_i+1)
 =(r+P_i)^2.
\]

This accounts for the entire prefix on the chosen invariant. It does not assert that the same scalar potential equals the least prefix radius for every nonflat law.

### 5.4 Exact fixed loss on the complete hierarchy

For either capacity choice, the one fixed two-atom function ends at +/-Q and starts at +/-(Q+S_m). Its actual payoff P_L(m) is

\[
 P_L(m)=\frac B{(Q+S_m)^2}.
\]

Since Q+S_m=(B+D_m)/2, (3.7) gives

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 B P_L(m)=\frac{4B^2}{(B+D_m)^2}
 <\left(\frac{94}{97}\right)^2
 =1-\frac{573}{9409}.
 \quad}
 \tag{5.6}
\]

This is a nonvanishing loss for every m>=1536. The inverse objective has changed materially from Approach 1 and avoids its rare-tail explosion, but it still fails the required benchmark on the complete inherited hierarchy.

## 6. What these attempts settle, and what remains open

Both policies have one static finite-dimensional convex 1-smooth function, a valid original minimizer, genuine nonnegative predetermined steps, exact original-radius accounting, and the actual final objective. Their failure is established on the complete hierarchy with inherited parameters, rather than on a fixed horizon or a child with silently refreshed parameters.

Approach 1 rules out the proposed invariant that greedy one-step variance minimization at benchmark terminal scale keeps the support and original cost controlled. Equations (4.3), (4.8), and (4.10) are the decisive actual-law inequalities. The obstruction applies even though every local minimization is exact.

Approach 2 rules out the proposed two-point/flat-face closure when its inverse choices minimize either the benchmark primitive-form potential or the physically realizable prefix-sum potential. Equations (5.1)--(5.5) derive the invariant and its cost; (5.6) gives its all-depth failure. In particular the PP root coefficient match, even combined with a correctly scaled terminal two-point law, does not make this closure work.

Neither argument proves that the two-point terminal law fails under the true globally optimal whole-prefix cost. That cost may differ materially from both objectives used here on intermediate nonflat laws. Nor do these arguments exclude terminal laws with several selected values, full-word-dependent boundary masses, or a different fixed projection body. Those are unexamined alternatives, not claims proved viable in this allocation.

The missing object remains an actual whole-word compatible construction with a uniform original-radius bound, not another identity for the coefficients. No bound V_0<=(1+o(1))B_n^2 has been derived for arbitrary high-mass words or for H_m. No all-word lower theorem or full-class asymptotic upper certificate follows from this report. There is no complete target-level candidate for a formal audit.
