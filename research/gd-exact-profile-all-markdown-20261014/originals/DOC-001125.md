## 3. A finite circular body and its elementary envelope

Fix 0<beta_arc<=1/4. Write beta=beta_arc locally in Sections3--10,
always distinct from beta_word in the ruler definition. Set

    R_circ=1+beta,
    c_arc=beta+sqrt(1+2 beta),
    p(q)=beta+sqrt(R_circ^2-(q+beta)^2), 0<=q<=1,
    v(q)=(p(q),-q),
    K_ctr=conv({0} union {v(q):0<=q<=1}).             (3.1)

The circular center is (beta,beta). K_ctr is fresh notation; the project glossary K_arc denotes an older
spiral body and is not changed. Its endpoints are (c_arc,0) and
(beta,-1). Its containing closed disk also contains0, because
2 beta^2 <= (1+beta)^2. Thus the circular radial vector
(p(q)-beta,-q-beta) supports K_ctr at v(q). The normal (0,1)
also supports at the horizontal endpoint.

Every (p,-q) in K_ctr satisfies

    0<=p<=c_arc, 0<=q<=1, p>=beta q,
    and (p,0) belongs to K_ctr.                       (3.2)

The last assertion follows because the whole horizontal segment from0
to(c_arc,0) is in K_ctr. On the arc, p>=beta and q<=1, so the third
inequality holds there and hence on the convex hull.

For a compact convex body C containing0 define the finite function

    E_C(r)=max_(g in C) [<g,r>-||g||^2/2].            (3.3)

Completing the square shows that its unique maximizer is Pi_C(r),
the Euclidean projection onto C. The projection inequalities at r,s
imply

    ||Pi_C(r)-Pi_C(s)||^2
        <= <Pi_C(r)-Pi_C(s),r-s>.                    (3.4)

In particular the maximizer is1-Lipschitz. Bounding the increment of
(3.3) above and below using its maximizers at r and r+d shows that the
linear part is <Pi_C(r),d> and the remainder is bounded by ||d||^2.
Consequently E_C is differentiable with gradient Pi_C and is1-smooth.
It is convex as a maximum of affine functions, and nonnegative because
0 is allowed. The projection criterion is

    g=Pi_C(r) iff <r-g,w-g><=0 for every w in C.      (3.5)

Its zero set is the polar cone of C: if a support value is positive,
a sufficiently small positive multiple of that supporting vector gives
a positive value in(3.3); if all support values are nonpositive the
maximum is0.

The cone of K_ctr is generated by its two nonzero endpoints, since

    (p,-q)=[(p-beta q)/c_arc](c_arc,0)+q(beta,-1).

Therefore

    E_(sigma K_ctr)(U,V)=0
        iff U<=0 and beta U-V<=0                    (3.6)

for every sigma>0.

We also use the one-sided Huber function

    Hub_delta(z)=0                    if z<=0,
                 z^2/2                if 0<=z<=delta,
                 delta z-delta^2/2    if z>=delta,   (3.7)

where delta>0. Its derivative is min(delta,max(z,0)); hence it is
convex, nonnegative, differentiable and1-smooth.

## 4. Exact local trajectory for the convex physical update

Let a_1,...,a_N be any finite nonnegative ordinary block, in its actual
physical order. Define alpha_j=a_j/2 and s=sum_j a_j. These alpha_j
are local times, not replacements for physical steps. Empty blocks
are permitted.

Construct r_j=(u_j,-y_j) backwards, with

    r_N=v(1)=(beta,-1).

If alpha_j=0 set r_(j-1)=r_j and keep its arc vector. Otherwise choose
q_(j-1) in[0,1) by

    (q_(j-1)+beta)/(p(q_(j-1))-beta)
       =max{ beta/(c_arc-beta),
               [y_j+(1-alpha_j)beta]
                    /[u_j-(1-alpha_j)beta] },       (4.1)

and put

    v_(j-1)=v(q_(j-1)),
    r_(j-1)=r_j+alpha_j v_(j-1).                     (4.2)

The denominator in(4.1) is positive because u_j>=beta and alpha_j>0.
The left side increases continuously from beta/(c_arc-beta) to
infinity, so this is a unique finite definition. No recurrence is
executed to prove any assertion in this document.

Here are the full checks for the backward prescription. The updates
give u_(j-1)>=u_j>=beta and y_(j-1)>=y_j>=1. The ratios in(4.1)
give q_(j-1)<=q_j: at an interior current point the current ratio is
(y_j+beta)/(u_j-beta), and subtracting alpha_j beta from its numerator
and adding alpha_j beta to its denominator can only lower it. At
q_j=1 there is no upper finite ratio to check. At q_j=0, the normal
cone inequality is

    y_j <= beta(u_j-c_arc)/(c_arc-beta),              (4.3)

which makes both entries of the new maximum no greater than the
horizontal endpoint ratio. Zero steps preserve everything.

For a selected interior q_(j-1)>0, equation(4.1) and(4.2) say that
r_(j-1) and v_(j-1) are collinear with (beta,beta). Since
y_(j-1)>=1>=q_(j-1), the former lies at or beyond the latter on the
outward ray. The circular normal thus proves(3.5). If q_(j-1)=0,
cross-multiplication in(4.1) instead gives

    y_(j-1) <= beta(u_(j-1)-c_arc)/(c_arc-beta).

Then r_(j-1)-(c_arc,0) is a nonnegative combination of the circular
endpoint normal and (0,1), again proving(3.5). The final point is on
K_ctr itself. This proves for every j

    Pi_(K_ctr)(r_j)=v_j,
    u_j>=beta, 1<=y_j<=y_0, 0<=q_j<=1.              (4.4)

In particular

    u_0=beta+sum_j alpha_j p(q_(j-1)),
    y_0=1+sum_j alpha_j q_(j-1),
    u_0>=beta y_0.                                  (4.5)

The last inequality follows termwise from p>=beta q. It is strict if
there is any positive ordinary step: its predecessor q is below1, so
p-beta q>0. A positive ordinary step also gives at least one actual
change of gradient before the checkpoint, because the last positive
step has predecessor q<1 and ends at q=1 after later zero steps.

Now take incoming threshold ell>=0 and amplitude D>0, and define

    M=u_0+y_0/beta,
    sigma=D/M,
    ell_out=sigma y_0,
    A=ell+ell_out/beta,
    Phi(X,Y)=E_(sigma K_ctr)(X-A,Y-ell_out).          (4.6)

This is one fixed finite smooth convex function. It is zero when
X<=ell and Y>=0, by(3.6), and its output derivative vanishes whenever
Y>=ell_out, for EVERY X. To prove the latter, replacing a candidate
gradient (p,-q) with (p,0) increases the objective by
q(Y-ell_out)+q^2/2 when q>0. Thus the unique maximizer has q=0.

Define the ordinary states

    (X_j,Y_j)=(A+sigma u_j, ell_out-sigma y_j).       (4.7)

The initial state is(ell+D,0), and (4.2)--(4.4) prove

    (X_j,Y_j)=(X_(j-1),Y_(j-1))
                   -(a_j/2) grad Phi(X_(j-1),Y_(j-1)).

Every ordinary and checkpoint query has X_j>ell and
0<=Y_j<=ell_out-sigma<ell_out. The last gradient is
sigma(beta,-1).

For ANY physical checkpoint b>2 put

    t=b/2-1,
    D_out=sigma t.                                  (4.8)

Its exact post-checkpoint state is

    X^+=A-beta D_out,
    Y^+=ell_out+D_out.                              (4.9)

Thus D_out>0, and the local function is exactly0 immediately after
its own checkpoint: its centered point is(-beta D_out,D_out), which
satisfies(3.6). A later checkpoint can lower Y and reactivate Phi.
That later effect, not a fictitious positive value at(4.9), is the
retained-value mechanism analyzed below.

## 5. One static global function and every physical update

Take any physical word h=(h_0,...,h_(n-1)) and selected zero-based
indices t_1<...<t_k, k>=1, with b_i=h_(t_i)>2. Let block i contain
all actual steps strictly between t_(i-1) and t_i, with t_0=-1.
Let block k+1 be the complete suffix after t_k. No unselected step
is removed, permuted or shortened. Write s_i for its physical mass.

For i=1,...,k, apply Section4 to the actual ordered block i, always
with the same beta. Denote the resulting dimensionless data by
u_i,y_i,M_i and the local full trajectory by r_(i,j),v_(i,j).
Starting with ell_1=0,D_1=1, set

    sigma_i=D_i/M_i,
    ell_(i+1)=sigma_i y_i,
    A_i=ell_i+ell_(i+1)/beta,
    D_(i+1)=sigma_i(b_i/2-1),
    Phi_i(X,Y)=E_(sigma_i K_ctr)(X-A_i,Y-ell_(i+1)). (5.1)

Finally set delta=D_(k+1)/(1+s_(k+1)) and define on R^(k+1)

    F(x)=1/2 sum_(i=1)^k Phi_i(x_i,x_(i+1))
                        +1/2 Hub_delta(x_(k+1)-ell_(k+1)).
    x_0=e_1.                                       (5.2)

All parameters in(5.2) are fixed from the predetermined word and its
selected indices before GD is run. Neither functions nor past values
are modified during the run.

Each block term is convex and1-smooth on its two coordinates. The
linear map stacking those coordinate pairs and the last scalar
coordinate has operator norm at most sqrt(2), because every coordinate
is counted at most twice. The gradient of the separable sum before
composition has Lipschitz constant1. Its coefficient1/2 therefore
makes grad F Lipschitz with constant at most1. The function is convex,
nonnegative, and F(0)=0, so the minimum is attained.

For completeness the entire physical update, including every old
component, has the following exact form. At any point x let

    (P_i(x),-Q_i(x))
        =Pi_(sigma_i K_ctr)(x_i-A_i,x_(i+1)-ell_(i+1)),
    P_(k+1)(x)=min(delta,max(x_(k+1)-ell_(k+1),0)),
    Q_0(x)=0.

Then for j=1,...,k+1,

    x_j^+=x_j - (h/2)[P_j(x)-Q_(j-1)(x)].           (5.3)

This is the ordinary Euclidean gradient of(5.2). In particular, old
negative output derivatives produce the nonnegative contributions in
the adjacent input coordinate; their magnitudes are the projections
of the SAME fixed components.

The active-coordinate induction is exact even when older values are
nonzero. At the start of block i its input is ell_i+D_i and all
coordinates after it are0. The local states(4.7) have x_i>=ell_i.
For i>1 this makes Q_(i-1)=0 by the preceding component's output
isolation property. Components before i-1 involve neither active
coordinate. The next component has input at most ell_(i+1), output0,
so its value and gradient vanish. All further components also vanish.
The terminal Huber is inactive if it is next. Equation(5.3) therefore
reduces on the two active coordinates to the physical half-step law
already proved. The checkpoint produces(4.9), establishing the next
incoming state. Earlier coordinates still obey(5.3); nothing in this
induction assumes their values or gradients are zero.

During the final block, x_(k+1) remains above ell_(k+1)+delta. Indeed
its exact excess after accumulated physical mass s' is

    D_(k+1)-s' delta/2 >= delta, 0<=s'<=s_(k+1).

Consequently Q_k=0, the terminal derivative is delta, and this formula
is its actual update. Earlier coordinates continue following(5.3).
The exact terminal value is

    2F(x_n)=sum_i Phi_i(x_(n,i),x_(n,i+1))
                          +D_(k+1)^2/[2(1+s_(k+1))]. (5.4)

The first summand in(5.4) is an equality term, not discarded mass.
The second term alone is a lower bound and is never used as an upper
bound for(5.2).

For general prescribed L>0, use f=L F. Then grad f=L grad F and
x^+=x-(h/L)grad f=x-h grad F. Thus the physical word is exactly h,
the function is L-smooth, and all normalized formulas below are
unchanged.

All directed smooth-convex interpolation rows hold because this is an
actual static convex1-smooth function. One can derive the inequality
directly: for V(z)=F(z)-<grad F(y),z>, its minimum is at y. The
1-smooth descent bound at x and displacement -grad V(x) gives

    F(x)-F(y)-<grad F(y),x-y>
        >= ||grad F(x)-grad F(y)||^2/2.

Relabeling x,y gives each ordered row, including minimizers and the
terminal point. Actual Euclidean Gram matrices are positive semidefinite.
