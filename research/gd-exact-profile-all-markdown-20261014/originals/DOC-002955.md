Fix h=(h_0,...,h_(n-1))>=0, s=sum h_i, x_(i+1)=x_i-h_i g_i, f_i=f(x_i), g_i=grad f(x_i). Define Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2 and V_h=2sum_(i<n)h_i(f_i-f_n)+sum_(i<n)h_i(1-h_i)||g_i||^2-s(s+1)||g_n||^2.

## 3. A negative-energy projection witness does not admit the simple core shear

For a compact convex K containing 0, put
F_K(x)=max_(g in K)[<x,g>-||g||^2/2]. Its gradient is P_K(x): the projection
normal inequalities imply <P_K(x)-P_K(z),x-z>>=||P_K(x)-P_K(z)||^2, and the
maximizing formula then proves differentiability and 1-smoothness. It is
convex with minimum F_K(0)=0. Along its GD trajectory define
y_i=x_i-g_i. Its values satisfy f_i=<g_i,x_i>-||g_i||^2/2, and hence

 Q_ij=<g_i-g_j,y_j>,
 V_h=||x_0||^2-||y_n+(1+s)g_n||^2.                  (6)

Let P_i=sum_(t<i)h_t, D=1+s. A tempting exact reset sets v=-y_n/D and

 X_i=x_i+P_i v, G_i=g_i-v,
 Y_i=X_i-G_i=y_i+(P_i+1)v,
 F_i=<G_i,X_i>-||G_i||^2/2.

The GD recursion holds, all incoming optimum residuals are zero, Y_n=0, and
the algebraic value of V_h is preserved exactly: X_0=x_0 and
Y_n+D G_n=y_n+D g_n. However, the finite residuals become

 Q'_ij=Q_ij+(P_j+1)<g_i-g_j,v>,                      (7)

which can be positive. Here is a fixed legal negative-energy input.

Use K=[-1,1], so F_K is the unit-threshold symmetric Huber function. Take
h=(5,10) and x_0=1. The full points, gradients and normals are

 x=(1,-4,6), g=(1,-1,1), y=(0,-3,5), f=(1/2,7/2,11/2).

Every item follows from the explicit Huber function and both GD equations
hold. Formula (6) gives V_h=1-(6+15)^2=-440. The reset has
D=16 and v=-5/16, and its first and second gradients are 21/16 and -11/16,
while Y_0=-5/16. Thus

 Q'_10=(G_1-G_0)Y_0=(-2)(-5/16)=5/8>0.              (8)

It is not a legal smooth-convex transcript despite keeping V_h=-440,
setting Y_2=0, and setting every incoming optimum residual to zero.
No conclusion about other witnesses for this schedule follows. This example
specifically prevents inferring simultaneous terminal saturation from this
energy-preserving shear.
