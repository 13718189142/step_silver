## 2. Smoothness in the full vertex space, including all overlaps

Let A be the oriented incidence map, (Ax)_(p->v)=x_p-x_v. Each
q_delta in (4) is nondecreasing and 1-Lipschitz. Thus F is everywhere
finite, convex, continuously differentiable, and

    grad F(x)=(1/kappa)*A^T q(Ax),                       (12)

where the edge coordinate of q uses that edge's fixed cap. It remains
to compute the FULL-space norm of A, not the norm of a convenient
subspace or a disjoint-edge approximation.

For any vertex vector x put

    U_l=(sum_(depth(v)=l) x_v^2)^(1/2), 0<=l<=D.

At depth l, each parent coordinate is repeated b times. The
Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives

    abs(sum_(edges p->v at depth l) x_p*x_v)
       <=sqrt(b)*U_(l-1)*U_l.

Consequently

    ||Ax||^2 <=sum_(l=1)^D (sqrt(b)*U_(l-1)+U_l)^2
              =||J U||^2,                              (13)

where J is the D by (D+1) matrix with sqrt(b) in position (l,l-1),
1 in position (l,l), and zero elsewhere, with rows1,...,D.
The symmetric matrix J J^T has diagonal b+1 and its two neighboring
diagonals equal sqrt(b). For r=1,...,D, the vector

    v_l^(r)=sin(l*r*pi/(D+1)), l=1,...,D,

is nonzero, has zero boundary values at l=0,D+1, and satisfies

    (J J^T)v^(r)
       =[b+1+2*sqrt(b)*cos(r*pi/(D+1))]*v^(r).           (14)

This follows by adding the sine values at l-1 and l+1. The D
eigenvalues are distinct, so the corresponding eigenvectors are
linearly independent and exhaust this D-dimensional symmetric matrix.
Their largest eigenvalue is kappa. Thus (13) proves ||A||^2<=kappa.

For equality, take the positive vector v_l=sin(l*pi/(D+1)) and let
U=J^T v. All its entries are positive. Equation (14) gives
J^T J U=kappa U. Set the actual vertex coordinates to

    x_v=(-1)^l*b^(-l/2)*U_l, depth(v)=l.

All adjacent coordinates have opposite signs, and within each level
their magnitudes are equal. Equality holds in every bound in (13).
This vector therefore has ||Ax||^2=kappa||x||^2, proving

    ||A||^2=kappa.                                    (15)

By (12) and the coordinatewise Lipschitz bound,

    ||grad F(x)-grad F(y)||
       <=||A||^2*||x-y||/kappa=||x-y||.

The constant1 is sharp. Choose a level-constant vector u with
u_0=0 and u_l=u_(l-1)-delta_l/2. Every edge difference is strictly
between0 and its cap. There is an open neighborhood of u where
F is quadratic with Hessian A^T A/kappa. By (15) this Hessian has
norm1; a sufficiently small displacement along its top eigenvector
stays in that neighborhood and attains gradient difference ratio1.
Every cap is positive and the tree is finite, so such a neighborhood
exists even though its radius can be very small. No smoothness
discount or coefficient-space norm is used in the physical updates.

## 3. Symmetry reduction with the correct Euclidean metric

Permuting the b children at any vertex leaves the entire static

For a general level-constant state let y_l denote the value at depth l.
The induced inner product and energy are

    <y,z>_tree=sum_(l=0)^D b^l*y_l*z_l,
    F(y)=(1/kappa)*sum_(l=1)^D b^l*
                         psi_(delta_l)(y_(l-1)-y_l).    (16)

Put r_l=q_(delta_l)(y_(l-1)-y_l) for1<=l<=D and set r_0=r_(D+1)=0.

    g_l=(b*r_(l+1)-r_l)/kappa, 0<=l<=D.               (17)

proves (17) directly. Equivalently, differentiating (16) and dividing
the l-th derivative by the metric weight b^l gives (17).

## 9. Why retuning caps within pure forward transport cannot repair it

This section describes a conditional obstruction to the SAME
transport template; it selects no second profile or run. Consider
any outward-tree sum of positive-weight one-sided Huber energies
on this same full b-ary tree, with weights and caps constant within
each depth, normalized to gradient Lipschitz constant at most1.
Start at e_r. Suppose its actual trajectory, on an arbitrary
schedule, places all nonzero coordinates entirely in generation k
at query k, for k=0,...,n. The depth symmetry makes the coordinates
equal within that generation. Every edge gradient has zero total
coordinate sum, so mass conservation forces its value to be b^(-k).
It follows necessarily that ||x_n||^2=b^(-n).

Because all weights and caps are positive, its full minimum set is
the SAME cone K and its initial radius is again R^2=1-1/N by (19).
Also0 is a minimizer with gradient0. Applying the line-segment
smoothness inequality at0 gives

    2*F(x_n)<=||x_n||^2=b^(-n),
    2*F(x_n)/R^2<=b^(-n)/(1-1/N)<2*b^(-n).          (34)

This conditional bound already prevents this generation-by-
generation pure transport template from providing benchmark-scale
error on (1), since multiplying it by (31) tends to0. Its hypotheses
are realized by the frozen instance, with the sharper exact (8).

The paid loss is conservation and dilution of a positive pulse.
There are b^n active terminal vertices, but their individual
amplitudes are b^(-n); their squared contributions add to b^(-n).
The true initial radius stays close to1 and cannot cancel this loss.
This is different from the rank-body profile's cost of initially
present modes that later disappear. Here the initial mass is only
at the root, and future generations are generated by actual
overlapping gradients, whose adjacent cross terms are negative.

The obstruction does NOT cover retained mass on old generations,
several simultaneous pulses, signed states, different initial
profiles, asymmetric within-level weights, inward or two-sided
Huber energies, branch recombination, or other trees. In particular
it supplies no assertion that all graph, probability or branching
methods fail. Those possibilities are not authorized new profiles
or a continuation allocation for this round.
