# A concrete final-step descent from a penultimate star-outgoing multiplier

Status: new self-contained candidate, unverified. No new verification allocation is assumed. This addresses one real component of the general-q descent problem; it does not prove that every q>tau tuple has such a component.

## 1. Input and exact schedule change

Normalize L=1 and f_*=0. Let h be any nonnegative schedule of length n>=1, with final step b=h_{n-1}. Use Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2<=0, and the standard full upper certificate

    q||x_0-x_*||^2-2f_n+sum lambda_ij Q_ij = M >=0,

where M is a PSD quadratic form after the exact GD substitutions, lambda_ij>=0, and the function-value coefficients cancel. The index * has x_*=g_*=0 and f_*=0. The result applies to every full feasible certificate, regardless of stationarity or Huber tightness.

Suppose

    nu=lambda_{*,n-1}>0.

For any 0<epsilon<=nu/(nu+2), put c=1-epsilon and replace ONLY the last step by

    b_new=c b+epsilon.

Then the new predetermined GD schedule has the rigorous uniform bound

    W_n(h_0,...,h_{n-2},b_new) <= c q < q.                    (D)

At the largest allowed epsilon this reads

    b_new=(2b+nu)/(nu+2),       q_new=2q/(nu+2).

The strict inequality is meaningful whenever q>0; every valid finite-horizon objective upper bound has q>0 by the canonical Huber witness.

## 2. Direct proof for all smooth convex functions

Fix any legal function and initial point, using the old and new schedules with the same prefix. Write

    x=x_{n-1}, g=grad f(x),
    z=x-b g                    (old endpoint),
    y=x-g                      (unit-step point),
    z_new=c z+epsilon y         (new endpoint),
    delta=f(x)-||g||^2/2 >=0.

Because Q_{*,n-1}=-delta, isolating this term in the original full PSD certificate yields

    2f(z)+nu delta <= q||x_0-x_*||^2.                        (A)

The remaining terms have the correct signs: the PSD quadratic form is nonnegative and every other -lambda_ij Q_ij is nonnegative.

The unit-step descent inequality gives f(y)<=delta. Convexity then yields

    2f(z_new) <=2c f(z)+2epsilon f(y)
                <=2c f(z)+2epsilon delta
                <=c[2f(z)+nu delta]
                <=c q||x_0-x_*||^2,

where 2epsilon<=c nu was the only restriction on epsilon. This proves (D) without selecting any hard function, differentiating W, or assuming a stationary worst-case primal.

The unit-step inequality can itself be derived from a single smooth-convex interpolation constraint:

    Q_{y,x}=f(y)-f(x)+<grad f(y),g>
              +||grad f(y)-g||^2/2
           =f(y)-delta+||grad f(y)||^2/2 <=0.

Thus the proof uses precisely the full smooth-convex class and is dimension-independent.

## 3. Explicit nonnegative-multiplier, PSD certificate

The construction is stronger than a sign argument: it gives an explicit enlarged interpolation certificate for the changed schedule. The two auxiliary points are the old endpoint z and the unit-step point y. The new algorithm still uses n steps and no auxiliary oracle calls; auxiliary points appear only in the proof and are defined from the same penultimate point and gradient.

Let g_z,g_y,g_new denote their actual gradients, and put

    d=c nu-2epsilon >=0.

Keep c times every old multiplier except lambda_{*,n-1}; assign d to Q_{*,n-1}. Add the new nonnegative multipliers

    2c       on Q_{z_new,z},
    2epsilon on Q_{z_new,y},
    2epsilon on Q_{y,x}.

All function-value terms cancel against the new objective -2f(z_new). After substituting z_new=c z+epsilon y and y=x-g, the full polynomial identity is

    c q||x_0-x_*||^2-2f(z_new)
      +c sum_{(i,j)!=(star,n-1)}lambda_ij Q_ij
      +d Q_{*,n-1}
      +2c Q_{z_new,z}+2epsilon Q_{z_new,y}
      +2epsilon Q_{y,x}

    = c M
      +c||g_new-g_z||^2
      +epsilon||g_new-g_y||^2
      +epsilon||g_y||^2.                                    (PSD)

The right side is an explicit PSD quadratic form, including the original full matrix M, not only its row sums or energy trace. The left side uses nonnegative multipliers throughout. All interpolation data concern the same globally legal function; no incompatible local transcript is glued in.

To check the identity, combine the first two new inequalities with weights c,epsilon. Their gradient-position terms cancel because z_new=c z+epsilon y. Then combine Q_{y,x}=f(y)-delta+||g_y||^2/2. The coefficient d is exactly what balances the old nu delta term.

The displayed certificate uses auxiliary analysis points. It is not asserted to be an explicit parameter-by-parameter map into the minimal n+2-point dual. Its full PSD identity proves the actual uniform upper bound directly, so it is already sufficient for a global-minimizer contradiction. When the new steps are positive, exact finite interpolation duality also gives a minimal-format certificate at its true worst-case value, but that existential statement is not needed for (D).

## 4. Consequence at global minimizers

Suppose h is a globally minimizing length-n schedule and its full certificate is tight: q=W_n(h)=W_n^*. If lambda_{*,n-1}>0, formula (D) constructs a length-n nonnegative predetermined schedule with strictly smaller true worst-case value. This contradicts global minimality.

Consequently EVERY tight full certificate at a global minimizer must satisfy

    lambda_{*,n-1}=0.                                      (V)

The contradiction uses an actual globally valid changed algorithm, so it is not merely a stationarity-implies-tightness shortcut. It does not need q=tau or a bound on the total step mass. It also handles a final step already equal to one: then the schedule does not change and (D) would improve the allegedly tight q itself.

This conclusion leaves lambda_{*,i} for i<n-1, lambda_{*,n}, and the PSD Schur-complement defect completely open. In particular it does not establish Huber tightness of a global minimizer.

## 5. Exact checks and the remaining q>tau case

For the one-step certificate at h_0=1+a, a>1, take

    q=a^2, lambda_{0,*}=2a^2,
    lambda_{*,0}=2a^2-2, lambda_{1,0}=2,

all other multipliers zero. Its exact slack is

    a^2||x_0-g_0||^2+||a g_0+g_1||^2,

and f(x)=x^2/2 attains q. The new formula gives c=1/a^2, h_new=1+1/a and the certified bound q_new=1. This is strictly smaller than a^2. The formula preserves nonnegativity and PSD through the explicit identity above; smaller epsilon gives a continuous descent.

The hypothesis nu>0 cannot be silently replaced by q>tau. For 1/2<a<1, the tight one-step certificate at h_0=1+a has q=a^2>tau=1/(2a+3), but no star-outgoing multipliers:

    lambda_{0,*}=2a, lambda_{1,*}=2(1-a), lambda_{1,0}=2a.

Its gradient block is [[2a,1],[1,1]], its star-directed vector is mu=(2a,2(1-a)), and its full Schur complement is

    Z=(2a-1)[[1,1/a],[1/a,1/a^2]] >=0.

The quadratic matches q. Thus the remaining non-Huber defect can be carried entirely by nonzero PSD slack even when all star-outgoing weights vanish. This example is not globally optimal; it is a hostile test of extending (V) into an unsupported general tightness claim.

## 6. Related terminal extension

If a certificate instead has lambda_{*,n}=nu>0, the same argument applied to an appended step gives, for 0<=epsilon<=nu/(nu+2),

    W_{n+1}(h,epsilon) <= (1-epsilon)q.

Here use the convex combination of x_n and x_n-grad f(x_n), and isolate the terminal residual delta_n=f_n-||g_n||^2/2 in the old certificate. This is a length-(n+1) improvement and must not be counted as a length-n contradiction. It may be useful for upper-bound composition, but no asymptotic comparison with the exact Zhang--Jiang profile follows automatically.
