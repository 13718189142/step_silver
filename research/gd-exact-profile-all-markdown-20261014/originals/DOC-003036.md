    lambda=1+sqrt(2), epsilon=1/m,

Define the single global function and its state coordinates by

    K_4={(r,s) in R^2:s>=r^4/4},

Section 2 proves global legality, the unique actual recurrence, and

    min F_4=0, argmin F_4={(0,y):y<=0},

## 2. The global function, ordered pairs, distance, and physical recursion

K_4 is nonempty, closed, and convex. Completing the square in the defining
maximum shows that its unique maximizing g is the Euclidean projection
Pi_(K_4)(X). The normal inequalities at projections of X and Y give

    ||Pi_(K_4)(X)-Pi_(K_4)(Y)||^2
       <=<Pi_(K_4)(X)-Pi_(K_4)(Y),X-Y>.

Thus the projection is 1-Lipschitz. Comparing the defining maxima gives

    0<=F_4(Y)-F_4(X)-<Pi_(K_4)(X),Y-X>
      <=<Pi_(K_4)(Y)-Pi_(K_4)(X),Y-X><=||Y-X||^2.

It follows that F_4 is differentiable with gradient Pi_(K_4), hence has
1-Lipschitz gradient. Convexity follows from its supremum of affine
functions representation.

For phi(r)=r^4/4, one has

    X(r,v)-G(r)=eta*(phi'(r),-1).

For any (z,s) in K_4 its inner product with (z,s)-G(r) is at most

    eta*[phi'(r)(z-r)-phi(z)+phi(r)]<=0.

The last inequality is the tangent inequality for the convex function
phi. This proves grad F_4(X(r,v))=G(r). Substitution into the maximum
proves the value in (2). In particular, if f_i=F_4(X_i), every ordered
pair of states in this construction has the exact residual

    Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<G_i,X_j-X_i>+||G_i-G_j||^2/2
        =<X_j-G_j,G_i-G_j>
        =eta_j*[r_j^3(r_i-r_j)-r_i^4/4+r_j^4/4]<=0.      (9)

There is no restriction on temporal separation in (9).

If x=0 and y<=0, the maximum is zero: zero is feasible, and every feasible
g=(r,s) has s>=0, so ys-||g||^2/2<=0. If x!=0, test g=(r,r^4/4)
with sufficiently small r of the sign of x; the positive term xr
dominates the terms of order 2 or higher. If x=0 and y>0, a sufficiently
small g=(0,s), s>0, gives positive value. This proves the exact minimizer
set in (2). Its closest point to (x,-v), v>=0, is (0,-v), proving the
true distance formula without retaining a spurious initial vertical norm.

The actual GD step of size h>=0 is exactly

    v_plus=v+h r^4/4,
    x_plus=x-hr,
    x_plus=T_(v_plus)(r_plus),
    T_v(r)=r+v r^3+r^7/4.                               (10)

T_v is odd and strictly increasing onto R, since

    T_v'(r)=1+3vr^2+7r^6/4>=1.

It therefore determines the unique actual r_plus. In particular
sign(r)=sign(x). If r reaches zero, x=0 and G(r)=0, so the trajectory is
absorbed with zero subsequent objective and clock cost.

On any interval where all states are nonzero and have the same sign,
R decreases and v increases. Equation (10) then shows that |r| decreases.
Reflection allows every such interval to be treated on the positive ray.

For later use, smoothness and the minimizer (0,-v) give 2F_4(X)<=R^2.
This can also be checked from the explicit state formula: with z=r^6/4,

    2F_4(X)/r^2=1+(3/2)u+z/4,
    R^2/r^2=(1+u)^2,

and their difference is u/2+u^2-z/4>=u/4+u^2>=0 because 0<=z<=u.

## 3. A no-turn block pays for the loss of normal state

Consider any actual occurrence of H_l, l>=1, along which every state is
nonzero and has the same sign. No entry v is reset. Let r>0 and u>0 be
the terminal gradient magnitude and effective normal state, and let
rho>0 and u_mid be their counterparts immediately BEFORE the middle
step 1+d_(l-1). Since that step does not turn or annihilate,

    u_mid>d_(l-1).                                      (11)

Monotonicity on this interval gives rho>=r and v_mid<=v_end. Consequently

    u_mid=v_mid rho^2+rho^6/4
          <=(rho/r)^6 (v_end r^2+r^6/4)
          =(rho/r)^6 u.                                (12)

Let Apre_l=S_(l-1)+1+d_(l-1)=M_(l-1)+d_(l-1) be the sum of steps
through the middle step. Every gradient used by those steps has
magnitude at least rho; every later gradient has magnitude at least r.
Telescoping the actual horizontal motion, with R_entry its input radius,
therefore gives

    R_entry/r
      >=M_l+u+Apre_l*((d_(l-1)/u)^(1/6)-1)_+.            (13)

Here (z)_+=max(z,0). If u>=d_(l-1)/2, the explicit u in (13) is at
least d_(l-1)/2. If u<d_(l-1)/2, then

    (d_(l-1)/u)^(1/6)>2^(1/6)>11/10,

because (11/10)^6=1771561/1000000<2. Since Apre_l>=d_(l-1), the
last term in (13) exceeds d_(l-1)/10. Thus in both cases

    R_entry >=Aend_l |r_end|,
    Aend_l:=M_l+d_(l-1)/10.                             (14)

For the empty word define Aend_0=1; the same gradient bound is the state
identity R=|r|(1+u)>=|r|. If an endpoint is absorbed, the gradient bound
with its zero terminal gradient holds as well.

This is the key replacement for a frozen-normal argument. A decrease in
terminal u is permitted. Equation (12) charges that decrease to earlier
gradient magnitudes, and equation (13) charges those magnitudes to the
TRUE horizontal entry distance. No inequality u_end>=u_mid is used.

It follows that 0<A_mass<1. In particular,

    M_l/d_l>=1/(c-2).                                  (16)

For m>=512, sqrt(2)<99/70 gives

    c<(169/70)(513/512)<121/50.

The function z-1/(z-2)-1/(10z) is strictly increasing for z>2. At

    1/(c-2)+1/(10c)>c.

Combining this with (14) and (16) proves

    Aend_l>=c d_l=a c^(l+1)        for l>=1.             (17)

For l=0 the same conclusion holds because

    Aend_0=1>ac=1-1/m.                                  (18)

Equations (17)--(18) are uniform over the block position, initial state,

## 5. A binary interval lemma and chronological turn accounting

We first prove the combinatorial statement required to combine real
one-sign intervals. If integers 0<=s<t satisfy

    q=b(t-1)-b(s)>=1,

then [s,t] contains an aligned closed dyadic interval [A,A+2^q], where
A is a multiple of 2^q.

Proof. Put B=2^q, write s=kB+r with 0<=r<B, and suppose no such
interval is contained. If r=0, the first possible interval starts at s,
so t<s+B. Then t-1 has high binary part k and a low part at most B-2;
hence b(t-1)-b(s)<=q-1, a contradiction.

If r>0, the first possible start is (k+1)B, so t<(k+2)B. If t-1
has high part k, its low part has at most q ones, whereas b(r)>=1,
again giving difference at most q-1. If t-1 has high part k+1, its
low part is at most B-2 and has at most q-1 ones. Adding 1 to k
increases its bit count by at most 1. Thus the difference is at most
1+(q-1)-b(r)<=q-1. This contradiction proves the lemma.             (19)

An aligned interval [A,A+2^q] corresponds to the actual subword H_q
at physical step indices A+1,...,A+2^q-1: for 0<j<2^q,
nu_2(A+j)=nu_2(j). This uses the same physical a,c.

Now let s<t be consecutive actual turn times, allowing s=0 for the
initial boundary before the first turn. The steps s+1,...,t-1 form
one same-sign interval. Set

    q=max{0,b(t-1)-b(s)}, ell=nu_2(t).

If q>=1, (19) finds an actual no-turn H_q inside this interval. Its
entry horizontal radius is at most R_s, and its terminal gradient
magnitude is at least |r_(t-1)|, by monotonicity on the entire interval.
Equations (14) and (17) therefore give

    R_s>=Aend_q |r_(t-1)|>=a c^(q+1)|r_(t-1)|.           (20)

For q=0, use R_s>=|r_(t-1)| and (18) to get the same result. At the
actual turn t, equation (10) gives

    R_t=(d_ell-u_(t-1))|r_(t-1)|
        <=a c^ell |r_(t-1)|
        <=c^(ell-q-1)R_s.                              (21)

The elementary binary identity

    b(t-1)=b(t)-1+nu_2(t)

and q>=b(t-1)-b(s) imply

    R_t<=c^(b(s)-b(t))R_s.                              (22)

Starting at s=0 and telescoping (22) over the ACTUAL turns proves the
second part of (3). Between turns the radius decreases, proving the
first part of (3). This argument allows arbitrary repeated rises and
falls in turn level. It neither optimizes right copies independently
nor assumes that u is monotone.

## 6. Global payment of the retained moving-normal cost

For context, on a positive one-sign interval define the local clock

    C_4(r,v)=log r+(3/2)vr^2+(7/24)r^6,
    Psi(x,v)=C_4(T_v^(-1)(x),v), x>0.

Differentiating T_v(r)=x gives

    partial_x Psi=1/r, partial_v Psi=r^2/2.

For one positive noncrossing step, first move horizontally at the old v
and then increase v at the new horizontal coordinate. Monotonicity of
T_v^(-1) bounds the two resulting integrals and yields

    C_4(r_plus,v_plus)-C_4(r,v)
       <=-h+(r^2/2)(v_plus-v).                          (23)

Reflection covers a negative one-sign interval. This derivation retains
the positive cost, and does not extend the logarithm across annihilation
or sign changes.

The global radius result (3), together with v_t>=v_0 and monotonicity of
T_v on the positive ray, implies

    |r_t|<=|r_0|  for every t.                           (24)

The first bound in (5) now follows by summing the defining weighted
vertical increments of J_all.

To obtain the second bound, set TV_x=sum_(t=1)^n h_t|r_(t-1)|, the
total absolute horizontal displacement. At a nonturn step (including
one that reaches zero), its contribution is R_(t-1)-R_t. At a turn it
is R_(t-1)+R_t. Consequently the exact telescoping identity is

    TV_x=R_0-R_n+2 sum_(t a turn) R_t.                   (25)

Use (3), and expand the product over the m independent binary digits:

    sum_(t=0)^(2^m-1)c^(-b(t))=(1+1/c)^m.

It follows that

    TV_x<=R_0[2(1+1/c)^m-1].                            (26)

Since J_all=(1/8)sum h_t|r_(t-1)|*|r_(t-1)|^5, (24)--(26) prove
the second bound in (5). Finally R_0>=|r_0|^7/4 by the state formula,
so R_0|r_0|^5<=4^(5/7)R_0^(12/7), proving (6).

Thus the sum of all one-sign J costs is bounded by (5)--(6), since it
is no larger than J_all. The bounds retain the initial radius and,
in the first version, the actual terminal vertical state. They remain
valid for trajectories that annihilate; their subsequent contributions
are zero. No lower bound on nonzero |r_t| is needed.

## 7. Terminal payoff, with every entry-radius factor retained

Consider any nonzero same-sign interval with total step mass T>=1/6.
Write r>0 for its terminal gradient magnitude and u=vr^2+r^6/4 for
its terminal normal state. The actual recursion, without resetting v,
gives

    R_entry>=r(T+1+u),
    v_end>=T r^4/4.

Put z=r^6/4. Then z<=u/(T+1), and (2) implies

    2F_4(X_end)/R_entry^2
       <=[1+k_T u]/(T+1+u)^2,
    k_T=3/2+1/[4(T+1)].

The derivative of the last rational function has the sign of
k_T(T+1)-2-k_T u. Since T>=1/6, its maximum on u>=0 is attained at
u=T+1-2/k_T and equals

    E_4(T)=(6T+7)^2/[48(T+1)^2(2T+1)]<=3/(8T).          (27)

For the last comparison the numerator of the difference, over denominator
48T(T+1)^2(2T+1), is 6T^2+23T+18>0. An absorbing terminal state has
zero payoff and satisfies the desired bound separately.

If the full run is nonabsorbed, let s be its last actual turn, or let
s=0 if it has no turns. Set b=b(s) and q=m-b. Apply (19) with
t=2^m. The final same-sign interval contains an actual H_q. If q>=1,
its full mass T is at least S_q>=S_1>1, so (3) and (27) give

    P_4<=c^(-2b)*3/(8S_q).                              (28)

The factor c^(-2b) in (28) is the ACTUAL entry-radius ratio bounded in
terms of the original R_0. A later block has not been renormalized as a
fresh problem.

For j>=1, (15) gives

    S_(j+1)-c S_j=c-1-(c-2)(1-A_mass)2^j<=c-1.

Since S_j>1 and c>1, this implies

    S_(j+1)<(2c-1)S_j<c^2 S_j.

Iterating yields S_m<=c^(2b)S_q, so (28) is at most 3/(8S_m).

If q=0, then b(s)=m and s=n, so the last step turns. Use the terminal
state bound 2F_4<=R_n^2 and (3) to obtain P_4<=c^(-2m). Since (15)
gives S_m<c^m and c^m>8/3 for m>=512, this is smaller than 3/(8S_m).
This proves (4) for every initial state, including all turn patterns.

## 8. The exact benchmark and the uniform comparison

Accepted fact b98bb807d514e80d defines the exact primitive and dominant
benchmark recursions. In their mass coordinates C_n=S_circle(n)+1 and
B_n=2S_bullet(n)+1 they are

    C_0=B_0=1,
    C_n=max_(k+l=n-1) Omega(C_k,C_l),
    B_n=max_(k+l=n-1) G(C_k,B_l),
    Omega(x,y)=(x+y+sqrt((x+y)^2+4xy))/2,
    G(x,y)=2x+y/2+sqrt(y^2+8xy)/2.

We prove directly from these recursions that

    B_(2^m-1)<=lambda^(m+1).                             (29)

Set alpha=2lambda-1=lambda(4-lambda) and
Llin=(4x+lambda^2 y)/alpha. Both coefficients in Llin exceed 1, so
Llin>=x+y. Direct expansion, using lambda^2=2lambda+1, gives

    alpha^2[Llin^2-(x+y)Llin-xy]
      =(2x/lambda-sqrt(2)lambda y)^2>=0.

Thus Llin>=Omega(x,y), the positive root of z^2-(x+y)z-xy. At
z=lambda Omega(x,y), the polynomial with larger root G(x,lambda y) is

    z^2-(4x+lambda y)z+4x^2
      =x[4x+lambda^2 y-alpha Omega(x,y)]>=0.

Also z>=lambda(x+y)>=(4x+lambda y)/2, so z is to the right of that
polynomial's vertex. Hence G(x,lambda y)<=lambda Omega(x,y).
Induction in the exact recursions proves B_n<=lambda C_n.

Put gamma=log_2(lambda)>1, D(t)=(1+t)^gamma-1, and
ell(z)=log D(exp z). For t=exp z, differentiation gives

    ell''(z)=gamma*t*(1+t)^(gamma-2)
      *[(1+t)^gamma-1-gamma*t]/D(t)^2>0.

The last bracket has value zero at t=0 and positive derivative for t>0.
Convexity therefore implies D(t)D(1/t)>=D(1)^2=2. For x,y>0 set
Z=(x^(1/gamma)+y^(1/gamma))^gamma. Taking t=(y/x)^(1/gamma) shows
(Z-x)(Z-y)>=2xy. Thus Z^2-(x+y)Z-xy>=0; also Z>=x+y, by convexity
of the positive power. Consequently Omega(x,y)<=Z. The zero cases
follow by direct substitution. Induction yields C_n<=(n+1)^gamma,
which, with B_n<=lambda C_n, proves (29).

For a finite comparison, c<121/50 and m>=512 imply

    A_mass=(1-1/m)/[c(c-2)]
      >(511/512)/(2541/2500)>49/50.

Since c>2 and m>=512, c^(-m)<1/50. Equation (15) therefore gives

    S_m/c^m>24/25.

The first three binomial terms show

    (1+1/m)^m>=2+(m-1)/(2m)>=12/5,

where the final inequality already holds for m>=5. Combining (4),
(29), lambda<5/2, and (lambda/c)^m<=5/12 gives

    B_n P_4
       <(3/8)*(5/2)*(5/12)*(25/24)
       =625/1536<1/2,

proving (7).

As m tends to infinity, A_mass tends to 1, c tends to lambda, and

    S_m/c^m=A_mass+(1-A_mass)(2/c)^m-c^(-m) ->1,
    (lambda/c)^m=(1+1/m)^(-m) ->1/e.

Every bound above is uniform over r_0!=0,v_0>=0, so (4) and (29)
give (8). Finally e>1+1+1/2=5/2>lambda, proving 3lambda/(8e)<3/8.
No limiting formula for the exact sequence B_n has been assumed.

## 9. Scope, literature, and symbolic audit

The proof addresses the whole unscaled quartic epigraph model stated in
Section 1, at all initial horizontal radii and nonnegative vertical
states, including horizon-dependent choices. Its global charge and
payoff combine arbitrary actual turn histories. Lower-level reactivation
is allowed. This is a proper-family exclusion, not a conclusion about
all functions, other powers, or the original all-h target.

The projection background is consistent with Propositions 3.2--3.3 of
Chierchia, Pustelnik, Pesquet, and Pesquet-Popescu, "Epigraphical splitting
for solving constrained convex formulations of inverse problems with
proximal tools", arXiv:1210.5844v3. Their Hilbert product is Euclidean;
their scalar generator has full domain and is proper closed convex;
beta=4, tau=1/4, zeta=-v yield T_v(r)=x. The exact locally downloaded
paper's definitions and projection proof were read. No external result
from it is used as a proof premise. The dedicated theorem searches for
the moving-state budget and the binary interval lemma returned no usable
result; both have been proved directly here.

Symbolic boundary checks:

* Consecutive actual turns have an empty intervening H_0. The necessary
  charge is exactly Aend_0>=ac; no unqueried lower-level step is invented.
* The aligned case [s,t]=[A,A+2^q] uses the full actual H_q, with no
  missing first or last step and no change of enclosing parameters.
* Negative binary-count differences are handled by q=max(0,difference)
  in (20)--(22), not by a fictitious word of negative length.
* A turn at the final step has q=0 and uses the smoothness bound, not
  formula (27) at zero mass.
* Exact zero is absorbing. No logarithm is evaluated there, and the
  main payoff proof uses no logarithm at all.
* Very small nonzero gradients cause no reciprocal estimate with an
  uncontrolled constant; (13) charges small terminal u explicitly.
* The proof does not freeze v, assume monotonicity of u, or discard the
  positive moving-normal cost. The complete all-pair function and true
  denominator were established before the block argument.

The separate possible concave-clock integral explored in rough local
notes is unnecessary here and is not a premise. No verification success
is asserted until an authorized fact_submit actually accepts this joint
statement and proof.

### 10.1. The general power function and exact inherited state

For real p>2 define

    phi_p(r)=|r|^p/p,
    K_p={(r,s):s>=phi_p(r)},
    F_p(X)=max_(g in K_p)(<X,g>-||g||^2/2),
    eta=v+phi_p(r), u=eta |r|^(p-2),
    X_p(r,v)=(r(1+u),-v), G_p(r)=(r,phi_p(r)), v>=0.

The epigraph K_p is nonempty, closed, and convex. Completing the square
makes the maximizing gradient its unique Euclidean projection.
The two projection normal inequalities give

    ||Pi_(K_p)(X)-Pi_(K_p)(Y)||^2
       <=<Pi_(K_p)(X)-Pi_(K_p)(Y),X-Y>.

Comparing the defining maxima bounds the first-order remainder between
zero and ||X-Y||^2, as in the explicit derivation in Section 2. Hence
F_p is everywhere finite, convex, differentiable, and has 1-Lipschitz
gradient equal to the projection.

Since phi_p'(r)=r|r|^(p-2), one has

    X_p-G_p=eta*(phi_p'(r),-1).

Its inner product with any feasible displacement (z,s)-G_p is at most
eta*[phi_p'(r)(z-r)-phi_p(z)+phi_p(r)]<=0. This proves the exact
gradient formula at every state, and substitution gives

    2F_p(X_p(r,v))
      =r^2+(2-2/p)v|r|^p+(2p-1)|r|^(2p)/p^2.            (34)

For arbitrary two such states, their ordered interpolation residual is

    Q_ij=eta_j*[phi_p'(r_j)(r_i-r_j)-phi_p(r_i)
                                  +phi_p(r_j)]<=0.       (35)

This follows by expanding the residual to
<X_j-G_j,G_i-G_j>, so includes every ordered pair, not merely neighbors.

Zero is feasible in the maximum. At (0,y), y<=0, every feasible
inner product minus the squared norm is nonpositive. At x!=0, choose a
sufficiently small r of the sign of x; the positive linear term xr
dominates terms of order 2,p,2p. At x=0,y>0 a small positive vertical
gradient gives positive value. Therefore

    min F_p=0, argmin F_p={(0,y):y<=0},
    R_0=dist(X_p(r_0,v_0),argmin F_p)=|r_0|(1+u_0).       (36)

The actual transition is

    v_plus=v+h|r|^p/p, x_plus=x-hr,
    x_plus=T_(p,v_plus)(r_plus),
    T_(p,v)(r)=r+v r|r|^(p-2)+r|r|^(2p-2)/p.            (37)

The last map is odd, continuous, strictly increasing onto R; off zero
its derivative is
1+(p-1)v|r|^(p-2)+(2p-1)|r|^(2p-2)/p>=1, and its derivative at
zero is 1. Thus the next state is uniquely that of the same F_p.

On a same-sign nonzero interval, R and |r| decrease while v increases.
At zero the state is absorbing. Put z=|r|^(2p-2)/p. Then

    2F_p(X)/r^2=1+(2-2/p)u+z/p,
    0<=z<=u.

It follows that 2F_p(X)<=R^2, since the difference after division by
r^2 is (2/p)u+u^2-z/p>=u/p+u^2>=0.

### 10.2. General-power endpoint capacity with every p factor explicit

Set

    delta_p=2^(1/(2p-2))-1.

Since p>2, one has 0<delta_p<sqrt(2)-1<1/2. Consider a nonzero
one-sign actual H_l, l>=1. Use r,u for its terminal magnitude and normal
state, and rho,u_mid immediately before its middle step. Equation (37)
and preservation of sign imply u_mid>d_(l-1). Monotonicity gives

    u_mid
       <=(rho/r)^(2p-2) u.

Indeed the v term has exponent p-2 and v_mid<=v_end, while rho/r>=1;
the pure-power term has exponent 2p-2. Summing horizontal displacements
through the middle step and the rest of the same actual block yields

    R_entry/r>=M_l+u
       +Apre_l*((d_(l-1)/u)^(1/(2p-2))-1)_+,
    Apre_l=M_(l-1)+d_(l-1)>=d_(l-1).                    (38)

If u>=d_(l-1)/2, its contribution alone is at least
delta_p d_(l-1), because delta_p<1/2. Otherwise the last factor in
(38) is greater than delta_p. Thus

    R_entry>=Aend_(p,l)|r_end|,
    Aend_(p,l)=M_l+delta_p d_(l-1) for l>=1,
    Aend_(p,0)=1.                                      (39)

Here the subscript p distinguishes this coefficient from the quartic
rational lower bound in Section 3.

We now prove the capacity needed for all actual turn gaps:

    Aend_(p,l)>=c d_l for every l>=0.                   (40)

For l=0, use ac=1-1/m<1. For l>=1, equation (15), derived from the
word recurrence and independent of p, gives

    Aend_(p,l)/d_l>=1/(c-2)+delta_p/c.

Thus it suffices to prove

    delta_p>= [c/(c-2)]*[c(c-2)-1].                     (41)

The constants in (41) are controlled explicitly. Since
lambda>12/5 and z/(z-2) decreases for z>2,

    c/(c-2)<6.

Also

    c(c-2)-1
      =2lambda(lambda-1)/m+lambda^2/m^2
      <(15/2)/m+(25/4)/m^2<8/m,

where the last inequality uses m>=512. The right side of (41) is
therefore less than 48/m. On the other hand log(2)>1/2, by integrating
1/t>1/2 on (1,2), and exp(z)-1>z for z>0. Hence

    delta_p>1/[4(p-1)]>1/(4p)>=64/m

under (30). This proves (41) and (40). No p-dependent loss is hidden
inside an asymptotic term.

### 10.3. Combining all sign changes and paying the full cost

The binary interval lemma (19) was proved for integers alone. For
consecutive actual turns s<t, with s=0 allowed initially, set

    q=max{0,b(t-1)-b(s)}, ell=nu_2(t).

If q>=1, that lemma locates a real, aligned, nonturn H_q inside
steps s+1,...,t-1. Its input radius is at most R_s and its endpoint
gradient magnitude is at least |r_(t-1)|. Formula (40) gives

    R_s>=a c^(q+1)|r_(t-1)|.

If q=0, this follows from R_s>=|r_(t-1)| and ac<1. At turn t,
the exact recurrence (37) gives

    R_t=(d_ell-u_(t-1))|r_(t-1)|
       <=c^(ell-q-1)R_s
       <=c^(b(s)-b(t))R_s.

The last inequality uses b(t-1)=b(t)-1+ell. Induction over the ACTUAL
turns, followed by monotonicity between them, proves

    R_t<=c^(-b(t))R_0 at every turn,
    R_t<=R_0, |r_t|<=|r_0| at every state.               (42)

The last assertion follows from v_t>=v_0 and monotonicity of the inverse
in (37). Nothing in this proof restricts the chronology of turn levels.

For the original power clock on a single positive interval,

    C_p(r,v)=log r+(p-1)v r^(p-2)/(p-2)
                         +(2p-1)r^(2p-2)/[p(2p-2)],

implicit differentiation at fixed horizontal x gives the positive
vertical derivative r^(p-2)/(p-2). Splitting a noncrossing step into
horizontal motion and then its actual vertical increment yields

    C_p(r_plus,v_plus)-C_p(r,v)
       <=-h+|r|^(p-2)(v_plus-v)/(p-2).

Accordingly define the full, nonnegative retained cost by

    J_(p,all)
      =sum |r_(t-1)|^(p-2)(v_t-v_(t-1))/(p-2)
      =sum h_t |r_(t-1)|^(2p-2)/[p(p-2)].

The exact total-variation identity is still
TV_x=R_0-R_n+2 sum_(t a turn) R_t. Expanding over all m binary digits
and using (42) gives TV_x<=R_0[2(1+1/c)^m-1]. Therefore

    J_(p,all)<=min{
      |r_0|^(p-2)(v_n-v_0)/(p-2),
      R_0|r_0|^(2p-3)[2(1+1/c)^m-1]/[p(p-2)]
    }.                                                  (43)

Since R_0>=|r_0|^(2p-1)/p, one also has the radius-only charge

    J_(p,all)
       <=p^(-2/(2p-1)) R_0^((4p-4)/(2p-1))
                         [2(1+1/c)^m-1]/(p-2).           (44)

The factor 1/(p-2) is explicit in every version. The charge (44) can
diverge as p approaches 2; no contrary uniform bound is asserted.
The objective proof below does not absorb this factor into o(1).
Absorbed trajectories have zero subsequent cost, and no logarithm is
taken across a zero or a sign change.

### 10.4. Uniform no-turn payoff and the full-word conclusion

On a same-sign nonzero interval of mass T, the exact recurrence gives

    R_entry>=r(T+1+u),
    v_end>=T r^p/p,
    z=r^(2p-2)/p<=u/(T+1).

Combining this with (34), and maximizing the resulting rational
function of u>=0, gives for T>=1/(2p-2)

    2F_p(X_end)/R_entry^2<=E_p(T),
    E_p(T)=[(2p-2)T+2p-1]^2
              /[4p(p-1)(T+1)^2(2T+1)].                  (45)

For completeness, the rational function is
[1+k_T u]/[T+1+u]^2 with
k_T=2-2/p+1/[p(T+1)]. Its derivative has the sign of
k_T(T+1)-2-k_T u; the displayed condition makes its maximizing
u=T+1-2/k_T nonnegative and substitution yields (45).

There is a bound independent of p>2:

    E_p(T)<=1/(2T).                                    (46)

To check it, put A=p-1>1 and clear the positive denominator
4pA T(T+1)^2(2T+1). The numerator of the difference between the
right and left sides of (46) is

    4A T^3+(2A^2+6A)T^2+(4A^2+4A-1)T+2A(A+1)>0.

If s is the last turn, or s=0 for no turns, put b=b(s), q=m-b.
When q>=1, the final interval contains a real H_q by (19). Its mass
T>=S_q>1 is above the threshold in (45), since p>2. Retaining the
original input radius via (42) gives

    P_p<=c^(-2b)/(2S_q)<=1/(2S_m).

The second inequality follows from S_(j+1)<c^2 S_j for j>=1,
proved directly from the word recurrence in Section 7. If q=0, the
last step turns and P_p<=c^(-2m)<1/(2S_m), since S_m<c^m and
c^m>2. Absorption gives P_p=0. This proves (31) for all initial
states and every allowed p, without excluding reactivation.
