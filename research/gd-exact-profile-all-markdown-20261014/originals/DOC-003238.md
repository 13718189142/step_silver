# A robust scalar clock obstruction with asymmetric nonperiodic derivatives

Status: complete candidate proof, unverified. No fact_submit is allocated to
this lane. Every ingredient used below is proved here; earlier unverified
findings are motivation only. This is a bound on a specified scalar function
class, not on all scalar functions and not on unrestricted multidimensional W.

## 1. The class and the bound

Set c=13/5, a=c^(-17), b_j=a c^j, and

    H_0=(),   H_(j+1)=(H_j,b_j,H_j),
    N_j=2^j-1,   s_j=sum H_j=a(c^j-2^j)/(c-2).

Let G_ref:[0,infinity)->[0,infinity) be nondecreasing and 1-Lipschitz,
G_ref(0)=0, and G_ref(cR)=c G_ref(R) for every R>=0. This is a reference profile,
not necessarily the derivative used by GD. Put kappa=97/100.

Independently choose two nondecreasing 1-Lipschitz functions g_+,g_- on
[0,infinity), both zero at zero, satisfying

    kappa G_ref(R) <= g_sigma(R) <= G_ref(R),   R>=0, sigma in {+,-}.  (1)

Define the single fixed function used by the algorithm by

    f(x)=integral_0^x g_+(t)dt                     if x>=0,
    f(x)=integral_0^(-x) g_-(t)dt                 if x<=0.     (2)

Its derivative is g_+(x) on the positive ray and -g_-(-x) on the negative
ray. It is continuous, nondecreasing and globally 1-Lipschitz: across zero,
g_+(R)+g_-(T)<=R+T. Thus f is finite, convex, differentiable and 1-smooth,
with f(0)=0=min f. It need not be even and neither g_sigma need satisfy
any radial scaling identity.

For every m>=17 and every x_0!=0, run GD on this same f with the full
predetermined schedule H_m. Then

    2 f(x_(N_m))/|x_0|^2 <= (1/(a c)) c^(-m).               (3)

All choices in (1), including the reference G_ref, may depend on m. In
particular, the number of knots, their locations, ray asymmetry and
nonperiodic changes can depend on the horizon. The constant in (3) is
independent of these choices.

The ratio of the supremum in (3) to the exact U_(N_m)^ZJ tends to zero.
An elementary estimate for that exact profile is proved in Section 5.

## 2. Quantitative closure for the reference clock

If G_ref is identically zero, (1) makes the objective zero. Otherwise G_ref(R)>0
for all R>0. Indeed, a zero at one positive radius, monotonicity and discrete
homogeneity would force zeros on arbitrarily large intervals. Define

    M_ref=max_(R>0) G_ref(R)/R,    ell_ref=1/M_ref,
    Clock_ref(R)=integral_1^R dt/G_ref(t),   R>0.

The ratio G_ref(R)/R is continuous and periodic under R->cR, so its positive
maximum exists on a compact radial period. Smoothness gives ell_ref>=1.
The clock Clock_ref is strictly increasing. Substitution t=cu gives a constant

    tau_ref=Clock_ref(cR)-Clock_ref(R)>0,                                    (4)

independent of R. Choose r>=17 minimal with b=b_r>=ell_ref. Thus

    b/c<ell_ref<=b.

Define

    D_ref=max_(R>0) [Clock_ref(b G_ref(R))-Clock_ref(R)].     (5)

The expression is continuous and radially periodic, so its maximum exists.
At a point attaining M_ref, b G_ref(R)>=R. Hence D_ref>=0.

We first prove

    tau_ref+D_ref <= (c-1)b.                                     (6)

Fix R>0, put u=G_ref(R)>0 and q=R/u>=1. If q<=b, monotonicity gives
Clock_ref(bu)-Clock_ref(R)<=(b-q). On [R,cR], the endpoint values u,cu and the
1-Lipschitz condition give

    G_ref(t)>=max(u, cu-(cR-t)).

The switch is at cR-(c-1)u, inside [R,cR]. Integration of the reciprocal
therefore yields

    tau_ref <= (c-1)(q-1)+log c.

Adding proves

    tau_ref+Clock_ref(bu)-Clock_ref(R)
      <= b+(c-2)q-(c-1)+log c < (c-1)b,

because q<=b, c>2 and log c<c-1. If q>=b, instead write the expression as
Clock_ref(cbu)-Clock_ref(R). When cbu<R it is negative. Otherwise G_ref(t)>=u on its integration
interval, so it is at most cb-q<=(c-1)b. This proves (6) after maximizing.

We need the slightly stronger block budget

    kappa s_r >= tau_ref+D_ref.                                 (7)

For r>=18, use (10/13)^18<1/100. One direct exact check is
13^9=10604499373>10^10, followed by squaring. Consequently

    s_r/b_r = [1-(10/13)^r]/(3/5) > 33/20,
    kappa s_r/b_r > (97/100)(33/20)=3201/2000>8/5=c-1.

This and (6) imply (7). If r=17, then b=1 and ell_ref=1. In that case G_ref is
identically R: a radius R_0 attaining G_ref(R_0)=R_0 forces G_ref(t)=t on
[0,R_0] by the Lipschitz bound in both directions; discrete homogeneity
extends this to every radius. Thus tau_ref=log c and D_ref=0. Also s_17>=13/8:
indeed (10/13)^3<1/2 and (10/13)^2<4/5 give (10/13)^17<1/40.
It follows that kappa s_17>=1261/800>1>log(13/5), proving (7) here too.
For the last strict inequality, exp(1)>1+1+1/2+1/6=8/3>13/5.

## 3. Updates for the actual, possibly nonperiodic and asymmetric function

Every step h within H_r is at most b/c<ell_ref. By (1), g_sigma(R)<=R/ell_ref,
so such a step preserves the sign and sends R to

    R'=R-hg_sigma(R)>0.

Monotonicity of the reference G_ref and the lower inequality in (1) give

    Clock_ref(R)-Clock_ref(R')
       >= (R-R')/G_ref(R)
       = h g_sigma(R)/G_ref(R) >= kappa h.                    (8)

Therefore every full copy of H_r decreases this same clock by at least
kappa s_r, regardless of which ray it visits or how many gradient knots
it traverses. This is an estimate on the actual fixed-function trajectory.

A checkpoint of level k has step h=b c^k. If it changes sign, its next
radius satisfies

    R'=b c^k g_sigma(R)-R <= c^k b G_ref(R).

Using (4)-(5),

    Clock_ref(R')-Clock_ref(R)<=k tau_ref+D_ref.                                 (9)

If it preserves the sign its radius decreases, so (9) still holds because
D_ref>=0. If a step lands at zero, all subsequent iterates are zero and (3)
holds immediately. No clock is evaluated at zero.

The same reference clock is used on both rays. There is no assumption
equating the two actual derivatives, their areas, or their scale periods.

## 4. Sum the exact word and retain the complete objective

Suppose m>=r and put J=m-r and S=s_r. The word H_m contains 2^J copies of
H_r and 2^J-1 checkpoints. Level k occurs 2^(J-k-1) times for 0<=k<J.
Their sum of levels is 2^J-J-1 (obtained, for example, by induction on J).
Summing (8)-(9) along the original trajectory gives the explicit recurrence
bound

    Clock_ref(|x_(N_m)|)-Clock_ref(|x_0|)
      <= -2^J kappa S+(2^J-J-1)tau_ref+(2^J-1)D_ref
      = -2^J(kappa S-tau_ref-D_ref)-(J+1)tau_ref-D_ref
      <= -(J+1)tau_ref.                                     (10)

By (4) and strict monotonicity of Clock_ref,

    |x_(N_m)|<=c^(-J-1)|x_0|.                            (11)

Every function-value area in (2) is included; (1) gives the global bound

    f(x)<=integral_0^|x| G_ref(t)dt <= |x|^2/(2ell).

As ell_ref>b/c,

    2f(x_(N_m))/|x_0|^2
       <= ell_ref^(-1)c^(-2J-2)
       <= b^(-1)c^(-2J-1)
       = a^(-1)c^(-m-J-1) <= (1/(a c))c^(-m).

If 17<=m<r, every step is less than ell_ref. The entire trajectory stays on
its initial ray with decreasing radius. Write R=|x_(N_m)| and
w=g_sigma(R). Monotonicity gives

    R+s_m w<=|x_0|.

Smoothness and monotonicity also give g_sigma(t)<=min(t,w) for 0<=t<=R,
so f(x_(N_m))<=Rw-w^2/2. Completing the square proves

    2f(x_(N_m))/|x_0|^2 <= 1/(1+2s_m)
       <= (1/(2(13/8)a))c^(-m) <= (1/(a c))c^(-m).

Here s_m>=(13/8)a c^m follows from the calculation for m>=17 above.
This completes (3) for every allowed m. Multiplication of f by any L>0
gives the same normalized ratio and the identical GD trajectory with steps
h_t/L, so the result uses exactly the project's normalization.

## 5. Comparison to the exact Zhang--Jiang profile

Use the exact sequences in PROBLEM.md, with C_n=S_circle(n)+1 and
B_n=2S_bullet(n)+1, so U_n^ZJ=1/B_n. Their shifted join maps are

    Omega(A,B)=(A+B+sqrt((A+B)^2+4AB))/2,
    Gamma(A,B)=2A+B/2+sqrt(B^2+8AB)/2.

For nonnegative A,B,

    Omega(A,B)<=(A^(3/4)+B^(3/4))^(4/3).                   (12)

To check (12), normalize A^(3/4)+B^(3/4)=1, write
A=t^(4/3), B=(1-t)^(4/3), and compare 1 with the positive root of
z^2-(A+B)z-AB. The needed inequality is
t^(4/3)+(1-t)^(4/3)+[t(1-t)]^(4/3)<=1. Put
x=t^(1/3), y=(1-t)^(1/3). Its difference from 1 is

    x^3 y^3 [1/(1+x+x^2)+1/(1+y+y^2)-xy]>=0,

since each reciprocal is at least 1/3 and xy<=2^(-2/3)<2/3.
The case A=B=0 holds directly. Induction in the primitive DP gives
C_n<=(n+1)^(4/3). Next Gamma(A,B)<=B+4A, by squaring
sqrt(B^2+8AB)<=B+4A. Induction in the dominant DP, and
u^(4/3)+v^(4/3)<=(u+v)^(4/3), give

    B_n<=1+4n^(4/3).                                     (13)

The superadditive power inequality follows, for example, by differentiating
(u+v)^p-u^p in v for p=4/3 and comparing its derivative to p v^(p-1).
Thus the ratio in question is at most

    (1/(a c)) c^(-m) [1+4(2^m-1)^(4/3)] -> 0,

because c^3=2197/125>16. This is an exact-profile comparison, not an appeal
to a decimal asymptotic coefficient or a computation over finitely many m.

## 6. Explicit deformations and finite trajectory-preserving versions

The class has genuinely nonperiodic members. Choose measurable functions
w_sigma(t) in [kappa,1], independently on the two rays, and set

    g_sigma(R)=integral_0^R w_sigma(t) G_ref'(t)dt,
    f(sigma R)=integral_0^R (R-t) w_sigma(t) G_ref'(t)dt.        (14)

The derivative G_ref' exists almost everywhere, lies in [0,1], and integrates
to G_ref because G_ref is Lipschitz. Formula (14) gives (1) and proves the required
monotonicity and Lipschitz bounds directly. A piecewise constant w_sigma
with arbitrary breakpoints yields independent spatial changes; no periodic
pattern of those breakpoints is required. The choices are made once for the
entire run and the integral in (14) is the complete function value.

If G_ref is piecewise affine with finitely many pieces in one radial period,
and each w_sigma has finitely many pieces on every compact subinterval of
(0,infinity), any finite trajectory avoiding zero has an equivalent legal
finite-knot realization. On each ray choose R below every nonzero sampled
radius on that ray, and set u=g_sigma(R), A=f(sigma R). Then

    u^2/2<=A<=Ru-u^2/2.

The lower bound follows from g_sigma(t)>=max(0,u-(R-t)); the upper bound
from g_sigma(t)<=min(t,u). Set delta=R-u/2-A/u. The bounds say
0<=delta<=R-u. Replace the inner ray by zero up to delta, a unit-slope ramp
of length u, then a plateau of height u up to R. Its endpoint gradient
remains u and its area is Ru-delta u-u^2/2=A. All larger gradients and
values are unchanged. If a ray is never queried, choose any such R or
retain a simple legal zero derivative there. Cap the outer derivative after
all sampled radii. The two ray replacements join legally at the same
minimizer zero and preserve the actual GD trajectory, all sampled values,
and the original initial distance. The number of retained pieces may grow
with m. An arbitrary unrelated replacement is not asserted to obey (3).

## 7. Boundary of this result

This removes evenness and exact radial homogeneity from the actual
objective, and it allows unbounded geometric complexity. It retains a
specific global restriction: both ray derivatives must lie in the common
relative band (1) beneath one discretely homogeneous reference profile.
It is not a bound on every asymmetric function or on every nonperiodic
profile. A future scalar witness at the benchmark scale must evade this
uniform band condition (or its trajectory-preserving finite versions).
The general scalar question and the all-schedule, all-dimensional exact
minimax target remain open.
