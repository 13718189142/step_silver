# Retained-plane costs, a three-cell repair, and exact chain normalization

Status: UNVERIFIED joint candidate, 2026-09-26, worker max. No new verifier
submission. This extends the explicit two-cell interface ce636998be58c115 as

## 1. Exact activation cost of an inherited increasing plane

Suppose a legal prefix ends with query x=p+g, proximal point p, proximal value
F, and nonzero gradient g. Let u=-g/||g||. A prescribed bridge b>1 sends the
query to p+D u, where D=(b-1)||g||. Suppose an old plane has value F-rho at
p and slope sigma>0 in direction u, so its value on p+t u is

    F-rho+sigma t,     rho>=0.

Try to append a cell with local initial query (R_x,-R_y), endpoint normal
(epsilon,-1), gradient scale d>0, a new orthogonal direction e, and center

    p_new=p+theta u+d R_y e,    theta=D-d R_x>=0.

Give its concurrent planes value F_new at p_new. At its terminal proximal
point p_new, the inherited plane requires

    F_new>=F-rho+sigma theta.

At the old proximal point p, the FUTURE endpoint plane requires

    F_new<=F+d epsilon theta-d^2 R_y.

Thus even these two necessary inequalities force

    rho >= (sigma-d epsilon)(D-d R_x)+d^2 R_y.            (1)

This is unchanged by subtracting a common global minimum from every value.
It is a scoped necessary condition for the stated aligned-cell join, not a
general obstruction to multidimensional adversaries.

When rho=0, a feasible d must exceed sigma/epsilon, and (1) is equivalent to

    D >= d R_x+d^2 R_y/(d epsilon-sigma).

Write B=R_y/epsilon. Minimizing the right side over d>sigma/epsilon gives

    D >= (sigma/epsilon)(sqrt(R_x+B)+sqrt(B))^2.          (2)

Indeed with t=d epsilon/sigma>1 the expression divided by sigma/epsilon
equals (R_x+B)t+B+B/(t-1); its minimum occurs at
t=1+sqrt(B/(R_x+B)). This also proves that (2) is sufficient for this PAIR
of inequalities, not for the omitted old/future inequalities.

For rho>0, the smallest necessary stored slack is explicitly

    max(0, min_(0<=d<=D/R_x)
          [sigma D-(sigma R_x+D epsilon)d
                     +(R_y+epsilon R_x)d^2]).            (3)

The minimizing d is the clipped quadratic vertex
(sigma R_x+D epsilon)/[2(R_y+epsilon R_x)]. Thus the cost is not hidden
inside a feasibility hypothesis.

In the saturated rational two-cell example of ce636998be58c115, the last
gradient is (-9/50,6/25,-3/5), with norm 3/(2sqrt(5)). The old cap plane
has value F at the last center and outgoing slope 3sqrt(5)/25. A further
cell with local ordinary step 3, cap normal (1,0), and endpoint (1/2,-1)
has R_x=7/2,R_y=1,epsilon=1/2. Formula (2) therefore forces

    b >= 7+(8/5)sqrt(11).                                (4)

In particular bridge b=3 cannot append that cell at ANY scale or concurrent
level within this ansatz. Merely allowing positive values did not remove
activation cost after saturation. The retained variable that repairs this
particular obstruction is the actual slack rho of EVERY increasing old
plane, together with the future-plane margins; the latest level alone is
insufficient.

## 2. An exact three-cell repair, including the global minimum rise

Here is a complete repair of the b=3 example after deliberately giving the
second cell more inherited slack. It is not a new periodic-family claim.
Work in R^4 with coordinates (x,y,z,w), and put

    L_0=7/25+x,
    L_1=7/25+(3/4)x-y,
    L_2=-7/40-(3/10)x+(2/5)y,
    L_3=21/80-(3/20)x+(1/5)y-z/2,
    L_4=1/40+(3/50)x-(2/25)y+z/5,
    L_5=1/8+(3/100)x-y/25+z/10-sqrt(5)w/10,
    J=max(0,L_0,...,L_5),     f=env_1 J.

The actual prescribed schedule is (5,3,3,3,3). Its exact queries and proposed
gradients are

    x_0=(23/4,-1,0,0),       g_0=(1,0,0,0),
    x_1=(3/4,-1,0,0),        g_1=(3/4,-1,0,0),
    x_2=(-3/2,2,0,0),        g_2=(-3/10,2/5,0,0),
    x_3=(-3/5,4/5,0,0),      g_3=(-3/20,1/5,-1/2,0),
    x_4=(-3/20,1/5,3/2,0),   g_4=(3/50,-2/25,1/5,0),
    x_5=(-33/100,11/25,9/10,0),
                              g_5=(3/100,-1/25,1/10,-sqrt(5)/10).

Direct subtraction checks every GD update. Here is the full plane table at

    5  -2/25    -47/100      1/8      1/80      1/8      1/8

At row i, L_i is maximal and nonnegative. Since g_i is its normal,
x_i-z_i=g_i belongs to partial J(z_i), proving all proximal points and
gradients. The single envelope is convex differentiable and 1-smooth by
the direct subgradient-monotonicity argument for the Moreau construction.
The first, second and third concurrent cell levels are 7/25,1/5,1/8.

The global minimum is NOT zero before shifting. In fact

    (L_4+(2/5)L_3)/(7/5) = 13/140 =: M                  (5)

identically, because the normals cancel. Thus J>=M everywhere. To exhibit
all minimizers, set

    t=(-3x+4y)/5,   v=(-4x-3y)/5.

The simultaneous inequalities L_i<=M are exactly

    131/875 <= t <= 15/28,

They are consistent; hence min J=min f=M. Subtracting M from f leaves the

    131/700, 3/28, 9/280.

Thus the repair carries positive values relative to the TRUE global minimum.
The rise (5) is a real objective cost and cannot be omitted.

The initial point has t=-17/4,v=-4,z=w=0. At fixed feasible t, the nearest

## 3. Closed signed-profile updates retaining all old and future planes

There is exact finite bookkeeping for further joins in this growing-dimensional
geometry. It is useful state compression, but by itself is NOT a quantitative
all-schedule theorem. Suppose all current prefix planes L_ell and all prefix
proximal samples z_i with heights J(z_i)=V_i are already fixed and legal.
The terminal proximal point is p, gradient g, and outgoing direction u=-g/||g||.
Define two finite profiles, including the constant zero plane in the first:

    Hist(t)=max_ell L_ell(p+t u),
    Past(s)=min_i [V_i-s<u,z_i-p>].                      (8)

Append a cell of scale d, center p_new=p+theta u+d R_y e, and concurrent
level F_new, with local queries r_j=(u_j,-y_j), selected normals (p_j,-q_j),
local heights h_j=<v_j,r_j-v_j>, and U_j=u_j-p_j. All old/new plane inequalities
are equivalent to the EXPLICIT interval

    max_j [Hist(theta+d U_j)-d^2 h_j] <= F_new
      <= min_(p,q in new cell)
                    [Past(d p)+d p theta-d^2 R_y q].    (9)

Indeed the left side is the largest old plane at each new proximal point,
minus the known local height. The right side is exactly each future plane's
comparison at every stored prefix proximal point. Thus there is no discarded
nonadjacent pair. A positive F_new handles the zero plane automatically.

The next outgoing direction is u_new=(-epsilon u+e)/lambda,
lambda=sqrt(1+epsilon^2). The profiles close under the formulas

    Hist_new(t)=max{Hist(theta-epsilon t/lambda),
             F_new+max_(p,q) [-d(epsilon p+q)t/lambda]},

    Past_new(s)=min{Past(-epsilon s/lambda)
                              +s(d R_y-epsilon theta)/lambda,
             min_j [F_new+d^2 h_j
                     +s d(epsilon U_j+y_j-q_j)/lambda]}.
                                                               (10)

To verify the past formula, old proximal coordinates transform as
<u_new,z_i-p_new>=-epsilon<u,z_i-p>/lambda
                         +(epsilon theta-d R_y)/lambda.
For new proximal points the corresponding coordinate is
-d(epsilon U_j+y_j-q_j)/lambda. The Hist formula follows by the same
orthogonality and by the explicit normal products. Each appended cell adds
one fresh orthogonal direction, so this is genuinely growing-dimensional.

The saturated-plane test (1) is a mandatory scalar consequence of (9).
Neither the interval (9) nor its closed updates establish existence at the
target payoff: that remains the quantitative obligation. They do identify
the signed inherited state that must be controlled, instead of assuming a
fresh decreasing envelope at each join.

## 4. The global minimum of a whole arc chain is an oblique box problem

The global-minimum issue has an additional exact simplification. For cells
i=0,...,k, let u_0 be a unit vector and introduce fresh orthogonal unit
vectors e_(i+1). Write

    lambda_i=sqrt(1+epsilon_i^2),
    u_(i+1)=(-epsilon_i u_i+e_(i+1))/lambda_i.

Cell i has gradient scale d_i>0, center p_i, concurrent value F_i, and a
finite normal list (p,-q) on a concave arc with endpoints (c_i,0) and
(epsilon_i,-1). It suffices that

    (p-epsilon_i q)/c_i >= 1-q,     0<=q<=1,             (11)

which follows from concavity and the endpoint chord for the circular arcs.
Its physical normal is

    d_i[(p-epsilon_i q)u_i-lambda_i q u_(i+1)].

In coordinates y_i=<u_i,z>, its cap and endpoint planes have the forms

    Cap_i=A_i y_i+C_i,       A_i=d_i c_i,
    End_i=-B_i y_(i+1)+E_i,  B_i=d_i lambda_i,
    C_i=F_i-A_i<u_i,p_i>,
    E_i=F_i+B_i<u_(i+1),p_i>.

The u_i form a basis of dimension k+2. For each internal coordinate j=1,...,k,
define the opposite-pair balancing level

    m_j=(B_(j-1) C_j+A_j E_(j-1))/(A_j+B_(j-1)),
    M=max(0,m_1,...,m_k).                                (12)

If M<=min_i F_i, then the maximum of zero and ALL cell planes has EXACT
minimum M. Its minimizer set is the oblique coordinate box

    y_0 <= (M-C_0)/A_0,
    (E_(j-1)-M)/B_(j-1) <= y_j <= (M-C_j)/A_j  (1<=j<=k),
    y_(k+1) >= (E_k-M)/B_k.                              (13)

Proof: each opposing pair has minimum m_j, so J>=M. The intervals (13)
are nonempty precisely because M>=m_j, and the coordinates are independent.
They make every cap and endpoint plane <=M. An intermediate cell plane is

    a Cap_i+q End_i+(1-a-q)F_i,
    a=(p-epsilon_i q)/c_i>=0,   a+q>=1.

Its value is <=(a+q)M+(1-a-q)F_i<=M because F_i>=M. Hence no arc plane
was omitted from the minimizer test. Conversely every minimizer must satisfy
all cap/endpoint bounds. This proves (12)--(13) in full. If M>min F_i this
particular reduction is not asserted; the interior planes must be retained.

The actual nearest-minimizer distance is also explicit. Set

    r_i=-epsilon_i/lambda_i,
    y_i^0=<u_i,x_0>,  delta_i=y_i-y_i^0.

Since y_(i+1)=r_i y_i+sqrt(1-r_i^2)<e_(i+1),z>, squared physical distance is

    ||z-x_0||^2=delta_0^2+
                  sum_(i=0)^k (delta_(i+1)-r_i delta_i)^2/(1-r_i^2). (14)

Its minimum over the explicit box (13) is exactly the squared distance to
the nearest minimizer. Alternatively, choosing each internal y_j equal to
(E_(j-1)-C_j)/(A_j+B_(j-1)), and choosing the two external coordinates at
their finite displayed bounds, supplies an explicit legal minimizer and an
exact, possibly larger, denominator via (14). This needs no numerical search.

For a legal chain ending at the last local endpoint query, its normalized
terminal objective numerator is exactly

    2(F_k-M)+d_k^2(1+epsilon_k^2).                       (15)

Thus a positive-level multi-cell analysis must retain BOTH the signed history
profiles controlling legality and the opposite-pair minimum level M. The
minimum is not an unspecified external feasibility problem in this subclass.
Proving a choice of the actual block parameters that makes (9) legal with
the required uniform benchmark ratio is still open. This candidate makes no
claim that the recursive hostile schedules or arbitrary schedules have been
covered at that ratio.
