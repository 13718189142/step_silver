## 6. General balanced-circulation contact obstruction

This calculation is not limited to the rank-product law. Let J_ij>=0
be ANY zero-diagonal finite-node array with equal row and column sums
at every node. On ANY finite nonnegative word define

    R_J(g)=sum_(i!=j)J_ij[<g_i,x_j-x_i>+||g_i-g_j||^2/2].

Its value terms cancel by balance and its x_0 terms cancel in position
differences. Let C_J be its full symmetric gradient matrix. At common
gradients, R_J(e)=0. Its row-sum vector satisfies

    (C_J e)_i=(1/2)sum_j J_ij(P_i-P_j),
    P^T C_J e=(1/4)sum_(i,j)J_ij(P_i-P_j)^2.         (33)

For the first equality, the derivative of the source gradient gives
sum_j J_ij(P_i-P_j). The derivatives of the positions cancel because
row and column masses agree. The difference-square derivative vanishes
at e. Divide the derivative by2. For the second equality, expand the
sum of squared differences and use balance to equate the P_i^2 and
P_j^2 sums. This proves both equalities directly without invoking a
graph theorem.

It follows that C_J e=0 if and only if every positive J_ij joins
equal P_i and P_j. Necessity follows from the nonnegative sum in(33);
sufficiency follows from its first equality. With nonnegative h,
P_i=P_j means all intervening steps are zero, hence x_i=x_j as a
FORMAL identity even with independent gradients. In this case

    R_J(g)=(1/2)sum J_ij||g_i-g_j||^2 >=0.           (34)

Conversely, if some positive edge has unequal physical prefixes,
C_J e!=0 while e^T C_J e=0. Taking v=C_J e and expanding
R_J(e+t v)=2t||C_J e||^2+t^2 v^T C_J v shows a negative value
for sufficiently small negative t. Thus C_J is not PSD. In particular,
on a strictly positive word EVERY nonzero nonnegative balanced finite
circulation is an indefinite correction, despite exact value balance.

Moreover no collection of such additions can cancel its physical-
prefix contact work: the right side of(33) is additive and nonnegative.
At an exactly contacting base with zero incoming optimum rows and
