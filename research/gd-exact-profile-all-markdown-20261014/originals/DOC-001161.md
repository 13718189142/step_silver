## 4. A quantitative flux bound and why its total-clock closure is empty

For any mean-zero vector X, let M=sum_i(DX)_i,+,
J=N||(DX)_+||^2, and osc(X)=max_i X_i-min_i X_i. A directed path from a maximizing vertex to a minimizing
vertex shows osc(X)<=M. The mean-zero condition then gives |X_i|<=M,
and Cauchy--Schwarz gives

    M<=sqrt(J).                                      (14)

At the actual terminal time put e=q*-q_(ell_m). From (9)--(11),
D^T e=X_(ell_m). Thus neighboring values of e differ by at most M_(ell_m).
Two vertices of the edge cycle are at distance at most floor(N/2), so

    osc(e)<=floor(N/2)M_(ell_m).                      (15)

At an index minimizing q*, e_i=-q_(ell_m,i)<=0. Equations (14)--(15)
therefore imply, at every coordinate,

    q_(ell_m,i)>=q*_i-floor(N/2)sqrt(J_m).            (16)

The finite sine sum is sum_(k=1)^N sin(k pi/N)=cot(theta): multiply it
by 2 sin(theta), telescope cosine differences, and use Ntheta=pi/2.
Consequently the explicit amount of comparison flux is

    Q*_m=sum_i q*_i=[N s_N-cot(theta)]/[2 sin(theta)]. (17)

The actual total emitted flux is

    Q_m=sum_i q_(ell_m,i)=(1/4)sum_(k<ell_m) h_k M_k. (18)

Summing (16) yields

    Q_m>=Q*_m-N floor(N/2)sqrt(J_m),                  (19)

and hence the quantitative transport estimate

    J_m >= [(Q*_m-Q_m)_+/(N floor(N/2))]^2.           (20)

This is a proved scalar consequence of actual chronological transport.
It is NOT an explicit lower bound in m until Q_m is usefully bounded.
It is recorded with that limitation, not as a solution of the terminal
estimate or merely as the original nonlinear recurrence rewritten.

Consider trying to close (20) using only the total clock and the favorable
extra assumption M_k<=M_0=2z at every time. This assumption has NOT been
proved for the specified run. Even granting it gives only

    Q_m<=z S_m/2,
    S_m=sum H_(m-1)
       =2^(m-1)-1+[a/(c-2)](c^(m-1)-2^(m-1)).       (21)

The sum follows by summing t_j with multiplicity2^(m-2-j). Since
d/lambda^m->exp(1)/lambda^2, c->lambda, and 2/lambda<1,

    N^2/d->pi^2/6, S_m/d->1/(lambda-2)=lambda,
    Q*_m/N^2->(pi-2)/pi^2,
    z S_m/(2N^2)->3lambda/pi^2.                       (22)

The first limit for d follows from
(1-1/m)(1+1/m)^(m-2)->exp(1); the ceiling error in N is negligible.
The flux limit uses sin(theta)/theta->1 and s_N->1. In particular,

    lim (Q*_m-z S_m/2)/N^2
      =(pi-2-3lambda)/pi^2<0,                        (23)

because pi<4 and lambda>2. Therefore this concrete attempted clock

mass nonincrease would not repair it. A useful application needs a
strictly stronger, chronological or spatial estimate on the weighted
mass history, or a different terminal functional.

Support alone has a separate quantitative limitation. Positive edges can

## 5. Every actual step avoids every sector's exact annihilation resonance

This section and the next jointly prove the explicit but very small
terminal floor (A). They do not depend on the unproved mass assumption.

For a vertex vector X, let B_X be the ordinary integer graph Laplacian
of the strictly active cycle edges, those with (DX)_i>0. Then

    grad F(X)=B_X X/4, T_t(X)=(I-t B_X/4)X.           (24)

The symmetric matrix B_X has integer entries and spectrum in [0,4].
Indeed its quadratic form is a sum over a subset of cycle-edge squares,
between zero and4||X||^2. Ties simply omit zero-contributing edges.

We show the stronger statement: for any symmetric integer matrix B with
spectrum in [0,4], I-t_j B/4 is nonsingular for EVERY j>=0 with the
specified m>=8 and the same formula t_j. For use on H_m, only j<m is needed.

Write sigma for the field map sqrt(2)->-sqrt(2). Each t_j belongs to
Q(sqrt(2)). If I-t_j B/4 were singular, w=4/t_j would be an eigenvalue
of B. It is a root of the monic integer characteristic polynomial.
If w is rational, the rational-root argument makes w an integer. If w
is irrational in Q(sqrt(2)), sigma(w) must also be a root, and

    w sigma(w)=16/[t_j sigma(t_j)] must be an integer. (25)

Here is the elementary integrality justification in (25). The monic
minimal polynomial of w over Q divides the integer characteristic
polynomial. It has integer coefficients: write that minimal polynomial
and its complementary factor as rational multiples of primitive integer
polynomials. A product of primitive integer polynomials is primitive,
since otherwise reduction modulo a common prime would have zero product
of nonzero polynomials. The monic integer characteristic polynomial is
primitive. Comparing contents and leading coefficients therefore forces
the monic minimal polynomial to have integer coefficients. Its constant
coefficient is w sigma(w). This also justifies the rational-root case.

All the possibilities can now be excluded using short exact inequalities.

* j=0: put r=(m-1)/(m+1). Then
  t_0=1+r(sqrt(2)-1) and sigma(t_0)=1-r lambda<0,
  since r>=7/9 and lambda>2. Hence sigma(w)<0, impossible for an
  eigenvalue of B.

* j=1: t_1=2-1/m and w=4m/(2m-1) is strictly between2 and3.
  It is rational and is not an integer, also impossible.

* Even j>=2: put rho=(1+1/m)/lambda, which lies in (0,1). Then
  sigma(t_j)=1-beta rho^(j-1) is strictly between0 and1.
  Therefore sigma(w)>4, outside the spectrum of B.

* j=3: put s=beta(1+1/m)^2=1+1/m-1/m^2-1/m^3.
  For m>=8, 1<s<9/8. Since lambda^2+(1-sqrt(2))^2=6 and
  lambda^2(1-sqrt(2))^2=1,

      t_3 sigma(t_3)=1+6s+s^2 lies strictly between8 and16.

  Its upper bound follows from 1+6(9/8)+(9/8)^2=577/64<16.
  The number in (25) then lies strictly between1 and2, not an integer.

* Odd j>=5: sigma(t_j)>1 and
  t_j>=1+(7/8)lambda^4>16. For the last inequality,
  lambda^2=3+2sqrt(2)>5, so 1+(7/8)lambda^4>1+175/8>16.
  Thus (25) lies strictly between0 and1, also not an integer.

In all irrational cases t_j is indeed irrational: the nonzero rational
coefficient multiplies a power of 1+sqrt(2) with nonzero sqrt(2) component.
These arguments prove nonsingularity, uniformly over every sector and tie
pattern, without computing its spectrum or following the trajectory.
In particular a nonconstant state on this mean-preserving cycle cannot
be mapped to a constant state by any of the specified steps. The next
section supplies an explicit quantitative version rather than stopping
at qualitative nonannihilation.

    |det C_j(X)| |det sigma(C_j(X))| >= (4Q_j)^(-2N).

    |det C_j(X)| >= (48 Q_j^2)^(-N).                  (28)

    Cbar_j=48 Q_j^2(1+t_j)>1,

    ||T_(t_j)(X)|| >= Cbar_j^(-N)||X||.               (29)

ALL physical suffix steps with their exact multiplicities to obtain

    ||X_(ell_m)||
      >= [product_(j=0)^(m-2) Cbar_j^(2^(m-2-j))]^(-N)||v||.  (30)

For clarity, convert norm into the requested positive edge energy directly.

||X||^2<=-Nbe<=N(e-b)^2/4. A directed path from a maximum to a minimum
and Cauchy--Schwarz, as in Section4, give

    N||(DX)_+||^2 >= 4||X||^2/N.                    (31)

Using ||v||^2=N/2 in (30)--(31) proves the first explicit floor

    J_m>=2[product_(j=0)^(m-2)
                    Cbar_j^(2^(m-2-j))]^(-2N)>0.    (32)

This is an estimate of the actual terminal energy, not an intermediate peak.
It holds even if many large-step updates create negative mass.

    sum_(j=0)^(m-2) 2^(m-2-j)=2^(m-1)-1,
    sum_(j=0)^(m-2) j 2^(m-2-j)=2^(m-1)-m.

The second follows by subtracting twice the reversed geometric sum from

    sum_j 2^(m-2-j)log Q_j

    log product_j Cbar_j^(2^(m-2-j))
      <=(2^(m-1)-1)log144 + 2^(m-1)log(m+1)
        +2(2^(m-1)-m)log m+(2^(m-1)-m)log c
      <=2^(m-1)log[144 c m^2(m+1)].

Insert this in (32) to prove exactly

    J_m>=2[144 c m^2(m+1)]^(-N2^m)>0.

The same proof also shows strict positive objective at every physical
