# C61: projection polytopes retain the unrestricted objective supremum

Status: complete self-contained UNVERIFIED candidate. No numerical evaluation
or verifier submission. Accepted predecessor1252b8823bb5a290 supplies the
finite smooth-convex interpolant. The concrete final application also uses
accepted facte9a55f57461f253c. All additional claims are proved below rather

The new point is normalization by the ACTUAL closest minimizer. Moving far
in a direction of minimizers need not increase initial distance. The earlier
primitive-energy epigraph lifts did not establish this objective comparison.

## 1. Statement and exact scope

Fix any integer n>=0 and finite nonnegative physical word h=(h_0,...,h_(n-1)).
Use the definition of W_n(h) from accepted1252b8823bb5a290, with smoothness1.
For a nonempty compact convex set K containing0 define

  PFun61_K(X)=max_(G in K)(<X,G>-||G||^2/2).

Let ProjPoly61_n(h) be the supremum of

  2 PFun61_K(X_n)/dist(X_0,argmin PFun61_K)^2,

over polytopes K=conv(0,G_0,...,G_n) in dimension at most n+1 and actual
GD X_(i+1)=X_i-h_i grad PFun61_K(X_i), with positive initial distance.
The symbols G_i in this display are the actual sampled gradients, including
the terminal gradient; repetition and redundant vertices are allowed.

The claim is

                       ProjPoly61_n(h)=W_n(h).                 (1)

This is an equality of SUPREMA; attainment in the projection subclass is
not asserted. Every n+2-vertex polytope is permitted, with arbitrary real
Gram matrix. There is no fixed dimension, conditioning, face-angle, jump,
normal-support, or correlation restriction independent of n. The closest
minimizer is generally NOT the origin, even though0 is a minimizer.

Before taking the compact hull, a quantitative construction uses one
polyhedral epigraph in one extra coordinate. Its minimizer set is exactly
a vertical ray, its closest-minimizer distance is EXACTLY the old distance,
and its normalized objective loss has an explicit O(Lambda^(-2)) bound.
Every queried step and every ordered interpolation pair are retained.

Equation(1) reformulates the full lower-witness problem. It supplies no
choice of K meeting the Zhang--Jiang bound for arbitrary h, and no scalable
description of such a polytope on the near-critical ruler.

## 2. Elementary projection and GD bounds used below

For any nonempty closed convex K containing0, completing the square shows
that the unique maximizer defining PFun61_K is the Euclidean projection
P_K(X). The minimizer of the squared distance exists by coercivity and
closedness, and is unique by strict convexity. Its normal inequality is

  <X-P_K(X),G-P_K(X)><=0 for every G in K.                     (2)

Adding the inequalities for two points gives

  ||P_K(X)-P_K(Y)||^2<=<P_K(X)-P_K(Y),X-Y>.                   (3)

Evaluating the two defining suprema at their maximizers shows
0<=PFun61_K(Y)-PFun61_K(X)-<P_K(X),Y-X>
 <=<P_K(Y)-P_K(X),Y-X><=||Y-X||^2.
Thus PFun61_K is finite, convex, differentiable,1-smooth and has gradientP_K.
It is nonnegative and has minimum0 because0 belongs to K.

For any convex1-smooth function F, the necessary two-point interpolation
inequalities in accepted1252b8823bb5a290 imply, on adding them,

  <grad F(x)-grad F(y),x-y>>=||grad F(x)-grad F(y)||^2.

Consequently T_h(x)=x-h grad F(x) is Lipschitz with constant

  nu(h)=max(1,h-1), h>=0.                                    (4)

Indeed the squared difference of the outputs is at most
||x-y||^2+h(h-2)||grad F(x)-grad F(y)||^2. If h<=2 this is
at most||x-y||^2. If h>2, use the1-Lipschitz gradient bound to obtain
(h-1)^2||x-y||^2. Formula(4) includes h=0 and is also valid for PFun61_K.

Put S=sum_i h_i and A=product_i nu(h_i), with empty product1.
Every partial product and every consecutive product of the nu(h_i) is
at most A because every factor is at least1. If F has a minimizer x_star
and R=||x_0-x_star||, then ||x_i-x_star||<=R A at EVERY queried or terminal
sample. These are elementary finite bounds, not an asymptotic estimate.

## 3. A finite max-affine conjugate core

For now assume a finite nonnegative convex max-affine function psi61 on R^d
is identically0 on a neighborhood of0. Write

  psi61(p)=max(0,max_(j in J){<y_j,p>-delta_j}),
  B=max_(j in J)||y_j||,                                     (5)

where J is finite; take B=0 for the empty family. It is sufficient that
all nonzero y_j have delta_j>0. Terms with y_j=0 and delta_j>=0 can be
removed. Thus psi61 is globally B-Lipschitz, psi61(0)=0, and
0<=psi61(p)<=B||p||. Every subgradient of psi61 has norm at most B: it is a
convex combination of slopes of the active affine functions.

Set

  F61_core(x)=max_p{<x,p>-||p||^2/2-psi61(p)}.                      (6)

The maximizing p is the unique minimizer of
||p-x||^2/2+psi61(p). The strong-subgradient and maximizing-difference
arguments from accepted1252b8823bb5a290 give that F61_core is convex1-smooth
with this maximizer as its gradient. The same arguments also prove these
properties directly for(6). Since psi61>=0 and psi61(0)=0,0 is a minimizer
of F61_core with value0. The flat neighborhood makes it the UNIQUE minimizer:
if x!=0, choose a sufficiently small positive multiple p of x in that
neighborhood; then <x,p>-||p||^2/2>0.

Fix x_0!=0 and run actual GD for F61_core with the supplied full word h.
Use z_i for these original iterates, z_0=x_0, and R=||x_0||.

## 4. The exact epigraph trajectory and its closest minimizer

For any Lambda>0, put e=Lambda^(-2) and define the closed convex polyhedron

  K61_Lambda={(p,s) in R^(d+1): Lambda*s>=psi61(p)}.               (7)

Run actual GD for PFun61_(K61_Lambda) from

  X_0=(x_0,-Lambda).

Write X_i=(x_i,-Lambda*tau_i), with tau_0=1. At any point with tau>=1,
the projection onto(7) has the form

  G=(p,psi61(p)/Lambda),
  p=argmin_q {||q-x||^2/2+tau*psi61(q)+e*psi61(q)^2/2}.           (8)

For fixed q the optimal vertical coordinate is its lowest permitted
coordinate psi61(q)/Lambda, because the point's vertical coordinate is
negative and psi61>=0. Substitution into squared distance proves(8).
The objective in(8) is strongly convex and coercive: psi61 and psi61^2 are
convex, the latter because psi61 is nonnegative. Hence there is one p.
In particular this is a definition of an actual projection, not a relaxed
interpolation or numerical feasibility prescription.

The actual GD recursion is EXACTLY

  x_(i+1)=x_i-h_i p_i,
  tau_(i+1)=tau_i+e*h_i*psi61(p_i).                             (9)

Thus every tau_i>=1. The same K61_Lambda is retained at all steps.

Its function has precisely the minimizer set

  argmin PFun61_(K61_Lambda)={(0,t):t<=0}.                        (10)

For such a point every inner product with(p,s) in K61_Lambda is nonpositive,
so0 maximizes. Conversely, if the horizontal part x is nonzero, a small
p in the flat neighborhood of psi61, of the sign/direction of x, with s=0,
makes <x,p>-||p||^2/2 positive. If the vertical coordinate t is positive,
the permitted vector(0,s) with small s>0 makes ts-s^2/2 positive. These
arguments prove(10) in all cases.

It follows that the closest minimizer to X_0 is EXACTLY

  X_star=(0,-Lambda),  dist(X_0,argmin PFun61_(K61_Lambda))=R.    (11)

No Lambda^2 enters the initial squared distance. The initial norm relative
to the OTHER minimizer0 would be R^2+Lambda^2 and is not the normalization
used here.

## 5. Every interpolation pair, including the closest minimizer

Let psi61_i=psi61(p_i). Optimality in(8) gives a subgradient
s_i in partial psi61(p_i) with

  x_i-p_i=alpha_i s_i, alpha_i=tau_i+e*psi61_i>0.

For a finite max-affine psi61 this chain rule follows, for example, by taking
directional derivatives: the derivative of t->tau*t+e*t^2/2 at psi61_i is
the positive number alpha_i, so the subdifferential of its composition is
alpha_i partial psi61(p_i). Hence

  X_i-G_i=(alpha_i s_i,-Lambda alpha_i).

Use the actual value f_i=<X_i,G_i>-||G_i||^2/2. Direct expansion of the
signed interpolation residual gives

  Q_ij=f_i-f_j+<G_i,X_j-X_i>+||G_i-G_j||^2/2
      =<X_j-G_j,G_i-G_j>
      =alpha_j[<s_j,p_i-p_j>-(psi61_i-psi_j)]<=0.              (12)

The last sign is the full convex subgradient inequality for psi61. It is
valid for EVERY ordered pair, irrespective of their temporal order.
For the optimum sample in(11), set p_star=0,psi_star=0,tau_star=1,s_star=0.
Flatness makes0 the subgradient there. Formula(12) still applies, giving
in particular Q_(i,star)=-psi61_i<=0. The lifted data therefore retain all
queried, terminal and optimum comparisons with the actual normalization.

## 6. An explicit finite-horizon error bound

All quantities in this section refer to the same fixed h and psi61.
The full GD distance estimate(4), applied about X_star, gives
||X_i-X_star||<=R A and hence ||x_i||<=R A. The projection optimality(8)
also gives ||p_i||<=||x_i||. To see this, write x_i-p_i=alpha_i s_i;
the subgradient inequality at0 gives <s_i,p_i>>=psi61_i>=0, and so
<x_i,p_i>>=||p_i||^2. Thus

  psi61_i<=B R A,
  0<=tau_i-1<=e*S*B R A.                                   (13)

Let p^0=grad F61_core(x_i), so x_i-p^0=s^0 in partial psi61(p^0).
Subtract the two optimality conditions and use subgradient monotonicity:

  ||p_i-p^0||^2
    <=-(alpha_i-1)<s_i,p_i-p^0>
    <=(alpha_i-1)B||p_i-p^0||.

If the difference vanishes there is nothing to divide; otherwise divide
by its positive norm. Since alpha_i-1=(tau_i-1)+e psi61_i, (13) implies

  ||p_i-grad F61_core(x_i)||<=e B^2 R A(S+1).                  (14)

Compare(9) with z_(i+1)=T_(h_i)(z_i) for the same F61_core. Formula(4)
and(14), iterated from x_0=z_0, give at every sample

  ||x_i-z_i||<=e B^2 R A^2 S(S+1).                          (15)

Explicitly each error contribution h_j times the right side of(14) is
multiplied by a subsequent product of nu(h_t), at most A; summing h_j
gives(15). No assumption of uniformly small A or S is made.

At X=(x,-Lambda*tau), formula(8) also yields the value identity

  PFun61_(K61_Lambda)(X)
    =max_p{<x,p>-||p||^2/2-tau psi61(p)-e psi61(p)^2/2}.         (16)

This is at most F61_core(x). Evaluating its maximum at p^0=grad F61_core(x)
gives a reverse lower bound differing by at most
(tau-1)psi61(p^0)+e psi61(p^0)^2/2. At the terminal point, ||p^0||<=||x_n||
<=R A, so(13) bounds this loss by e B^2 R^2 A^2(S+1/2).
Both x_n and z_n lie in the radius R A ball; the gradient of F61_core has
norm at most R A throughout their line segment. Therefore(15) gives

  PFun61_(K61_Lambda)(X_n)
     >=F61_core(z_n)-e R^2 C61_lift(h),

  C61_lift(h)=B^2 A^2[A S(S+1)+S+1/2].                        (17)

Combining with(11), the normalized objective loss is at most

                         2 C61_lift(h)/Lambda^2.              (18)

Every constant is explicit and finite for every finite nonnegative word,
including zeros and the empty word. It is not claimed to be economical.

## 7. Every legal objective can be approximated by such a flat core

Take any original convex differentiable1-smooth F with minimizer0,
F(0)=0 and initial x_0!=0, R=||x_0||. It is harmless if0 was not its
closest minimizer: this is still one of the admissible normalizations in
the definition of W_n(h). For 0<beta<1 set

  F_beta(x)=(1-beta)F(x)+(beta/4)||x||^2.                    (19)

It is beta/2-strongly convex, has UNIQUE minimizer0, and its gradient is
L_beta-Lipschitz with L_beta=1-beta/2<1. It approaches F with its gradient
uniformly on bounded sets. The following explicit finite bound avoids any
unquantified exchange of limits. Let z_i and z_i^beta be their actual
trajectories for h. Both have norm at most R A. On this ball,

  ||grad F_beta-grad F||<=3 beta R A/2,
  |F_beta-F|<=3 beta R^2 A^2/4.

The first inequality follows from ||grad F(x)||<=||x|| and the second
from 0<=F(x)<=||x||^2/2. Iterating(4) as in(15) gives
||z_n^beta-z_n||<=3 beta R A^2 S/2. Consequently the normalized terminal
objective for F_beta differs from that for F by at most

                  beta[3 A^3 S+(3/2)A^2].                 (20)

Use its complete finite jets, including sample n and the zero optimum.
At each finite sample let y_i=z_i^beta-grad F_beta(z_i^beta) and
delta_i=F_beta(z_i^beta)-||grad F_beta(z_i^beta)||^2/2.
The smooth descent inequality with step1/L_beta gives
F_beta(z_i^beta)>=||grad F_beta(z_i^beta)||^2/(2L_beta).
Thus delta_i>0 unless the gradient vanishes. In the latter case strong
convexity gives z_i^beta=0 and y_i=0.

It follows that the finite function

  psi61(p)=max(0,max_(0<=i<=n){<y_i,p>-delta_i})                (21)

has exactly the properties in(5), in particular a flat neighborhood of0.
For example take a positive radius smaller than every
delta_i/||y_i|| with y_i!=0; if none exist, psi61=0 everywhere.
By accepted1252b8823bb5a290, proof paragraph2 applied to the complete
finite index set, F61_core interpolates ALL these jets. Its actual GD for
the unchanged word therefore has the same trajectory and terminal value
as F_beta. This invocation uses only the accepted all-pair construction,
not a reduced or conjecturally legal prefix.

Given any desired normalized loss eta>0, choose beta in(0,1) with the
right side of(20) below eta/2. Then form(21) and choose Lambda>0 with
2C61_lift(h)/Lambda^2<eta/2. Equations(18)--(21) give a real projection-function
instance of normalized payoff at least the original payoff minus eta.
Its exact closest-minimizer distance remains R.

## 8. Compactification and dimension compression prove(1)

For the lifted instance just constructed let G_0,...,G_n be its ACTUAL
gradients and put

  K_fin=conv(0,G_0,...,G_n).

This is a compact polytope contained in K61_Lambda. For every sample i,
G_i belongs to K_fin and was already the unique maximizer over K61_Lambda.
It is consequently also the unique maximizer over K_fin, with the SAME
function value. Induction on the actual GD recursion proves that replacing
K61_Lambda by K_fin preserves every sample, gradient and value in the entire
word, not just its endpoint.

The point X_star=(0,-Lambda) remains a minimizer for the compact hull:
its inner product with each G_i is -psi61_i<=0 and with0 is0. Therefore

  dist(X_0,argmin PFun61_(K_fin))<=||X_0-X_star||=R.            (22)

Shrinking this denominator cannot decrease a nonnegative objective ratio.
If the terminal value is positive, initial distance is positive, since
starting at a minimizer would make the actual GD stationary.

Finally let V=span(G_0,...,G_n), of dimension at most n+1, and orthogonally
project all X_i onto V. The function PFun61_(K_fin) depends only on this
projection, every gradient is already in V, and the same GD recursion and
values hold there. Its full minimizer set is the minimizer set inside V
plus V-perp, so its true initial distance is EXACTLY preserved by this
compression. Choose an orthonormal basis of V to realize it as R^dim(V).
The compact hull still has at most n+2 listed vertices.

This proves ProjPoly61_n(h)>=W_n(h) by arbitrarily accurate approximation of
each positive-payoff legal instance. Conversely every member of this
projection subclass is convex1-smooth with an attained minimum and positive
initial distance, so its ratio is at most W_n(h) by definition. This proves(1).
The positive value of W_n(h), including n=0, is part of the accepted fact;
it also follows by choosing a sufficiently small scalar quadratic curvature.

## 10. What this changes and what remains missing

The projection-function route itself is not too small for the objective
problem when true closest-minimizer distance is allowed: arbitrary compact
polytopes already have the full supremum, in the sense of(1). Earlier scoped
failures of cones, distance-squared functions, incidence relays, six-jump
coordinates, or special parabolas do not cover this class.

The target requires an explicit useful K or a lower bound on the entire
polytope game for each physical word. In the actual critical sequence
c_m=(1+sqrt2)(1+1/m), a_m=(1-1/m)/c_m,
H_0=(), H_(j+1)=(H_j,1+a_m c_m^j,H_j), parameters remain fixed within
each full horizon. This construction cannot replace them by the finite
C59 parameters or reset the vertical state in the right child.

For a chosen near-optimal full transcript, all the information about the
unknown solution is still present in psi61's planes and their joint Gram.
Equation(18) makes any desired finite accuracy attainable, but is not an
analytic policy for choosing those planes as m grows. The compactification
is performed only after the whole actual trajectory, retaining all its
normal comparisons. Treating vertices or copies independently would lose
the load-bearing joint constraints.
