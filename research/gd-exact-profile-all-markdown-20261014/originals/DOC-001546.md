## statement
Exact single-cut Huber lower bounds are asymptotically insufficient for the Zhang–Jiang profile.

For any integer n>=0 and h=(h_0,...,h_{n-1}) in [0,infinity)^n, let W_n(h) be the supremum of 2(f(x_n)-f_star)/(L||x_0-x_star||^2) over all finite-dimensional convex differentiable functions f with L-Lipschitz gradient and a minimizer x_star, all x_0!=x_star, and GD x_{t+1}=x_t-(h_t/L)grad f(x_t). Here L>0 is fixed and f_star=f(x_star). Put s(h)=sum_{t=0}^{n-1}h_t. For 0<=j<n let P_j=sum_{t<j}h_t and T_j=sum_{t>j}h_t, and let (u)_+=max(u,0). Define
K_n(h)=max{1/(1+2s(h)), max_{0<=j<n} (h_j-1)_+^2/[(1+P_j)^2(1+2T_j)]},
where the inner maximum is omitted at n=0.
Then W_n(h)>=K_n(h) for every such schedule. Every nonzero term indexed by j is attained as the normalized error of an explicit one-dimensional convex L-smooth instance with a minimizer.

For x,y>=0 define
phi(x,y)=(-x-y+sqrt((x+y+2)^2+4(x+1)(y+1)))/2,
psi(x,y)=(3-2y+sqrt((2y+1)(2y+8x+9)))/4.
Define S_circle(0)=S_bullet(0)=0 and, for n>=1,
S_circle(n)=max_{0<=k<n}[S_circle(k)+phi(S_circle(k),S_circle(n-k-1))+S_circle(n-k-1)],
S_bullet(n)=max_{0<=k<n}[S_circle(k)+psi(S_circle(k),S_bullet(n-k-1))+S_bullet(n-k-1)].
Put B_n=2S_bullet(n)+1, U_n^ZJ=1/B_n, and p=log_2 3.
For all n>=0, S_circle(n)+1<=(n+1)^p and B_n<=1+4n^p.
For every integer n>=2, the schedule ell^(n)_t=t+1, 0<=t<n, satisfies
K_n(ell^(n))<=16/n^2 and
K_n(ell^(n))/U_n^ZJ<=16(1+4n^p)/n^2 -> 0 as n->infinity.
Consequently this particular maximum of the global Huber witness and all these single-cut witnesses cannot itself yield a uniform asymptotic lower bound (1-o(1))U_n^ZJ. This is only a limitation of K_n; it makes no small-error assertion about W_n(ell^(n)) and does not resolve the unrestricted minimax target.

## proof
## Legal single-cut witness

Scaling permits L=1. Fix j with b=h_j>1; put P=P_j,T=T_j,
c=1/(P+1), a=c(b-1)/(2T+1). Define f(0)=0 and
f'(x)=max(-a,min(c,x)). This derivative is continuous, nondecreasing, and
1-Lipschitz. Hence f is differentiable, convex, 1-smooth, and minimized at0.
Start at x_0=1. Up to the checkpoint all gradients equal c: nonnegative steps
give x_i=1-c sum_{t<i}h_t>=1-cP=c for i<=j. Thus x_j=c, and the checkpoint
sends x_{j+1}=c(1-b)=-a(2T+1).

On the suffix all gradients equal -a. Every suffix partial sum is at most T,
so each iterate is at most -a(2T+1)+aT=-a(T+1)<=-a. Therefore
x_n=-a(T+1), f(x_n)=a^2(T+1/2), and

\[
2f(x_n)=a^2(2T+1)=\frac{(b-1)^2}{(P+1)^2(2T+1)}.
\]

The initial squared distance is1. Multiplying f by L proves the same normalized
statement for every L>0. Terms with h_j<=1 are zero and require no construction.
The global Huber term is proved in predecessor fact 0be8b54e0a33381c, proof
part 1, including zero and empty schedules. Taking a finite maximum preserves
the lower bound, because the worst-case function may depend on h and j.

## A crude exact-recursion bound sufficient for the separation

Set p=log_2 3, C_n=S_circle(n)+1 and B_n=2S_bullet(n)+1.
The primitive recurrence becomes

\[
C_n=\max_{0\leq k<n}\Omega(C_k,C_{n-k-1}),\qquad
\Omega(a,b)=\frac{a+b+\sqrt{(a+b)^2+4ab}}2.
\]

The function Omega is increasing in both nonnegative arguments. When a>=b>=0,
the inequality sqrt((a+b)^2+4ab)<=a+3b follows by squaring, with the right
square exceeding the left by8b^2. Thus Omega(a,b)<=a+2b.
For real u>=v>0, put r=u/v>=1. The derivative of
(r+1)^p-r^p is p[(r+1)^(p-1)-r^(p-1)]>0, so
(r+1)^p-r^p>=2^p-1=2. Consequently
Omega(u^p,v^p)<=u^p+2v^p<=(u+v)^p.
Symmetry handles v>=u. Since C_0=1, induction on n using
u=k+1, v=n-k proves C_n<=(n+1)^p.

The dominant mass recurrence, obtained directly from psi and also displayed
in predecessor 0be8b54e0a33381c, is

\[
B_n=\max_{0\leq k<n}G(C_k,B_{n-k-1}),\qquad
G(a,d)=2a+\frac d2+\frac12\sqrt{d^2+8ad}.
\]

Since sqrt(d^2+8ad)<=d+4a for nonnegative a,d, G(a,d)<=d+4a.
Starting with B_0=1, induction and the primitive bound give, for m=n-k-1,

\[
G(C_k,B_m)\leq1+4m^p+4(k+1)^p\leq1+4n^p.
\]

The last inequality uses u^p+v^p<=(u+v)^p for p>=1 and nonnegative u,v;
this follows, when v>0, by the increasing function (r+1)^p-r^p being at
least its value1 at r=0, and is equality when v=0. Hence B_n<=1+4n^p.

## Linear schedule defeats the single-cut functional

For h_t=t+1, the total sum is n(n+1)/2, so the global Huber term is at most
1/n^2. At checkpoint j, P_j=j(j+1)/2 and (h_j-1)_+=j.
When j>=n/2,

\[
\frac{j^2}{(1+P_j)^2(1+2T_j)}
\leq\frac{4}{j^2}\leq\frac{16}{n^2}.
\]

When j<n/2, the integers satisfy j+1<=(n+1)/2 and j+2<=(n+3)/2. Thus

\[
1+2T_j=1+n(n+1)-(j+1)(j+2)\geq(3n^2+1)/4\geq n^2/2.
\]

Also 1+P_j>=j, so this cut term is at most2/n^2. This includes j=0,
where the numerator is zero. Combining the cases gives K_n<=16/n^2.
Because U_n^ZJ=1/B_n, the ratio is at most16(1+4n^p)/n^2 and tends to0.
All estimates are analytic and valid for every integer n>=2.

The schedules ell^(n) are only counterexamples to the strength of K_n. No
small upper bound on their actual W_n is asserted; they may be very unstable.

## intuition
The selected cut gives an exact legal asymmetric-Huber witness with no parameter losses. Nevertheless even maximizing all such witnesses misses the benchmark by a vanishing relative factor on explicit linear schedules; richer cross-cut geometry is necessary for this approach.
