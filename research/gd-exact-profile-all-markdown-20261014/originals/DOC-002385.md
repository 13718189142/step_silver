## 2. The literal stress word and elementary transport quantities

Fix one enclosing m >= 1536 and retain its parameters in every child:

\[
 \sigma=1+\sqrt2,\quad \epsilon=\frac1{4m},\quad
 \beta=1-\epsilon,\quad c=\sigma(1+\epsilon),\quad a_h=\frac\beta c,
\]
\[
 H_0=\varnothing,\qquad H_{j+1}=(H_j,1+d_j,H_j),
 \quad d_j=a_hc^j,\quad S_j=\sum H_j,\quad D_j=1+2S_j.
 \tag{2.1}
\]

Every physical step is strictly larger than 1. For a word of length n write

\[
 P_0=1,\qquad P_t=\prod_{k<t}(h_k-1),\qquad
 A(h)=\sum_{t=0}^{n-1}h_t P_t.
 \tag{2.2}
\]

These are analytic finite sums and products, not numerical evaluations or an executed array. Let `Pi_j` be the complete product for H_j and `A_j=A(H_j)`. With `u=a_h/(c-2)` the exact identities are

\[
 S_j+1=uc^j+(1-u)2^j,
 \quad \Pi_j=\beta^{2^j-1}c^{-j},
 \quad q_j:=d_j\Pi_j=\frac{\beta^{2^j}}c.
 \tag{2.3}
\]

Here `1/2<u<1` and `2<c<5/2`; in particular `D_j<2c^j` for j>=1. Every prefix product of excesses in H_j is at most 1. To check this by induction, a prefix either lies inside the left child, ends just after the bridge, or consists of that left child and bridge followed by a right-child prefix. The latter two cases have prefix multiplier `q_(j-1)<1/c`. The empty prefix has product 1.

Splitting the actual sum in (2.2) into its left child, bridge, and right child gives

\[
 A_{j+1}=(1+q_j)A_j+\Pi_j+q_j.
 \tag{2.4}
\]

Since `Pi_j<=1` and `q_j<1/2`,

\[
 A_j<3(3/2)^j<2^{j+2}\qquad(j\ge1).
 \tag{2.5}
\]

Also `A_(j+1)>A_j`. None of these estimates resets the inherited parameter at a child.

At the entrance to the final H_(m-1) child in H_m, the true prefix product is

\[
 q:=d_{m-1}\Pi_{m-1}=\frac{\beta^{2^{m-1}}}{c}.
 \tag{2.6}
\]

For m>=16, `2^(m-1)>=4m^3`: it holds at 16, and the ratio between successive left and right sides is at least one because `2m^3>=(m+1)^3` for m>=4. Therefore

\[
 q<2^{-m^2},\qquad \Pi_m<2^{-m^2},\qquad
 D_m<2^{2m+1}.
 \tag{2.7}
\]

Indeed `(1-1/(4m))^(2^(m-1))<=exp(-2^(m-1)/(4m))<=exp(-m^2)<2^(-m^2)`. The very strong loss in (2.7) is specific to this same-parameter complete word; it is not claimed for arbitrary schedules.

## 3. Approach 1: actual repeated faces on a truncated cone

### 3.1 The general homogeneity boundary, including the original minimum

For a closed convex cone C, let

\[
 F_C(x)=\max_{g\in C}\{\langle x,g\rangle-\tfrac12\|g\|^2\}
       =\tfrac12\|P_Cx\|^2.
\]

The equality follows from the projection variational inequality: if `x=p+z`, with `p=P_Cx`, then `z` belongs to the polar cone `C^o` and `<p,z>=0`. Thus the minimum set is `C^o` and the distance from x to this entire minimum set is `||p||`. This also proves the asserted original closest-minimum radius; it is not a denominator replacement at a child.

For h=1+d>=1 and the next gradient `q=P_C(x-hp)`, write `x-hp=z-dp`. Cone projection gives

\[
 \|q\|^2=\langle q,z-dp\rangle
 \le-d\langle q,p\rangle\le d\|q\|\|p\|.
\]

Hence `||q||<=d||p||`. For any word with every step at least 1, and any original selected minimum x_*,

\[
 \frac{2F_C(x_n)}{\|x_0-x_*\|^2}
 \le\prod_t(h_t-1)^2.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

The same conclusion applies to a compact truncation when all queried projections coincide with the cone projections and the polar minimum cone is unchanged. This is the old homogeneity obstruction in direct form. Merely arranging many face reentries does not remove it.

### 3.2 A finite two-dimensional realization at every physical step

This realization makes the failure concrete rather than merely conditional on a possible itinerary. For the complete H_m let `S=S_m`, `n=2^m-1`, and

\[
 p=\min_{0\le t\le n}P_t>0,
 \qquad s^2=\frac{p}{16S(S+1)},\quad a=\sqrt{1-s^2},
 \quad u=(a,s),\quad v=(-a,s).
 \tag{3.2}
\]

Let C be the cone generated by u,v and use the compact triangle

\[
 K=\operatorname{conv}\{0,2u,2v\},\qquad x_0=u,
 \quad F_K(x)=\max_{g\in K}\{\langle x,g\rangle-\tfrac12\|g\|^2\}.
\]

The function is convex, globally 1-smooth, and has minimizer 0. Its full minimum set is C^o, and 0 is the original closest minimizer to x_0, so the original squared radius is exactly 1.

At time 0 the gradient is u. At time 1 the point is `-(h_0-1)u`. Thereafter write the candidate physical point as

\[
 x_t=((-1)^t r_t,-w_t),\quad r_t>0,\quad w_t>0,
 \quad \alpha_t=ar_t-sw_t>0.
\]

Projection onto C is then `alpha_t u` for even t and `alpha_t v` for odd t. To check this, its residual is orthogonal to the selected ray; its inner product with the other ray is nonpositive, since r_t,w_t>=0. Projection onto K is identical whenever `alpha_t<2`.

With the notation `r_0=a,w_0=-s,alpha_0=1`, the exact alternating update is

\[
 r_{t+1}=(h_ta^2-1)r_t-h_tasw_t,
 \qquad w_{t+1}=w_t+h_ts\alpha_t.
 \tag{3.3}
\]

The first transition gives `r_1=aP_1,w_1=sP_1`. Induction using every actual step gives

\[
 \frac{15}{16}aP_t\le r_t\le aP_t,\qquad
 0<w_t\le sS,\qquad \frac12P_t<\alpha_t\le P_t
 \quad(1\le t\le n).
 \tag{3.4}
\]

For the lower estimate, expand the first recurrence after time 1:

\[
 r_t=aP_t-P_t\sum_{k=1}^{t-1}
 \frac{h_ks(s r_k+a w_k)}{P_{k+1}}.
\]

Using `r_k<=a`, `w_k<=sS`, and `P_(k+1)>=p`, its relative loss is at most `s^2 S(S+1)/p=1/16`. Then

\[
 \alpha_t\ge(15/16)a^2P_t-s^2S
 \ge(225/256)P_t-p/[16(S+1)]>P_t/2,
\]

because `a^2>=15/16` and S>1. This proves positivity at the next query and closes the induction, including the assumed sign alternation. The upper estimates follow from `w_t>=0`, `r_(t+1)<=(h_t-1)r_t`, and `alpha_t<=a r_t` after the first query. Thus the truncating third edge never binds.

The trajectory really revisits the same slanted face after every two steps; it is not a collection of independent single-cut paths. Its complete terminal payoff satisfies

\[
 \Pi_m^2/4<\frac{2F_K(x_n)}{\|x_0\|^2}=\alpha_n^2\le\Pi_m^2.
 \tag{3.5}
\]

By (2.7) this is far below `1/D_m`. This policy is fully realizable at all depths, but conic homogeneity makes it quantitatively unusable. The parent identified this mechanism as an instantiation of an old route boundary; it is not credited as a new lower-bound advance.

## 4. Approach 2: translate the vertex and couple drift to repeated returns

### 4.1 One fixed triangle and its exact projection law

Fix b>0 and `0<s<1`, put `a=sqrt(1-s^2)`, and retain the rays `u=(a,s),v=(-a,s)`. Let

\[
 e=(0,-b),\qquad
 K=\operatorname{conv}\{e,e+Ru,e+Rv\},
 \qquad R>b/s.
 \tag{4.1}
\]

The origin lies strictly inside this triangle. Consequently F_K is convex, globally 1-smooth, and has unique minimum 0 with value 0: for x nonzero a sufficiently small positive multiple of x belongs to K and gives a strictly positive envelope value. There is no minimum-cone reset in this approach.

At a query

\[
 x=(\zeta r,-b-w),\qquad \zeta\in\{-1,1\},\quad r>0,\quad w\ge0,
 \qquad \alpha=ar-sw>0,
 \tag{4.2}
\]

provided `alpha<R`, its gradient is

\[
 g=e+\alpha(\zeta a,s).
 \tag{4.3}
\]

Indeed `x-e=(zeta r,-w)` projects onto the selected cone ray with coefficient alpha. That projected point lies on the corresponding finite triangle edge, so it is also the triangle projection. The opposing-ray normal inequality is the same as in Section 3. No projected point touches the third face.

The value at this query is exactly

\[
 2F_K(x)=b^2+2bw+\alpha^2.
 \tag{4.4}
\]

This follows by shifting the maximization variable to `g=e+z`: the value is `<x,e>-||e||^2/2+||P_C(x-e)||^2/2`. The shift has a genuine cross effect in the dynamics because e is not orthogonal to either ray.

Suppose the sign flips at the next physical step while (4.2) remains valid. Then the exact recurrence is

\[
 r_{t+1}=(h_ta^2-1)r_t-h_tasw_t
         =(h_t-1)r_t-h_ts(s r_t+a w_t),
\]
\[
 w_{t+1}=w_t-h_tb+h_ts\alpha_t,
 \qquad \alpha_t=ar_t-sw_t.
 \tag{4.5}
\]

The same alpha feeds both equations. This coupling is absent in an orthogonal sum of Huber components. There is no splitting into radius-weighted independent single-cut payoffs.

### 4.2 An explicit complete-H_m instance with no unproved itinerary condition

For H_m, take

\[
 b=1,\quad r_0=1,\quad w_0=2S_m,\quad
 s=\min\left\{\frac14,\frac{p}{16S_m(3S_m+1)}\right\},
 \qquad R=2/s+2,
 \tag{4.6}
\]

where p is the positive finite minimum in (3.2). All of these choices depend on the whole physical word and are fixed before running it. Use the one triangle (4.1) and original start

\[
 x_0=(1,-D_m),\qquad \|x_0-0\|^2=D_m^2+1.
 \tag{4.7}
\]

The candidate recursion (4.5) is feasible through every depth and every physical step. More precisely,

\[
 S_m\le w_t<3S_m,\qquad
 \frac{15}{16}P_t\le r_t\le P_t,\qquad
 \alpha_t>P_t/2>0.
 \tag{4.8}
\]

Here is an induction that proves feasibility rather than assumes it. Let `T_t=sum_(k<t)h_k` be the physical prefix sum, distinct from the hierarchy sums S_j, and let `Q_t=sum_(k<t)h_k alpha_k`. The second recurrence gives

\[
 w_t=2S_m-T_t+sQ_t.
\]

When the preceding gradients are valid and `r_k<=P_k<=1`, their positive coefficients obey `alpha_k<=1`. Hence `S_m<=w_t<=2S_m+sS_m<3S_m`. Unrolling the first recurrence gives

\[
 r_t=P_t\left[1-\sum_{k<t}
 \frac{h_k s(s r_k+a w_k)}{P_{k+1}}\right]
 \ge P_t\left[1-\frac{s(s+3S_m)S_m}{p}\right]
 \ge(15/16)P_t.
\]

Because `a>=3/4`, `S_m>1`, and `3sS_m<=3p/[16(3S_m+1)]<=3p/64`, the next alpha is at least `(45/64)P_t-(3/64)p>P_t/2`. Thus the next r is positive, the sign really flips, and the next point satisfies (4.2). This closes the induction. All alpha values are below 1<R, so the third face is inactive for the entire forward run.

The terminal shape and exact payoff are

\[
 x_n=((-1)^n r_n,-1-S_m-sQ),\qquad
 Q=\sum_{t=0}^{n-1}h_t\alpha_t>0,
\]
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \frac{2F_K(x_n)}{\|x_0\|^2}
 =\frac{D_m+2sQ+\alpha_n^2}{D_m^2+1}.
 \quad}
 \tag{4.9}
\]

The original radius is fully paid, including the transverse drift. The two edges are visited in the pattern `u,v,u,v,...` throughout the literal complete H_m. There is one objective and one original minimizer; no future trajectory is overwritten, no wall is enlarged after use, and no child is renormalized.

At fixed finite word, sending s to zero through positive legal values gives the precise limiting payoff `(D_m+Pi_m^2)/(D_m^2+1)`: the tangential endpoint tends to Pi_m and must not be dropped. Thus the translated policy recovers the baseline order while keeping actual repeated-edge coupling. The next section shows that enlarging its angle or changing its original b,w_0,r_0 within the whole specified policy cannot pass the benchmark stress test.

## 5. A whole-class ceiling for this coupled policy

The class in this section permits arbitrary b>0, `0<s<1`, r_0>0, w_0>=0, and a finite R for which the whole physical trajectory satisfies (4.2)--(4.5), with strict alternation of the two slanted edges and no third-face projection. It is not restricted to the tiny-angle choice (4.6).

The conditions are geometric: every physical query, including the endpoint, lies on or below the horizontal line through the translated vertex; every projection lies strictly along the appropriate slanted edge; and the transverse sign changes at every step. Querying the vertex, entering the triangle, reaching above the vertex, holding one edge for several steps, or activating the third face is outside this policy. The selected minimum is necessarily the unique original 0.

### 5.1 Exact radius identity and two elementary consequences

Write `S=S_m`, `D=D_m`, `A=A_m`, `P=Pi_m`, `Q=sum h_t alpha_t`, and `v_0=b+w_0`. From (4.5),

\[
 0<r_t\le P_t r_0,\quad 0<\alpha_t\le ar_t,
 \quad Q\le aA r_0,
 \quad w_n\le ar_n/s\le aP r_0/s.
 \tag{5.1}
\]

The strict alpha inequality gives the last bound. The original radius and terminal twice-value are

\[
 R_0^2=r_0^2+v_0^2,
 \qquad N=b^2+2bw_n+\alpha_n^2
           =2b(v_0+sQ)-Db^2+\alpha_n^2.
 \tag{5.2}
\]

Completing the square in the actual b, followed by Cauchy--Schwarz in the original coordinates, gives

\[
 \frac{N}{R_0^2}
 \le\frac{1+s^2a^2A^2}{D}+P^2.
 \tag{5.3}
\]

This is only one estimate for an already specified legal trajectory. The proof below obtains a uniform quantitative ceiling from the actual final child, rather than treating (5.3) as an unproved global cost condition.

### 5.2 Small angle

If `sA<=1/8`, then by (2.7)

\[
 \frac{N}{R_0^2}\le\frac{65}{64D}+P^2
 \le\frac{33}{32D}.
 \tag{5.4}
\]

The last inequality uses `DP^2<2^(2m+1-2m^2)<1/64`, valid already at m>=16. This controls even angles chosen to depend on the entire horizon and word.

### 5.3 Larger angle: charge the full final child

Suppose `sA>1/8`, so `1/s<8A`. At entrance time tau to the final H_(m-1), (2.6) and (5.1) give

\[
 r_\tau\le q r_0,\qquad
 w_\tau\le a q r_0/s.
\]

Let `S_L=S_(m-1)` and `A_L=A_(m-1)`. Since the entire right child also obeys (4.5), its accumulated ray coefficient satisfies

\[
 Q_{\rm right}\le aA_L r_\tau\le aqA_Lr_0.
\]

Its terminal w is nonnegative, so the exact second recurrence requires

\[
 bS_L\le w_\tau+sQ_{\rm right},
 \qquad
 \frac b{r_0}\le\frac{aq(1/s+sA_L)}{S_L}\le9qA.
 \tag{5.5}
\]

Here `S_L>=1`, `A_L<=A`, `a<=1`, and `s<=1`. This is the cost of sustaining the drift through the actual final child. It retains the actual inherited product q; the denominator is still R_0.

Furthermore `w_n/r_0<8AP` and `alpha_n/r_0<=P`. Since `R_0^2>=r_0^2`,

\[
 \frac N{R_0^2}
 \le81q^2A^2+144qPA^2+P^2
 <2^{2m+12-2m^2}<\frac1D.
 \tag{5.6}
\]

For the middle inequality insert `q,P<2^(-m^2)` and `A<2^(m+2)` from (2.5)--(2.7); the coefficient is bounded by 226<256. For the last inequality multiply by `D<2^(2m+1)` and use `4m+13-2m^2<0` for m>=16. Every estimate applies to the whole physical suffix, regardless of how the original geometry was chosen.

Combining (5.4) and (5.6),

\[
 \boxed{\qquad
 \frac{2F_K(x_n)}{\|x_0-0\|^2}\le\frac{33}{32D_m}
 \quad\text{for the entire below-vertex, alternating-two-edge policy on }H_m.
 \qquad}
 \tag{5.7}
\]

Using only the supplied signed benchmark comparison,

\[
 B_{2^m-1}\frac{2F_K(x_n)}{\|x_0\|^2}
