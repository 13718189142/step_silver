## statement
A tighter exact all-split benchmark envelope and a conditional GD certificate threshold.

Fix L>0. For every integer n>=0 and predetermined nonnegative finite word h=(h_0,...,h_(n-1)), run gradient descent
x_(t+1)=x_t-(h_t/L)*grad f(x_t), 0<=t<n.
Let W_n(h) be the supremum of 2(f(x_n)-f(x_star))/(L||x_0-x_star||^2) over every finite Euclidean dimension, every convex differentiable function f with L-Lipschitz gradient and an attained minimizer x_star, and every x_0!=x_star. Let R_n(h) be the same supremum without the numerator factor 2, so W_n(h)=2R_n(h). Put W_n_star=inf_(h in [0,infinity)^n) W_n(h), including the empty word at n=0.

Define lambda=1+sqrt(2), rho=log_2(lambda). For nonnegative real x,y put
Omega(x,y)=(x+y+sqrt(x^2+6xy+y^2))/2,
G(x,y)=2x+y/2+sqrt(y^2+8xy)/2.
Define C_0=B_0=1 and, for EVERY integer n>=1,
C_n=max_(integer 0<=k<n) Omega(C_k,C_(n-k-1)),
B_n=max_(integer 0<=k<n) G(C_k,B_(n-k-1)).
These are the original primitive and dominant benchmark capacities, with EVERY integer split retained. Put U_n^ZJ=1/B_n.

For every integer n>=0,
C_n<=(n+1)^rho and B_n<=(12/5)(n+1)^rho.

For each integer m>=1536, set epsilon=1/(4m), beta=1-epsilon, c=lambda(1+epsilon), and a=beta/c. Define the finite words
H_0=(), H_(j+1)=(H_j,1+a*c^j,H_j), 0<=j<m.
The same enclosing m, epsilon, beta, c, and a are used at every child. Here the hierarchy symbols H_j,a,c are local to this statement. Put S_j=sum H_j, n_m=2^m-1, and D_m=1+2S_m. Then H_m is a predetermined strictly positive word of length n_m and
B_(n_m)/D_m<47/50
for every integer m>=1536.

Finally, the following is a CONDITIONAL implication, with its entire hypothesis left unproved: IF
W_(n_m)(H_m)<=17/(16D_m) for EVERY integer m>=1536,
as a bound on the full function-class supremum W defined here, THEN for every such m,
B_(n_m) W_(n_m)(H_m)<799/800,
W_(n_m)_star/U_(n_m)^ZJ<799/800,
and

No unconditional full-function upper bound on W_(n_m)(H_m) is asserted. The unconditional conclusions are the two all-n benchmark envelopes and the hierarchy benchmark ratio. The original unrestricted minimax problem remains unresolved by this result alone.

## proof
The accepted predecessor b98bb807d514e80d is used only to identify the original benchmark coordinates and normalization: C_n=S_circle(n)+1, B_n=2S_bullet(n)+1, the displayed Omega and G recurrences, and the factor-2 definition of W_n. All inequalities needed here are proved in this proof. No saved numerical enclosure, evaluated recurrence, other accepted estimate, or unverified finding is used.

1. Exact exponent and positivity.

The identity lambda^2=2lambda+1 gives lambda(lambda-2)=1 and lambda^3=7+5sqrt(2). Since 2>49/25, sqrt(2)>7/5, so lambda^3>14. Therefore
lambda^15>14^5=537824>524288=2^19.
Taking the increasing base-2 logarithm gives rho>19/15>1. In particular rho/2>19/30. Also sqrt(2)<3/2, whence lambda<5/2 and lambda^2=2lambda+1<6.
The finite recurrences define positive finite capacities: C_0=B_0=1, and Omega and G map positive finite pairs to positive finite numbers. Each maximum is over a nonempty finite set when n>=1.

2. Primitive power inequality, including zero arguments.

For t>0 let E_be(t)=(1+t)^rho-1. It is positive. For z in the real line set Q_be(z)=log E_be(exp z), where log is natural. With t=exp z,
Q_be'(z)=rho*t*(1+t)^(rho-1)/E_be(t).
Since dt/dz=t, differentiation and collection of factors gives
Q_be''(z)
=rho*t*(1+t)^(rho-2)*[(1+rho*t)E_be(t)-rho*t*(1+t)^rho]/E_be(t)^2
=rho*t*(1+t)^(rho-2)*[(1+t)^rho-1-rho*t]/E_be(t)^2.
The function of t in the last square bracket is zero at t=0 and has derivative rho*((1+t)^(rho-1)-1)>0 for t>0. Consequently Q_be''(z)>0 for every real z. Convexity applied at z and -z yields
Q_be(z)+Q_be(-z)>=2Q_be(0).
Exponentiating and using 2^rho=lambda gives
E_be(t)*E_be(1/t)>=E_be(1)^2=(lambda-1)^2=2 for every t>0.

Take x,y>0, let u=x^(1/rho), w=y^(1/rho), and T_be=(u+w)^rho. Then
T_be-x=x E_be(w/u), T_be-y=y E_be(u/w).
Multiplying proves (T_be-x)(T_be-y)>=2xy, or
T_be^2-(x+y)T_be-xy>=0.
The roots of the latter polynomial are
[(x+y) +/- sqrt((x+y)^2+4xy)]/2.
For x,y>0 the smaller root is negative, the larger is Omega(x,y), and T_be>0. Thus T_be>=Omega(x,y). If one of x,y is zero, Omega(x,0)=x and Omega(0,y)=y, so the same inequality is equality; at (0,0) both sides are zero. This proves
Omega(x,y)<=(x^(1/rho)+y^(1/rho))^rho for all x,y>=0.

The polynomial x^2+6xy+y^2 is nondecreasing in each nonnegative variable, as is x+y. Monotonicity of the square root shows that Omega is nondecreasing in each argument on the whole nonnegative quadrant. For the primitive induction, C_0=1=(0+1)^rho. Suppose the bound is proved at all indices below n>=1. For every integer k with 0<=k<n, both k and n-k-1 are below n, so
Omega(C_k,C_(n-k-1))
<=Omega((k+1)^rho,(n-k)^rho)
<=(k+1+n-k)^rho=(n+1)^rho.
Taking the maximum over every allowed integer k proves C_n<=(n+1)^rho.

3. A scalar inequality that will control every dominant split.

For every real p>=19/30 and x in [0,1], we claim
(5/6)x^(2p)+(1-x)^p<=1.
The exponents are positive, so zero bases present no ambiguity. Both summands are nonincreasing functions of p for each fixed x in [0,1]. It suffices to establish the claim at p=19/30. Define the continuous function
Psi_be(x)=(5/6)x^(19/15)+(1-x)^(19/30), 0<=x<=1.
It has endpoint values Psi_be(0)=1 and Psi_be(1)=5/6. On the open interval,
Psi_be'(x)=(19/18)x^(4/15)-(19/30)(1-x)^(-11/30).
Thus each interior critical point satisfies
x^(4/15)*(1-x)^(11/30)=3/5,
equivalently x^8*(1-x)^11=(3/5)^30.
For x in (0,1), logarithmic differentiation of x^8*(1-x)^11 gives
8/x-11/(1-x)=(8-19x)/(x(1-x)).
It is strictly negative for x>8/19. Since 2/3>8/19, every x in [2/3,1) satisfies
x^8*(1-x)^11<=2^8/3^19<(3/5)^30.
For the strict inequality, multiply positive denominators and use the exact integer comparison
2^8*5^30=238418579101562500000000
<239299329230617529590083=3^49.
It follows that every interior critical point of Psi_be is in (0,2/3).

At an interior critical point, the derivative equation gives
(5/6)x^(19/15)=(x/2)*(1-x)^(-11/30).
Hence its value is
Psi_be(x)=(1-x/2)*(1-x)^(-11/30)=g_be(x).
The right side defines a positive continuously differentiable function on [0,2/3]. Its logarithmic derivative is
g_be'(x)/g_be(x)=-1/(2-x)+11/[30(1-x)]
=(19x-8)/[30(1-x)(2-x)].
The denominator is positive on [0,2/3]. Therefore g_be decreases on [0,8/19] and increases on [8/19,2/3]. Its maximum on [0,2/3] is at an endpoint. At the endpoints,
g_be(0)=1,
g_be(2/3)^30=2^30/3^19<1,
where 2^30=1073741824<1162261467=3^19.
Because g_be(2/3)>0, this proves g_be(2/3)<1. In particular every interior critical value of Psi_be is at most 1.
The continuous function Psi_be attains a maximum on [0,1]. If a maximizing point is an endpoint the value is at most 1 by the endpoint calculation; if it is interior its derivative vanishes and the critical-value argument applies. This proves Psi_be<=1 and therefore the claimed scalar inequality for all p>=19/30 and x in [0,1]. No differentiability at x=1 is required.

4. Dominant power inequality and induction over all splits.

Put kappa_be=12/5. For x,y>=0 define
r_be=(sqrt(y)+sqrt(y+8x))/2.
Direct squaring yields
r_be^2=y/2+2x+sqrt(y^2+8xy)/2=G(x,y).
Thus sqrt(G(x,y))=r_be, the larger nonnegative root of
z^2-sqrt(y)z-2x=0.
The other root is nonpositive. For a positive trial z, if this polynomial is nonnegative, then z is at least its larger root. This includes x=0 or y=0; when both vanish the larger root is zero.
The formula for G is coordinatewise nondecreasing on x,y>=0, because its linear terms and its square-root radicand have that property.

We prove B_n<=kappa_be(n+1)^rho by strong induction. At n=0 this is 1<=12/5. Suppose it holds below n>=1. For each original integer split k with 0<=k<n set u=k+1, w=n-k, and N_be=u+w=n+1. Then u,w>=1 and the primitive bound already proved gives
G(C_k,B_(n-k-1))<=G(u^rho,kappa_be*w^rho).
Take the positive trial z=sqrt(kappa_be)*N_be^(rho/2). The root polynomial for this last G value is nonnegative at z if and only if
kappa_be*N_be^rho-kappa_be*w^(rho/2)*N_be^(rho/2)-2u^rho>=0,
or, after division by the positive quantity kappa_be*N_be^rho,
(2/kappa_be)*(u/N_be)^rho+(w/N_be)^(rho/2)<=1.
Apply part 3 with x=u/N_be in (0,1) and p=rho/2>19/30. Because 2/kappa_be=5/6 and w/N_be=1-x, that part gives exactly this inequality. Consequently
G(u^rho,kappa_be*w^rho)<=z^2=kappa_be(n+1)^rho.
The same bound holds for EVERY split k, so its maximum B_n satisfies it. This completes the dominant induction, including n=0.

5. Exact mass of the same-enclosing-m hierarchy.

Fix an arbitrary integer m>=1536. The parameters epsilon,beta,c,a are those in the statement and do not change with j. They satisfy
0<epsilon=1/(4m)<=1/6144<1/8,
0<beta<1, c>lambda>2, a>0.
Every bridge 1+a*c^j is strictly positive. Length starts at zero and satisfies length(H_(j+1))=2length(H_j)+1, so induction gives length(H_j)=2^j-1 for all 0<=j<=m.
Summing the literal concatenation gives S_0=0 and
S_(j+1)=2S_j+1+a*c^j.
Define A_be,j=1+S_j for 0<=j<=m and v_be=a/(c-2)=beta/[c(c-2)].
Then A_be,0=1 and A_be,j+1=2A_be,j+a*c^j.
For all these indices the exact solution is
A_be,j=(1-v_be)2^j+v_be*c^j.
Indeed the initial value is 1, and substituting the right side into the recurrence gives
(1-v_be)2^(j+1)+(2v_be+a)c^j
=(1-v_be)2^(j+1)+v_be*c^(j+1),
using v_be(c-2)=a.

For t>2, t(t-2) is strictly increasing since its derivative is 2t-2>0. Thus c>lambda>2 and lambda(lambda-2)=1 imply c(c-2)>1. Since 0<beta<1, one has 0<v_be<1, so A_be,m>=v_be*c^m.

Expanding c=lambda(1+epsilon) gives
c(c-2)=1+2(lambda+1)epsilon+lambda^2 epsilon^2
<1+7epsilon+6epsilon^2,
where the strict upper bound uses lambda<5/2 and lambda^2<6.
The numerator beta=1-epsilon is positive, so
v_be>(1-epsilon)/(1+7epsilon+6epsilon^2).
Furthermore
(1-8epsilon)(1+7epsilon+6epsilon^2)
=1-epsilon-50epsilon^2-48epsilon^3<1-epsilon.
Division by the positive denominator shows v_be>1-8epsilon=1-2/m>0.

The binomial expansion at integer m>=2 has nonnegative terms. Its first three terms give
(1+1/(4m))^m
>=1+1/4+[m(m-1)/2]/(16m^2)
=41/32-1/(32m)>0.
Also 2^m>m for integers m>=1: at m=1 this is 2>1; if 2^m>m then 2^(m+1)>2m>=m+1. Since lambda>2, lambda^m>m and lambda^(-m)<1/m.

Now D_m=1+2S_m=2A_be,m-1 is positive. Using the exact A_be,m formula, then the positive lower bounds just proved, yields
D_m/lambda^m
>=2v_be*(1+1/(4m))^m-lambda^(-m)
>2(1-2/m)*(41/32-1/(32m))-1/m
=41/16-99/(16m)+1/(8m^2)
>41/16-99/(16m)
>=41/16-99/(16*1536)
=20959/8192
>120/47.
The last strict comparison follows from
20959*47=985073>983040=8192*120.
Every division and multiplication in this chain is by a positive quantity, and m=1536 is included.

At the actual horizon n_m=2^m-1, part 4 gives
B_(n_m)<=(12/5)(2^m)^rho=(12/5)lambda^m.
Combining the upper bound on B with the strict lower bound on D gives
B_(n_m)/D_m<(12/5)/(120/47)=47/50.
The strictness is retained because D_m/lambda^m>120/47 and B_(n_m)>0. This proves the unconditional hierarchy comparison with the SAME enclosing parameters at every child.

6. Conditional conclusion and normalization.

The definitions of W_n and R_n have identical suprema and differ only by multiplication of each nonnegative payoff by 2. Multiplication by this positive constant commutes with the supremum, so W_n=2R_n. We use W throughout, with no rescaling of the supplied hypothetical bound.

For this part ONLY, assume the full-class upper bound
W_(n_m)(H_m)<=17/(16D_m)
for every integer m>=1536. H_m is an admissible predetermined word of length n_m, so the infimum defining W_(n_m)_star satisfies
W_(n_m)_star<=W_(n_m)(H_m).
Because B_(n_m)>0 and U_(n_m)^ZJ=1/B_(n_m), part 5 gives
W_(n_m)_star/U_(n_m)^ZJ
=B_(n_m)*W_(n_m)_star
<=B_(n_m)*W_(n_m)(H_m)
<=(17/16)*B_(n_m)/D_m
<(17/16)*(47/50)=799/800.
The same displayed chain also proves B_(n_m)*W_(n_m)(H_m)<799/800.

For every integer cutoff N there is an m>=1536 with n_m>=N, since n_m=2^m-1 tends to infinity. Thus the infimum of W_n_star/U_n^ZJ over all n>=N is at most 799/800. Taking the increasing limit of these tail infima proves
liminf_(n->infinity) W_n_star/U_n^ZJ<=799/800<1.
This conclusion is conditional on the full-class upper hypothesis, which has not been established in this proof. Only the benchmark envelopes and the hierarchy benchmark comparison are unconditional.

## statement
Projection-simplex universality for finite words and exact full-class value of the actual seven-step H_3 block.

    2(f(x_n)-f(x_star))/(L||x_0-x_star||^2)
over every convex differentiable function f with gradient Lipschitz constant at most L and an attained minimum, every selected minimizer x_star, and every x_0!=x_star, using the actual updates
    x_(i+1)=x_i-(h_i/L)grad f(x_i), 0<=i<n.

(A) For each fixed word h, W_n(h) equals the supremum restricted, at normalized smoothness one, to the functions
    F_D(x)=max_(g in D)(<x,g>-||g||^2/2),

## 2. Elementary projection facts and the complete minimum set

For a nonempty compact convex set `D`, the maximizer defining `F_D` is unique:
completing the square identifies it with the unique nearest point `P_D(q)`.
For `v in D`, the exact characterization is

    P_D(q)=v  iff  <q-v,w-v><=0 for every w in D.     (2.1)

Necessity follows by differentiating the squared distance on the segment
from `v` to `w` at zero. For sufficiency, expand

    ||q-w||^2 = ||q-v||^2 + ||w-v||^2
                -2<q-v,w-v> >= ||q-v||^2.

For a finite convex hull it is enough to check every generating point,
because the left side of (2.1) is affine in `w`.

The two normal inequalities for `v=P_D(q)` and `v'=P_D(q')` imply

    <q-q',v-v'> >= ||v-v'||^2.

Thus the projection is 1-Lipschitz. Evaluating the affine maxima at each
other's optimizers gives

    0 <= F_D(q')-F_D(q)-<P_D(q),q'-q>
      <= <P_D(q')-P_D(q),q'-q> <= ||q'-q||^2.

Consequently `F_D` is differentiable with gradient `P_D` and is globally
1-smooth. It is convex as a maximum of affine functions of `q` and finite
because `D` is compact.

When `0 in D`, the minimum is zero and its COMPLETE minimum set is the cone

    C_D = { a : <a,v><=0 for every v in D }.          (2.2)

Membership makes every defining expression nonpositive, while `v=0` gives
zero. Conversely, if `<a,v>>0` for some `v in D`, then `theta v in D` for
`0<=theta<=1`, and

    <a,theta v>-||theta v||^2/2 > 0

for sufficiently small positive `theta`. Thus `a` is not a minimizer.
The polar here always means the cone with threshold ZERO, not a body polar
with threshold one. It is nonempty, closed, and convex, so every point has
a closest point in it. Existence follows, for example, by restricting a
distance-minimizing sequence to a bounded ball and taking a convergent

The scope remains nonnegative finite steps and finite horizons. Negative

M_cert=||g_n-V_alpha||^2+R_alpha.
Consequently full PSD of M_cert is equivalent to PSD of the entire prefix quadratic R_alpha in (x_0,g_0,...,g_(n-1)). For n>=1, with w_(-1)=alpha_star and w_t=alpha_star+sum_(j=0)^t alpha_j, one has 0<=w_(-1)<=w_0<=...<=w_(n-1)=1 and
V_alpha=alpha_star x_0-sum_(t<n)[h_t w_t-(w_t-w_(t-1))]g_t.
For n=0, alpha_star=1, V_alpha=x_0, and R_alpha=(q_cert-1)||x_0||^2. Any actual normalized trajectory attaining a feasible q_cert has g_n=V_alpha, R_alpha=0, and multiplier complementarity.

This result deletes no interpolation inequalities inside the queried prefix. It asserts existence of a selected optimal certificate, not that every optimal certificate has the selected support. It neither establishes Huber tightness nor removes earlier remote forward edges, transfers earlier-cut stationarity under arbitrary re-selection, or proves the project's asymptotic exact-profile conjecture.

## proof
1. Normalization, interpolation necessity, and elementary bounds.

For an original instance let R=||x_0-x_star||>0. The normalized function
F_0(z)=[f(x_star+R z)-f(x_star)]/(L R^2)
is convex differentiable with gradient Lipschitz constant1, a minimizer0,
and initial norm1. Its GD uses the same h and its normalized objective
is 2F_0(z_n). This also proves the converse scaling correspondence.
We may work with smoothness1 and the optimum sample zero.

For a differentiable function K with 1-Lipschitz gradient, integration
of its gradient along a segment gives
K(x+d)<=K(x)+<grad K(x),d>+||d||^2/2.
For convex K, apply this to K(z)-<grad K(x_i),z>, whose minimum is
at x_i, taking a unit negative-gradient step from x_j. Its minimum
value is no larger than the stepped value, so
K(x_j)-K(x_i)-<grad K(x_i),x_j-x_i>
    >=||grad K(x_j)-grad K(x_i)||^2/2.
This is exactly Q_ij<=0 for the actual samples, with our stated
orientation of Q.

For a normalized legal instance, put D_i=product_(t<i)(1+h_t), D_0=1.
Since grad K(0)=0, ||g_i||<=||x_i|| and ||x_(i+1)||<=(1+h_i)||x_i||.
Thus ||x_i||,||g_i||<=D_i, and
0<=K(x_i)<=||x_i||^2/2.
In particular W_n(h)<=D_n^2<infinity.
It is strictly positive: choose the scalar quadratic K(x)=kappa*x^2/2
with kappa=1/[2(1+sum_t h_t)] in (0,1], starting at1.
Every factor 1-kappa h_t is positive, and its normalized error is
kappa*product_t(1-kappa h_t)^2>0.

2. Explicit interpolation and the maximal prefix extension.

Let any finite collection of prefix jets (x_j,g_j,f_j), j in J_pre,
satisfy every ordered prefix inequality and include the zero optimum jet.
Define cjet_j,H_j,H,F as in the statement, on the same finite-dimensional
Euclidean space. The function H(v)-||v||^2/2 is the maximum of finitely
many affine functions and hence is convex. H is continuous, finite,
1-strongly convex, and has quadratic growth. For each x, the function
<v,x>-H(v) therefore has a unique maximizer, denoted v(x).

The maximizer condition is x in partial H(v(x)). For any x,x',
the two strong subgradient inequalities for H, added together, give
<x-x',v(x)-v(x')>>=||v(x)-v(x')||^2.
Consequently ||v(x)-v(x')||<=||x-x'||.
The definition of F, evaluated at v(x) and v(x'), gives
0<=F(x')-F(x)-<v(x),x'-x>
    <=<v(x')-v(x),x'-x><=||x'-x||^2.
Thus F is differentiable with grad F(x)=v(x), and its gradient is
1-Lipschitz. It is convex as a supremum of affine functions and finite
by the stated quadratic growth.

For i,j in J_pre direct expansion gives H_j(g_i)-a_i=Q_ij.
The term with j=i equals zero and every other term is nonpositive;
hence H(g_i)=a_i. The active quadratic H_i has gradient x_i at g_i,
and H>=H_i with equality there, so x_i is a subgradient of H at g_i.
The unique maximizer defining F(x_i) is consequently g_i, and
F(x_i)=<x_i,g_i>-a_i=f_i. This proves interpolation of all prefix jets.
In particular F(0)=0 and grad F(0)=0, so zero is a global minimizer.
The same construction, for any finite index set including the zero
jet, proves sufficiency of ALL pairwise Q inequalities for a legal
convex 1-smooth interpolant; repeated positions cause no exception.

If K is any other legal function interpolating the prefix, apply the
necessity calculation in paragraph1 between its sample at an arbitrary
x and each prefix jet j. Writing g=grad K(x), those inequalities give
H_j(g)<=<x,g>-K(x) for every j. Thus
K(x)<=<x,g>-H(g)<=F(x).
This proves the claimed pointwise maximality without importing an
interpolation or conjugacy theorem from a paper.

3. Exact value of the reduced Gram problem and primal attainment.

Realize any feasible Gram matrix by vectors in dimension at most n+2;
if its rank is zero, one may embed its zero vectors in R.
The complete prefix inequalities give the function F of paragraph2.
Its gradients at every queried prefix point equal the supplied g_i,
so the GD recursion reaches the same supplied x_n. No terminal gradient
is used to determine x_n, even when some steps vanish.

For the reduced terminal pair, the retained inequalities give
H_j(g_n)<=<x_n,g_n>-f_n for all j in J_pre. Therefore
f_n<=<x_n,g_n>-H(g_n)<=F(x_n).
If ||x_0||>0, normalization of this actual function yields
2F(x_n)<=W_n(h)||x_0||^2<=W_n(h).
If ||x_0||=0, all actual prefix iterates and x_n are the minimizer0,
so F(x_n)=0 and 2f_n<=0<W_n(h).
Thus the reduced supremum is at most W_n(h).
Every actual legal normalized trajectory is feasible for the reduced
Gram problem, so the reverse inequality holds.

For clarity the supremum is attained. Consider first the FULL Gram
problem, including every incoming terminal inequality. Its feasible
set is closed. Paragraph2 applied to the entire finite index set
I_n realizes every feasible point as a legal function; hence paragraph1
bounds all sampled gradients, all Gram entries by Cauchy--Schwarz,
and all values 0<=f_i<=D_i^2/2. The feasible set is therefore compact
in finite-dimensional matrix/value space. It is nonempty and has an
objective maximizer. Necessity and the interpolation construction
show its value is exactly W_n(h). The same full optimizer is reduced
feasible, so it also attains the reduced value.

At any reduced optimizer, the chain
W_n(h)=2f_n<=2F(x_n)<=W_n(h)||x_0||^2<=W_n(h)
has equal endpoints and W_n(h)>0. Thus ||x_0||=1,
f_n=F(x_n), and <x_n,g_n>-H(g_n)=F(x_n).
The maximizer of that conjugate expression is unique, so
g_n=grad F(x_n). The supplied terminal jet therefore belongs to the
same legal F as the prefix and satisfies every deleted incoming
inequality. This also proves the assertion for every reduced optimizer,
not just one optimizer chosen from the full feasible set.

4. Dual attainment for positive schedules, by separation.

Assume n>=1 and h_t>0 for every t<n. Let E_red be the retained
ordered pair set:
{(i,j): i,j in J_pre, i!=j} union {(n,j):j in J_pre}.
Use the matrix/value variables of the reduced primal.

There is a strictly feasible point with G_pr positive definite,
G_pr_(x0,x0)<1, and every retained Q strictly negative.
Choose n+2 distinct real theta_l in (0,1), let A_quad be the diagonal
matrix with these entries, let K(x)=<x,A_quad x>/2, and choose x_0
with every coordinate nonzero and norm less than1.
Write Poly_i(z)=product_(t<i)(1-h_t z), including Poly_0=1.
The columns (x_0,g_0,...,g_n) are evaluations at the theta_l of the
n+2 polynomials 1,z Poly_0,...,z Poly_n, times nonzero initial
coordinate factors. These polynomials have distinct degrees
0,1,...,n+1 and nonzero leading coefficients. A polynomial of degree
at most n+1 vanishing at n+2 distinct nodes is zero, so the evaluation
matrix is invertible and G_pr is positive definite.
For i!=j finite, Poly_i-Poly_j is a nonzero polynomial of degree
at most n; it cannot vanish at all chosen nodes. Poly_i cannot vanish
at all nodes either. Thus all finite sample positions are distinct
from each other and from star. Direct substitution for this quadratic
gives, with I the identity matrix,
Q_ij=-<x_i-x_j,A_quad(I-A_quad)(x_i-x_j)>/2<0.
This proves the full stated strictness.

Let w_opt=W_n(h), which is finite by paragraph1. Consider the open
convex set O of tuples (s,t,c), with s in R^(E_red) and t,c real,
for which there exist G_pr>=0 and real
sample values f with
s_ij>Q_ij(f,G_pr) for ij in E_red,
t>G_pr_(x0,x0)-1, and c<2f_n.
It is open since it is a union of open orthants; it is convex by
linearity of every Q in the Gram/value variables and convexity
of the PSD cone. The point (0,0,w_opt) is not in O.
Finite-dimensional separation of a point from a nonempty open convex
set gives a nonzero linear functional, chosen nonnegative on O
relative to that point. Independent upward changes of each s_ij
and of t, and downward changes of c, force its coefficients to be
(lambda_ij,rho,-eta) with lambda_ij,rho,eta>=0.
Taking limits to the boundary of each strict inequality yields,
for ALL G_pr>=0 and ALL real sample values f,
sum_(E_red)lambda_ij Q_ij(f,G_pr)
    +rho(G_pr_(x0,x0)-1)-eta*2f_n >= -eta*w_opt.       (S)

If eta=0, some lambda_ij or rho is positive, since the functional is
nonzero. Substitution of the strict primal point constructed above
makes the left side of (S) strictly negative, a contradiction.
Hence eta>0 and we divide the coefficients by eta to set eta=1.

The f_i are unrestricted real variables in (S). Their coefficients
must vanish:
out_i-in_i=2*1_(i=n), for 0<=i<=n.
After their cancellation, (S) says
trace(M_cert(rho,lambda,h) G_pr)-rho>=-w_opt for every G_pr>=0.
If this matrix had a negative quadratic direction, scaling the
corresponding rank-one PSD G_pr would contradict this inequality.
It is therefore PSD. Taking G_pr=0 gives rho<=w_opt.
Conversely the flow, lambda>=0, and full PSD give, for every reduced
feasible point,
2f_n<=rho G_pr_(x0,x0)<=rho
because rho>=0. Thus w_opt<=rho. Equality follows.
Setting lambda_(j,n)=0 on the omitted pairs gives the required
tight full certificate on the original positive-step formal space.

5. Zero-step compression and lifting in the ORIGINAL formal space.

Suppose n>=1 and exactly m>=1 of the original steps are positive.
List their original indices as
0<=i_0<i_1<...<i_(m-1)<=n-1,
and define the positive compressed schedule hhat_s=h_(i_s),
0<=s<m. Deleting zero steps changes no actual iterate; therefore
W_m(hhat)=W_n(h) by the same function/initial-point trajectories in
both directions.

Apply paragraph4 to hhat, obtaining a tight incoming-terminal-free
certificate lambdahat on {star,0,...,m}.
Define the injective index map
iota(star)=star, iota(s)=i_s for 0<=s<m, and iota(m)=n.
On the original formal space define
xhat_0=x_0, ghat_s=g_(i_s) for s<m, ghat_m=g_n.
Its compressed formal positions satisfy
xhat_s=x_0-sum_(r<s)h_(i_r)g_(i_r)=x_(i_s) for s<m,
and
xhat_m=x_0-sum_(r<m)h_(i_r)g_(i_r)=x_n.
These are polynomial identities in independent original formal vectors:
the omitted coefficients h_t are ZERO. They do not use any equality
between gradients sampled at identical actual positions.

Put lambda_(iota(s),iota(t))=lambdahat_(s,t) on mapped pairs, and
put every other original multiplier equal to zero.
At each mapped finite node the original flow equals its compressed
flow; at each unmapped node both in-flow and out-flow vanish.
The mapped terminal is n, so the original finite flow is exactly
out_i-in_i=2*1_(i=n). No multiplier points into original n.
Every mapped Q expression equals the corresponding compressed
Q expression after identifying the mapped values, whose coefficients
cancel by flow.

Let T_pick be the linear map selecting
(x_0,g_(i_0),...,g_(i_(m-1)),g_n)
from (x_0,g_0,...,g_n). The ORIGINAL full certificate matrix is precisely
T_pick^T Mhat_cert T_pick and is PSD because Mhat_cert is PSD.
Unmapped gradient columns are zero. No duplicate-gradient constraint
or auxiliary squared penalty is imposed or needed. This proves the
claimed original-index, full-formal-space lift, including leading,
interior, and trailing zero steps.

If m=0, including the empty schedule n=0, every actual iterate is x_0.
Smoothness at the minimizer gives 2f(x_0)<=||x_0||^2, and the scalar
quadratic x^2/2 attains equality. Thus W_n(h)=1.
Set lambda_(n,star)=2 and every other multiplier to zero.
It has exactly the required flow and no incoming terminal edges.
Since x_n=x_0 as a formal identity, its full quadratic is
||x_0||^2-2f_n+2Q_(n,star)=||x_0-g_n||^2.
This treats all-zero schedules and n=0 without any Slater assertion
about duplicated points. Parts(a) and the maximal extension remain
valid independently for these schedules by paragraphs2--3.

6. Exact terminal-square and full prefix-PSD parameterization.

Take ANY certificate whose incoming terminal multipliers vanish.
The finite terminal flow gives sum_(j in J_pre)lambda_(n,j)=2.
Consequently alpha_j=lambda_(n,j)/2 is a nonnegative probability
vector. All other multipliers lie inside the prefix, and their finite
flow is out_i^pre-in_i^pre=2alpha_i for i<n.
For any such alpha and prefix multipliers define y_j,delta_j,V_alpha,
R_alpha as in the statement, taking the star quantities equal to zero.

Expanding the outgoing terminal sum gives
2sum_j alpha_j Q_(n,j)
 =2f_n-2sum_j alpha_j f_j
       +2<g_n,sum_j alpha_j x_j-x_n>
       +sum_j alpha_j||g_n-g_j||^2
 =2f_n-2sum_j alpha_j delta_j
       +||g_n-V_alpha||^2-||V_alpha||^2.
Substituting into the full certificate proves
M_cert=||g_n-V_alpha||^2+R_alpha.
In R_alpha the coefficient of each finite f_i is
(out_i^pre-in_i^pre)-2alpha_i=0; it contains no f_n.
It is the complete quadratic form on ALL original prefix vectors.
The vector V_alpha is independent of the formal terminal gradient g_n.
Hence full PSD is equivalent to prefix PSD: prefix PSD implies full
PSD from the square identity, and full PSD implies prefix PSD by
setting the free formal vector g_n equal to V_alpha.
This is true even with zero-step duplicates; it is not a test only
on legal transcripts.

Conversely any alpha and nonnegative prefix multipliers with the
stated prefix flow define a full multiplier family by
lambda_(n,j)=2alpha_j and incoming terminal multipliers zero.
Their full finite flow is the required objective flow. If R_alpha
is PSD the full square identity certifies it. This proves both
directions of the parameterization for any real q_cert.

For n>=1, expand positions in
V_alpha=x_n-sum_(j in J_pre)alpha_j(x_j-g_j).
The coefficient of x_0 is
1-sum_(j=0)^(n-1)alpha_j=alpha_star.
For t<n, the coefficient of g_t is
-h_t+alpha_t+h_t sum_(j=t+1)^(n-1)alpha_j
 =-h_t[alpha_star+sum_(j=0)^t alpha_j]+alpha_t.
With w_t as in the statement, alpha_t=w_t-w_(t-1), giving
the claimed chronological formula and the nondecreasing w sequence.
For n=0 the probability simplex contains only alpha_star=1,
there are no prefix pairs, V_alpha=x_0, and
R_alpha=(q_cert-1)||x_0||^2.

Finally take an actual normalized trajectory attaining a feasible
certificate level q_cert. The certificate identity evaluated there is
M_cert=sum lambda_ij Q_ij<=0, while full PSD makes it nonnegative.
Every term lambda_ij Q_ij is nonpositive, so each is zero.
The square identity and prefix PSD then imply
g_n=V_alpha and R_alpha=0. This is an assertion about matching
actual trajectories, not a presumed rank condition on a primal.

7. Scope and proof dependencies.

The proof uses elementary smoothness, the displayed explicit convex
conjugate construction, finite-dimensional linear algebra, compactness
of the bounded finite Gram system, and a fully specified separating
hyperplane argument. No historical project KKT claim or unverified
finding is a premise. No external paper theorem is used as a black
box. In particular the separation argument is used only for genuinely
positive compressed steps, and the zero-step conclusion is obtained
by an explicit embedding into the original formal space.

Only incoming constraints at n, which is unqueried by the recursion,
are removed. Every ordered interpolation constraint inside J_pre
remains. The statement implies neither Huber tightness nor a removal
of earlier remote edges or a transfer of earlier-cut stationarity
under arbitrary re-selection; it does not decide the asymptotic
exact-profile problem.

## intuition
Only the final gradient is unqueried. The maximum of the prefix conjugate quadratics constructs a maximal legal extension at the already fixed final position, making all incoming terminal constraints value-redundant. Strict positive-step duality supplies a selected certificate, and choosing the query indices of positive steps embeds it into the original formal space when zero steps are present. The terminal outgoing multipliers sum to two, yielding an exact full Schur-square decomposition. No statement about removing queried-prefix edges or global Huber tightness is included.

## statement
Exact single-cut Huber lower bounds are asymptotically insufficient for the Zhang–Jiang profile.

For any integer n>=0 and h=(h_0,...,h_{n-1}) in [0,infinity)^n, let W_n(h) be the supremum of 2(f(x_n)-f_star)/(L||x_0-x_star||^2) over all finite-dimensional convex differentiable functions f with L-Lipschitz gradient and a minimizer x_star, all x_0!=x_star, and GD x_{t+1}=x_t-(h_t/L)grad f(x_t). Here L>0 is fixed and f_star=f(x_star). Put s(h)=sum_{t=0}^{n-1}h_t. For 0<=j<n let P_j=sum_{t<j}h_t and T_j=sum_{t>j}h_t, and let (u)_+=max(u,0). Define
K_n(h)=max{1/(1+2s(h)), max_{0<=j<n} (h_j-1)_+^2/[(1+P_j)^2(1+2T_j)]},
where the inner maximum is omitted at n=0.
Then W_n(h)>=K_n(h) for every such schedule. Every nonzero term indexed by j is attained as the normalized error of an explicit one-dimensional convex L-smooth instance with a minimizer.

For x,y>=0 define
phi(x,y)=(-x-y+sqrt((x+y+2)^2+4(x+1)(y+1)))/2,
psi(x,y)=(3-2y+sqrt((2y+1)(2y+8x+9)))/4.
Define S_circle(0)=S_bullet(0)=0 and, for n>=1,
S_circle(n)=max_{0<=k<n}[S_circle(k)+phi(S_circle(k),S_circle(n-k-1))+S_circle(n-k-1)],
S_bullet(n)=max_{0<=k<n}[S_circle(k)+psi(S_circle(k),S_bullet(n-k-1))+S_bullet(n-k-1)].
Put B_n=2S_bullet(n)+1, U_n^ZJ=1/B_n, and p=log_2 3.
For all n>=0, S_circle(n)+1<=(n+1)^p and B_n<=1+4n^p.
For every integer n>=2, the schedule ell^(n)_t=t+1, 0<=t<n, satisfies
K_n(ell^(n))<=16/n^2 and
K_n(ell^(n))/U_n^ZJ<=16(1+4n^p)/n^2 -> 0 as n->infinity.
Consequently this particular maximum of the global Huber witness and all these single-cut witnesses cannot itself yield a uniform asymptotic lower bound (1-o(1))U_n^ZJ. This is only a limitation of K_n; it makes no small-error assertion about W_n(ell^(n)) and does not resolve the unrestricted minimax target.

## proof
## Legal single-cut witness

Scaling permits L=1. Fix j with b=h_j>1; put P=P_j,T=T_j,
c=1/(P+1), a=c(b-1)/(2T+1). Define f(0)=0 and
f'(x)=max(-a,min(c,x)). This derivative is continuous, nondecreasing, and
1-Lipschitz. Hence f is differentiable, convex, 1-smooth, and minimized at0.
Start at x_0=1. Up to the checkpoint all gradients equal c: nonnegative steps
give x_i=1-c sum_{t<i}h_t>=1-cP=c for i<=j. Thus x_j=c, and the checkpoint
sends x_{j+1}=c(1-b)=-a(2T+1).

On the suffix all gradients equal -a. Every suffix partial sum is at most T,
so each iterate is at most -a(2T+1)+aT=-a(T+1)<=-a. Therefore
x_n=-a(T+1), f(x_n)=a^2(T+1/2), and

\[
2f(x_n)=a^2(2T+1)=\frac{(b-1)^2}{(P+1)^2(2T+1)}.
\]

The initial squared distance is1. Multiplying f by L proves the same normalized
statement for every L>0. Terms with h_j<=1 are zero and require no construction.
The global Huber term is proved in predecessor fact 0be8b54e0a33381c, proof
part 1, including zero and empty schedules. Taking a finite maximum preserves
the lower bound, because the worst-case function may depend on h and j.

## A crude exact-recursion bound sufficient for the separation

Set p=log_2 3, C_n=S_circle(n)+1 and B_n=2S_bullet(n)+1.
The primitive recurrence becomes

\[
C_n=\max_{0\leq k<n}\Omega(C_k,C_{n-k-1}),\qquad
\Omega(a,b)=\frac{a+b+\sqrt{(a+b)^2+4ab}}2.
\]

The function Omega is increasing in both nonnegative arguments. When a>=b>=0,
the inequality sqrt((a+b)^2+4ab)<=a+3b follows by squaring, with the right
square exceeding the left by8b^2. Thus Omega(a,b)<=a+2b.
For real u>=v>0, put r=u/v>=1. The derivative of
(r+1)^p-r^p is p[(r+1)^(p-1)-r^(p-1)]>0, so
(r+1)^p-r^p>=2^p-1=2. Consequently
Omega(u^p,v^p)<=u^p+2v^p<=(u+v)^p.
Symmetry handles v>=u. Since C_0=1, induction on n using
u=k+1, v=n-k proves C_n<=(n+1)^p.

The dominant mass recurrence, obtained directly from psi and also displayed
in predecessor 0be8b54e0a33381c, is

\[
B_n=\max_{0\leq k<n}G(C_k,B_{n-k-1}),\qquad
G(a,d)=2a+\frac d2+\frac12\sqrt{d^2+8ad}.
\]

Since sqrt(d^2+8ad)<=d+4a for nonnegative a,d, G(a,d)<=d+4a.
Starting with B_0=1, induction and the primitive bound give, for m=n-k-1,

\[
G(C_k,B_m)\leq1+4m^p+4(k+1)^p\leq1+4n^p.
\]

The last inequality uses u^p+v^p<=(u+v)^p for p>=1 and nonnegative u,v;
this follows, when v>0, by the increasing function (r+1)^p-r^p being at
least its value1 at r=0, and is equality when v=0. Hence B_n<=1+4n^p.

## Linear schedule defeats the single-cut functional

For h_t=t+1, the total sum is n(n+1)/2, so the global Huber term is at most
1/n^2. At checkpoint j, P_j=j(j+1)/2 and (h_j-1)_+=j.
When j>=n/2,

\[
\frac{j^2}{(1+P_j)^2(1+2T_j)}
\leq\frac{4}{j^2}\leq\frac{16}{n^2}.
\]

When j<n/2, the integers satisfy j+1<=(n+1)/2 and j+2<=(n+3)/2. Thus

\[
1+2T_j=1+n(n+1)-(j+1)(j+2)\geq(3n^2+1)/4\geq n^2/2.
\]

Also 1+P_j>=j, so this cut term is at most2/n^2. This includes j=0,
where the numerator is zero. Combining the cases gives K_n<=16/n^2.
Because U_n^ZJ=1/B_n, the ratio is at most16(1+4n^p)/n^2 and tends to0.
